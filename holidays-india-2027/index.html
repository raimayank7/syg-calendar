Central Government holidays 2027

The 17 gazetted holidays and 32 restricted holidays for Central Government offices, from the official list.

Source and date. Department of Personnel and Training, Government of India, Office Memorandum F. No. 12/2/2023-JCA dated 16 July 2026: "Holidays to be observed in Central Government Offices during the year 2027". Read by us on 12 November 2026. This is not a government website. Dates that depend on the Moon may be changed by the government.

The short version

  • Central Government administrative offices in Delhi and New Delhi observe the 17 gazetted holidays in the first table. Offices are closed on these days.
  • Each employee may also take any two of the 32 restricted holidays in the second table.
  • Offices outside Delhi keep 14 of the holidays compulsorily and choose 3 more for their state, so their list can differ slightly. The rules are explained below the tables.

Two of the 17 holidays share a date, so they fall on 16 different dates. Of those, 5 fall on a Saturday or Sunday: Independence Day (Sunday 15 August), Milad-un-Nabi (Sunday 15 August), Mahatma Gandhi's Birthday (Saturday 2 October), Dussehra (Vijaya Dashmi) (Saturday 9 October), Guru Nanak's Birthday (Sunday 14 November) and Christmas Day (Saturday 25 December). That leaves 11 holidays on working days. The order allows no substitute holiday when a festival falls on a weekly off.

The months with the most gazetted holidays are March, August and October. There is none in February, July and September. Each row also shows the date in the Indian national calendar, as the order prints it.

How the list works

The order sets out one system for Delhi and another for the rest of the country.

In Delhi and New Delhi, all 17 holidays in Annexure I are observed. Fourteen of them are fixed for the whole country, and three were selected for Delhi by the Department of Personnel and Training.

Outside Delhi and New Delhi, Central Government administrative offices observe these 14 holidays compulsorily: Republic Day, Independence Day, Mahatma Gandhi's Birthday, Buddha Purnima, Christmas Day, Dussehra (Vijaya Dashmi), Diwali (Deepavali), Good Friday, Guru Nanak's Birthday, Idu'l Fitr, Idu'l Zuha, Mahavir Jayanti, Muharram and Prophet Mohammad's Birthday (Id-e-Milad).

In addition they observe three more holidays, chosen for each state by the Central Government Employees Welfare Coordination Committee in the state capital from this list of twelve:

  • An additional day for Dussehra
  • Holi
  • Janmashtami (Vaishnav)
  • Ram Navami
  • Maha Shivratri
  • Ganesh Chaturthi / Vinayak Chaturthi
  • Makar Sankranti
  • Rath Yatra
  • Onam
  • Pongal
  • Sri Panchami / Basant Panchami
  • Vishu / Vaisakhi / Vaisakhadi / Bahag Bihu / Mashadi / Ugadi / Chaitra Sukladi / Cheti Chand / Gudi Padava / 1st Navratra / Nauraz / Chhath Pooja / Karva Chauth

The final list then applies to all Central Government offices in that state. The nine occasions not chosen are added to that state's restricted holidays. So an office in Chennai, Kolkata or Guwahati may be closed for Pongal, an extra day of Dussehra or Bihu where Delhi is closed for Holi or Janmashtami. If you work in a Central Government office outside Delhi, your office's own circular is the list that counts.

Restricted holidays

A restricted holiday is an optional holiday. The office stays open, and an employee who wants the day off for an occasion on the list may take it. Each employee may take two in the year. The list in Annexure II is for offices in Delhi and New Delhi; coordination committees in the states may draw up their own lists with occasions of local importance.

Dates that may change

For Id-ul-Fitr, Id-ul-Zuha (Bakrid), Muharram and Id-e-Milad, the order says the date may be changed depending on the sighting of the Moon. For Delhi the change is declared by the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions; elsewhere the coordination committees may change the date in line with the state government's decision. Because such a change can come at very short notice, the order allows offices to act on an announcement made through the Press Information Bureau, television, radio or newspapers without waiting for a formal order.

Diwali in 2027

The order notes that Diwali (Deepavali) falls on Friday, 29 October 2027. In some states the festival is celebrated a day earlier, on Naraka Chaturdasi. Central Government offices in a state may observe the Diwali holiday on that day instead, if that day alone is the state government's compulsory Diwali holiday.

Who else the order mentions

  • Industrial, commercial and trading establishments of the Central Government observe up to 16 holidays in a year, including the three national holidays (Republic Day, Independence Day and Mahatma Gandhi's Birthday) as compulsory holidays, and decide the rest themselves.
  • Union Territory administrations decide their lists under instructions of the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Banks are not covered: their holidays are regulated under instructions of the Department of Financial Services, Ministry of Finance.
  • Indian missions abroad notify their own lists under separate instructions.

Who this list does not cover

State government offices, banks, courts, stock exchanges, schools, colleges and private employers each have their own holiday lists. Many private offices follow a shorter list. Use this page for Central Government offices, and as a planning guide only for anything else.

How many Central Government holidays are there in 2027?

17 gazetted holidays for offices in Delhi and New Delhi, plus two restricted holidays that each employee chooses from a list of 32. 5 of the gazetted dates fall on a weekend.

When is Diwali in 2027?

The official list gives Friday, 29 October 2027 for Diwali (Deepavali).

Is this list valid for state government employees?

No. Each state government issues its own list. Many holidays are the same, but the dates and the number of days differ.

Do employees get another day off if a holiday falls on a Sunday?

No. The order says no substitute holiday is allowed when a festival holiday falls on a weekly off or other non-working day, or when two festivals fall on the same day.
