Using moon times for photos, fishing and planning

How to use full and new moon times: night photography, stargazing, tides, fishing and gardening claims, with what is and is not supported by evidence.

Moon times are used for several practical things. A few of them rest on solid facts; others are folk belief.

Things that are real

  • Stargazing: the best nights for faint stars and the Milky Way are the days around the new moon, when there is no moonlight. A full moon brightens the sky for the whole night.
  • Night photography: a full moon rising at sunset, when the sky is still blue, gives the best moon-and-landscape photographs. Plan for the evening of the full moon or the one before it.
  • Tides: the highest tides, called spring tides, come a day or two after the full and new moon, because the sun and moon pull together. Quarter moons give the weakest, neap tides. Exact times need a tide table for your port.
  • Walking at night: a moon near full is up for most of the night and lights country roads and trails.

Things that are belief, not proven

Sleep, mood, birth rates and crime are often said to change with the full moon. Careful studies have mostly not found a clear effect. Planting by the moon and fishing "by the moon" are traditions; if they work for you, keep them, but do not treat them as science.

How to use the tables on this site

Times are given in UTC, the world standard time, and in Indian Standard Time (IST, UTC plus 5:30). For other places add or subtract the difference to UTC. If the clock time falls after midnight in your time zone, the local date is the day after the date in the table.
