2030 at a glance

There are 12 full moons in 2030, the first on Saturday, 19 January 2030 and the last on Monday, 9 December 2030. The shortest gap between two full moons is 29.3 days and the longest 29.8 days; the average lunation is 29.53 days, so the dates move about 29 or 30 days from month to month and about 11 days earlier each year. There is no month with two full moons in %d, so there is no blue moon by the calendar-month rule.

New moons and dark skies

The 13 new moons are the best nights for stargazing and photographing the Milky Way: the first is on Friday, 4 January 2030 and the last on Tuesday, 24 December 2030. A lunation from one new moon to the next takes between 29.4 and 29.7 days in 2030.

Indian festivals and the full moon

Purnima and Amavasya, the full and new moon days of the Indian calendar, follow the lunar day (tithi) rather than the clock time shown here, so a festival can fall a day before or after the dates in this table. The IST column helps you see when the phase falls in India: where it falls after midnight, it belongs to the next calendar date.
