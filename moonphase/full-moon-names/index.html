Full moon names and what they mean

Wolf, Snow, Worm, Pink, Flower, Strawberry, Buck, Sturgeon, Harvest, Hunter's, Beaver and Cold: where the popular full moon names come from and how to use them.

The popular names of the full moons, such as the Wolf Moon in January or the Harvest Moon in autumn, are folk names, not science. They are used in North America and describe the season in the old northern calendar: the howling of wolves in midwinter, the digging of worms in spring, the ripening of strawberries in early summer. Many are said to come from Native American and colonial traditions and were popularised by almanacs. They are good for storytelling and photography, but they are not names an astronomer uses, and different regions and traditions use different ones.

The usual twelve

  1. Wolf Moon: the full moon of January
  2. Snow Moon: the full moon of February
  3. Worm Moon: the full moon of March
  4. Pink Moon: the full moon of April
  5. Flower Moon: the full moon of May
  6. Strawberry Moon: the full moon of June
  7. Buck Moon: the full moon of July
  8. Sturgeon Moon: the full moon of August
  9. Harvest or Corn Moon: the full moon of September
  10. Hunter's Moon: the full moon of October
  11. Beaver Moon: the full moon of November
  12. Cold Moon: the full moon of December

Harvest and Hunter's moon

The Harvest Moon is the full moon closest to the September equinox, so it can fall in September or early October. Around then the moon rises only a little later each evening, which in the days before electric light gave farmers extra light for harvesting. The next full moon is the Hunter's Moon.

Blue moon and supermoon

A blue moon in its modern meaning is a second full moon in one calendar month; it happens about every 2.7 years, and the moon is not blue. A supermoon is a full or new moon near its closest point to Earth; it can look a little bigger and brighter, but the difference is hard to notice without a comparison. Neither term has a strict scientific definition.

Indian full moon days

In the Indian calendar the full moon day, Purnima, belongs to a lunar day (tithi) and many festivals are tied to it, such as Guru Purnima, Buddha Purnima and Kartik Purnima. The tithi rules differ from the clock time of the full moon, so the festival date can be a day before or after the date shown here.
