2028 and the leap-year rule

2028 is a leap year, because it is divisible by 4 and not by 100. The rule of the Gregorian calendar is: a year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4, except years divisible by 100, which are leap years only if they are also divisible by 400. The extra day, 29 February 2028, is a Tuesday. The year has 366 days, which is 52 weeks and 2 days, so two weekdays occur 53 times.

Weekdays in 2028

2028 starts on a Saturday and ends on a Sunday, so the weekdays Saturday and Sunday occur 53 times in the year. Friday the 13th falls on 13 October in 2028, 1 time.

Working days and weeks in 2028

2028 has 260 weekdays from Monday to Friday before any public holiday is taken off, 53 Sundays and 52 ISO weeks. The month with the most weekdays is March (23) and the month with the fewest is April (20). Planners who count working days start from these figures and then subtract the holidays of their own country.

Nearby leap years

The previous leap year was 2024 and the next is 2032, so there are 8 years between them. Leap years usually come every four years; the exception is a century year such as 1900 or 2100, which is skipped unless divisible by 400, so the gap between 2096 and 2104 is eight years.
