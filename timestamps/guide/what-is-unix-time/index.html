What is Unix time?

One number for every moment: the seconds since 1 January 1970 UTC.

Unix time, also called epoch time or POSIX time, is the number of seconds since 1 January 1970 at 00:00:00 UTC, the Unix epoch. It is one number for every moment, the same for everyone in the world, which is why computers like it: no time zones, no months of different lengths, and a difference of two times is just a subtraction.

Seconds or milliseconds

Some systems, such as JavaScript, count in milliseconds. A current timestamp has 10 digits in seconds and 13 digits in milliseconds. If a date comes out in 1970 or in the year 50,000, the unit is probably wrong by a factor of 1,000.

Leap seconds

Unix time treats every day as exactly 86,400 seconds. When a leap second is added to UTC, the Unix clock repeats or smears a second, so Unix time is not a perfect count of elapsed seconds. For most uses the difference is invisible.

Julian date

The Julian date counts days from noon on 1 January 4713 BC. Unix time zero, 1 January 1970 00:00 UTC, is Julian date 2,440,587.5. To convert, divide the Unix time by 86,400 and add 2,440,587.5. The converter shows the Julian date of any timestamp.
