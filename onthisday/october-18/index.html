October 18: what happened on this day

What happened on 18 October: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 10 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2019: Protests in Santiago that started 11 days prior escalated into open battle against the Chilean national police, forcing President Sebastián Piñera to declare a state of emergency. Santiago
  • 1993: Nihilism, the first professional runway show from the British designer Alexander McQueen, was staged in London. Nihilism (collection)
  • 1968: At the Summer Olympics in Mexico City, American athlete Bob Beamon (pictured) achieved a distance of 8.90 m (29.2 ft) in the long jump event, setting a world record that stood for 23 years. 1968 Summer Olympics
  • 1963: The first cat in space, later known as Félicette, launched aboard a French Véronique rocket. Félicette
  • 1943: Pere Areny, convicted of the murder of his brother, became the last person to be executed by Andorra. Pere Areny
  • 1906: The Robert Burns Memorial Statue, the first public statue erected in New Brunswick, was dedicated in Fredericton. Robert Burns Memorial Statue (Fredericton)
  • 1873: Renton defeated Kilmarnock 2–0 in the opening match of the inaugural Scottish Cup. Renton F.C.
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: In an act of retaliation against ports that supported Patriot activities in the early stages of the war, the Royal Navy destroyed what is now Portland, Maine. American Revolutionary War
  • 1748: The War of the Austrian Succession ended with the signing of the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle. War of the Austrian Succession
  • 1565: The first recorded naval battle between Europeans and the Japanese occurred when a flotilla of samurai attacked two Portuguese trade vessels at the Battle of Fukuda Bay in Nagasaki. Samurai
  • 1356: The most significant earthquake to have occurred in Central Europe in recorded history destroyed Basel, Switzerland. 1356 Basel earthquake
  • 1081: Byzantine–Norman wars: Robert Guiscard led an invasion of the Byzantine Empire, capturing Dyrrhachium in present-day Albania. Byzantine–Norman wars

More events

  • 2019: NASA Astronauts Jessica Meir and Christina Koch take part in the first all-female spacewalk when they venture out of the International Space Station to replace a power controller. NASA Astronaut Corps
  • 2019: Riots in Chile's capital Santiago escalate into open battles, with attacks reported at nearly all of the city's 164 Metro stations. President Sebastián Piñera later announces a 15-day state of emergency in the capital. Estallido social
  • 2003: Bolivian gas conflict: Bolivian President Gonzalo Sánchez de Lozada is forced to resign and leave Bolivia. Bolivian gas war
  • 1992: Merpati Nustantara Airlines Flight 5601 crashes into Mount Papandayan near the town of Garut in West Java, Indonesia, killing 31. Merpati Nusantara Airlines Flight 5601
  • 1989: The Space Shuttle Atlantis launches on STS-34 to deploy the Jupiter-bound Galileo space probe. Space Shuttle Atlantis
  • 1979: The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) begins allowing people to have home satellite earth stations without a federal government license. Federal Communications Commission
  • 1978: Based on the world's first children's art museum, the Henrik Igityan National Centre for Aesthetics opens in Yerevan. Henrik Igityan National Centre for Aesthetics
  • 1967: The Soviet probe Venera 4 reaches Venus and becomes the first spacecraft to measure the atmosphere of another planet. Soviet Union
  • 1954: Texas Instruments announces the Regency TR-1, the first mass-produced transistor radio. Regency TR-1
  • 1945: A group of the Venezuelan Armed Forces, led by Mario Vargas, Marcos Pérez Jiménez and Carlos Delgado Chalbaud, stages a coup d'état against president Isaías Medina Angarita, who is overthrown by the end of the day. Venezuela
  • 1944: World War II: Soviet Union begins the liberation of Czechoslovakia from Nazi Germany. World War II
  • 1944: World War II: The state funeral of Field Marshal Erwin Rommel takes place in Ulm, Germany. Erwin Rommel
  • 1922: The British Broadcasting Company (later Corporation) is founded by a consortium, to establish a nationwide network of radio transmitters to provide a national broadcasting service. British Broadcasting Company
  • 1914: The Schoenstatt Apostolic Movement is founded in Germany. Schoenstatt Apostolic Movement
  • 1912: The Kingdom of Italy and the Ottoman Empire sign the treaty of Ouchy, by which the Italo-Turkish War is ended and the Ottomans loose Libya to Italy. Kingdom of Italy
  • 1898: The United States takes possession of Puerto Rico from Spain. Puerto Rico
  • 1887: Johannes Brahms conducts the premiere of his Double Concerto, composed for violinist Joseph Joachim and cellist Robert Hausmann. Johannes Brahms
  • 1860: The Second Opium War finally ends at the Convention of Peking with the ratification of the Treaty of Tientsin, an unequal treaty. Second Opium War
  • 1779: American Revolutionary War: The Franco-American Siege of Savannah is lifted. Siege of Savannah (1779)
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: The Burning of Falmouth (now Portland, Maine). American Revolutionary War
  • 1648: Boston shoemakers form the first American labor organization. Trade union
  • 1630: Frendraught Castle in Scotland, the home of James Crichton of Frendraught, burns down. Frendraught Castle
  • 1597: King Philip II of Spain sends his third and final armada against England, but it ends in failure due to storms. The remaining ships are captured or sunk by the English. Philip II of Spain
  • 1561: In Japan the fourth Battle of Kawanakajima is fought between the forces of Uesugi Kenshin and Takeda Shingen, resulting in a draw. Japan
  • 1377: Andronikos IV Palaiologos and his young son John are crowned by patriarch Macarius of Constantinople. Andronikos IV Palaiologos
  • 1281: Pope Martin IV excommunicates King Peter III of Aragon for usurping the crown of Sicily (a sentence renewed on 7 May and 18 November 1282). Pope Martin IV
  • 1166: Michael the Syrian, one of the most important Syriac historians, is consecrated as Syriac Orthodox Patriarch at the Mor Bar Sauma Monastery. Michael the Syrian
  • 1016: The Danes defeat the English in the Battle of Assandun. Danes
  • 629: Dagobert I is crowned King of the Franks. Dagobert I
  • 320: Pappus of Alexandria, Greek philosopher, observes an eclipse of the Sun and writes a commentary on The Great Astronomer (Almagest). Pappus of Alexandria

About the date itself

It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 18 October 2027 in 2027 is 18 Jumada al-awwal 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 42.
