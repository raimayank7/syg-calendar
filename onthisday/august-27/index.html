August 27: what happened on this day

What happened on 27 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2013: Religious violence between Hindus and Muslims erupted in Muzaffarnagar district, Uttar Pradesh, India, leaving 62 people dead. 2013 Muzaffarnagar riots
  • 2009: The Myanmar military junta and ethnic armies began three days of violent clashes in the region of Kokang. Tatmadaw Government (1988–2011)
  • 2006: Comair Flight 5191 crashed (wreckage pictured) while inadvertently attempting to take off from the wrong runway at Blue Grass Airport in Lexington, Kentucky, killing 49 of the 50 people on board, and causing the Federal Aviation Administration to modify air traffic control rules. Comair Flight 5191
  • 1990: American musician Stevie Ray Vaughan, one of the most influential guitarists in the revival of blues in the 1980s, was killed in a helicopter crash. Stevie Ray Vaughan
  • 1979: The Troubles: the IRA ambushed and killed 18 British soldiers near Warrenpoint, and assassinated Lord Mountbatten on his boat at Mullaghmore. The Troubles
  • 1964: South Vietnamese junta leader Nguyễn Khánh entered into a triumvirate power-sharing arrangement with rival generals Trần Thiện Khiêm and Dương Văn Minh, both of whom had been involved in plots to unseat Khánh. South Vietnam
  • 1896: In the shortest recorded war in history, the Sultanate of Zanzibar surrendered to the United Kingdom after less than an hour of conflict. Anglo-Zanzibar War
  • 1859: Edwin Drake successfully drilled for oil in Titusville, Pennsylvania, resulting in the Pennsylvania oil rush, the first oil boom in the United States. Edwin Drake
  • 1832: Black Hawk (pictured), the leader of the Sauk tribe of Native Americans, surrendered to U.S. authorities to end the Black Hawk War. Black Hawk (Sauk leader)
  • 1810: Napoleonic Wars: The French Navy defeated the Royal Navy, preventing them from capturing the harbour of Grand Port on Mauritius. Napoleonic Wars
  • 1776: British forces led by William Howe defeated the Continental Army under George Washington at the Battle of Long Island, the largest battle of the American Revolutionary War. William Howe, 5th Viscount Howe
  • 1656: The Netherlands and the Ahanta people signed the Treaty of Butre, creating a Dutch protectorate over the country of Upper Ahanta in modern-day Ghana. Ahanta people

More events

  • 2011: Hurricane Irene strikes the United States east coast, killing 47 and causing an estimated $15.6 billion in damage. Hurricane Irene
  • 2009: Internal conflict in Myanmar: The Burmese military junta and ethnic armies begin three days of violent clashes in the Kokang Special Region. Myanmar conflict
  • 2003: Mars makes its closest approach to Earth in nearly 60,000 years, passing 34,646,418 miles (55,758,005 km) distant. Mars
  • 1992: Aeroflot Flight 2808 crashes in Lebyazhy Lug during approach to Ivanovo Yuzhny Airport, killing all 84 aboard. Aeroflot Flight 2808
  • 1991: Moldova declares independence from the USSR. Moldova
  • 1985: Space Shuttle Discovery is launched on STS-51-I to deploy three communication satellites and repair a fourth malfunctioning one. Space Shuttle Discovery
  • 1980: South Korean presidential election: After successfully staging the Coup d'état of May Seventeenth, General Chun Doo-hwan, running unopposed, has the National Conference for Unification elect him President of the Fourth Republic of Korea. 1980 South Korean presidential election
  • 1975: The Governor of Portuguese Timor abandons its capital, Dili, and flees to Atauro Island, leaving control to a rebel group. Portuguese Timor
  • 1964: South Vietnamese junta leader Nguyễn Khánh enters into a triumvirate power-sharing arrangement with rival generals Trần Thiện Khiêm and Dương Văn Minh, who had both been involved in plots to unseat Khánh. Army of the Republic of Vietnam
  • 1962: The Mariner 2 unmanned space mission is launched to Venus by NASA. Mariner 2
  • 1955: The first edition of the Guinness Book of Records is published in Great Britain. Guinness World Records
  • 1943: World War II: Japanese forces evacuate New Georgia Island in the Pacific Theater of Operations during World War II. World War II
  • 1942: The Holocaust: first day of the Sarny Massacre, perpetrated by German military forces. The Holocaust
  • 1933: The first Afrikaans Bible is introduced during a Bible Festival in Bloemfontein. Bible translations into Afrikaans
  • 1927: Five Canadian women file a petition to the Supreme Court of Canada, asking: "Does the word 'Persons' in Section 24 of the British North America Act, 1867, include female persons?" The Famous Five (Canada)
  • 1918: Mexican Revolution: Battle of Ambos Nogales: U.S. Army forces skirmish against Mexican Carrancistas as part of the Mexican Border War. Mexican Revolution
  • 1915: Attempted assassination of Bishop Patrick Heffron, bishop of the Diocese of Winona, by Rev. Louis M. Lesches. Assassination
  • 1914: World War I: Siege of Tsingtao: A Japanese fleet commanded by Vice Admiral Sadakichi Kato imposes a blockade along the whole coastline of German Tsingtao, initiating the Siege of Tsingtao. Siege of Tsingtao
  • 1896: Anglo-Zanzibar War: The shortest war in world history (09:02 to 09:40) occurs, between the United Kingdom and Zanzibar. Anglo-Zanzibar War
  • 1893: The Sea Islands hurricane strikes the United States near Savannah, Georgia, killing between 1,000 and 2,000 people. 1893 Sea Islands hurricane
  • 1881: The Georgia hurricane makes landfall near Savannah, Georgia, resulting in an estimated 700 deaths. 1881 Atlantic hurricane season
  • 1859: Petroleum is discovered in Titusville, Pennsylvania, leading to the world's first commercially successful oil well. Petroleum
  • 1828: Brazil and Argentina recognize the sovereignty of Uruguay in the Treaty of Montevideo. Preliminary Peace Convention (1828)
  • 1810: Napoleonic Wars: The French Navy defeats the British Royal Navy, preventing them from taking the harbour of Grand Port on Île de France. Napoleonic Wars
  • 1793: French Revolutionary Wars: The city of Toulon revolts against the French Republic and admits the British and Spanish fleets to seize its port, leading to the Siege of Toulon by French Revolutionary forces. French Revolutionary Wars
  • 1776: American Revolutionary War: British forces under General William Howe flank and defeat the Continental Army under General George Washington during the Battle of Long Island, with the 1st Maryland Regiment sustaining heavy casualties to cover retreating American troops. American Revolutionary War
  • 1600: Ishida Mitsunari's Western Army commences the Siege of Fushimi Castle, which is lightly defended by a much smaller Tokugawa garrison led by Torii Mototada. Ishida Mitsunari
  • 1593: Pierre Barrière fails in an attempt to assassinate Henry IV of France. Pierre Barrière
  • 1353: War of the Straits and Sardinian–Aragonese war: The Battle of Alghero results in a crushing victory of the allied Aragonese and Venetian fleet over the Genoese fleet, most of which is captured. War of the Straits
  • 1172: Henry the Young King and Margaret of France are crowned junior king and queen of England. Henry the Young King
