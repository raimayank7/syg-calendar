March 22: what happened on this day

What happened on 22 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2024: At least 145 people were killed and 551 injured in a bombing and mass shooting at the Crocus City Hall in Krasnogorsk, Russia. Crocus City Hall attack
  • 2014: A massive landslide in Oso, Washington, killed 43 people after engulfing a rural neighborhood, the largest death toll for a standalone landslide in U.S. history. 2014 Oso landslide
  • 1997: Comet Hale–Bopp (pictured) reached its closest approach to Earth at 1.315 AU. Comet Hale–Bopp
  • 1992: USAir Flight 405 crashed in Flushing Bay shortly after takeoff from New York City's LaGuardia Airport, killing 27 people, and leading to studies into the effects of ice on aircraft. USAir Flight 405
  • 1984: Teachers at a preschool in Manhattan Beach, California, were falsely charged with the sexual abuse of schoolchildren, leading to the longest and costliest criminal trial in United States history. Manhattan Beach, California
  • 1943: British propaganda radio station Deutscher Kurzwellensender Atlantik began broadcasting to German submariners. Deutscher Kurzwellensender Atlantik
  • 1942: Second World War: British and Italian naval forces fought the Second Battle of Sirte in the Gulf of Sidra north of Libya. World War II
  • 1934: In golf, the first Masters Tournament was held at Augusta National Golf Club in Georgia, United States. Golf
  • 1896: Charilaos Vasilakos (pictured) won the first modern marathon in preparation for the inaugural Summer Olympics. Charilaos Vasilakos
  • 1794: The Slave Trade Act, which banned the export of slaves from the United States, was signed by George Washington Slave Trade Act of 1794
  • 1784: The Emerald Buddha, considered to be the sacred palladium of Thailand, was installed in its current location at Wat Phra Kaew on the grounds of the Grand Palace in Bangkok. Emerald Buddha
  • 1638: Anne Hutchinson was expelled from the Massachusetts Bay Colony for her participation in the Antinomian Controversy. Anne Hutchinson

More events

  • 2026: Air Canada Express Flight 8646, a Bombardier CRJ-900 operated by Jazz Aviation, collided with a fire truck while landing at LaGuardia Airport in New York City. Two fatalities were reported, both being the captain and the co-pilot. This marked the first fatal accident involving a CRJ-900. Air Canada Express Flight 8646
  • 2021: Ten people are killed in a mass shooting in Boulder, Colorado. 2021 Boulder shooting
  • 2020: Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis announces a national lockdown and the country's first ever self-imposed curfew, in an effort to fight the spread of COVID-19. Kyriakos Mitsotakis
  • 2019: Two buses crash in Kitampo, a town north of Ghana's capital Accra, killing at least 50 people. Ghana
  • 2017: Syrian civil war: Five hundred members of the Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) are airlifted south of the Euphrates by United States Air Force helicopters, beginning the Battle of Tabqa. Syrian civil war
  • 2006: Three Christian Peacemaker Team (CPT) hostages are freed by British forces in Baghdad after 118 days of captivity and the murder of their colleague from the U.S., Tom Fox. Community Peacemaker Teams
  • 1997: Tara Lipinski, aged 14 years and nine months, becomes the youngest women's World Figure Skating Champion. Tara Lipinski
  • 1996: NASA's Space Shuttle Atlantis is launched on its 16th mission, STS-76. Space Shuttle Atlantis
  • 1993: The Intel Corporation ships the first Pentium chips (80586), featuring a 60 MHz clock speed, 100+ MIPS, and a 64 bit data path. Intel
  • 1992: Fall of communism in Albania: The Democratic Party of Albania wins a decisive majority in the parliamentary election. Fall of communism in Albania
  • 1978: Karl Wallenda of The Flying Wallendas dies after falling off a tight-rope suspended between two hotels in San Juan, Puerto Rico. Karl Wallenda
  • 1972: The United States Congress sends the Equal Rights Amendment to the states for ratification. Equal Rights Amendment
  • 1963: The Beatles release their debut album Please Please Me. The Beatles
  • 1957: A United States Air Force aircraft disappears with all 67 people on board somewhere over the Pacific Ocean. United States Air Force
  • 1946: The United Kingdom grants full independence to Transjordan. Jordan
  • 1943: World War II: The entire village of Khatyn (in present-day Republic of Belarus) is burnt alive by Schutzmannschaft Battalion 118. Khatyn massacre
  • 1939: Germany takes Memel from Lithuania. 1939 German ultimatum to Lithuania
  • 1933: Cullen–Harrison Act: President Franklin D. Roosevelt signs an amendment to the Volstead Act, legalizing the manufacture and sale of "3.2 beer" (3.2% alcohol by weight, approximately 4% alcohol by volume) and light wines. Cullen–Harrison Act
  • 1920: Azeri and Turkish army soldiers with participation of Kurdish gangs attack the Armenian inhabitants of Shushi (Nagorno Karabakh). Azerbaijan Democratic Republic
  • 1913: Mystic Phan Xích Long, the self-proclaimed Emperor of Vietnam, is arrested for organising a revolt against the colonial rule of French Indochina, which was nevertheless carried out by his supporters the following day. Phan Xích Long
  • 1895: Before the Société pour L'Encouragement à l'Industrie, brothers Auguste and Louis Lumière demonstrate movie film technology publicly for the first time. Lumière brothers
  • 1873: The Spanish National Assembly abolishes slavery in Puerto Rico. Cortes Generales
  • 1849: The Austrians defeat the Piedmontese at the Battle of Novara. Austrian Empire
  • 1794: The Slave Trade Act of 1794 bans the export of slaves from the United States, and prohibits American citizens from outfitting a ship for the purpose of importing slaves. Slave Trade Act of 1794
  • 1784: The Emerald Buddha is moved with great ceremony to its current location in Wat Phra Kaew, Thailand. Emerald Buddha
  • 1668: Notable Privateer Henry Morgan lands in Cuba to raid and plunder the inland town of Puerto del Príncipe during the latter stages of the Anglo-Spanish War (1654–1660). Privateer
  • 1631: The Massachusetts Bay Colony outlaws the possession of cards, dice, and gaming tables. Massachusetts Bay Colony
  • 1621: The Pilgrims of Plymouth Colony, led by governor John Carver, sign a peace treaty with Massasoit, sachem of the Wampanoags; Squanto serves as an interpreter between the two sides. Pilgrims (Plymouth Colony)
  • 1312: Vox in excelso: Pope Clement V dissolves the Order of the Knights Templar. Vox in excelso
  • 871: Æthelred of Wessex is defeated by a Danish invasion army at the Battle of Marton. Æthelred I of Wessex

About the date itself

March 22 is the 81st day of an ordinary year, with 284 days left after it (285 in a leap year, where it is the 82nd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 22 March 2027 in 2027 is 14 Shawwal 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 12.
