May 21: what happened on this day

What happened on 21 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2024: The Greenfield tornado, estimated to have produced winds in excess of 309 miles per hour (497 km/h), moved across Iowa. Greenfield tornado
  • 2015: Islamic State militants entered the ancient city of Palmyra. Islamic State
  • 2005: The world's tallest roller coaster, Kingda Ka, opened at Six Flags Great Adventure in Jackson, New Jersey, U.S. Roller coaster
  • 1998: Indonesian president Suharto resigned after a collapse of support for his presidency amid economic and political crises, ending 32 years in power. Suharto
  • 1946: While working with a mass of plutonium known as the demon core, Manhattan Project physicist Louis Slotin accidentally exposed himself to a lethal dose of hard radiation, making him the second victim of a criticality accident in history. Plutonium
  • 1927: Aboard the Spirit of St. Louis, American aviator Charles Lindbergh (pictured) completed the first solo non-stop transatlantic flight, flying from Roosevelt Field near New York City to Paris–Le Bourget Airport. Spirit of St. Louis
  • 1924: University of Chicago students Nathan Leopold Jr. and Richard Loeb (both pictured) murdered a 14-year-old boy in a thrill killing out of a desire to commit a "perfect crime". University of Chicago
  • 1917: The Imperial War Graves Commission was established by royal charter to mark, record and maintain the graves and places of commemoration of British Empire military forces. Commonwealth War Graves Commission
  • 1864: American Civil War: The inconclusive Battle of Spotsylvania Court House in Virginia ended with combined Union and Confederate casualties totaling around 31,000. American Civil War
  • 1856: A crowd of about 800 pro-slavery Americans ransacked the town of Lawrence, Kansas. Sacking of Lawrence
  • 1703: English writer Daniel Defoe was imprisoned for seditious libel after publishing a pamphlet that was perceived to satirise the Tory publications about Dissenters. Daniel Defoe
  • 1138: The Crusades: The siege of Shaizar ended, and the Emir of Shaizar became a vassal of the Byzantine Empire. Crusades

More events

  • 2024: The Greenfield tornado kills five and injures 35 across rural Iowa, United States. Wind speeds in excess of 480 kilometres per hour (300 mph) are estimated from measurements for the third time in history. Greenfield tornado
  • 2017: Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus perform their final show at Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum. Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus
  • 2012: A suicide bombing kills more than 120 people in Sanaa, Yemen. 2012 Sanaa parade rehearsal bombing
  • 2010: JAXA, the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency, launches the solar-sail spacecraft IKAROS aboard an H-IIA rocket. The vessel would make a Venus flyby late in the year. JAXA
  • 2003: The 6.8 Mw Boumerdès earthquake shakes northern Algeria with a maximum Mercalli intensity of X (Extreme). More than 2,200 people were killed and a moderate tsunami sank boats at the Balearic Islands. 2003 Boumerdès earthquake
  • 2000: Nineteen people are killed in a plane crash in Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania. 2000 Executive Airlines British Aerospace Jetstream crash
  • 1996: The ferry MV Bukoba sinks in Tanzanian waters on Lake Victoria, killing nearly 1,000. MV Bukoba
  • 1994: The Democratic Republic of Yemen unsuccessfully attempts to secede from the Republic of Yemen; a war breaks out. Democratic Republic of Yemen
  • 1991: Mengistu Haile Mariam, president of the People's Democratic Republic of Ethiopia, flees Ethiopia, effectively bringing the Ethiopian Civil War to an end. Mengistu Haile Mariam
  • 1982: Falklands War: A British amphibious assault during Operation Sutton leads to the Battle of San Carlos. Falklands War
  • 1979: White Night riots in San Francisco following the manslaughter conviction of Dan White for the assassinations of George Moscone and Harvey Milk. White Night riots
  • 1972: Michelangelo's Pietà in St. Peter's Basilica in Rome is damaged by a vandal, the mentally disturbed Hungarian geologist Laszlo Toth. Michelangelo
  • 1961: American civil rights movement: Alabama Governor John Malcolm Patterson declares martial law in an attempt to restore order after race riots break out. Civil rights movement
  • 1946: Physicist Louis Slotin is fatally irradiated in a criticality incident during an experiment with the demon core at Los Alamos National Laboratory. Louis Slotin
  • 1936: Sada Abe is arrested after wandering the streets of Tokyo for days with her dead lover's severed genitals in her handbag. Her story soon becomes one of Japan's most notorious scandals. Sada Abe
  • 1932: Bad weather forces Amelia Earhart to land in a pasture in Derry, Northern Ireland, and she thereby becomes the first woman to fly solo across the Atlantic Ocean. Amelia Earhart
  • 1924: University of Chicago students Richard Loeb and Nathan Leopold, Jr. murder 14-year-old Bobby Franks in a "thrill killing". University of Chicago
  • 1917: The Great Atlanta fire of 1917 causes $5.5 million in damages, destroying some 300 acres including 2,000 homes, businesses and churches, displacing about 10,000 people but leading to only one fatality (due to heart attack). Great Atlanta fire of 1917
  • 1894: The Manchester Ship Canal in the United Kingdom is officially opened by Queen Victoria, who later knights its designer Sir Edward Leader Williams. Manchester Ship Canal
  • 1879: War of the Pacific: Two Chilean ships blocking the harbor of Iquique (then belonging to Peru) battle two Peruvian vessels in the Battle of Iquique. War of the Pacific
  • 1864: Russia declares an end to the Russo-Circassian War and many Circassians are forced into exile. The day is designated the Circassian Day of Mourning. Russo–Circassian War
  • 1864: The Ionian Islands reunite with Greece. Ionian Islands
  • 1851: Slavery in Colombia is abolished. Slavery in Colombia
  • 1799: The end of the Siege of Acre (1799): Napoleon Bonaparte abandons his siege of the Ottoman city of Acre after two months. This was the turning point of Bonaparte's Egyptian campaign and one of the first major defeats he suffered in his military career. Siege of Acre (1799)
  • 1725: The Order of St. Alexander Nevsky is instituted in Russia by Empress Catherine I. It would later be discontinued and then reinstated by the Soviet government in 1942 as the Order of Alexander Nevsky. Order of Saint Alexander Nevsky
  • 1674: The nobility elect John Sobieski King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania. Szlachta
  • 1554: Queen Mary I grants a royal charter to Derby School, as a grammar school for boys in Derby, England. Mary I
  • 1403: Henry III of Castile sends Ruy González de Clavijo as ambassador to Timur to discuss the possibility of an alliance between Timur and Castile against the Ottoman Empire. Henry III of Castile
  • 1294: Coronation of Michael IX Palaiologos as Byzantine Emperor. Michael IX Palaiologos
  • 879: Pope John VIII gives blessings to Branimir of Croatia and to the Croatian people, considered to be international recognition of the Croatian state. Pope John VIII

About the date itself

May 21 is the 141st day of an ordinary year, with 224 days left after it (225 in a leap year, where it is the 142nd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Friday, 2028: Sunday, 2029: Monday, 2030: Tuesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 21 May 2027 in 2027 is 15 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 20.
