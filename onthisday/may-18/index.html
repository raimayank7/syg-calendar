May 18: what happened on this day

What happened on 18 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 14 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2018: Cubana de Aviación Flight 0972 crashed near Santiago de las Vegas shortly after takeoff from José Martí International Airport, killing 112 of the 113 people on board. Cubana de Aviación Flight 0972
  • 2013: Mark Carson, an openly gay man, was murdered in a hate crime incident in New York City, prompting a 1,500-person march against anti-LGBTQ violence. Murder of Mark Carson
  • 2006: The Parliament of Nepal unanimously voted to strip King Gyanendra of many of his powers. Federal Parliament of Nepal
  • 1996: Ireland won the Eurovision song contest for the seventh time, the highest number of wins for any country before Sweden tied it in 2023. Republic of Ireland
  • 1974: India conducted its first nuclear test explosion at Pokhran, the first confirmed nuclear test by a nation outside the five permanent members of the United Nations Security Council. Smiling Buddha
  • 1965: Eli Cohen, a spy who is credited with gathering significant intelligence used by Israel during the Six-Day War, was publicly hanged in Syria. Eli Cohen
  • 1952: First Indochina War: Viet Minh forces overran a French and Laotian garrison at Muong Khoua, leaving only four survivors. First Indochina War
  • 1936: In a crime that captivated Japan, Sada Abe strangled her lover, cut off his genitals, and carried them around with her for several days until her arrest. Sada Abe
  • 1927: Disgruntled school board treasurer Andrew Kehoe set off explosives with timers and a rifle, causing the Bath School disaster in the Bath Consolidated School in Michigan, killing 44 people in the deadliest mass murder in a school in United States history. Board of education
  • 1926: Pentecostal evangelist Aimee Semple McPherson (pictured) was reportedly kidnapped near Venice Beach in Los Angeles before reappearing five weeks later in Mexico. Pentecostalism
  • 1863: American Civil War: General Ulysses S. Grant led his Army of the Tennessee across the Big Black River in preparation for the Siege of Vicksburg. American Civil War
  • 1388: At the Battle of Buir Lake, a Ming Chinese army led by general Lan Yu defeated the forces of Tögüs Temür, the Mongol khan of Northern Yuan. Battle of Buir Lake

More events

  • 2026: A shooting carried out by two gunmen at the Islamic Center of San Diego results in the deaths of five people, including the perpetrators. 2026 Islamic Center of San Diego shooting
  • 2018: A school shooting at Santa Fe High School in Texas kills ten people. 2018 Santa Fe High School shooting
  • 2015: At least 78 people die in a landslide caused by heavy rains in the Colombian town of Salgar. 2015 Colombian landslide
  • 2006: The post Loktantra Andolan government passes a landmark bill curtailing the power of the monarchy and making Nepal a secular country. 2006 Nepalese revolution
  • 1994: Israeli troops finish withdrawing from the Gaza Strip, ceding the area to the Palestinian National Authority to govern. Gaza Strip
  • 1991: Northern Somalia declares independence from the rest of Somalia as the Republic of Somaliland. Somalia
  • 1980: Mount St. Helens erupts in Washington, United States, killing 57 people and causing $3 billion in damage. Mount St. Helens
  • 1980: Rioting spreads to Miami's mostly African-American neighborhoods of Overtown and Liberty City following the previous day's courthouse protests. 1980 Miami riots
  • 1974: Nuclear weapons testing: Under project Smiling Buddha, India successfully detonates its first nuclear weapon becoming the sixth nation to do so. Nuclear weapons testing
  • 1972: During approach to Kharkiv International Airport, Aeroflot Flight 1491 crashes near Ruska Lozova, killing all 112 aboard. Kharkiv International Airport
  • 1962: The French government and representatives of the Provisional Government of the Algerian Republic reach a settlement to end the Algerian war in the Évian Accords. Provisional Government of the Algerian Republic
  • 1953: Jacqueline Cochran becomes the first woman to break the sound barrier. Jacqueline Cochran
  • 1944: World War II: Battle of Monte Cassino: Conclusion after seven days of the fourth battle as German paratroopers evacuate Monte Cassino. World War II
  • 1933: New Deal: President Franklin D. Roosevelt signs an act creating the Tennessee Valley Authority. New Deal
  • 1927: After being founded for 20 years, the Nationalist government approves Tongji University to be among its first national universities. Nationalist government
  • 1922: Seamus Woods and Roger McCorley lead an Irish Republican Army attack on the headquarters of the Royal Irish Constabulary in Belfast. Seamus Woods
  • 1912: The first Indian film, Shree Pundalik by Dadasaheb Torne, is released in Mumbai. Cinema of India
  • 1896: The United States Supreme Court rules in Plessy v. Ferguson that the "separate but equal" doctrine is constitutional. Supreme Court of the United States
  • 1863: American Civil War: Union forces under Ulysses S. Grant begin the Siege of Vicksburg during the Vicksburg campaign in order to take full control of the Mississippi River. American Civil War
  • 1848: Opening of the first German National Assembly (Nationalversammlung) in Frankfurt, Germany. National Assembly
  • 1811: Battle of Las Piedras: The first great military triumph of the revolution of the Río de la Plata in Uruguay led by José Artigas. Battle of Las Piedras (1811)
  • 1803: Napoleonic Wars: The United Kingdom revokes the Treaty of Amiens and declares war on France. Napoleonic Wars
  • 1783: First United Empire Loyalists reach Parrtown (later called Saint John, New Brunswick), Canada, after leaving the United States. United Empire Loyalist
  • 1695: The 1695 Linfen earthquake in Shannxi, Qing dynasty causes extreme damage and kills at least 52,000 people. 1695 Linfen earthquake
  • 1631: In Dorchester, Massachusetts, John Winthrop takes the oath of office and becomes the first Governor of Massachusetts. Dorchester, Boston
  • 1565: The Great Siege of Malta begins, in which Ottoman forces attempt and fail to conquer Malta. Great Siege of Malta
  • 1388: During the Battle of Buyur Lake, General Lan Yu leads a Ming army forward to crush the Mongol hordes of Tögüs Temür, the Khan of Northern Yuan. Battle of Buir Lake
  • 1291: Fall of Acre, the end of Crusader presence in the Holy Land. Siege of Acre (1291)
  • 1152: The future Henry II of England marries Eleanor of Aquitaine. He would become king two years later, after the death of his cousin once removed King Stephen of England. Henry II of England
  • 1012: Pope Benedict VIII begins his papacy following the death of his predecessor Pope Sergius IV earlier that month. Pope Benedict VIII

About the date itself

May 18 is the 138th day of an ordinary year, with 227 days left after it (228 in a leap year, where it is the 139th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 18 May 2027 in 2027 is 12 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
