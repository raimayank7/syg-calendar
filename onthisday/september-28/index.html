September 28: what happened on this day

What happened on 28 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2023: The Sycamore Gap tree (pictured) in Northumberland, England, was illegally felled. Sycamore Gap tree
  • 2012: War in Somalia: Somali National Army forces and their AMISOM and Raskamboni allies launched an offensive against Al-Shabaab in the latter's last major stronghold of Kismayo. Somali Civil War (2009–present)
  • 2006: Typhoon Xangsane passed Manila on its way to causing more than 300 deaths, mostly in the Phillippines and Vietnam. Typhoon Xangsane
  • 1978: Pope John Paul I died only 33 days after his papal election due to an apparent myocardial infarction, resulting in the first year of three popes since 1605. Pope John Paul I
  • 1972: Against the backdrop of the Cold War, the Canadian ice hockey team defeated the Soviet team in the Summit Series. Cold War
  • 1963: Whaam!, now considered one of Roy Lichtenstein's most important works, debuted at an exhibition held at the Leo Castelli Gallery, New York City. Whaam!
  • 1928: Scottish biologist and pharmacologist Alexander Fleming (pictured) discovered penicillin when he noticed a bacteria-killing mould growing in his laboratory. Alexander Fleming
  • 1924: A team of U.S. Army Air Service aviators landed in Seattle, Washington, to complete the first aerial circumnavigation of the world. United States Army Air Service
  • 1821: The Declaration of Independence of the Mexican Empire from Spain was drafted in the National Palace in Mexico City. Declaration of Independence (Mexico)
  • 1106: In the Battle of Tinchebray in Normandy, the invading King Henry I of England captured his brother Robert Curthose. Battle of Tinchebray
  • 351: The Eastern Roman armies under Constantius II defeated those of the usurper Magnentius at the Battle of Mursa Major. Constantius II
  • 235: Pope Pontian resigned after being exiled to Sardinia, becoming the first pope to relinquish the position. Pope Pontian

More events

  • 2023: The 2023 Rotterdam shootings occurred, during which two people were killed in a shooting and arson incident at a residence in Delfshaven, Rotterdam. Additionally, one person lost their life in a classroom at the Erasmus University Medical Center. 2023 Rotterdam shootings
  • 2018: The 7.5 Mw 2018 Sulawesi earthquake, which triggered a large tsunami, leaves 4,340 dead and 10,679 injured. 2018 Sulawesi earthquake and tsunami
  • 2014: The 2014 Hong Kong protests begin in response to restrictive political reforms imposed by the NPC in Beijing. 2014 Hong Kong protests
  • 2012: Sita Air Flight 601 crashes in Madhyapur Thimi, Nepal, killing all 19 passengers and crew. Sita Air Flight 601
  • 2008: Falcon 1 becomes the first privately developed liquid-fuel ground-launched vehicle to put a payload into orbit by the RatSat mission. Falcon 1
  • 2006: Typhoon Xangsane passes over Manila after impacting parts of Southern Luzon and Eastern Visayas, becoming the strongest to affect the Philippine capital in 11 years. Typhoon Xangsane
  • 1995: Bob Denard and a group of mercenaries take the islands of the Comoros in a coup. Bob Denard
  • 1994: The cruise ferry MS Estonia sinks in the Baltic Sea, killing 852 people. MS Estonia
  • 1986: The Democratic Progressive Party becomes the first opposition party in Taiwan. Democratic Progressive Party
  • 1975: The Spaghetti House siege, in which nine people are taken hostage, takes place in London. Spaghetti House siege
  • 1970: Egyptian President Gamal Abdel Nasser dies of a heart attack in Cairo. Gamal Abdel Nasser
  • 1958: Fernando Rios, a Mexican tour guide in New Orleans, dies of injuries sustained in an incident of gay bashing. Killing of Fernando Rios
  • 1944: World War II: Soviet Army troops liberate Klooga concentration camp in Estonia. Klooga concentration camp
  • 1941: Ted Williams achieves a .406 batting average for the season, and becomes the last major league baseball player to bat .400 or better. Ted Williams
  • 1939: World War II: The siege of Warsaw comes to an end. Siege of Warsaw (1939)
  • 1924: The first aerial circumnavigation is completed by a team from the US Army. First aerial circumnavigation
  • 1918: World War I: The Fifth Battle of Ypres begins. World War I
  • 1912: Corporal Frank S. Scott of the United States Army becomes the first enlisted man to die in an airplane crash. Frank S. Scott
  • 1892: The first night game for American football takes place in a contest between Wyoming Seminary and Mansfield State Normal. 1892 Wyoming Seminary vs. Mansfield Normal football game
  • 1871: The Brazilian Parliament passes a law that frees all children thereafter born to slaves, and all government-owned slaves. Rio Branco Law
  • 1867: Toronto becomes the capital of Ontario, having also been the capital of Ontario's predecessors since 1796. History of Toronto
  • 1821: The Declaration of Independence of the Mexican Empire is drafted. It will be made public on 13 October. Declaration of Independence (Mexico)
  • 1781: American Revolutionary War: French and American forces backed by a French fleet begin the siege of Yorktown. American Revolutionary War
  • 1644: A Hospitaller galley squadron defeats an Ottoman convoy in the action of 28 September 1644. Navy of the Order of Saint John
  • 1538: Ottoman–Venetian War: The Ottoman Navy scores a decisive victory over a Holy League fleet in the Battle of Preveza. Battle of Preveza
  • 1238: King James I of Aragon conquers Valencia from the Moors. Shortly thereafter, he proclaims himself king of Valencia. James I of Aragon
  • 1106: King Henry I of England defeats his brother Robert Curthose at the Battle of Tinchebray. Henry I of England
  • 995: Boleslaus II, Duke of Bohemia, kills most members of the rival Slavník dynasty. Boleslaus II of Bohemia
  • 365: Roman usurper Procopius bribes two legions passing by Constantinople, and proclaims himself emperor. AD 365
  • 235: Pope Pontian resigns. He is exiled to the mines of Sardinia, along with Hippolytus of Rome. Pope Pontian

About the date itself

September 28 is the 271st day of an ordinary year, with 94 days left after it (95 in a leap year, where it is the 272nd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 28 September 2027 in 2027 is 27 Rabi' al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 39.
