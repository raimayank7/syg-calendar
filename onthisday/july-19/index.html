July 19: what happened on this day

What happened on 19 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 11 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2024: Neary's, a New York City Irish pub popular with political elites, closed. Neary's
  • 2014: Gunmen ambushed an Egyptian military checkpoint in the Libyan Desert near Farafra, killing 22 soldiers. 2014 Farafra ambush
  • 1997: The Troubles: The Provisional Irish Republican Army announced that it would resume its ceasefire, ending its 28-year campaign against British rule in Northern Ireland. The Troubles
  • 1976: Environmental activists bombed the port facilities in Bunbury, Western Australia, in an attempt to disrupt the woodchipping industry. Bunbury woodchip bombing
  • 1957: The Ordeal of Gilbert Pinfold, a largely autobiographical novel by Evelyn Waugh, was published. The Ordeal of Gilbert Pinfold
  • 1919: Following Peace Day celebrations marking the end of the First World War, English ex-servicemen unhappy with unemployment and other grievances rioted and burned down Luton Town Hall. Luton Peace Day Riots
  • 1903: French cyclist Maurice Garin won the first edition of the Tour de France. Maurice Garin
  • 1848: The two-day Seneca Falls Convention, the first women's-rights and feminist convention held in the United States, opened in Seneca Falls, New York. Seneca Falls Convention
  • 1843: SS Great Britain, the first ocean-going ship with both an iron hull and a screw propeller, was launched (pictured) in Bristol, England. SS Great Britain
  • 1702: Great Northern War: Polish–Saxon forces were defeated by a Swedish army half their size at the Battle of Kliszów. Great Northern War
  • 1553: Mary I was proclaimed Queen of England, deposing Lady Jane Grey after nine days of de facto rule. Mary I
  • 1333: Second War of Scottish Independence: Scottish forces under Sir Archibald Douglas were heavily defeated by the English at the Battle of Halidon Hill while trying to relieve Berwick-upon-Tweed. Second War of Scottish Independence

More events

  • 2018: The Knesset passes the controversial Nationality Bill, which defines the State of Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people. Knesset
  • 2012: Syrian civil war: The People's Protection Units (YPG) capture the city of Kobanî without resistance, starting the Rojava conflict in Northeast Syria. Syrian civil war
  • 1997: The Troubles: The Provisional Irish Republican Army resumes a ceasefire to end their 25-year paramilitary campaign to end British rule in Northern Ireland. The Troubles
  • 1992: A car bomb kills Judge Paolo Borsellino and five members of his escort. Via D'Amelio bombing
  • 1985: The Val di Stava dam collapses, killing 268 people in Val di Stava, Italy. Val di Stava dam collapse
  • 1982: In one of the first militant attacks by Hezbollah, David S. Dodge, president of the American University of Beirut, is kidnapped. Hezbollah
  • 1980: Opening of the Summer Olympics in Moscow. 1980 Summer Olympics
  • 1979: The oil tanker SS Atlantic Empress collides with another oil tanker, causing the largest ever ship-borne oil spill. SS Atlantic Empress
  • 1976: Sagarmatha National Park in Nepal is created. Sagarmatha National Park
  • 1969: Chappaquiddick incident: U.S. Senator Ted Kennedy crashes his car into a tidal pond at Chappaquiddick Island, Massachusetts, killing his passenger Mary Jo Kopechne. Chappaquiddick incident
  • 1964: Vietnam War: At a rally in Saigon, South Vietnamese Prime Minister Nguyễn Khánh calls for expanding the war into North Vietnam. Vietnam War
  • 1961: Tunisia imposes a blockade on the French naval base at Bizerte; the French would capture the entire town four days later. Bizerte crisis
  • 1952: Opening of the Summer Olympics in Helsinki, Finland. 1952 Summer Olympics
  • 1947: Korean politician Lyuh Woon-hyung is assassinated. Lyuh Woon-hyung
  • 1942: World War II: The Second Happy Time of Hitler's submarines comes to an end, as the increasingly effective American convoy system compels them to return to the central Atlantic. Second Happy Time
  • 1940: Field Marshal Ceremony: First occasion in World War II that Adolf Hitler appoints field marshals due to military achievements. 1940 Field Marshal Ceremony
  • 1936: Spanish Civil War: The CNT and UGT call a general strike in Spain – mobilizing workers' militias against the Nationalist forces. People's Olympiad of Barcelona cancelled. Spanish Civil War
  • 1916: World War I: Battle of Fromelles: British and Australian troops attack German trenches as part of the Battle of the Somme. World War I
  • 1900: The first line of the Paris Metro opens for operation. Paris Metro Line 1
  • 1864: Taiping Rebellion: Third Battle of Nanking: The Qing dynasty finally defeats the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom. Taiping Rebellion
  • 1848: Women's rights: A two-day Women's Rights Convention opens in Seneca Falls, New York. Women's rights
  • 1843: Brunel's steamship the SS Great Britain is launched, becoming the first ocean-going craft with an iron hull and screw propeller, becoming the largest vessel afloat in the world. Isambard Kingdom Brunel
  • 1821: Coronation of George IV as King of the United Kingdom. George IV
  • 1733: The Ottoman army defeats the Iranian army under Nader Shah in the battle of Baghdad. Military of the Ottoman Empire
  • 1701: Representatives of the Iroquois Confederacy sign the Nanfan Treaty, ceding a large territory north of the Ohio River to England. Haudenosaunee
  • 1553: The attempt to install Lady Jane Grey as Queen of England collapses after only nine days. Lady Jane Grey
  • 1544: Italian War of 1542–46: The first Siege of Boulogne begins. Italian War of 1542–1546
  • 998: Arab–Byzantine wars: Battle of Apamea: Fatimids defeat a Byzantine army near Apamea. Arab–Byzantine wars
  • 803: Bardanes Tourkos is proclaimed emperor by rebellious Byzantine troops. Bardanes Tourkos
  • 484: Leontius, Roman usurper, is crowned Eastern emperor at Tarsus (modern Turkey). He is recognized in Antioch and makes it his capital. Leontius (usurper)

About the date itself

July 19 is the 200th day of an ordinary year, with 165 days left after it (166 in a leap year, where it is the 201st day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 19 July 2027 in 2027 is 15 Safar 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 29.
