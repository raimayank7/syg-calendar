January 15: what happened on this day

What happened on 15 January: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2009: US Airways Flight 1549 struck a flock of Canada geese during its climb out from New York City and made an emergency landing in the Hudson River (featured). US Airways Flight 1549
  • 2001: The first edit to the internet encyclopedia Wikipedia was made. First Wikipedia edit
  • 1981: The American serial police-procedural television show Hill Street Blues aired its pilot episode, "Hill Street Station". Police procedural
  • 1974: American serial killer Dennis Rader, also known as the "BTK killer", murdered his first four victims. Dennis Rader
  • 1967: The Green Bay Packers defeated the Kansas City Chiefs in the American football championship game now known as Super Bowl I. Green Bay Packers
  • 1951: Ilse Koch, wife of Karl-Otto Koch, the Nazi commander of the Buchenwald and Majdanek concentration camps, was sentenced to life imprisonment by a West German court. Ilse Koch
  • 1943: The highest-capacity office building in the world, the headquarters of the United States Department of Defense known as the Pentagon, was dedicated. United States Department of Defense
  • 1934: At least 10,700 people died when a magnitude-8.0 earthquake struck Nepal and the Indian state of Bihar. 1934 Nepal–India earthquake
  • 1919: A large molasses tank in Boston, Massachusetts, burst and a wave of molasses rushed through the streets , killing 21 people and injuring 150 others. Molasses
  • 1885: Wilson Bentley (pictured) took the first known photograph of a snowflake by attaching a bellows camera to a microscope. Wilson Bentley
  • 1867: In Regent's Park, London, the ice on the lake broke, plunging skaters into the water and causing 40 deaths from drowning or hypothermia. Regent's Park
  • 1857: In British Hong Kong, hundreds of Europeans were non-lethally poisoned by arsenic in bread from a locally owned bakery, leading to geopolitical tension. British Hong Kong

More events

  • 2023: Yeti Airlines Flight 691 crashes near Pokhara International Airport, killing all 72 people on board. Yeti Airlines Flight 691
  • 2022: The Hunga Tonga-Hunga Haʻapai volcano erupts, cutting off communications with Tonga and causing a tsunami across the Pacific. 2022 Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai eruption and tsunami
  • 2020: The Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare confirms the first case of COVID-19 in Japan. Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare
  • 2019: Theresa May's UK government suffers the biggest government defeat in modern times, when 432 MPs voting against the proposed European Union withdrawal agreement, giving her opponents a majority of 230. Theresa May
  • 2016: The Kenyan Army suffers its worst defeat ever in a battle with Al-Shabaab Islamic insurgents in El-Adde, Somalia. An estimated 150 Kenyan soldiers are killed in the battle. Kenya Defence Forces
  • 2013: A train carrying Egyptian Army recruits derails near Giza, Greater Cairo, killing 19 and injuring 120 others. Badrashin railway accident
  • 2005: ESA's SMART-1 lunar orbiter discovers elements such as calcium, aluminum, silicon, iron, and other surface elements on the Moon. European Space Agency
  • 1991: The United Nations deadline for the withdrawal of Iraqi forces from occupied Kuwait expires, preparing the way for the start of Operation Desert Storm. United Nations
  • 1991: Elizabeth II, in her capacity as Queen of Australia, signs letters patent allowing Australia to become the first Commonwealth realm to institute its own Victoria Cross in its honours system. Elizabeth II
  • 1977: Linjeflyg Flight 618 crashes in Kälvesta near Stockholm Bromma Airport in Stockholm, Sweden, killing 22 people. Linjeflyg Flight 618
  • 1975: The Alvor Agreement is signed, ending the Angolan War of Independence and giving Angola independence from Portugal. Alvor Agreement
  • 1970: Nigerian Civil War: Biafran rebels surrender following an unsuccessful 32-month fight for independence from Nigeria. Nigerian Civil War
  • 1969: The Soviet Union launches Soyuz 5. Soviet Union
  • 1966: The First Nigerian Republic, led by Abubakar Tafawa Balewa is overthrown in a military coup d'état. First Nigerian Republic
  • 1962: Netherlands New Guinea Conflict: Indonesian Navy fast patrol boat RI Macan Tutul commanded by Commodore Yos Sudarso sunk in Arafura Sea by the Dutch Navy. Dutch New Guinea
  • 1947: The Black Dahlia murder: The dismembered corpse of Elizabeth Short is found in Los Angeles. Black Dahlia
  • 1943: World War II: The Soviet counter-offensive at Voronezh begins. World War II
  • 1937: Spanish Civil War: Nationalists and Republicans both withdraw after suffering heavy losses, ending the Second Battle of the Corunna Road. Spanish Civil War
  • 1934: The 8.0 Mw Nepal–Bihar earthquake strikes Nepal and Bihar with a maximum Mercalli intensity of XI (Extreme), killing an estimated 6,000–10,700 people. 1934 Nepal–India earthquake
  • 1919: Great Molasses Flood: A wave of molasses released from an exploding storage tank sweeps through Boston, Massachusetts, killing 21 and injuring 150. Great Molasses Flood
  • 1910: Construction ends on the Buffalo Bill Dam in Wyoming, United States, which was the highest dam in the world at the time, at 99 m (325 ft). Buffalo Bill Dam
  • 1892: James Naismith publishes the rules of basketball. James Naismith
  • 1876: The first newspaper in Afrikaans, Die Afrikaanse Patriot, is published in Paarl. Afrikaans
  • 1870: Thomas Nast publishes a political cartoon symbolizing the Democratic Party with a donkey ("A Live Jackass Kicking a Dead Lion") for Harper's Weekly. Thomas Nast
  • 1865: American Civil War: Fort Fisher in North Carolina falls to the Union, thus cutting off the last major seaport of the Confederacy. American Civil War
  • 1818: A paper by David Brewster is read to the Royal Society, belatedly announcing his discovery of what we now call the biaxial class of doubly-refracting crystals. On the same day, Augustin-Jean Fresnel signs a "supplement" (submitted four days later) on reflection of polarized light. David Brewster
  • 1782: Superintendent of Finance Robert Morris addresses the U.S. Congress to recommend establishment of a national mint and decimal coinage. Robert Morris (financier)
  • 1759: The British Museum opens to the public. British Museum
  • 1559: Elizabeth I is crowned Queen of England and Ireland in Westminster Abbey, London. Elizabeth I
  • 1535: King Henry VIII issues letters patent incorporating the title Supreme Head of the Church of England into his royal title. Henry VIII

About the date itself

January 15 is the 15th day of an ordinary year, with 350 days left after it (351 in a leap year, where it is the 15th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Friday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Monday, 2030: Tuesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 15 January 2027 in 2027 is 7 Sha'ban 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 2.
