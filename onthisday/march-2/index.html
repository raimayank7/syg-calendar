March 2: what happened on this day

What happened on 2 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2022: Russian forces captured the city of Kherson, the only regional capital to be taken during the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Battle of Kherson
  • 2014: The Oscar selfie, considered to be one of the most influential photographs of all time, was taken at the 86th Academy Awards ceremony. Oscar selfie
  • 1978: As a cosmonaut on Soyuz 28, Czechoslovak military pilot Vladimír Remek (pictured) became the first person from outside the Soviet Union or the United States to go into space. Astronaut
  • 1969: The maiden flight of Concorde, the Anglo-French supersonic jet, was conducted in Toulouse, France using a prototype aircraft with the registration F-WTSS. Concorde
  • 1965: The Sound of Music (poster pictured) was released in American theatres; it became the highest-grossing film until 1971. The Sound of Music (film)
  • 1962: Playing for the Philadelphia Warriors, American basketball player Wilt Chamberlain (pictured) scored 100 points in a game against the New York Knicks, which remains an NBA record. Golden State Warriors
  • 1943: World War II: Australian and U.S. air forces attacked and destroyed a large Japanese naval convoy in the Bismarck Sea, north of Papua New Guinea. World War II
  • 1919: Communist, revolutionary-socialist, and syndicalist delegates met in Moscow to establish the Communist International. Communism
  • 1877: The Electoral Commission awarded twenty disputed electoral votes to Rutherford B. Hayes, thus assuring his victory in the 1876 U.S. presidential election. Electoral Commission (United States)
  • 1859: The Great Slave Auction, the largest single sale of slaves in U.S. history, with more than 400 people sold, began in Georgia. Great Slave Auction
  • 1791: French inventor Claude Chappe and his brothers first demonstrated the semaphore telegraph, a system to convey information by means of visual signals, using towers with pivoting crossarms. Claude Chappe
  • 1484: The College of Arms, one of the few remaining official heraldic authorities in Europe, was incorporated by royal charter in the City of London. College of Arms

More events

  • 2026: The Lebanese militant group Hezbollah launches several projectiles into northern Israel as a response to the assassination of Ali Khamenei, formally initiating the 2026 Lebanon War. Hezbollah
  • 2017: The elements Moscovium, Tennessine, and Oganesson are officially added to the periodic table at a conference in Moscow, Russia. Moscovium
  • 2012: A tornado outbreak occurs over a large section of the Southern United States and into the Ohio Valley region, resulting in 40 tornado-related fatalities. Tornado outbreak of March 2–3, 2012
  • 2004: War in Iraq: Al-Qaeda carries out the Ashoura Massacre in Iraq, killing 170 and wounding over 500. Iraq War
  • 1998: Data sent from the Galileo spacecraft indicates that Jupiter's moon Europa has a liquid ocean under a thick crust of ice. Galileo (spacecraft)
  • 1992: Start of the war in Transnistria. Transnistrian War
  • 1991: Establishment of Kuwait Democratic Forum, center-left political organization in Kuwait. Kuwait Democratic Forum
  • 1990: Nelson Mandela is elected deputy president of the African National Congress. Nelson Mandela
  • 1986: Aeroflot Flight F-77 crashes near Bugulma Airport, killing all 38 people aboard. Bugulma Airport
  • 1978: Czech Vladimír Remek becomes the first non-Soviet or non-American to go into space, when he is launched aboard Soyuz 28. Czechoslovakia
  • 1972: The Pioneer 10 space probe is launched from Cape Canaveral, Florida with a mission to explore the outer planets. Pioneer 10
  • 1969: In Toulouse, France, the first test flight of the Anglo-French Concorde is conducted. Toulouse
  • 1965: The US and Republic of Vietnam Air Force begin Operation Rolling Thunder, a sustained bombing campaign against North Vietnam. United States Air Force
  • 1962: Wilt Chamberlain sets the single-game scoring record in the National Basketball Association by scoring 100 points. Wilt Chamberlain
  • 1949: Captain James Gallagher lands his B-50 Superfortress Lucky Lady II in Fort Worth, Texas, after completing the first non-stop around-the-world airplane flight in 94 hours and one minute. Captain (United States O-3)
  • 1939: Cardinal Eugenio Pacelli is elected Pope and takes the name Pius XII. Cardinal (Catholic Church)
  • 1933: The film King Kong premieres in Radio City Music Hall and RKO Roxy in New York City. King Kong (1933 film)
  • 1919: The first Communist International meets in Moscow. Communist International
  • 1903: In New York City the Martha Washington Hotel opens, becoming the first hotel exclusively for women. Martha Washington Hotel
  • 1901: The U.S. Congress passes the Platt Amendment limiting the autonomy of Cuba, as a condition of the withdrawal of American troops. Platt Amendment
  • 1867: The U.S. Congress passes the first Reconstruction Act. Reconstruction Acts
  • 1864: Ulysses S. Grant is promoted to lieutenant general, giving him command of all Union Armies. Ulysses S. Grant
  • 1855: Alexander II becomes Tsar of Russia. Alexander II of Russia
  • 1815: Signing of the Kandyan Convention treaty by British invaders and the leaders of the Kingdom of Kandy. Kandyan Convention
  • 1807: The U.S. Congress passes the Act Prohibiting Importation of Slaves, disallowing the importation of new slaves into the country. United States Congress
  • 1791: Claude Chappe demonstrates the first semaphore line near Paris. Claude Chappe
  • 1657: The Great Fire of Meireki begins in Edo (now Tokyo), Japan, causing more than 100,000 deaths before it exhausts itself three days later. Great Fire of Meireki
  • 1484: The College of Arms is formally incorporated by Royal Charter signed by King Richard III of England. College of Arms
  • 1458: George of Poděbrady is chosen as the king of Bohemia. George of Poděbrady
  • 1331: Fall of Nicaea to the Ottoman Turks after a siege. Siege of Nicaea (1328–1331)

About the date itself

March 2 is the 61st day of an ordinary year, with 304 days left after it (305 in a leap year, where it is the 62nd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 2 March 2027 in 2027 is 23 Ramadan 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 9.
