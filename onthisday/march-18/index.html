March 18: what happened on this day

What happened on 18 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2019: Syrian civil war: The U.S. Air Force carried out an airstrike in al-Baghuz Fawqani, killing as many as 80, a disputed number of whom were civilians. Syrian civil war
  • 2015: Gunmen attacked the Bardo National Museum in Tunisia, killing 24 people. Bardo National Museum attack
  • 1996: The deadliest fire in Philippine history broke out at a nightclub in Quezon City, causing 162 deaths. Ozone Disco fire
  • 1985: The first episode of the soap opera Neighbours was broadcast on the Seven Network, later becoming the longest-running drama in Australian television history. Soap opera
  • 1970: U.S. postal workers began an eight-day strike after Congress raised their wages by only 4 percent despite increasing its own pay by 41 percent. 1970 United States postal strike
  • 1965: Cosmonaut Alexei Leonov ventured outside the Soviet spacecraft Voskhod 2, becoming the first person to walk in space. Alexei Leonov
  • 1925: The deadliest tornado in U.S. history spawned in Missouri and traveled more than 219 miles (352 km) across Illinois and Indiana, leaving one of the longest continuous tornado tracks ever recorded. 1925 Tri-State tornado
  • 1915: First World War: In one of the largest naval battles of the Gallipoli campaign, Ottoman forces sank three Allied battleships and severely damaged three others. World War I
  • 1901: The construction of the House with Chimaeras (pictured) commenced in Kyiv. House with Chimaeras
  • 1838: The British soldier-explorer John Wood came close to rediscovering the ancient Greco-Bactrian city of Ai-Khanoum in modern Afghanistan. John Wood (explorer)
  • 1608: Susenyos I was formally crowned Emperor of Ethiopia. Susenyos I
  • 1241: First Mongol invasion of Poland: Mongol forces defeated the Polish armies of Sandomierz and Kraków at the Battle of Chmielnik. First Mongol invasion of Poland

More events

  • 2025: Israel launches widespread aerial bombardments and attacks on the Gaza Strip, killing at least 591 people, including children. March 2025 Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip
  • 2015: The Bardo National Museum in Tunisia is attacked by gunmen. Twenty-four people, almost all tourists, are killed, and at least 50 other people are wounded. Bardo National Museum (Tunis)
  • 1997: The tail of a Russian Antonov An-24 charter plane breaks off while en route to Turkey, causing the plane to crash and killing all 50 people on board. Antonov An-24
  • 1994: Bosnia's Bosniaks and Croats sign the Washington Agreement, ending war between the Croatian Republic of Herzeg-Bosnia and the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina, and establishing the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Bosnia and Herzegovina
  • 1990: In the largest art theft in US history, 12 paintings, collectively worth around $500 million, are stolen from the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston. Art theft
  • 1974: Güzel İstanbul, a nude sculpture by Gürdal Duyar in Istanbul, is torn down in the middle of the night. Güzel İstanbul
  • 1970: Lon Nol ousts Prince Norodom Sihanouk of Cambodia. Lon Nol
  • 1968: Gold standard: The U.S. Congress repeals the requirement for a gold reserve to back US currency. Gold standard
  • 1966: United Arab Airlines Flight 749 crashes on approach to Cairo International Airport in Cairo, Egypt, killing 30 people. United Arab Airlines Flight 749
  • 1962: The Évian Accords end the Algerian War of Independence, which had begun in 1954. Évian Accords
  • 1953: An earthquake hits western Turkey, killing at least 1,070 people. 1953 Yenice–Gönen earthquake
  • 1945: World War II: 40th Infantry Division, spearheaded by the 185th US Infantry Regiment, lands unopposed in Tigbauan, forcing the Japanese forces to surrender and General Macario Peralta and Gen. Gen. Eichelberger to declare the Liberation of Panay, Romblon and Guimaras. Tigbauan
  • 1942: The War Relocation Authority is established in the United States to take Japanese Americans into custody. War Relocation Authority
  • 1938: Mexico creates Pemex by expropriating all foreign-owned oil reserves and facilities. Pemex
  • 1937: Spanish Civil War: Spanish Republican forces defeat the Italians at the Battle of Guadalajara. Spanish Civil War
  • 1922: In India, Mohandas Gandhi is sentenced to six years in prison for civil disobedience, of which he serves only two. Mahatma Gandhi
  • 1921: The Kronstadt rebellion is suppressed by the Red Army. Kronstadt rebellion
  • 1915: World War I: During the Battle of Gallipoli, three battleships are sunk during a failed British and French naval attack on the Dardanelles. World War I
  • 1902: Macario Sakay issues Presidential Order No. 1 of his Tagalog Republic. Macario Sakay
  • 1899: Phoebe, a satellite of Saturn, becomes the first to be discovered with photographs, taken in August 1898, by William Henry Pickering. Phoebe (moon)
  • 1865: American Civil War: The Congress of the Confederate States adjourns for the last time. American Civil War
  • 1848: The premiere of William Henry Fry's Leonora in Philadelphia is the first known performance of a grand opera by an American composer. William Henry Fry
  • 1834: Six farm labourers from Tolpuddle, Dorset, England are sentenced to be transported to Australia for forming a trade union. Tolpuddle Martyrs
  • 1793: Flanders Campaign of the French Revolution, Battle of Neerwinden. Low Countries theatre of the War of the First Coalition
  • 1741: New York governor George Clarke's complex at Fort George is burned in an arson attack, starting the New York Conspiracy of 1741. George Clarke (governor)
  • 1644: The Third Anglo-Powhatan War begins in the Colony of Virginia. Anglo-Powhatan Wars
  • 1571: Valletta is made the capital city of Malta. Valletta
  • 1314: Jacques de Molay, the 23rd and final Grand Master of the Knights Templar, is burned at the stake. Jacques de Molay
  • 1229: Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor, declares himself King of Jerusalem in the Sixth Crusade. Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor
  • 417: Pope Zosimus is elected following the death of Pope Innocent I. Pope Zosimus

About the date itself
