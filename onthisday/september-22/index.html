September 22: what happened on this day

What happened on 22 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2014: The NASA spacecraft MAVEN entered into orbit around Mars to study the planet's atmosphere. NASA
  • 2013: Insurgency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: All Saints Church in Peshawar, Pakistan, was attacked by two suicide bombers who killed 127 people. Insurgency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
  • 1994: The Nordhordland Bridge (pictured), crossing Salhusfjorden between Klauvaneset and Flatøy in Vestland, and Norway's second-longest bridge, officially opened. Nordhordland Bridge
  • 1993: A tugboat towing a barge collided with a rail bridge in Mobile, Alabama, U.S., deforming the tracks and causing the derailment of a passenger train eight minutes later, which killed 47 people and injured an additional 103. Tugboat
  • 1975: Sara Jane Moore attempted to assassinate U.S. president Gerald Ford, but failed due to unfamiliarity with her weapon. Sara Jane Moore
  • 1957: François Duvalier (pictured), nicknamed Papa Doc, was elected President of Haiti as a populist before consolidating power and ruling as a dictator for the rest of his life. François Duvalier
  • 1939: A joint military parade by the troops of Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union took place in Brest-Litovsk to celebrate their partition of Poland. German–Soviet military parade in Brest-Litovsk
  • 1934: One of Britain's worst mining accidents took place when an explosion at Gresford Colliery in Wales killed 266 men. Gresford disaster
  • 1914: First World War: The German submarine U-9 sank three Royal Navy cruisers, resulting in approximately 1,450 deaths. World War I
  • 1914: World War I: German naval forces bombarded Papeete in French Polynesia and sank a French gunboat. World War I
  • 1726: Bach led the first performance of his cantata Wer Dank opfert, der preiset mich, BWV 17, in Leipzig. Johann Sebastian Bach
  • 1586: Eighty Years' War: Spanish forces defeated an Anglo-Dutch army at the Battle of Zutphen.* 1789 – The office of United States Postmaster General was formally established. Eighty Years' War

More events

  • 1965: The Indo-Pakistani War of 1965 between India and Pakistan over Kashmir ends after the United Nations calls for a ceasefire and after a last engagement near Dograi. India–Pakistan war of 1965

  • 1953: The Four Level Interchange, first stack interchange in the world opened in Los Angeles. Four Level Interchange

  • 1941: The Holocaust in Ukraine: On the Jewish New Year Day, the German SS murders 6,000 Jews in Vinnytsia, Ukraine. Those are the survivors of the previous killings that took place a few days earlier in which about 24,000 Jews were executed. The Holocaust in Ukraine
  • 1939: World War II: A joint German–Soviet military parade in Brest-Litovsk is held to celebrate the successful invasion of Poland. World War II
  • 1919: The steel strike of 1919, led by the Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers, begins in Pennsylvania before spreading across the United States. 1919 General Steel Strike
  • 1910: The Duke of York's Picture House opens in Brighton, now the oldest continually operating cinema in Britain. Duke of York's Picture House, Brighton
  • 1896: Queen Victoria surpasses her grandfather King George III as the longest reigning monarch in British history (later surpassed by her great-great-granddaughter Elizabeth II on 9 September 2015). Queen Victoria
  • 1891: The first hydropower plant of Finland is commissioned along the Tammerkoski rapids in Tampere, Pirkanmaa. Hydroelectricity
  • 1885: Lord Randolph Churchill makes a speech in Ulster in opposition to the Irish Home Rule movement. Lord Randolph Churchill
  • 1862: A preliminary version of the Emancipation Proclamation is released by Abraham Lincoln. Emancipation Proclamation
  • 1857: The Russian warship Lefort capsizes and sinks during a storm in the Gulf of Finland, killing all 826 aboard. Russian ship of the line Lefort
  • 1792: Primidi Vendémiaire of year one of the French Republican Calendar as the French First Republic comes into being. Vendémiaire
  • 1789: Battle of Rymnik: Alexander Suvorov's Russian and allied army defeats superior Ottoman Empire forces. Battle of Rymnik
  • 1776: Nathan Hale is hanged for spying during the American Revolution. Nathan Hale
  • 1726: Bach leads the first performance of his cantata Wer Dank opfert, der preiset mich, BWV 17, in Leipzig. Johann Sebastian Bach
  • 1711: The first attacks of the Tuscarora War begin in present-day North Carolina. Tuscarora War
  • 1586: Eighty Years' War: A Spanish force led by the Marquis del Vasto successfully fights its way past a joint English/Dutch ambush in the Battle of Zutphen. Eighty Years' War
  • 1499: The Treaty of Basel concludes the Swabian War. Treaty of Basel (1499)
  • 1236: The Samogitians defeat the Livonian Brothers of the Sword in the Battle of Saule. Battle of Saule

About the date itself

September 22 is the 265th day of an ordinary year, with 100 days left after it (101 in a leap year, where it is the 266th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 22 September 2027 in 2027 is 21 Rabi' al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 38.
