August 10: what happened on this day

What happened on 10 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2019: Having already caused severe flooding in the Philippines, Typhoon Lekima made landfall in Zhejiang, China, killing 45 people in the province. Typhoon Lekima
  • 2019: Jeffrey Epstein, an American financier and convicted sex offender, was found dead in his cell at the Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York. Jeffrey Epstein
  • 2007: Amid large protests against the impending demolition of the Queen's Pier, the Hong Kong High Court dismissed legal attempts to preserve the landmark. Queen's Pier
  • 1966: The Heron Road Bridge in Ottawa, Canada, collapsed during its construction, killing nine workers. Heron Road Workers Memorial Bridge
  • 1953: First Indochina War: The French Union withdrew its forces from Operation Camargue against the Việt Minh in central modern-day Vietnam. First Indochina War
  • 1901: The Amalgamated Association of Iron, Steel and Tin Workers began an ultimately unsuccessful strike to reverse its declining fortunes and organize large numbers of new members. Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers
  • 1861: American Civil War: The first major battle west of the Mississippi River, the Battle of Wilson's Creek, was fought. American Civil War
  • 1844: From measurements of its motion, German astronomer Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel deduced that Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, had an unseen companion. Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel
  • 1793: The Louvre (pyramid pictured) in Paris, today the world's most-visited museum, opened with an exhibition of 537 paintings and 184 objets d'art. Louvre
  • 1755: The first wave of the Expulsion of the Acadians from the present-day Canadian Maritime provinces by the British began with the Bay of Fundy campaign at Chignecto. Expulsion of the Acadians
  • 1628: The Swedish warship Vasa sank shortly after departing Stockholm on her maiden voyage to take part in the Thirty Years' War. Vasa (ship)
  • 1270: Yekuno Amlak deposed the last Zagwe king and seized the imperial throne of Ethiopia, beginning the reign of the Solomonic dynasty, which would last for more than 700 years. Yekuno Amlak

More events

  • 2024: Israel strikes Al-Tabaeen school in eastern Gaza City, killing at least 80 Palestinians. Israel Defense Forces
  • 2019: Thirty-two are killed and one million are evacuated as Typhoon Lekima makes landfall in Zhejiang, China. Earlier it had caused flooding in the Philippines. Typhoon Lekima
  • 2018: Horizon Air employee Richard Russell hijacks and performs an unauthorized takeoff on a Horizon Air Bombardier Dash 8 Q400 plane at Seattle–Tacoma International Airport in Washington, flying it for more than an hour before crashing the plane and killing himself on Ketron Island in Puget Sound. Horizon Air
  • 2014: Forty people are killed when Sepahan Airlines Flight 5915 crashes at Tehran's Mehrabad International Airport. Sepahan Airlines Flight 5915
  • 2009: Twenty people are killed in Handlová, Trenčín Region, in the deadliest mining disaster in Slovakia's history. Handlová
  • 2001: The 2001 Angola train attack occurs, causing 252 deaths. 2001 Angola train attack
  • 1999: One person is killed and five people are injured in the Los Angeles Jewish Community Center shooting. 1999 Los Angeles Jewish Community Center shooting
  • 1997: Sixteen people are killed when Formosa Airlines Flight 7601 crashes near Beigan Airport in the Matsu Islands of Taiwan. Formosa Airlines Flight 7601
  • 1990: The Magellan space probe reaches Venus. Magellan (spacecraft)
  • 1981: Murder of Adam Walsh: The head of John Walsh's son is found. This inspires the creation of the television series America's Most Wanted and the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children. Murder of Adam Walsh
  • 1977: In Yonkers, New York, 24-year-old postal employee David Berkowitz ("Son of Sam") is arrested for a series of killings in the New York City area over the period of one year. Yonkers, New York
  • 1969: A day after murdering actress Sharon Tate and four others, members of Charles Manson's cult kill Leno and Rosemary LaBianca. Sharon Tate
  • 1961: Vietnam War: The U.S. Army begins Operation Ranch Hand, spraying an estimated 20 million US gallons (76,000 m3) of defoliants and herbicides over rural areas of South Vietnam in an attempt to deprive the Viet Cong of food and vegetation cover. Vietnam War
  • 1953: First Indochina War: The French Union withdraws its forces from Operation Camargue against the Viet Minh in central Vietnam. First Indochina War
  • 1948: Candid Camera makes its television debut after being on radio for a year as The Candid Microphone. Candid Camera
  • 1944: World War II: The Battle of Narva on the Eastern Front ends with a defensive German victory. Battle of Narva (1944)
  • 1920: World War I: Ottoman sultan Mehmed VI's representatives sign the Treaty of Sèvres that divides up the Ottoman Empire between the Allies. World War I
  • 1905: Russo-Japanese War: Peace negotiations begin in Portsmouth, New Hampshire. Portsmouth, New Hampshire
  • 1901: The U.S. Steel recognition strike by the Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers begins. U.S. Steel recognition strike of 1901
  • 1864: After a severe electric storm, Providence Spring emerged at the Confederate States Army's Andersonville Prison in Sumter County, Georgia, making it the only source of clean drinking water for 32,000 Union Army prisoners on 27 acres (11 ha) of ground. Providence Spring
  • 1856: The Last Island hurricane strikes Louisiana, resulting in over 200 deaths. 1856 Last Island hurricane
  • 1808: Finnish War: Swedish forces led by General von Döbeln defeat Russian forces led by General Šepelev in the Battle of Kauhajoki. Finnish War
  • 1755: Under the direction of Charles Lawrence, the British begin to forcibly deport the Acadians from Nova Scotia to the Thirteen Colonies and France. Charles Lawrence (British Army officer)
  • 1664: The Habsburg empire and the Ottoman empire sign the peace treaty of Vasvár, which results in peace until 1683. Habsburg monarchy
  • 1628: The Swedish warship Vasa sinks on her maiden voyage off Stockholm. Vasa (ship)
  • 1557: Battle of St. Quentin: Spanish victory over the French in the Italian War of 1551–59. Battle of St. Quentin (1557)
  • 1512: The naval Battle of Saint-Mathieu, during the War of the League of Cambrai, sees the simultaneous destruction of the Breton ship La Cordelière and the English ship The Regent. Battle of Saint-Mathieu
  • 1316: The Second Battle of Athenry takes place near Athenry during the Bruce campaign in Ireland. Second Battle of Athenry
  • 1258: Manfred, illegitimate son of Frederick II, is crowned king of Sicily. Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor
  • 991: Battle of Maldon: The English, led by Byrhtnoth, Ealdorman of Essex, are defeated by a band of inland-raiding Vikings near Maldon, Essex. Battle of Maldon

About the date itself
