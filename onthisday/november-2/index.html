November 2: what happened on this day

What happened on 2 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2008: Lewis Hamilton overtook Timo Glock in the final corners of the Brazilian Grand Prix to become the Formula One World Drivers' Champion by one point. Lewis Hamilton
  • 2000: As members of Expedition 1, American astronaut William Shepherd and Russian cosmonauts Sergei Krikalev and Yuri Gidzenko (all pictured) became the first resident crew to arrive at the International Space Station. Expedition 1
  • 1997: Tropical Storm Linda made landfall in the Mekong Delta in Vietnam, causing more than 3,000 deaths. Tropical Storm Linda (1997)
  • 1994: A lightning strike ruptured three oil tanks near Dronka, Egypt, causing a flood that killed 469 people. 1994 Durunka floods and lightning strike
  • 1984: Velma Barfield, a convicted serial killer, became the first woman to be executed in the United States since 1962. Velma Barfield
  • 1963: South Vietnamese president Ngô Đình Diệm (pictured) was arrested and assassinated in Saigon, marking the culmination of a coup d'état led by Dương Văn Minh. South Vietnam
  • 1957: A large number of people witnessed a fiery object in the sky near Levelland, Texas, which the United States Air Force said was ball lightning. Levelland UFO case
  • 1949: The Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference ended with the Netherlands agreeing to transfer sovereignty of the Dutch East Indies to the United States of Indonesia. Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference
  • 1943: World War II: A U.S. Navy task force turned away an Imperial Japanese Navy formation at the Battle of Empress Augusta Bay, thus protecting the landings at Cape Torokina. World War II
  • 1932: The Australian military began a "war against emus", flightless native birds blamed for widespread damage to crops in Western Australia. Australian Defence Force
  • 1917: The British government issued the Balfour Declaration in support of a Jewish homeland in Palestine, then an Ottoman region with a small Jewish minority. Balfour Declaration
  • 1880: James A. Garfield was elected as president of the United States; the election is the closest to date by popular vote margin. James A. Garfield

More events

  • 2022: A peace agreement is signed between the Ethiopian government and the Tigray People's Liberation Front, ending the Tigray War. Ethiopia–Tigray peace agreement
  • 2020: In Vienna's Innere Stadt district, an ISIL sympathizer shoots and kills four people and injures 23 more, before being shot and killed by the police. Vienna
  • 2008: Lewis Hamilton secured his maiden Formula One Drivers' Championship Title by one point ahead of Felipe Massa at the Brazilian Grand Prix, after a pass for fifth place against the Toyota of Timo Glock on the final lap of the race. Lewis Hamilton
  • 2000: Expedition 1 arrived at the International Space Station for the first long-duration stay onboard. From this day to present, a continuous human presence in space on the station remains uninterrupted. Expedition 1
  • 1997: Tropical Storm Linda makes landfall in the Mekong Delta in Vietnam, causing more than 3,000 deaths. Tropical Storm Linda (1997)
  • 1988: The Morris worm, the first Internet-distributed computer worm to gain significant mainstream media attention, is launched from MIT. Morris worm
  • 1988: LOT Polish Airlines Flight 703 crashes in Białobrzegi, Podkarpackie Voivodeship, Poland, killing one person and injuring several more. LOT Polish Airlines Flight 703
  • 1984: Capital punishment: Velma Barfield becomes the first woman executed in the United States since 1962. Capital punishment
  • 1982: Channel 4, the British free-to-air public broadcast television channel funded by its commercial activities, starts broadcasting. Channel 4
  • 1973: Aeroflot Flight 19 is hijacked and diverted to Vnukovo International Airport, where the aircraft is stormed by authorities. Aeroflot Flight 19
  • 1966: The Cuban Adjustment Act comes into force, allowing 123,000 Cubans the opportunity to apply for permanent residence in the United States. Cuban Adjustment Act
  • 1965: Norman Morrison, a 31-year-old Quaker, sets himself on fire in front of the river entrance to the Pentagon to protest the use of napalm in the Vietnam war. Norman Morrison
  • 1963: South Vietnamese President Ngô Đình Diệm is assassinated following a military coup. South Vietnam
  • 1959: Quiz show scandals: Twenty-One game show contestant Charles Van Doren admits to a Congressional committee that he had been given questions and answers in advance. 1950s quiz show scandals
  • 1959: The first section of the M1 motorway, the first inter-urban motorway in the United Kingdom, is opened between the present junctions 5 and 18, along with the M10 motorway and M45 motorway. M1 motorway
  • 1956: Suez Crisis: Israel occupies the Gaza Strip. Suez Crisis
  • 1949: The Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference ends with the Netherlands agreeing to transfer sovereignty of the Dutch East Indies to the United States of Indonesia. Dutch–Indonesian Round Table Conference
  • 1947: In California, designer Howard Hughes performs the maiden (and only) flight of the Hughes H-4 Hercules (also known as the "Spruce Goose"), the largest fixed-wing aircraft ever built until Scaled Composites rolled out their Stratolaunch in May 2017. Howard Hughes
  • 1936: The BBC Television Service, the world's first regular, "high-definition" (then defined as at least 200 lines) service begins. Renamed BBC1 in 1964, the channel still runs to this day. BBC Television
  • 1920: In the United States, KDKA of Pittsburgh starts broadcasting as the first commercial radio station. The first broadcast is the result of the 1920 United States presidential election. KDKA (AM)
  • 1917: The Balfour Declaration proclaims British support for the "establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people" with the clear understanding "that nothing shall be done which may prejudice the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities". Balfour Declaration
  • 1916: Hussein bin Ali proclaims himself "King of the Arab Countries". Hussein, King of Hejaz
  • 1914: World War I: The Russian Empire declares war on the Ottoman Empire and the Dardanelles is subsequently closed. World War I
  • 1899: The Boers begin their 118-day siege of British-held Ladysmith during the Second Boer War. Boers
  • 1882: The great fire destroys a large part of Oulu's city center in Oulu Province, Finland. Great Oulu fire of 1882
  • 1868: Dost Mohammad Khan surrenders to the British and is exiled, but the First Anglo-Afghan War continues. Dost Mohammad Khan
  • 1795: The French Directory, a five-man revolutionary government, is created. French Directory
  • 1675: Plymouth Colony governor Josiah Winslow leads a colonial militia against the Narragansett during King Philip's War. Plymouth Colony
  • 1622: The Imperial-Spanish army under the Count of Tilly captures Mannheim. Johann Tserclaes, Count of Tilly
  • 676: Election of pope Donus following the death of pope Adeodatus II some months prior. Pope Donus

About the date itself

November 2 is the 306th day of an ordinary year, with 59 days left after it (60 in a leap year, where it is the 307th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 2 November 2027 in 2027 is 3 Jumada al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 44.
