May 26: what happened on this day

What happened on 26 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2025: A car rammed into a crowd on Water Street, Liverpool, injuring 65 people. 2025 Liverpool parade attack
  • 2020: Protests following the murder of George Floyd erupted in Minneapolis–Saint Paul before spreading across the United States and internationally. George Floyd protests
  • 1999: Manchester United won the UEFA Champions League final to become the first English football club to win three major championships in the same season. Manchester United F.C.
  • 1967: The Beatles' Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band, one of the first progressive rock albums, was released. The Beatles
  • 1940: Second World War: The Allies began a mass evacuation of British, French and Belgian troops cut off by the German army during the Battle of Dunkirk. World War II
  • 1933: American singer Jimmie Rodgers died at the Taft Hotel in New York City. Jimmie Rodgers
  • 1906: Vauxhall Bridge in London opened, crossing the River Thames between Vauxhall and Westminster. Vauxhall Bridge
  • 1897: The Church of England returned the original manuscript of William Bradford's Of Plymouth Plantation, an account of the Pilgrims and the early years of the Plymouth Colony, to the state of Massachusetts. Church of England
  • 1888: The comic novel The Diary of a Nobody by brothers George and Weedon Grossmith first appeared in serial form in Punch. The Diary of a Nobody
  • 1865: American Civil War: General Edmund Kirby Smith negotiated the surrender of his army, the only significant Confederate Army force remaining in the war. American Civil War
  • 1822: The deadliest fire in Norwegian history occurred at a church in Grue, killing at least 113 people. Grue Church fire
  • 1328: English friar William of Ockham, who popularised the methodological principle known as Occam's razor, secretly left Avignon under threat from Pope John XXII. Friar

More events

  • 2025: 65 people are injured when a car rams into a crowd on Water Street, near Liverpool F.C.'s Premier League trophy parade. 2025 Liverpool parade attack
  • 2020: Protests triggered by the murder of George Floyd erupt in Minneapolis–Saint Paul, later becoming widespread across the United States and around the world. George Floyd protests
  • 2008: Severe flooding begins in eastern and southern China that will ultimately cause 148 deaths and force the evacuation of 1.3 million. 2008 South China floods
  • 2002: The tugboat Robert Y. Love collides with a support pier of Interstate 40 on the Arkansas River near Webbers Falls, Oklahoma, resulting in 14 deaths and 11 others injured. I-40 bridge disaster
  • 1998: The first "National Sorry Day" is held in Australia. Reconciliation events are held nationally, and attended by over a million people. National Sorry Day
  • 1991: Zviad Gamsakhurdia becomes the first elected President of the Republic of Georgia in the post-Soviet era. Zviad Gamsakhurdia
  • 1986: The European Community adopts the European flag. European Economic Community
  • 1981: Italian Prime Minister Arnaldo Forlani and his coalition cabinet resign following a scandal over membership of the pseudo-masonic lodge P2 (Propaganda Due). Prime Minister of Italy
  • 1972: Cold War: The United States and the Soviet Union sign the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty. Cold War
  • 1970: The Soviet Tupolev Tu-144 becomes the first commercial transport to exceed Mach 2. Tupolev Tu-144
  • 1968: H-dagurinn in Iceland: Traffic changes from driving on the left to driving on the right overnight. H-dagurinn
  • 1966: British Guiana gains independence, becoming Guyana. British Guiana
  • 1942: World War II: The Battle of Gazala begins, in present-day Libya. Battle of Gazala
  • 1940: World War II: The Siege of Calais ends with the surrender of the British and French garrison. Siege of Calais (1940)
  • 1937: Walter Reuther and members of the United Auto Workers (UAW) clash with Ford Motor Company security guards at the River Rouge Complex in Dearborn, Michigan, during the Battle of the Overpass. Walter Reuther
  • 1927: The last Ford Model T rolls off the assembly line after a production run of 15,007,003 vehicles. Ford Model T
  • 1923: The first 24 Hours of Le Mans is held in France. Run annually in June thereafter, it became the oldest endurance racing event in the world. 24 Hours of Le Mans
  • 1908: The first major commercial oil strike in the Middle East is made at Masjed Soleyman in southwest Persia. The rights to the resource were quickly acquired by the Anglo-Persian Oil Company. Masjed Soleyman
  • 1900: Thousand Days' War: The Colombian Conservative Party turns the tide of war in their favor with victory against the Colombian Liberal Party in the Battle of Palonegro. Thousand Days' War
  • 1896: Charles Dow publishes the first edition of the Dow Jones Industrial Average. Charles Dow
  • 1869: Boston University is chartered by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts. Boston University
  • 1865: Conclusion of the American Civil War: The Confederate General Edmund Kirby Smith, commander of the Trans-Mississippi division, is the last full general of the Confederate Army to surrender, at Galveston, Texas. Conclusion of the American Civil War
  • 1822: At least 113 people die in the Grue Church fire, the biggest fire disaster in Norway's history. Grue Church fire
  • 1805: Napoléon Bonaparte assumes the title of King of Italy and is crowned with the Iron Crown of Lombardy in Milan Cathedral, the gothic cathedral in Milan. Napoleon
  • 1736: The Battle of Ackia is fought near the present site of Tupelo, Mississippi. British and Chickasaw soldiers repel a French and Choctaw attack on the then-Chickasaw village of Ackia. Chickasaw Campaign of 1736
  • 1637: Pequot War: A combined English and Mohegan force under John Mason attacks a village in Connecticut, massacring approximately 500 Pequots. Pequot War
  • 1538: Geneva expels John Calvin and his followers from the city. Calvin lives in exile in Strasbourg for the next three years. Geneva
  • 1293: An earthquake strikes Kamakura, Kanagawa, Japan, killing about 23,000. 1293 Kamakura earthquake
  • 961: King Otto I elects his six-year-old son Otto II as heir apparent and co-ruler of the East Frankish Kingdom. He is crowned at Aachen, and placed under the tutelage of his grandmother Matilda. Otto the Great
  • 866: Basil I is crowned as co-emperor of the Byzantine Empire by Michael III. Basil I

About the date itself

May 26 is the 146th day of an ordinary year, with 219 days left after it (220 in a leap year, where it is the 147th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 26 May 2027 in 2027 is 20 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 21.
