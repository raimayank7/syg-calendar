July 27: what happened on this day

What happened on 27 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 12 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2020: A major oil spill from the Colonial Pipeline was discovered in Mecklenburg County, North Carolina (system map pictured). 2020 Colonial Pipeline oil spill
  • 2016: The last nine Aston Martin DB9 (example pictured) units rolled off the production line, marking the end of a twelve-year manufacturing period. Aston Martin DB9
  • 2007: While covering a police pursuit in Phoenix, Arizona, two news helicopters collided in mid-air, killing both crews. Car chase
  • 1983: American musician Madonna released her self-titled debut album, which set the standard for the genre of dance-pop for decades. Madonna
  • 1955: The Austrian State Treaty came into effect, ending the Allied occupation of Austria, although the country was not free of Allied troops until October. Austrian State Treaty
  • 1953: An armistice was signed to end hostilities in the Korean War, officially making the division of Korea indefinite by creating a 4 km wide (2.5 mi) demilitarized zone across the Korean Peninsula. Korean Armistice Agreement
  • 1943: As the Surprise Hurricane struck Texas, a United States Army Air Forces pilot made the first reconnaissance flight into a hurricane. 1943 Surprise Hurricane
  • 1942: Second World War: Allied forces halted the Axis invasion of Egypt at the First Battle of El Alamein. World War II
  • 1916: First World War: British mariner Charles Fryatt was executed in Bruges, Belgium, after a German court-martial found him guilty of being a franc-tireur. World War I
  • 1689: First Jacobite rising: Scottish and Irish Jacobites defeated Williamite forces at Killiecrankie, Scotland. Jacobite rising of 1689
  • 1225: Saint Mary's Church on Gotland, later to become Visby Cathedral (pictured), was consecrated. Gotland
  • 1054: During his invasion of Scotland, Siward, Earl of Northumbria, defeated Macbeth, King of Scotland, in an engagement north of the Firth of Forth. Siward, Earl of Northumbria

More events

  • 2015: Seven people are killed and many injured after gunmen attack an Indian police station in Punjab. 2015 Gurdaspur attack
  • 2002: Ukraine airshow disaster: A Sukhoi Su-27 fighter crashes during an air show at Lviv, Ukraine killing 77 and injuring more than 500 others, making it the deadliest air show disaster in history. Sknyliv air show disaster
  • 1996: In Atlanta, United States, a pipe bomb explodes at Centennial Olympic Park during the 1996 Summer Olympics. Atlanta
  • 1995: The Korean War Veterans Memorial is dedicated in Washington, D.C. Korean War Veterans Memorial
  • 1989: While attempting to land at Tripoli International Airport in Libya, Korean Air Flight 803 crashes just short of the runway. Seventy-five of the 199 passengers and crew and four people on the ground are killed, in the second accident involving a DC-10 in less than two weeks, the first being United Airlines Flight 232. Tripoli International Airport
  • 1983: Black July: Eighteen Tamil political prisoners at the Welikada high security prison in Colombo are massacred by Sinhalese prisoners, the second such massacre in two days. Black July
  • 1975: Mayor of Jaffna and former MP Alfred Duraiappah is shot dead. List of mayors of Jaffna
  • 1974: Watergate scandal: The House of Representatives Judiciary Committee votes 27 to 11 to recommend the first article of impeachment (for obstruction of justice) against President Richard Nixon. Watergate scandal
  • 1963: The Puijo observation tower is opened to the general public at Puijo Hill in Kuopio, Finland. Puijo tower
  • 1955: The Austrian State Treaty restores Austrian sovereignty. Austrian State Treaty
  • 1955: El Al Flight 402 is shot down by two fighter jets after straying into Bulgarian air space. All 58 people on board are killed. El Al Flight 402
  • 1949: Initial flight of the de Havilland Comet, the first jet-powered airliner. De Havilland Comet
  • 1947: In Vatican City, Rome, canonization of Catherine Labouré, the saint whose apparitions of the Virgin Mary originated the worldwide distribution of the Miraculous Medal. Canonization
  • 1940: The animated short A Wild Hare is released, introducing the character of Bugs Bunny. A Wild Hare
  • 1929: The Geneva Convention of 1929, dealing with treatment of prisoners-of-war, is signed by 53 nations. Geneva Convention on Prisoners of War
  • 1919: The Chicago Race Riot erupts after a racial incident occurred on a South Side beach, leading to 38 fatalities and 537 injuries over a five-day period. Chicago race riot of 1919
  • 1900: Kaiser Wilhelm II makes a speech comparing Germans to Huns; for years afterwards, "Hun" would be a disparaging name for Germans. Wilhelm II
  • 1890: Vincent van Gogh shoots himself and dies two days later. Vincent van Gogh
  • 1866: The first permanent transatlantic telegraph cable is successfully completed, stretching from Valentia Island, Ireland, to Heart's Content, Newfoundland. Transatlantic telegraph cable
  • 1865: Welsh settlers arrive at Chubut in Argentina. Y Wladfa
  • 1816: Seminole Wars: The Battle of Negro Fort ends when a hot shot cannonball fired by US Navy Gunboat No. 154 explodes the fort's Powder Magazine, killing approximately 275. It is considered the deadliest single cannon shot in US history. Negro Fort
  • 1794: French Revolution: Maximilien Robespierre is arrested after encouraging the execution of more than 17,000 "enemies of the Revolution". French Revolution
  • 1778: American Revolution: First Battle of Ushant: British and French fleets fight to a standoff. American Revolution
  • 1714: The Great Northern War: The first significant victory of the Russian Navy in the naval battle of Gangut against the Swedish Navy near the Hanko Peninsula. Great Northern War
  • 1694: A Royal charter is granted to the Bank of England. Royal charter
  • 1663: The English Parliament passes the second Navigation Act requiring that all goods bound for the American colonies have to be sent in English ships from English ports. After the Acts of Union 1707, Scotland would be included in the Act. Parliament of England
  • 1549: The Jesuit priest Francis Xavier's ship reaches Japan. Jesuits
  • 1299: According to Edward Gibbon, Osman I invades the territory of Nicomedia for the first time, usually considered to be the founding day of the Ottoman state. Edward Gibbon
  • 1214: Battle of Bouvines: Philip II of France decisively defeats Imperial, English and Flemish armies, effectively ending John of England's Angevin Empire. Battle of Bouvines
  • 1189: Friedrich Barbarossa arrives at Niš, the capital of Serbian King Stefan Nemanja, during the Third Crusade. Frederick Barbarossa

About the date itself

It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 27 July 2027 in 2027 is 23 Safar 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
