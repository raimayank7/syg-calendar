November 9: what happened on this day

What happened on 9 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2019: The Alabama Crimson Tide and LSU Tigers football teams, both with undefeated records thus far that season, played in a "Game of the Century". Alabama Crimson Tide football
  • 2019: Pakistani prime minister Imran Khan inaugurated the Kartarpur Corridor, a visa-free border crossing connecting the Gurdwara Darbar Sahib to the India–Pakistan border. Imran Khan
  • 2016: A tram derailment in Croydon, London, killed seven people. 2016 Croydon tram derailment
  • 1989: East German official Günter Schabowski mistakenly announced the immediate opening of the inner German border, resulting in the fall of the Berlin Wall that night (border crossing pictured). Günter Schabowski
  • 1985: At age 22, Garry Kasparov became the then-youngest World Chess Champion by defeating then-champion Anatoly Karpov. Garry Kasparov
  • 1967: The first issue of Rolling Stone, an American magazine focusing on music, politics and popular culture, was published. Rolling Stone
  • 1938: Kristallnacht began as SA stormtroopers and civilians destroyed and ransacked Jewish homes, businesses and synagogues in Germany and Austria, resulting in at least 90 deaths and the deportation of 30,000 men to concentration camps. Kristallnacht
  • 1928: The Great Mecca Feast, the first documentary film on the Hajj, premiered in the Netherlands. The Great Mecca Feast
  • 1926: Paul Hindemith's opera Cardillac (scene pictured) premiered at the Dresden State Opera, conducted by Fritz Busch. Paul Hindemith
  • 1914: World War I: Off the Cocos (Keeling) Islands, the Australian light cruiser Sydney sank Emden, the last active German warship in the Indian Ocean, at the Battle of Cocos. World War I
  • 1913: A severe blizzard reached its maximum intensity in the Great Lakes Basin of North America, destroying 19 ships and 68,300 tons of cargo, and killing more than 250 people. Great Lakes Storm of 1913
  • 1888: Mary Jane Kelly, widely believed to be the fifth and final victim of the notorious unidentified serial killer Jack the Ripper, was murdered in London. Mary Jane Kelly

More events

  • 2023: U.S. surgeons at NYU Langone Health announce the world's first whole eye transplant. NYU Langone Health
  • 2014: A non-binding self-determination consultation is held in Catalonia, asking Catalan citizens their opinion on whether Catalonia should become a state and, if so, whether it should be an independent state. 2014 Catalan self-determination referendum
  • 2011: The first national test of the Emergency Alert System is activated in the United States at 2:00 p.m. EST. Emergency Alert System
  • 2005: Suicide bombers attack three hotels in Amman, Jordan, killing at least 60 people. 2005 Amman bombings
  • 1999: TAESA Flight 725 crashes after takeoff from Uruapan International Airport in Uruapan, Michoacán, Mexico, killing all 18 people on board. TAESA Flight 725
  • 1998: Capital punishment in the United Kingdom, already abolished for murder, is completely abolished for all remaining capital offences. Capital punishment in the United Kingdom
  • 1989: Cold War: Fall of the Berlin Wall: East Germany opens checkpoints in the Berlin Wall, allowing its citizens to travel to West Berlin. Fall of the Berlin Wall
  • 1979: Cold War: Nuclear false alarm: The NORAD computers and the Alternate National Military Command Center in Fort Ritchie, Maryland, detect a purported massive Soviet nuclear strike. After reviewing the raw data from satellites and checking the early-warning radars, the alert is cancelled. Cold War
  • 1967: Apollo program: NASA launches the unmanned Apollo 4 test spacecraft, atop the first Saturn V rocket, from Florida's Cape Kennedy. Apollo program
  • 1963: At a coal mine in Miike, Japan, an explosion kills 458 and hospitalises 839 with carbon monoxide poisoning. Miike coal mine
  • 1960: Robert McNamara is named president of the Ford Motor Company, becoming the first non-Ford family member to serve in that post. He resigns a month later to join the newly elected John F. Kennedy administration. Robert McNamara
  • 1943: An agreement for the founding of the United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration is signed by 44 countries in the White House, Washington, D.C. White House
  • 1940: Warsaw is awarded the Virtuti Militari by the Polish government-in-exile. Warsaw
  • 1936: American fashion designer Ruth Harkness seeks and captures a nine-week-old panda cub in Sichuan; named Su Lin, he becomes the first live giant panda to enter the United States. Ruth Harkness
  • 1926: Paul Hindemith's Cardillac premieres at the Staatsoper in Dresden, conducted by Fritz Busch. Paul Hindemith
  • 1918: Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany abdicates after the German Revolution, and Germany is proclaimed a Republic. Kaiser
  • 1913: The Great Lakes Storm of 1913, the most destructive natural disaster ever to hit the lakes, reaches its greatest intensity after beginning two days earlier. The storm destroys 19 ships and kills more than 250 people. Great Lakes Storm of 1913
  • 1906: Theodore Roosevelt is the first sitting President of the United States to make an official trip outside the country, doing so to inspect progress on the Panama Canal. Theodore Roosevelt
  • 1900: Russian invasion of Manchuria: Russia completes its occupation of Manchuria with 100,000 troops. Russian invasion of Manchuria
  • 1887: The United States receives rights to Pearl Harbor, Hawaii. Pearl Harbor
  • 1872: The Great Boston Fire of 1872. Great Boston Fire of 1872
  • 1867: The Tokugawa shogunate hands back power to the Emperor of Japan, starting the Meiji Restoration. Tokugawa shogunate
  • 1799: Napoleon Bonaparte leads the Coup of 18 Brumaire ending the Directory government, and becoming First Consul of the successor Consulate Government. Coup of 18 Brumaire
  • 1729: Spain, France and Great Britain sign the Treaty of Seville. Treaty of Seville
  • 1719: In a treaty between Sweden and Hanover at the close of the Great Northern War, Sweden cedes the Duchies of Bremen and Verden (in northern Germany) to Hanover. Treaties of Stockholm (Great Northern War)
  • 1580: Second Desmond Rebellion: The Siege of Smerwick ends with the Catholic garrison surrendering to the English forces under Arthur Grey. The majority of the garrison is massacred the next day. Second Desmond Rebellion
  • 1456: Ulrich II, Count of Celje, last ruler of the County of Cilli, is assassinated in Belgrade. Ulrich II of Celje
  • 1330: At the Battle of Posada, Basarab I of Wallachia defeats the Hungarian army of Charles I Robert. Battle of Posada
  • 1313: Louis the Bavarian defeats his cousin Frederick I of Austria at the Battle of Gammelsdorf. Louis IV, Holy Roman Emperor
  • 1225: Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor, marries Yolanda of Jerusalem in Brindisi. Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor

About the date itself

November 9 is the 313th day of an ordinary year, with 52 days left after it (53 in a leap year, where it is the 314th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 9 November 2027 in 2027 is 10 Jumada al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 45.
