August 20: what happened on this day

What happened on 20 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2018: Silent Sam, a Confederate monument on the campus of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, was toppled by protestors. Silent Sam
  • 2008: Spanair Flight 5022 crashed just after take-off from Madrid's Barajas Airport, killing 154 people. Spanair Flight 5022
  • 1998: The Al-Shifa pharmaceutical factory (pictured) in Sudan was destroyed by a missile attack launched by the United States in retaliation for the August 7 U.S. embassy bombings. Al-Shifa pharmaceutical factory
  • 1989: After colliding with the dredger Bowbelle on the River Thames in London, the pleasure boat Marchioness sank in thirty seconds, killing 51 people. Dredging
  • 1988: The Troubles: The Provisional Irish Republican Army bombed a bus carrying British Army soldiers in Northern Ireland, killing eight of them and wounding twenty-eight. The Troubles
  • 1988: More than 150,000 acres (610 km2) were burned on the worst day of the largest wildfire in the recorded history of Yellowstone National Park in the United States. Yellowstone fires of 1988
  • 1950: Korean War: United Nations forces repelled an attempt by North Korea to capture the city of Taegu. Korean War
  • 1920: The American Professional Football Conference, a predecessor of the National Football League, was established in Canton, Ohio. National Football League
  • 1909: Pluto (pictured) was photographed for the first time at the Yerkes Observatory in Williams Bay, Wisconsin, U.S., 21 years before it was officially discovered by Clyde Tombaugh. Pluto
  • 1892: Celtic Park, the largest football stadium in Scotland and home of Celtic F.C., opened. Celtic Park
  • 1710: War of the Spanish Succession: A Spanish Bourbon army commanded by the Marquis de Bay was soundly defeated by a multinational army led by the Austrian commander Guido Starhemberg. War of the Spanish Succession
  • 1707: The first Siege of Pensacola came to an end with the British and Creek abandoning their attempt to capture Pensacola in Spanish Florida. Siege of Pensacola (1707)

More events

  • 2020: Joe Biden gives his acceptance speech virtually for the 2020 Democratic presidential nomination at the 2020 Democratic National Convention. Joe Biden
  • 2014: Seventy-two people are killed in Japan's Hiroshima Prefecture by a series of landslides caused by a month's worth of rain that fell in one day. Hiroshima Prefecture
  • 2011: First Air Flight 6560 crashes 1 mile (1.6 km) from the Resolute Bay runway, killing 12 of the 15 aboard. First Air Flight 6560
  • 2007: China Airlines Flight 120 catches fire and explodes after landing at Naha Airport in Okinawa, Japan. China Airlines Flight 120
  • 2002: A group of Iraqis opposed to the regime of Saddam Hussein take over the Iraqi embassy in Berlin, Germany for five hours before releasing their hostages and surrendering. Saddam Hussein
  • 1998: U.S. embassy bombings: The United States launches cruise missile attacks against alleged al-Qaeda camps in Afghanistan and a suspected chemical weapons plant in Sudan in retaliation for the August 7 bombings of American embassies in Kenya and Tanzania. 1998 United States embassy bombings
  • 1995: The Firozabad rail disaster kills 358 people in Firozabad, India. Firozabad rail collision
  • 1991: Dissolution of the Soviet Union, August Coup: More than 100,000 people rally outside the Soviet Union's parliament building protesting the coup aiming to depose President Mikhail Gorbachev. Dissolution of the Soviet Union
  • 1989: The pleasure boat Marchioness sinks on the River Thames following a collision. Fifty-one people are killed. Marchioness disaster
  • 1988: Iran–Iraq War: A ceasefire is agreed after almost eight years of war. Iran–Iraq War
  • 1986: In Edmond, Oklahoma, U.S. Postal employee Patrick Sherrill shoots and kills 14 of his co-workers and then commits suicide. Edmond, Oklahoma
  • 1975: Viking program: NASA launches the Viking 1 planetary probe toward Mars. Viking program
  • 1968: Cold War: Warsaw Pact troops invade Czechoslovakia, crushing the Prague Spring. East German participation is limited to a few specialists due to memories of the recent war. Only Albania and Romania refuse to participate. Cold War
  • 1960: Senegal breaks from the Mali Federation, declaring its independence. Senegal
  • 1949: Hungary adopts the Hungarian Constitution of 1949 and becomes a People's Republic. Hungary
  • 1944: World War II: The Battle of Romania begins with a major Soviet Union offensive. Battle of Romania
  • 1940: World War II: British Prime Minister Winston Churchill makes the fourth of his famous wartime speeches, containing the line "Never was so much owed by so many to so few". World War II
  • 1926: Japan's public broadcasting company, Nippon Hōsō Kyōkai (NHK) is established. Public broadcasting
  • 1920: The National Football League is organized as the American Professional Football Conference in Canton, Ohio. National Football League
  • 1910: Extreme fire weather in the Inland Northwest of the United States causes many small wildfires to coalesce into the Great Fire of 1910, burning approximately 3 million acres (12,000 km2) and killing 87 people. National Fire Danger Rating System
  • 1882: Tchaikovsky's 1812 Overture debuts in Moscow, Russia. Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky
  • 1864: Bakumatsu: Kinmon incident: The Chōshū Domain attempts to expel the Satsuma and Aizu Domains from Japan's imperial court. Bakumatsu
  • 1852: Steamboat Atlantic sinks on Lake Erie after a collision, with the loss of at least 150 lives. Atlantic (1848 ship)
  • 1775: The Spanish establish the Presidio San Augustin del Tucson in the town that became Tucson, Arizona. Presidio San Agustín del Tucsón
  • 1707: The first Siege of Pensacola comes to an end with the failure of the British to capture Pensacola, Florida. Siege of Pensacola (1707)
  • 1648: The Battle of Lens is the last major military confrontation of the Thirty Years' War, contributing to the signing of the Peace of Westphalia in October that year. Battle of Lens
  • 1467: The Second Battle of Olmedo takes places as part of a succession conflict between Henry IV of Castile and his half-brother Alfonso, Prince of Asturias. Second Battle of Olmedo
  • 1191: Believing Saladin had reneged on ransom promises, Richard I of England initiates the massacre at Ayyadieh, beheading 2,700 captive Muslim soldiers and another 300 women and children seized at the Fall of Acre. Saladin
  • 1083: The first King of Hungary, Stephen I, and his son, Prince Emeric, are canonized, a date now celebrated as a National Day in Hungary. King of Hungary
  • 917: Battle of Acheloos: Tsar Simeon I of Bulgaria decisively defeats a Byzantine army. Battle of Achelous (917)

About the date itself
