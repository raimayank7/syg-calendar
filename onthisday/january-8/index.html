January 8: what happened on this day

What happened on 8 January: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2011: Jared Lee Loughner opened fire at a public meeting held by U.S. representative Gabby Giffords in Tucson, Arizona, killing six people and injuring twelve others. Jared Lee Loughner
  • 2010: Gunmen from an offshoot of the Front for the Liberation of the Enclave of Cabinda attacked the bus transporting the Togo national football team to the Africa Cup of Nations in Angola, killing three people. Front for the Liberation of the Enclave of Cabinda
  • 1991: Jeremy Wade Delle committed suicide in his high-school class in Richardson, Texas, an event that inspired the Pearl Jam song "Jeremy". Richardson, Texas
  • 1977: Three bombs attributed to Armenian nationalists exploded across Moscow, killing seven people and injuring 37 people. 1977 Moscow bombings
  • 1972: Following Pakistan's defeat in the Bangladesh Liberation War, President Zulfikar Ali Bhutto released Bangladeshi politician Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (pictured) from prison in response to international pressure. Pakistani Instrument of Surrender
  • 1939: The New Deal for Aborigines was formally announced by the Australian government, providing for full civil rights for Indigenous Australians in exchange for cultural assimilation. New Deal for Aborigines
  • 1936: Reza Shah issued the Kashf-e hijab decree, ordering Iranian police to remove hijabs from any women in public. Reza Shah
  • 1928: The Committee for Union Action is established in Guatemala. Comité Pro Acción Sindical
  • 1904: Blackstone Library (pictured), the first branch of the Chicago Public Library system, was dedicated. Blackstone Library
  • 1889: American statistician Herman Hollerith received a patent for his electromechanical tabulating machine for punched-card data. Herman Hollerith
  • 1735: George Frideric Handel's opera Ariodante premiered at the Covent Garden Theatre (pictured) in London. George Frideric Handel
  • 1697: Scottish student Thomas Aikenhead became the last person in Great Britain to be executed for blasphemy. Thomas Aikenhead

More events

  • 2023: Supporters of former Brazil president Jair Bolsonaro storm the Brazilian Congress. Jair Bolsonaro
  • 2021: Twenty-three people are killed in what is described as a police ″massacre″ in La Vega, Caracas, Venezuela. La Vega raid
  • 2016: Joaquín Guzmán, widely regarded as the world's most powerful drug trafficker, is recaptured following his escape from a maximum security prison in Mexico. El Chapo
  • 2011: Sitting US Congresswoman Gabby Giffords is shot in the head along with 18 others in a mass shooting in Tucson, Arizona. Giffords survived the assassination attempt, but six others died, including John Roll, a federal judge. Gabby Giffords
  • 2009: A 6.1-magnitude earthquake in northern Costa Rica kills 15 people and injures 32. 2009 Cinchona earthquake
  • 2004: The RMS Queen Mary 2, then the largest ocean liner ever built, is christened by her namesake's granddaughter, Queen Elizabeth II. Queen Mary 2
  • 2003: Air Midwest Flight 5481 crashes at Charlotte-Douglas Airport, in Charlotte, North Carolina, killing all 21 people on board. Air Midwest Flight 5481
  • 1996: An Antonov An-32 cargo aircraft crashes into a crowded market in Kinshasa, Zaire, killing up to 223 people on the ground; two of six crew members are also killed. Antonov An-32
  • 1989: Kegworth air disaster: British Midland Flight 92, a Boeing 737-400, crashes into the M1 motorway, killing 47 of the 126 people on board. Kegworth air disaster
  • 1981: A local farmer reports a UFO sighting in Trans-en-Provence, France, claimed to be "perhaps the most completely and carefully documented sighting of all time". Trans-en-Provence case
  • 1975: Ella T. Grasso becomes Governor of Connecticut, the first woman to serve as a governor in the United States other than by succeeding her husband. Ella Grasso
  • 1973: Watergate scandal: The trial of seven men accused of illegal entry into Democratic Party headquarters at Watergate begins. Watergate scandal
  • 1964: President Lyndon B. Johnson declares a "War on Poverty" in the United States. Lyndon B. Johnson
  • 1959: Charles de Gaulle is proclaimed as the first President of the French Fifth Republic. Charles de Gaulle
  • 1946: Andrei Zhdanov, Chairman of the Finnish Allied Commission, submits to the Finnish War Criminal Court an interrogation report by General Erich Buschenhagen, a German prisoner of war, on the contacts between Finnish and German military personnel before the Continuation War and a copy of Hitler's Barbarossa plan. Andrei Zhdanov
  • 1940: World War II: Britain introduces food rationing. World War II
  • 1936: Kashf-e hijab decree is made and immediately enforced by Reza Shah, Iran's head of state, banning the wearing of Islamic veils in public. Kashf-e hijab
  • 1928: The Committee for Union Action is established in Guatemala. Comité Pro Acción Sindical
  • 1926: Abdul-Aziz ibn Saud is crowned King of Hejaz. Ibn Saud
  • 1918: U.S. President Woodrow Wilson announces his "Fourteen Points" as conditions for ending World War I. Woodrow Wilson
  • 1912: The city of San Diego passes an ordinance restricting free speech, provoking months of civil unrest between socialist Wobblies and business leaders that become the bloodiest free speech fight in history. San Diego
  • 1889: Herman Hollerith is issued US patent #395,791 for the 'Art of Applying Statistics' — his punched card calculator. Herman Hollerith
  • 1867: The United States Congress passes the bill to allow African American men the right to vote in Washington, D.C. African Americans
  • 1835: US President Andrew Jackson announces a celebratory dinner after having reduced the United States national debt to zero for the only time. Andrew Jackson
  • 1815: War of 1812: Battle of New Orleans: Andrew Jackson leads American forces in victory over the British. War of 1812
  • 1806: The Dutch Cape Colony in southern Africa becomes the British Cape Colony as a result of the Battle of Blaauwberg. Dutch Cape Colony
  • 1746: Second Jacobite rising: Bonnie Prince Charlie occupies Stirling. Jacobite rising of 1745
  • 1547: The first Lithuanian-language book, the Catechism of Martynas Mažvydas, is published in Königsberg. Lithuanian language
  • 1454: The papal bull Romanus Pontifex awards the Kingdom of Portugal exclusive trade and colonization rights to all of Africa south of Cape Bojador. Papal bull
  • 871: Æthelred I and Alfred the Great lead a West Saxon army to repel an invasion by Danelaw Vikings. Æthelred I of Wessex

About the date itself

January 8 is the 8th day of an ordinary year, with 357 days left after it (358 in a leap year, where it is the 8th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Friday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Monday, 2030: Tuesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 8 January 2027 in 2027 is 30 Rajab 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 1.
