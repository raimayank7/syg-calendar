August 16: what happened on this day

What happened on 16 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 14 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2015: Suicide bombers assassinated Pakistani politician Shuja Khanzada and killed at least 21 others at his home in Attock District. Assassination of Shuja Khanzada
  • 2009: At the World Championships in Athletics in Berlin, Usain Bolt ran the 100 metres in 9.58 seconds, breaking his own record set a year earlier. 2009 World Championships in Athletics
  • 1977: American singer and actor Elvis Presley (pictured), often referred to as the "King of Rock and Roll", was found dead in his home in Memphis, Tennessee. Elvis Presley
  • 1946: Widespread riots between Hindus and Muslims took place in Calcutta following the All-India Muslim League's call for an independent Pakistan. Direct Action Day
  • 1945: The Stanley Internment Camp in Hong Kong was liberated following the announcement of the surrender of Japan in World War II. Stanley Internment Camp
  • 1929: A long-running dispute between Muslims and Jews over access to the Western Wall in Jerusalem escalated into a week-long period of violent riots throughout Palestine. Western Wall
  • 1920: In the United Kingdom, the Blind Persons Act received royal assent. Blind Persons Act 1920
  • 1920: Ray Chapman of the Cleveland Indians was hit by a pitch and died the following day, becoming the only Major League Baseball player to die directly as a result of injuries sustained during a game. Ray Chapman
  • 1900: Second Boer War: A 10,000-strong column of soldiers led by Lord Kitchener broke a 13-day siege of a small garrison. Second Boer War
  • 1891: San Sebastian Church (pictured), an all-iron church in Manila, was officially consecrated. San Sebastian Church (Manila)
  • 1837: Daaga and two other African soldiers in the British Army's 1st West India Regiment were executed for their role in the St. Joseph Mutiny in Trinidad. Daaga
  • 1777: American Revolutionary War: American forces routed British and German troops at the Battle of Bennington in Walloomsac, New York. American Revolutionary War

More events

  • 2020: The August Complex fire in California burns more than one million acres of land. August Complex fire
  • 2015: Trigana Air Flight 267, an ATR 42, crashes in Oksibil, Bintang Mountains Regency, killing all 54 people on board. Trigana Air Flight 267
  • 2012: South African police fatally shoot 34 miners and wound 78 more during an industrial dispute at Marikana near Rustenburg. South African Police Service
  • 2008: The Trump International Hotel and Tower in Chicago is topped off at 1,389 feet (423 m), at the time becoming the world's highest residence above ground-level. Trump International Hotel and Tower (Chicago)
  • 1989: A solar particle event affects computers at the Toronto Stock Exchange, forcing a halt to trading. Solar particle event
  • 1986: A Sudan Airways Fokker F27 Friendship is shot down near Malakal in present day South Sudan, killing 60. Sudan Airways
  • 1972: In an unsuccessful coup d'état attempt, the Royal Moroccan Air Force fires upon Hassan II of Morocco's plane while he is traveling back to Rabat. Coup d'état
  • 1960: Cyprus gains its independence from the United Kingdom. Cyprus
  • 1954: The first issue of Sports Illustrated is published. Sports Illustrated
  • 1946: The All Hyderabad Trade Union Congress is founded in Secunderabad. All Hyderabad Trade Union Congress
  • 1943: World War II: 317 Greek civilians are murdered by soldiers of the German 1st Mountain Division in the village of Kommeno, Greece. Massacre of Kommeno
  • 1933: Christie Pits riot takes place in Toronto, Ontario. Christie Pits riot
  • 1930: The first British Empire Games are opened in Hamilton, Ontario, by the Governor General of Canada, the Viscount Willingdon. Commonwealth Games
  • 1927: The Dole Air Race begins from Oakland, California, to Honolulu, Hawaii, during which six out of the eight participating planes crash or disappear. Dole Air Race
  • 1920: The congress of the Communist Party of Bukhara opens. The congress would call for armed revolution. Communist Party of Bukhara
  • 1918: The Battle of Lake Baikal is fought between the Czechoslovak Legion and the Red Army. Battle of Lake Baikal
  • 1914: German admiral Wilhelm Souchon hands over the two warships under his command to the Ottomans and becomes commander of the Turkish navy, leading later to the entry of the Ottoman Empire in World War I. Wilhelm Souchon
  • 1906: The 8.2 Mw Valparaíso earthquake hits central Chile, killing 3,882 people. Seismic magnitude scales
  • 1896: Skookum Jim Mason, George Carmack and Dawson Charlie discover gold in a tributary of the Klondike River in Canada, setting off the Klondike Gold Rush. Keish
  • 1876: Richard Wagner's Siegfried, the penultimate opera in his Ring cycle, is premiered at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus. Richard Wagner
  • 1863: The Dominican Restoration War begins when Gregorio Luperón raises the Dominican flag in Santo Domingo after Spain had recolonized the country. Dominican Restoration War
  • 1858: U.S. President James Buchanan inaugurates the new transatlantic telegraph cable by exchanging greetings with Queen Victoria of the United Kingdom. However, a weak signal forces a shutdown of the service in a few weeks. James Buchanan
  • 1841: U.S. President John Tyler vetoes a bill which called for the re-establishment of the Second Bank of the United States. Enraged Whig Party members riot outside the White House in the most violent demonstration on White House grounds in U.S. history. President of the United States
  • 1819: Peterloo Massacre: Seventeen people die and over 600 are injured in cavalry charges at a public meeting at St. Peter's Field, Manchester, England. Peterloo Massacre
  • 1792: Maximilien Robespierre presents the petition of the Commune of Paris to the Legislative Assembly, which demanded the formation of a revolutionary tribunal. Maximilien Robespierre
  • 1777: American Revolutionary War: The Americans led by General John Stark rout British and Brunswick troops under Friedrich Baum at the Battle of Bennington in Walloomsac, New York. American Revolutionary War
  • 1685: Great Turkish War: An imperial coalition army under Charles of Lorraine defeats the Ottoman army that had been besieging Esztergom. Great Turkish War
  • 1550: Rabbi Moses Isserles issues his ruling in the Bragadin-Giustiniani dispute, one of the earliest instances of a copyright suit over any book. Moses Isserles
  • 1328: The House of Gonzaga seizes power in the Duchy of Mantua, and will rule until 1708. House of Gonzaga
  • 963: Nikephoros II Phokas is crowned emperor of the Byzantine Empire, swearing to protect the two sons of the previous emperor Romanos II, who are already crowned co-emperors. Nikephoros II Phokas

About the date itself

August 16 is the 228th day of an ordinary year, with 137 days left after it (138 in a leap year, where it is the 229th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 16 August 2027 in 2027 is 14 Rabi' al-awwal 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 33.
