May 27: what happened on this day

What happened on 27 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2014: The football club Kerala Blasters FC and its first supporters' group Manjappada were formed. Kerala Blasters FC
  • 2001: Twenty tourists were kidnapped by Abu Sayyaf militants in Palawan, Philippines, triggering a hostage crisis that lasted for more than a year. Dos Palmas kidnappings
  • 1995: American actor Christopher Reeve was thrown from his horse, leaving him quadriplegic; he later became an activist on behalf of people with spinal cord injuries. Christopher Reeve
  • 1983: An explosion at an illegal fireworks factory near Benton, Tennessee, killed eleven people. Benton fireworks disaster
  • 1975: The deadliest road accident in England took place when the brakes on a coach failed and it crashed in North Yorkshire, killing 32 people. Bus
  • 1963: American singer Bob Dylan (pictured) released The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan, his first album to include a significant number of original songs. Bob Dylan
  • 1954: The security clearance of American nuclear physicist J. Robert Oppenheimer, head of Project Y, was revoked. J. Robert Oppenheimer
  • 1940: World War II: Ninety-seven soldiers of the British Royal Norfolk Regiment were killed after surrendering to German forces. World War II
  • 1935: The United States Supreme Court ruled in A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States that the National Industrial Recovery Act, a major component of the New Deal, was unconstitutional. Supreme Court of the United States
  • 1923: French drivers André Lagache and René Léonard completed the most laps during the first edition of the sports car race the 24 Hours of Le Mans. André Lagache
  • 1896: One of the deadliest and most destructive tornadoes in U.S. history struck St. Louis, Missouri, and East St. Louis, Illinois, killing more than 255 people and injuring at least 1,000 others. 1896 St. Louis–East St. Louis tornado
  • 1644: Manchu regent Dorgon defeated rebel leader Li Zicheng of the Shun dynasty at the Battle of Shanhai Pass, allowing the Manchus to enter and conquer the capital city of Beijing. Manchu people

More events

  • 2018: Maryland Flood Event: A flood occurs throughout the Patapsco Valley, causing one death, destroying the entire first floors of buildings on Main Street in Ellicott City, and causing cars to overturn. 2018 Central Maryland flood
  • 2017: Andrew Scheer takes over after Rona Ambrose as the leader of the Conservative Party of Canada. Andrew Scheer
  • 2014: The football club Kerala Blasters FC and its first supporters' group Manjappada are formed. Kerala Blasters FC
  • 2001: Members of Abu Sayyaf, an Islamist separatist group, seize twenty hostages from an affluent island resort on Palawan in the Philippines; the hostage crisis would not be resolved until June 2002. Abu Sayyaf
  • 1998: Oklahoma City bombing: Michael Fortier is sentenced to 12 years in prison and fined $200,000 for failing to warn authorities about the terrorist plot. Oklahoma City bombing
  • 1996: First Chechen War: Russian president Boris Yeltsin meets with Chechen rebels for the first time and negotiates a cease-fire. First Chechen War
  • 1984: The Danube–Black Sea Canal is opened, in a ceremony attended by the Ceaușescus. It had been under construction since the 1950s. Danube–Black Sea Canal
  • 1980: The Gwangju Massacre: Airborne and army troops of South Korea retake the city of Gwangju from civil militias, killing at least 207 and possibly many more. Gwangju Uprising
  • 1975: Dibbles Bridge coach crash near Grassington, in North Yorkshire, England, kills 33, the highest ever death toll in a road accident in the United Kingdom. 1975 Dibbles Bridge coach crash
  • 1971: Pakistani forces massacre over 200 civilians, mostly Bengali Hindus, in the Bagbati massacre. Bengali Hindus
  • 1967: The U.S. Navy aircraft carrier USS John F. Kennedy is launched by Jacqueline Kennedy and her daughter Caroline. USS John F. Kennedy (CV-67)
  • 1962: The Centralia mine fire is ignited in the town's landfill above a coal mine. Centralia mine fire
  • 1958: First flight of the McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II. McDonnell Douglas F-4 Phantom II
  • 1950: The Linnanmäki amusement park is opened for the first time in Helsinki. Linnanmäki
  • 1941: World War II: U.S. president Franklin D. Roosevelt proclaims an "unlimited national emergency". Franklin D. Roosevelt
  • 1940: World War II: In the Le Paradis massacre, 99 soldiers from a Royal Norfolk Regiment unit are shot after surrendering to German troops; two survive. World War II
  • 1935: New Deal: The Supreme Court of the United States declares the National Industrial Recovery Act to be unconstitutional in A.L.A. Schechter Poultry Corp. v. United States, (295 U.S. 495). Supreme Court of the United States
  • 1930: The 1,046 feet (319 m) Chrysler Building in New York City, the tallest man-made structure at the time, opens to the public. Chrysler Building
  • 1919: The NC-4 aircraft arrives in Lisbon after completing the first transatlantic flight. Curtiss NC-4
  • 1917: Pope Benedict XV promulgates the 1917 Code of Canon Law, the first comprehensive codification of Catholic canon law in the legal history of the Catholic Church. Pope Benedict XV
  • 1905: Russo-Japanese War: The Battle of Tsushima begins. Russo-Japanese War
  • 1883: Alexander III is crowned Tsar of Russia. Alexander III of Russia
  • 1863: American Civil War: The first Union infantry assault of the Siege of Port Hudson occurs. American Civil War
  • 1832: An Egyptian army under Ibrahim Pasha captures Acre from the Ottomans after a five-months siege. Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt
  • 1799: War of the Second Coalition: Austrian forces defeat the French at Winterthur, Switzerland. War of the Second Coalition
  • 1798: The Pitt–Tierney duel takes place on Putney Heath outside London. A bloodless duel between the prime minister of Great Britain William Pitt the Younger and his political opponent George Tierney. Pitt–Tierney duel
  • 1703: Tsar Peter the Great founds the city of Saint Petersburg. Peter the Great
  • 1595: A Gaelic Irish army successfully ambushes an English force in the battle of Clontibret during the Nine Years' War. Battle of Clontibret
  • 1199: John is crowned King of England. John, King of England
  • 1120: Richard III of Capua is anointed as Prince two weeks before his untimely death. Richard III of Capua

About the date itself

May 27 is the 147th day of an ordinary year, with 218 days left after it (219 in a leap year, where it is the 148th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 27 May 2027 in 2027 is 21 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 21.
