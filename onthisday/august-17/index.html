August 17: what happened on this day

What happened on 17 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 11 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2008: Michael Phelps won his eighth gold medal of the Summer Olympics in Beijing, setting the record for the most gold medals won by an athlete at a single games. Michael Phelps
  • 1959: American musician Miles Davis released Kind of Blue, which became one of the best-selling and most critically acclaimed jazz recordings of all time. Miles Davis
  • 1950: Korean War: Forty-two American prisoners of war were massacred by the Korean People's Army on a hill above Waegwan, South Korea. Korean War
  • 1945: Animal Farm, George Orwell's satirical allegory of Soviet totalitarianism, was first published. Animal Farm
  • 1943: World War II: The Royal Air Force began a strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany's V-weapon programme by attacking the Peenemünde Army Research Center. World War II
  • 1943: Winston Churchill and Franklin D. Roosevelt met in a secret conference in Quebec. Winston Churchill
  • 1915: A category 4 hurricane made landfall in Galveston, Texas, leaving at least 275 people dead. 1915 Galveston hurricane
  • 1914: World War I: Ignoring orders to retreat, Hermann von François led a successful counterattack defending East Prussia at the Battle of Stallupönen and scored the first German victory in the Eastern Front. World War I
  • 1876: The premiere of Götterdämmerung by Richard Wagner (pictured) closed the first Bayreuth Festival. Götterdämmerung
  • 1676: Scanian War: Swedish forces defeated Danish troops at the Battle of Halmstad. Scanian War
  • 1668: An earthquake struck the North Anatolia region, killing over 8,000 people. 1668 North Anatolia earthquake
  • 1560: The Scottish Reformation Parliament approved a Protestant confession of faith, initiating the Scottish Reformation and disestablishing Catholicism as the national religion. Scottish Reformation Parliament

More events

  • 2019: A bomb explodes at a wedding in Kabul killing 63 people and leaving 182 injured. 17 August 2019 Kabul bombing
  • 2015: A bomb explodes near the Erawan Shrine in Bangkok, Thailand, killing at least 19 people and injuring 123 others. 2015 Bangkok bombing
  • 2008: American swimmer Michael Phelps becomes the first person to win eight gold medals at one Olympic Games. Michael Phelps
  • 2004: The National Assembly of Serbia unanimously adopts new state symbols for Serbia: Bože pravde becomes the new anthem and the coat of arms is adopted for the whole country. National Assembly (Serbia)
  • 1998: Lewinsky scandal: US President Bill Clinton admits in taped testimony that he had an "improper physical relationship" with White House intern Monica Lewinsky; later that same day he admits before the nation that he "misled people" about the relationship. Clinton–Lewinsky scandal
  • 1985: The 1985–86 Hormel strike begins in Austin, Minnesota. 1985–1986 Hormel strike
  • 1977: The Soviet icebreaker Arktika becomes the first surface ship to reach the North Pole. Arktika (1972 icebreaker)
  • 1970: Soviet Union Venera program: Venera 7 launched. It will become the first spacecraft to successfully transmit data from the surface of another planet (Venus). Venera program
  • 1960: Aeroflot Flight 036 crashes in Soviet Ukraine, killing 34. Aeroflot Flight 036
  • 1958: Pioneer 0, America's first attempt at lunar orbit, is launched using the first Thor-Able rocket and fails. Notable as one of the first attempted launches beyond Earth orbit by any country. Pioneer 0
  • 1950: Korean War: Forty-two American prisoners of war are massacred by the Korean People's Army on a hill above Waegwan, South Korea. Korean War
  • 1949: Matsukawa derailment: Unknown saboteurs cause a passenger train to derail and overturn in Fukushima Prefecture, Japan, killing three crew members and igniting a political firestorm between the Japanese Communist Party and the government of Occupied Japan that will eventually lead to the Japanese Red Purge. Matsukawa derailment
  • 1945: Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta proclaim the independence of Indonesia, igniting the Indonesian National Revolution against the Dutch Empire. Sukarno
  • 1943: World War II: The U.S. Eighth Air Force suffers the loss of 60 bombers on the Schweinfurt–Regensburg mission. Eighth Air Force
  • 1943: World War II: First Québec Conference of Winston Churchill, Franklin D. Roosevelt, and William Lyon Mackenzie King begins. First Quebec Conference
  • 1918: Bolshevik revolutionary leader Moisei Uritsky is assassinated. Bolsheviks
  • 1915: Jewish American Leo Frank is lynched in Marietta, Georgia, USA after his death sentence is commuted by Governor John M. Slaton. Leo Frank
  • 1914: World War I: Battle of Stallupönen: The German army of General Hermann von François defeats the Russian force commanded by Paul von Rennenkampf near modern-day Nesterov, Russia. World War I
  • 1883: The first public performance of the Dominican Republic's national anthem, Himno Nacional. Dominican Republic
  • 1866: The Grand Duchy of Baden announces its withdrawal from the German Confederation and signs a treaty of peace and alliance with Prussia. Grand Duchy of Baden
  • 1862: American Indian Wars: The Dakota War of 1862 begins in Minnesota as Dakota warriors attack white settlements along the Minnesota River. American Indian Wars
  • 1836: British parliament accepts registration of births, marriages and deaths. Parliament of the United Kingdom
  • 1808: The Finnish War: The Battle of Alavus is fought. Finnish War
  • 1784: Classical composer Luigi Boccherini receives a pay rise of 12,000 reals from his employer, the Infante Luis, Count of Chinchón. Luigi Boccherini
  • 1723: Ioan Giurgiu Patachi becomes Bishop of Făgăraș and is festively installed in his position at the St. Nicolas Cathedral in Făgăraș, after being formally confirmed earlier by Pope Clement XI. Ioan Giurgiu Patachi
  • 1648: The Parliamentarians defeat a English Royalist and Scottish army in the battle of Preston. Roundhead
  • 1585: Eighty Years' War: Siege of Antwerp: Antwerp is captured by Spanish forces under Alexander Farnese, Duke of Parma, who orders Protestants to leave the city and as a result over half of the 100,000 inhabitants flee to the northern provinces. Eighty Years' War
  • 1560: The Catholic Church is overthrown and Protestantism is established as the national religion in Scotland. Catholic Church
  • 1488: Konrad Bitz, the Bishop of Turku, marks the date of his preface to Missale Aboense, the oldest known book of Finland. List of bishops of Turku
  • 1386: Karl Topia, the ruler of Princedom of Albania, forges an alliance with the Republic of Venice, committing to participate in all wars of the Republic and receiving coastal protection against the Ottomans in return. Karl Thopia
