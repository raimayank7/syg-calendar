March 5: what happened on this day

What happened on 5 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 8 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 1981: The ZX81, a pioneering British home computer, was launched by Sinclair Research, and went on to sell more than 1.5 million units around the world. ZX81
  • 1966: BOAC Flight 911 disintegrated and crashed near Mount Fuji shortly after departure from Tokyo International Airport, killing all 113 passengers and 11 crew members on board. BOAC Flight 911
  • 1963: American country-music performers Patsy Cline, Cowboy Copas, and Hawkshaw Hawkins were killed when their PA-24 crashed shortly after takeoff in Camden, Tennessee. Country music
  • 1960: Cuban photographer Alberto Korda took his iconic photograph Guerrillero Heroico of Marxist revolutionary Che Guevara. Alberto Korda
  • 1943: World War II: The Gloster Meteor, the Allies' only operational jet aircraft, made its maiden flight. World War II
  • 1936: The prototype (pictured) of the Supermarine Spitfire flew for the first time. Supermarine Spitfire prototype K5054
  • 1825: Roberto Cofresí, one of the last Caribbean pirates, was apprehended after his flagship sloop Anne was captured by authorities. Roberto Cofresí
  • 1824: The First Anglo-Burmese War began. First Anglo-Burmese War
  • 1811: Peninsular War: At the Battle of Barrosa, Anglo-Iberian forces trying to lift the Siege of Cádiz defeated a French attack but could not break the siege itself. Peninsular War
  • 1770: American Revolution: British soldiers fired into a crowd in Boston, killing five people. American Revolution
  • 1279: The Livonian branch of the Teutonic Order suffered a great loss when 71 knights died in the Battle of Aizkraukle. Livonian Order
  • 363: Roman–Persian Wars: Roman emperor Julian and his army set out from Antioch to attack the Sasanian Empire. Roman–Persian wars

More events

  • 2023: The 2023 Estonian parliamentary election is held, with two centre-right liberal parties gaining an absolute majority for the first time. 2023 Estonian parliamentary election
  • 2023: A group of four prisoners escape from the Nouakchott Civil Prison, before being caught the next day. 2023 Nouakchott prison break
  • 2021: Twenty people are killed and 30 injured in a suicide car bombing in Mogadishu, Somalia. March 2021 Mogadishu bombing
  • 2012: Tropical Storm Irina kills over 75 as it passes through Madagascar. Tropical Storm Irina
  • 2011: An Antonov An-148 crashes in Russia's Alexeyevsky District, Belgorod Oblast during a test flight, killing all seven aboard. Antonov An-148
  • 2002: An earthquake in Mindanao, Philippines, kills 15 people and injures more than 100. 2002 Mindanao earthquake
  • 2001: In Mina, Saudi Arabia, 35 pilgrims are killed in a stampede on the Jamaraat Bridge during the Hajj. Mina, Saudi Arabia
  • 1991: Aeropostal Alas de Venezuela Flight 109 crashes in Venezuela, killing 45. Aeropostal Alas de Venezuela Flight 109
  • 1981: The ZX81, a pioneering British home computer, is launched by Sinclair Research and would go on to sell over 11⁄2 million units around the world. ZX81
  • 1978: The Landsat 3 is launched from Vandenberg Air Force Base in California. Landsat 3
  • 1973: An Iberia McDonnell Douglas DC-9 collides in mid-air with a Spantax Convair 990 Coronado over Nantes, France, killing all 68 people aboard the DC-9, including music manager Michael Jeffery. Iberia (airline)
  • 1970: The Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons goes into effect after ratification by 43 nations. Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons
  • 1967: Lake Central Airlines Flight 527 crashes near Marseilles, Ohio, killing 38. Lake Central Airlines Flight 527
  • 1965: March Intifada: A Leftist uprising erupts in Bahrain against the British colonial presence. March Intifada
  • 1963: Aeroflot Flight 191 crashes while landing at Aşgabat International Airport, killing 12. Aeroflot Flight 191
  • 1957: Sutton Wick air crash: A Blackburn Beverley of 53 Squadron, Royal Air Forces, crashes into the village of Sutton Wick, Berkshire (now Oxfordshire), killing most of the crew and passengers and two local residents. Sutton Wick air crash
  • 1953: Joseph Stalin, the longest serving leader of the Soviet Union, dies at his Volynskoe dacha in Moscow after suffering a cerebral hemorrhage four days earlier. Joseph Stalin
  • 1944: World War II: The Red Army begins the Uman–Botoșani offensive in the western Ukrainian SSR. Red Army
  • 1942: World War II: Japanese forces capture Batavia, capital of Dutch East Indies, which is left undefended after the withdrawal of the KNIL garrison and Australian Blackforce battalion to Buitenzorg and Bandung. World War II
  • 1939: Spanish Civil War: The National Defence Council seizes control of the republican government in a coup d'etat, with the intention of negotiating an end to the war. Spanish Civil War
  • 1931: The British Raj: Gandhi–Irwin Pact is signed. British Raj
  • 1912: Italo-Turkish War: Italian forces are the first to use airships for military purposes, employing them for reconnaissance behind Turkish lines. Italo-Turkish War
  • 1872: George Westinghouse patents the air brake. George Westinghouse
  • 1860: Parma, Tuscany, Modena and Romagna vote in referendums to join the Kingdom of Sardinia. Parma
  • 1836: Samuel Colt establishes his first factory to produce the recently patented production-model revolver, the .34-caliber "Paterson". Samuel Colt
  • 1824: First Anglo-Burmese War: The British officially declare war on Burma. First Anglo-Burmese War
  • 1811: Peninsular War: A French force under the command of Marshal Victor is routed while trying to prevent an Anglo-Spanish-Portuguese army from lifting the Siege of Cádiz in the Battle of Barrosa. Peninsular War
  • 1766: Antonio de Ulloa, the first Spanish governor of Louisiana, arrives in New Orleans. Antonio de Ulloa
  • 1496: King Henry VII of England issues letters patent to John Cabot and his sons, authorising them to explore unknown lands. Henry VII of England
  • 1046: Nasir Khusraw begins the seven-year Middle Eastern journey which he will later describe in his book Safarnama. Nasir Khusraw

About the date itself

March 5 is the 64th day of an ordinary year, with 301 days left after it (302 in a leap year, where it is the 65th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Friday, 2028: Sunday, 2029: Monday, 2030: Tuesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 5 March 2027 in 2027 is 26 Ramadan 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 9.
