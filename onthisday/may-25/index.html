May 25: what happened on this day

What happened on 25 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2020: George Floyd, a black American man, was murdered (memorial pictured) during an arrest by a white police officer in Minneapolis, sparking protests in the U.S. and other countries. George Floyd
  • 2013: Naxalite insurgents of the Communist Party of India (Maoist) attacked a convoy of Indian National Congress leaders in the state of Chhattisgarh, causing at least 27 deaths. Naxalite–Maoist insurgency
  • 2011: The final episode of The Oprah Winfrey Show, the highest-rated daytime talk show in U.S. television history, hosted by Oprah Winfrey (pictured), was broadcast. The Oprah Winfrey Show
  • 1979: Six-year-old Etan Patz disappeared on his way to school in New York City, and later became one of the first missing children to have his picture featured on milk cartons. Disappearance of Etan Patz
  • 1971: Joetha Collier, a recent high school graduate, was killed in Drew, Mississippi, in a case that attracted widespread attention from civil rights activists. Killing of Joetha Collier
  • 1962: The Baltimore Steam Packet Company, the last overnight steamboat service in the United States, went out of business. Baltimore Steam Packet Company
  • 1961: A fire broke out at a squatter settlement in Bukit Ho Swee, Singapore, rendering approximately 16,000 people homeless. Bukit Ho Swee fire
  • 1944: The Wehrmacht and their collaborationist allies launched Operation Rösselsprung, a failed attempt to assassinate the Yugoslav Partisan leader Josip Broz Tito. Wehrmacht
  • 1936: Employees of the Remington Rand company began an 11-month strike action, during which time the company executives developed the notorious "Mohawk Valley formula" to intimidate the strikers. Remington Rand
  • 1878: Gilbert and Sullivan's comic opera H.M.S. Pinafore (poster featured) premiered at the Opera Comique in London. Gilbert and Sullivan
  • 1810: The Primera Junta, the first independent government in Argentina, was established in an open cabildo in Buenos Aires, marking the end of the May Revolution. Primera Junta
  • 1782: American Revolutionary War: US Colonel William Crawford began a failed expedition to destroy British-allied American Indian towns along the Sandusky River in the Ohio Country. American Revolutionary War

More events

  • 2018: The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) becomes enforceable in the European Union. General Data Protection Regulation
  • 2013: Suspected Maoist rebels kill at least 28 people and injure 32 others in an attack on a convoy of Indian National Congress politicians in Chhattisgarh, India. Naxalism
  • 2011: Oprah Winfrey airs her last show, ending her 25-year run of The Oprah Winfrey Show. Oprah Winfrey
  • 2008: NASA's Phoenix lander touches down in the Green Valley region of Mars to search for environments suitable for water and microbial life. NASA
  • 2001: Erik Weihenmayer becomes the first blind person to reach the summit of Mount Everest, in the Himalayas, with Dr. Sherman Bull. Erik Weihenmayer
  • 1999: The United States House of Representatives releases the Cox Report which details China's nuclear espionage against the U.S. over the prior two decades. United States House of Representatives
  • 1986: The Hands Across America event takes place. Hands Across America
  • 1982: Falklands War: HMS Coventry is sunk by Argentine Air Force A-4 Skyhawks. Falklands War
  • 1979: John Spenkelink, a convicted murderer, is executed in Florida; he is the first person to be executed in the state after the reintroduction of capital punishment in 1976. John Spenkelink
  • 1978: The first of a series of bombings orchestrated by the Unabomber detonates at Northwestern University resulting in minor injuries. Ted Kaczynski
  • 1977: The Chinese government removes a decade-old ban on William Shakespeare's work, effectively ending the Cultural Revolution started in 1966. William Shakespeare
  • 1971: Joetha Collier, a recent high school graduate, is killed in a shooting in Drew, Mississippi, attracting extensive attention from the media and civil rights activists. Killing of Joetha Collier
  • 1966: Explorer program: Explorer 32 launches. Explorers Program
  • 1961: Apollo program: U.S. President John F. Kennedy announces, before a special joint session of the U.S. Congress, that the United States "should commit itself to achieving the goal, before this decade is out, of landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely to the Earth." Apollo program
  • 1955: First ascent of Mount Kangchenjunga: On the British Kangchenjunga expedition led by Charles Evans, Joe Brown and George Band reach the summit of the third-highest mountain in the world (8,586 meters); Norman Hardie and Tony Streather join them the following day. Kangchenjunga
  • 1953: The first public television station in the United States officially begins broadcasting as KUHT from the campus of the University of Houston. Public broadcasting
  • 1940: World War II: The German 2nd Panzer Division captures the port of Boulogne-sur-Mer; the surrender of the last French and British troops marks the end of the Battle of Boulogne. World War II
  • 1935: Jesse Owens of Ohio State University breaks three world records and ties a fourth at the Big Ten Conference Track and Field Championships in Ann Arbor, Michigan. Jesse Owens
  • 1926: Sholom Schwartzbard assassinates Symon Petliura, the head of the government of the Ukrainian People's Republic, which is in government-in-exile in Paris. Sholem Schwarzbard
  • 1914: The House of Commons of the United Kingdom passes the Home Rule Bill for devolution in Ireland. House of Commons of the United Kingdom
  • 1895: The Republic of Formosa is formed, with Tang Jingsong as its president. Republic of Formosa
  • 1865: In Mobile, Alabama, around 300 people are killed when an ordnance depot explodes. Mobile, Alabama
  • 1819: The Argentine Constitution of 1819 is promulgated. Argentine Constitution of 1819
  • 1809: Chuquisaca Revolution: Patriot revolt in Chuquisaca (modern-day Sucre) against the Spanish Empire, sparking the Latin American wars of independence. Chuquisaca Revolution
  • 1798: United Irishmen Rebellion: Battle of Carlow begins; executions of suspected rebels at Carnew and at Dunlavin Green take place. Irish Rebellion of 1798
  • 1763: First issue of Norske Intelligenz-Seddeler, the first regular Norwegian newspaper (1763–1920). Norske Intelligenz-Seddeler
  • 1660: Charles II lands at Dover at the invitation of the Convention Parliament, which marks the end of the Cromwell-proclaimed Commonwealth of England, Scotland and Ireland and begins the Restoration of the British monarchy. Charles II of England
  • 1644: Ming general Wu Sangui forms an alliance with the invading Manchus and opens the gates of the Great Wall of China at Shanhaiguan pass, letting the Manchus through towards the capital Beijing. Ming dynasty
  • 1420: Henry the Navigator is appointed governor of the Order of Christ. Prince Henry the Navigator
  • -240: First recorded perihelion passage of Halley's Comet. Apsis

About the date itself

May 25 is the 145th day of an ordinary year, with 220 days left after it (221 in a leap year, where it is the 146th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 25 May 2027 in 2027 is 19 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 21.
