July 29: what happened on this day

What happened on 29 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 14 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 1981: An estimated worldwide television audience of 750 million watched the wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer (combined coat of arms pictured) at St Paul's Cathedral in London. Wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer
  • 1954: The Fellowship of the Ring, the first part of J. R. R. Tolkien's novel The Lord of the Rings, was published by Allen & Unwin. The Fellowship of the Ring
  • 1950: Korean War: Over fears that North Korean soldiers were infiltrating refugee columns, U.S. forces concluded a four-day massacre of hundreds of civilians through shootings and air attacks near the village of Nogeun-ri. Korean War
  • 1938: The Essential Commodities Reserves Act, which allowed the British government to build up reserves of essential commodities in anticipation of war, received royal assent. Essential Commodities Reserves Act 1938
  • 1921: Adolf Hitler became leader of the National Socialist German Workers' Party. Adolf Hitler
  • 1914: The Cape Cod Canal (pictured), connecting Cape Cod Bay and Buzzards Bay in the U.S. state of Massachusetts, opened on a limited basis. Cape Cod Canal
  • 1911: In a case later termed a "legal lynching", Will P. Brady obtained a guilty verdict and death sentence against 15-year-old León Martínez Jr., five days after his arrest for killing a white woman. Will P. Brady
  • 1900: Italian anarchist Gaetano Bresci (pictured) assassinated King Umberto I of Italy in Monza. Gaetano Bresci
  • 1818: French physicist Augustin-Jean Fresnel submitted a memoir on the diffraction of light to the Royal Academy of Sciences, providing strong support for the wave theory of light. Augustin-Jean Fresnel
  • 1693: Nine Years' War: French troops defeated the forces of the Grand Alliance led by William III of England at the Battle of Landen in present-day Neerwinden, Belgium. Nine Years' War
  • 1567: The infant James VI was crowned King of Scotland at Stirling. James VI and I
  • 1014: Byzantine–Bulgarian wars: Byzantine forces defeated troops of the Bulgarian Empire at the Battle of Kleidion in the mountains of Belasica near present-day Klyuch. Byzantine–Bulgarian wars

More events

  • 2019: The 2019 Altamira prison riot between rival Brazilian drug gangs leaves 62 dead. 2019 Altamira prison riot
  • 2013: Two passenger trains collide in the Swiss municipality of Granges-près-Marnand near Lausanne injuring 25 people. Granges-près-Marnand train crash
  • 2005: Astronomers announce their discovery of the dwarf planet Eris. Eris (dwarf planet)
  • 1993: The Supreme Court of Israel acquits alleged Nazi death camp guard John Demjanjuk of all charges and he is set free. Supreme Court of Israel
  • 1987: Prime Minister of India Rajiv Gandhi and President of Sri Lanka J. R. Jayewardene sign the Indo-Sri Lanka Accord on ethnic issues. Rajiv Gandhi
  • 1981: A worldwide television audience of around 750 million people watch the wedding of Charles, Prince of Wales, and Lady Diana Spencer at St Paul's Cathedral in London. Wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer
  • 1980: Iran adopts a new "holy" flag after the Islamic Revolution. Flag of Iran
  • 1973: Greeks vote to abolish the monarchy, beginning the first period of the Metapolitefsi. 1973 Greek republic referendum
  • 1972: Two Avianca Douglas DC-3 airliners collide over Colombia, killing 38. Avianca
  • 1967: During the fourth day of celebrating its 400th anniversary, the city of Caracas, Venezuela is shaken by an earthquake, leaving approximately 500 dead. Caracas
  • 1958: U.S. President Dwight D. Eisenhower signs into law the National Aeronautics and Space Act, which creates the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA). Dwight D. Eisenhower
  • 1957: Tonight Starring Jack Paar premieres on NBC with Jack Paar beginning the modern day talk show. Tonight Starring Jack Paar
  • 1948: Olympic Games: The Games of the XIV Olympiad: After a hiatus of 12 years caused by World War II, the first Summer Olympics to be held since the 1936 Summer Olympics in Berlin, open in London. Olympic Games
  • 1945: The BBC Light Programme radio station is launched for mainstream light entertainment and music. BBC Light Programme
  • 1921: Adolf Hitler becomes leader of the National Socialist German Workers' Party. Adolf Hitler
  • 1914: The Cape Cod Canal opened. Cape Cod Canal
  • 1907: Sir Robert Baden-Powell sets up the Brownsea Island Scout camp in Poole Harbour on the south coast of England. The camp runs from August 1 to August 9 and is regarded as the foundation of the Scouting movement. Robert Baden-Powell, 1st Baron Baden-Powell
  • 1900: In Italy, King Umberto I of Italy is assassinated by the anarchist Gaetano Bresci. His son, Victor Emmanuel III, 31 years old, succeeds to the throne. Umberto I of Italy
  • 1871: The Connecticut Valley Railroad opens between Old Saybrook, Connecticut and Hartford, Connecticut in the United States. Valley Railroad (Connecticut)
  • 1858: United States and Japan sign the Harris Treaty. Treaty of Amity and Commerce (United States–Japan)
  • 1848: Great Famine of Ireland: Tipperary Revolt: In County Tipperary, Ireland, then in the United Kingdom, an unsuccessful nationalist revolt against British rule is put down by police. Great Famine (Ireland)
  • 1832: An Egyptian army under Ibrahim Pascha defeats a larger Ottoman army in the battle of Belen Pass. Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt
  • 1775: Founding of the U.S. Army Judge Advocate General's Corps: General George Washington appoints William Tudor as Judge Advocate of the Continental Army. United States Army Judge Advocate General's Corps
  • 1602: Elizabeth I visits Harefield Place at Hillingdon and the Harefield Entertainment is performed. Elizabeth I
  • 1567: The infant James VI is crowned King of Scotland at Stirling. James VI and I
  • 1148: The Siege of Damascus ends in a decisive crusader defeat and leads to the disintegration of the Second Crusade. Siege of Damascus (1148)
  • 1018: Count Dirk III defeats an army sent by Emperor Henry II in the Battle of Vlaardingen. Dirk III of Holland
  • 923: Battle of Firenzuola: Lombard forces under King Rudolph II and Adalbert I, margrave of Ivrea, defeat the dethroned Emperor Berengar I of Italy at Firenzuola (Tuscany). Battle of Fiorenzuola
  • 615: Pakal ascends the throne of Palenque at the age of 12. Kʼinich Janaabʼ Pakal

About the date itself

It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 29 July 2027 in 2027 is 25 Safar 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
