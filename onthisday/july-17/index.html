July 17: what happened on this day

What happened on 17 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2014: Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 was shot down over eastern Ukraine, killing all 298 people on board. Malaysia Airlines Flight 17
  • 2007: TAM Airlines Flight 3054 overran the runway at Congonhas Airport in São Paulo, Brazil, killing 199 people. TAM Airlines Flight 3054
  • 1996: TWA Flight 800 exploded in mid-air and crashed into the Atlantic Ocean near East Moriches, New York. TWA Flight 800
  • 1992: The Manchester Metrolink, the first modern street-running light-rail system in the United Kingdom, was officially opened. Manchester Metrolink
  • 1968: Led by Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr (pictured), the Arab Socialist Ba'ath Party overthrew Iraqi president Abdul Rahman Arif in a bloodless coup. Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr
  • 1948: In Olympia, Greece, the Summer Olympics torch relay, nicknamed the "relay of peace", began. Olympia, Greece
  • 1936: Nationalist military officers launch a coup d'état against the Spanish Republic, beginning the Spanish Civil War Nationalist faction (Spanish Civil War)
  • 1918: RMS Carpathia, which had rescued survivors of the 1912 Titanic sinking, was sunk by a German U-boat with the loss of five crew. RMS Carpathia
  • 1918: Russian Revolution: Tsar Nicholas II and his family (pictured) were murdered by Bolsheviks at Yekaterinburg. Russian Revolution
  • 1862: The garrotting and robbery of James Pilkington, a British member of Parliament, led to a moral panic in London. Garrote
  • 1850: William Cranch Bond and John Adams Whipple took a daguerreotype of Vega, the first astrophotograph of a star other than the Sun. William Cranch Bond
  • 1771: Dene men who were acting as guides to Samuel Hearne on his exploration of the Coppermine River in present-day Nunavut, Canada, massacred a group of about twenty Copper Inuit. Dene

More events

  • 2015: At least 120 people are killed and 130 injured by a suicide bombing in Diyala Governorate, Iraq. 2015 Khan Bani Saad bombing
  • 2014: Malaysia Airlines Flight 17, a Boeing 777, crashes near the border of Ukraine and Russia after being shot down. All 298 people on board are killed. Malaysia Airlines Flight 17
  • 2014: Eric Garner is killed by police officer Daniel Pantaleo in New York City, after the latter put him in a prohibited chokehold while arresting him. Killing of Eric Garner
  • 2007: TAM Airlines Flight 3054, an Airbus A320, crashes into a warehouse after landing too fast and missing the end of the São Paulo–Congonhas Airport runway, killing 199 people. TAM Airlines Flight 3054
  • 2000: During approach to Lok Nayak Jayaprakash Airport, Alliance Air Flight 7412 suddenly crashes into a residential neighborhood in Patna, India, killing 60 people. Jay Prakash Narayan Airport
  • 1998: The 7.0 Mw Papua New Guinea earthquake triggers a tsunami that destroys ten villages in Papua New Guinea, killing up to 2,700 people, and leaving several thousand injured. 1998 Papua New Guinea earthquake
  • 1997: After takeoff from Husein Sastranegara International Airport, Sempati Air Flight 304 crashes into a residential neighborhood in Bandung, killing 28 people. Husein Sastranegara Airport
  • 1996: TWA Flight 800: Off the coast of Long Island, New York, a Paris-bound TWA Boeing 747 explodes, killing all 230 on board. TWA Flight 800
  • 1981: A structural failure leads to the collapse of a walkway at the Hyatt Regency in Kansas City, Missouri, killing 114 people and injuring more than 200. Hyatt Regency walkway collapse
  • 1976: The opening of the Summer Olympics in Montreal is marred by 25 African teams boycotting the games because of New Zealand's participation. Contrary to rulings by other international sports organizations, the IOC had declined to exclude New Zealand because of their participation in South African sporting events during apartheid. 1976 Summer Olympics
  • 1975: Apollo–Soyuz Test Project: An American Apollo and a Soviet Soyuz spacecraft dock with each other in orbit marking the first such link-up between spacecraft from the two nations. Apollo–Soyuz
  • 1968: Abdul Rahman Arif is overthrown and the Ba'ath Party is installed as the governing power in Iraq with Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr as the new Iraqi President. Abdul Rahman Arif
  • 1962: Nuclear weapons testing: The "Small Boy" test shot Little Feller I becomes the last atmospheric test detonation at the Nevada National Security Site. Nuclear weapons testing
  • 1953: The largest number of United States midshipman casualties in a single event results from an aircraft crash in Florida, killing 44. Midshipman
  • 1945: World War II: The main three leaders of the Allied nations, Winston Churchill, Harry S. Truman and Joseph Stalin, hold the Potsdam Conference in the German city of Potsdam to decide the future of a defeated Germany. World War II
  • 1938: Douglas Corrigan takes off from Brooklyn to fly the "wrong way" to Ireland and becomes known as "Wrong Way" Corrigan. Douglas Corrigan
  • 1932: Altona Bloody Sunday: A riot between the Nazi Party paramilitary forces, the SS and SA, and the German Communist Party ensues. Altona Bloody Sunday
  • 1919: The form of government in the Republic of Finland is officially confirmed with the enactment of the Constitution of Finland. For this reason, July 17 is known as the Day of Democracy (Kansanvallan päivä) in Finland. Finland
  • 1918: The RMS Carpathia is sunk off Ireland by the German SM U-55; five lives are lost. RMS Carpathia
  • 1899: NEC Corporation is organized as the first Japanese joint venture with foreign capital. NEC
  • 1850: Vega becomes the first star (other than the Sun) to be photographed. Vega
  • 1821: The Kingdom of Spain cedes the territory of Florida to the United States. Spain
  • 1791: Members of the French National Guard under the command of General Lafayette open fire on a crowd of radical Jacobins at the Champ de Mars, Paris, during the French Revolution, killing scores of people. National Guard (France)
  • 1762: Former emperor Peter III of Russia is murdered. Peter III of Russia
  • 1717: King George I of Great Britain sails down the River Thames with a barge of 50 musicians, where George Frideric Handel's Water Music is premiered. George I of Great Britain
  • 1453: Battle of Castillon: The last battle of the Hundred Years' War, the French under Jean Bureau defeat the English under the Earl of Shrewsbury, who is killed in the battle in Gascony. Battle of Castillon
  • 1429: Hundred Years' War: Charles VII of France is crowned the King of France in the Reims Cathedral after a successful campaign by Joan of Arc. Hundred Years' War
  • 1203: The Fourth Crusade assaults Constantinople. The Byzantine emperor Alexios III Angelos flees from his capital into exile. Fourth Crusade
  • 1048: Damasus II is elected pope, and dies 23 days later. Pope Damasus II
  • 561: Consecration of Pope John III following the death of Pope Pelagius I earlier that year. Pope John III

About the date itself

July 17 is the 198th day of an ordinary year, with 167 days left after it (168 in a leap year, where it is the 199th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Saturday, 2028: Monday, 2029: Tuesday, 2030: Wednesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 17 July 2027 in 2027 is 13 Safar 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 28.
