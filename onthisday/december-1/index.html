December 1: what happened on this day

What happened on 1 December: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2019: Vivianne Miedema scored six goals and had four assists for Arsenal W.F.C. in their 11–1 victory over Bristol City W.F.C., which broke the record for the most goals scored in a FA Women's Super League match. Vivianne Miedema
  • 1991: A referendum held to ratify the Act of Declaration of Independence of Ukraine passed with more than 92 percent of the vote. 1991 Ukrainian independence referendum
  • 1974: Two Boeing 727s, TWA Flight 514 and Northwest Orient Airlines Flight 6231, crashed in the eastern United States in unrelated circumstances, killing 95 people on board both aircraft. Boeing 727
  • 1971: A period of political and economic reforms in the Socialist Republic of Croatia came to an end as the League of Communists of Yugoslavia decided to purge the state's reformist leadership. Croatian Spring
  • 1966: The first Gävle goat, a Swedish Yule goat tradition, was constructed in Gävle and then burned to the ground on New Year's Eve. Gävle goat
  • 1959: Twelve countries signed the Antarctic Treaty, the first arms control agreement established during the Cold War, banning military activity in the Antarctic and setting the continent aside as a scientific preserve. Antarctic Treaty System
  • 1955: Rosa Parks was arrested for refusing to give up her seat on a public bus to a white man in Montgomery, Alabama, United States, sparking the Montgomery bus boycott. Rosa Parks
  • 1948: In "one of Australia's most profound mysteries", the body of an unidentified man was found on Somerton beach in Adelaide, a case which remains unsolved. Somerton Man
  • 1941: The Civil Air Patrol, the civilian auxiliary of the United States Air Force, was founded. Civil Air Patrol
  • 1923: The Gleno Dam in the Italian province of Bergamo failed due to poor workmanship, flooding the downstream valley and killing at least 356 people. Gleno Dam
  • 1918: With the signing of the Act of Union, Denmark recognized the Kingdom of Iceland as a fully sovereign state in personal union through a common monarch. Danish–Icelandic Act of Union
  • 1822: Pedro I was crowned the first emperor of Brazil, seven weeks after his reign began on his 24th birthday. Pedro I of Brazil

More events

  • 2020: The Arecibo Telescope collapses. Arecibo Telescope
  • 2019: The outbreak of coronavirus infection begins in Wuhan. COVID-19 pandemic
  • 2011: The Alma-Ata Metro was opened. Almaty Metro
  • 2006: The law on same-sex marriage comes into force in South Africa, legalizing same-sex marriage for the first time on the African continent. Same-sex marriage in South Africa
  • 2001: The United Russia political party was founded. United Russia
  • 1997: In the Indian state of Bihar, Ranvir Sena attacks the CPI (ML) Party Unity stronghold Lakshmanpur-Bathe, killing 63 lower-caste people. Bihar
  • 1991: Cold War: Ukrainian voters overwhelmingly approve a referendum for independence from the Soviet Union. Ukraine
  • 1989: Philippine coup attempt: The right-wing military rebel Reform the Armed Forces Movement attempts to oust Philippine President Corazon Aquino in a failed bloody coup d'état. 1989 Philippine coup attempt
  • 1988: World AIDS Day is proclaimed worldwide by the UN member states. World AIDS Day
  • 1984: NASA conducts the Controlled Impact Demonstration, wherein an airliner is deliberately crashed in order to test technologies and gather data to help improve survivability of crashes. NASA
  • 1974: Northwest Orient Airlines Flight 6231, another Boeing 727, crashes northwest of John F. Kennedy International Airport. Northwest Orient Airlines Flight 6231
  • 1971: Cambodian Civil War: Khmer Rouge rebels intensify assaults on Cambodian government positions, forcing their retreat from Kompong Thmar and nearby Ba Ray. Cambodian Civil War
  • 1969: Vietnam War: The first draft lottery in the United States is held since World War II. Vietnam War draft
  • 1963: Nagaland, became the 16th state of India. Nagaland
  • 1959: Cold War: Opening date for signature of the Antarctic Treaty, which sets aside Antarctica as a scientific preserve and bans military activity on the continent. Cold War
  • 1958: The Our Lady of the Angels School fire in Chicago kills 92 children and three nuns. Our Lady of the Angels School fire
  • 1952: The New York Daily News reports the news of Christine Jorgensen, the first notable case of sex reassignment surgery. New York Daily News
  • 1941: World War II: Fiorello La Guardia, Mayor of New York City and Director of the Office of Civilian Defense, signs Administrative Order 9, creating the Civil Air Patrol. Fiorello La Guardia
  • 1939: The Soviet Union establishes the Finnish Democratic Republic puppet state in Terijoki. Finnish Democratic Republic
  • 1924: The National Hockey League's first United States–based franchise, the Boston Bruins, plays their first game in league play at home, at the still-extant Boston Arena indoor hockey facility. National Hockey League
  • 1918: Transylvania unites with Romania, following the incorporation of Bessarabia (March 27) and Bukovina (November 28) and thus concluding the Great Union. Transylvania
  • 1918: The Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later known as the Kingdom of Yugoslavia) is proclaimed. Kingdom of Yugoslavia
  • 1913: The Buenos Aires Metro, the first underground railway system in the Southern Hemisphere and in Latin America, begins operation. Buenos Aires Underground
  • 1900: Nicaragua sells canal rights to U.S. for $5 million. The canal agreement fails in March 1901. Great Britain rejects the amended treaty.
  • 1865: Shaw University, the first historically black university in the southern United States, is founded in Raleigh, North Carolina. Shaw University
  • 1834: Slavery is abolished in the Cape Colony in accordance with the Slavery Abolition Act 1833. Cape Colony
  • 1824: United States presidential election: Since no candidate received a majority of the total electoral college votes in the election, the United States House of Representatives is given the task of deciding the winner in accordance with the Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution. 1824 United States presidential election
  • 1821: José Núñez de Cáceres wins the independence of the Dominican Republic from Spain and names the new territory the Republic of Spanish Haiti. José Núñez de Cáceres
  • 1662: Diarist John Evelyn records skating on the frozen lake in St James's Park, London, watched by Charles II and Queen Catherine. John Evelyn
  • 1577: English Courtiers Christopher Hatton and Thomas Heneage are knighted by Queen Elizabeth I. Christopher Hatton

About the date itself

December 1 is the 335th day of an ordinary year, with 30 days left after it (31 in a leap year, where it is the 336th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 1 December 2027 in 2027 is 3 Rajab 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 48.
