May 24: what happened on this day

What happened on 24 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2026: A suicide bombing targeting a shuttle train in Quetta, Balochistan, Pakistan, kills at least 47 people. 2026 Quetta train bombing
  • 2014: A gunman involved in Islamic extremism opened fire at the Jewish Museum of Belgium in Brussels, killing four people. Islamic extremism
  • 1991: The Israel Defense Forces began Operation Solomon, a covert operation to bring thousands of Ethiopian Jews to Israel (evacuees pictured). Israel Defense Forces
  • 1986: A stationary front began over the central Caribbean Sea, leading to severe floods that over two weeks killed dozens of people in Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Haiti and Jamaica. Stationary front
  • 1970: On the Kola Peninsula in Russia, drilling began on the Kola Superdeep Borehole, which eventually reached a depth of 12,262 metres (40,230 ft), making it the deepest borehole ever drilled and the lowest artificial point on Earth. Kola Peninsula
  • 1962: Project Mercury: American astronaut Scott Carpenter orbited the Earth three times in the Aurora 7 space capsule. Project Mercury
  • 1948: Arab–Israeli War: After five days of fighting, Egyptian forces captured the Israeli community of Yad Mordechai after the defenders had abandoned it. 1948 Arab–Israeli War
  • 1930: English aviator Amy Johnson landed in Darwin, becoming the first woman to fly solo from England to Australia. Amy Johnson
  • 1883: New York City's Brooklyn Bridge opened as the longest suspension bridge in the world at the time. Brooklyn Bridge
  • 1798: The Irish Rebellion of 1798 began, with battles beginning in County Kildare and fighting later spreading across the country. Irish Rebellion of 1798
  • 1689: The Act of Toleration became law, granting freedom of worship to English nonconformists under certain circumstances, but deliberately excluding Catholics. Toleration Act 1688
  • 1667: Led by King Louis XIV, the French army invaded the Spanish Netherlands, beginning the War of Devolution. Louis XIV

  • 1861: American Civil War: Union troops occupy Alexandria, Virginia, with Colonel Elmer E. Ellsworth becoming the first Union officer to be killed during the war. American Civil War
  • 1844: Samuel Morse sends the message "What hath God wrought" (a biblical quotation, Numbers 23:23) from a committee room in the United States Capitol to his assistant, Alfred Vail, in Baltimore, Maryland, to inaugurate a commercial telegraph line between Baltimore and Washington D.C. Samuel Morse
  • 1822: Battle of Pichincha: Antonio José de Sucre secures the independence of the Presidency of Quito. Battle of Pichincha

  • 1689: The English Parliament passes the Act of Toleration protecting dissenting Protestants but excluding Roman Catholics. Parliament of England
  • 1667: The French Royal Army crosses the border into the Spanish Netherlands, starting the War of Devolution opposing France to the Spanish Empire and the Triple Alliance. French Royal Army
  • 1621: The Protestant Union is formally dissolved. Protestant Union
  • 1595: Nomenclator of Leiden University Library appears, the first printed catalog of an institutional library. Leiden University Library
  • 1487: The ten-year-old Lambert Simnel is crowned in Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin, Ireland, with the name of Edward VI in a bid to threaten King Henry VII's reign. Lambert Simnel
  • 1218: The Fifth Crusade leaves Acre for Egypt. Fifth Crusade

About the date itself

May 24 is the 144th day of an ordinary year, with 221 days left after it (222 in a leap year, where it is the 145th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 24 May 2027 in 2027 is 18 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 21.
