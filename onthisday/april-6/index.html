April 6: what happened on this day

What happened on 6 April: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2026: During the Artemis II mission, the Orion made its closest approach to the far side of the Moon and reached its greatest distance from Earth . Artemis II
  • 2018: A bus carrying the Humboldt Broncos collided with a semi-truck, killing 16 and injuring 13. Humboldt Broncos
  • 2011: Mexican drug war: Authorities discovered 59 bodies in clandestine mass graves linked to earlier bus hijackings in San Fernando, Tamaulipas. Mexican drug war
  • 2009: An earthquake measuring 6.3 Mw struck near L'Aquila in the central Italian region of Abruzzo, killing 308 people and leaving more than 66,000 homeless. 2009 L'Aquila earthquake
  • 2005: Kurdish leader Jalal Talabani was elected by the National Assembly as the first non-Arab president of Iraq. Kurds
  • 1994: The aircraft carrying Rwandan president Juvénal Habyarimana and Burundian president Cyprien Ntaryamira was shot down in Kigali; the event became the catalyst for the Rwandan genocide. Juvénal Habyarimana
  • 1970: Four California Highway Patrol officers were killed in a shootout following a traffic stop in Newhall, north of Los Angeles. California Highway Patrol
  • 1941: World War II: The Luftwaffe began Operation Retribution, bombing the Yugoslav capital of Belgrade in retaliation for a coup d'état that overthrew the government that had signed the Tripartite Pact. World War II
  • 1926: Varney Air Lines made its first commercial flight (flight covers pictured), later forming the basis of United Airlines. Varney Air Lines
  • 1812: Peninsular War: After a three-week siege, the Anglo-Portuguese Army, under the Earl of Wellington, captured Badajoz, Spain, and forced the surrender of the French garrison. Peninsular War
  • 1808: John Jacob Astor founded the American Fur Company, the profits from which made him the first multi-millionaire in the United States. John Jacob Astor
  • 1712: A group of 23 slaves in New York City revolted, setting fire to a building, but were soon recaptured. New York Slave Revolt of 1712

More events

  • 2026: The crew of Artemis II makes the first crewed circumlunar flight since Apollo 17 in 1972, returning humanity to the Moon's vicinity and breaking Apollo 13's record by ~4,000 miles as the furthest crewed spaceflight. Artemis II
  • 2017: U.S. military launches 59 Tomahawk cruise missiles at an air base in Syria. Russia describes the strikes as an "aggression", adding they significantly damage US-Russia ties. 2017 Shayrat missile strike
  • 2011: In San Fernando, Tamaulipas, Mexico, over 193 victims of Los Zetas were exhumed from several mass graves. San Fernando, Tamaulipas
  • 2009: A 6.3 magnitude earthquake strikes near L'Aquila, Italy, killing 307. 2009 L'Aquila earthquake
  • 2005: Kurdish leader Jalal Talabani becomes Iraqi president; Shiite Arab Ibrahim al-Jaafari is named premier the next day. Jalal Talabani
  • 1998: Nuclear weapons testing: Pakistan tests medium-range missiles capable of reaching India. Nuclear weapons testing
  • 1994: The Rwandan genocide begins when the aircraft carrying Rwandan president Juvénal Habyarimana and Burundian president Cyprien Ntaryamira is shot down. Rwandan genocide
  • 1985: Sudanese President Gaafar Nimeiry is ousted from power in a coup d'état led by Field Marshal Abdel Rahman Swar al-Dahab. Gaafar Nimeiry
  • 1974: In Brighton, United Kingdom, ABBA wins the 1974 edition of the Eurovision Song Contest with "Waterloo", the first of a joint-record seven Swedish wins. Brighton
  • 1973: Launch of Pioneer 11 spacecraft. Pioneer 11
  • 1972: Vietnam War: Easter Offensive: American forces begin sustained air strikes and naval bombardments. Vietnam War
  • 1968: In the downtown district of Richmond, Indiana, a double explosion kills 41 and injures 150. Richmond, Indiana
  • 1965: Launch of Early Bird, the first commercial communications satellite to be placed in geosynchronous orbit. Intelsat I
  • 1957: The flag carrier airline of Greece for decades, Olympic Airways, is founded by Aristotle Onassis following the acquisition of "TAE - Greek National Airlines". Greece
  • 1947: The first Tony Awards are presented for theatrical achievement. Tony Awards
  • 1941: World War II: Nazi Germany launches Operation 25 (the invasion of Kingdom of Yugoslavia) and Operation Marita (the invasion of Greece). World War II
  • 1930: At the end of the Salt March, Gandhi raises a lump of mud and salt and declares, "With this, I am shaking the foundations of the British Empire." Salt March
  • 1926: Varney Airlines makes its first commercial flight (Varney is the root company of United Airlines). Varney Air Lines
  • 1917: World War I: The United States declares war on Germany. World War I
  • 1909: Robert Peary and Matthew Henson become the first people to reach the North Pole; Peary's claim has been disputed because of failings in his navigational ability. Robert Peary
  • 1866: The Grand Army of the Republic, an American patriotic organization composed of Union veterans of the American Civil War, is founded. It lasts until 1956. Grand Army of the Republic
  • 1862: American Civil War: The Battle of Shiloh begins: In Tennessee, forces under Union General Ulysses S. Grant meet Confederate troops led by General Albert Sidney Johnston. American Civil War
  • 1841: U.S. President John Tyler is sworn in, two days after having become president upon William Henry Harrison's death. John Tyler
  • 1814: Nominal beginning of the Bourbon Restoration; anniversary date that Napoleon abdicates and is exiled to Elba. Bourbon Restoration in France
  • 1808: John Jacob Astor incorporates the American Fur Company, that would eventually make him America's first millionaire. John Jacob Astor
  • 1793: During the French Revolution, the Committee of Public Safety becomes the executive organ of the republic. French Revolution
  • 1776: American Revolutionary War: Ships of the Continental Navy fail in their attempt to capture a Royal Navy dispatch boat. American Revolutionary War
  • 1652: At the Cape of Good Hope, Dutch sailor Jan van Riebeeck establishes a resupply camp that eventually becomes Cape Town. Cape of Good Hope
  • 1453: Mehmed II begins his siege of Constantinople. The city falls on May 29 and is renamed Istanbul. Mehmed II
  • 945: Byzantine Emperor Constantine VII crowns his son Romanos II as co-emperor. List of Byzantine emperors

About the date itself

April 6 is the 96th day of an ordinary year, with 269 days left after it (270 in a leap year, where it is the 97th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 6 April 2027 in 2027 is 29 Shawwal 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 14.
