December 11: what happened on this day

What happened on 11 December: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2024: Urfan Sharif and Beinash Batool were found guilty of the murder of Sara Sharif, and were sentenced to life imprisonment. Murder of Sara Sharif
  • 2008: American stockbroker Bernie Madoff was arrested and charged with securities fraud in a $64.8 billion Ponzi scheme, the largest in history. Bernie Madoff
  • 2008: American stockbroker Bernie Madoff was arrested and charged with securities fraud in a $64.8 billion Ponzi scheme, the largest in history. Bernie Madoff
  • 2006: The first action in the Mexican drug war began as President Felipe Calderón ordered Mexican soldiers and Federal Police units into the state of Michoacán. Mexican drug war
  • 2005: Demonstrations in Cronulla, a suburb of Sydney, against recent violence towards locals turned into a series of race riots. Cronulla
  • 1972: Apollo 17 (Lunar Roving Vehicle pictured), the last Apollo mission, landed on the Moon. Apollo 17
  • 1925: Pope Pius XI promulgated the encyclical Quas primas, establishing the Feast of Christ the King. Pope Pius XI
  • 1920: Irish War of Independence: Following an Irish Republican Army ambush of an Auxiliary patrol, British forces burned and looted numerous buildings in Cork. Irish War of Independence
  • 1899: Second Boer War: In the Battle of Magersfontein, Boers defeated British forces trying to relieve the Siege of Kimberley. Second Boer War
  • 1886: The London-based football club Arsenal, then known as Dial Square, played their first match on the Isle of Dogs. Association football
  • 1789: The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (structure pictured), one of the oldest public universities in the United States and the only one to award degrees in the 18th century, was chartered. University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill
  • 1640: A crowd of 1,500 people presented the Root and Branch petition to the Long Parliament, calling for abolishing the episcopacy of the Church of England. Root and Branch petition

More events

  • 2020: The Food and Drug Administration issues an Emergency Use Authorization on the Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine, the first COVID-19 vaccine to be approved by the agency. Food and Drug Administration
  • 2017: New York City Subway bombing: A pipe bomb partially detonates in the New York City Subway, in the Times Square–42nd Street/Port Authority Bus Terminal. Four people are injured, including the perpetrator. 2017 New York City Subway bombing
  • 2009: Finnish game developer Rovio Entertainment releases the hit mobile game Angry Birds internationally on iOS. Rovio Entertainment
  • 2007: Insurgency in the Maghreb: Two car bombs explode in Algiers, Algeria, one near the Supreme Constitutional Court and the other near the offices of the United Nations. Insurgency in the Maghreb (2002–present)
  • 2006: Felipe Calderón, the president of Mexico, launches a military-led offensive to put down the drug cartel violence in the state of Michoacán. This effort is often regarded as the first event in the Mexican drug war. Felipe Calderón
  • 2005: Cronulla riots: Thousands of White Australians demonstrate against ethnic violence resulting in a riot against anyone thought to be Lebanese in Cronulla, New South Wales; these are followed up by retaliatory ethnic attacks on Cronulla. 2005 Cronulla riots
  • 1999: SATA Air Açores Flight 530M crashes into Pico da Esperança on São Jorge Island in the Azores, killing 35. SATA Air Açores Flight 530M
  • 1997: The Kyoto Protocol, an agreement committing countries to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, opens for signature. Kyoto Protocol
  • 1993: A block of the Highland Towers condominium complex collapses following a landslide caused by heavy rain and water flowing from a construction site at Ampang district in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. 48 of its residents die, including one who died in hospital after being rescued alive, leaving only two survivors. Highland Towers collapse
  • 1990: Several fatal collisions in the 1990 Interstate 75 fog disaster result in a total of 12 deaths and 42 being injured 1990 Interstate 75 fog pileup
  • 1981: El Mozote massacre: Armed forces in El Salvador kill an estimated 900 civilians in an anti-guerrilla campaign during the Salvadoran Civil War. El Mozote massacre
  • 1978: The Lufthansa heist is committed by a group led by Lucchese family associate Jimmy Burke. It was the largest cash robbery ever committed on American soil, at that time. Lufthansa heist
  • 1964: Che Guevara speaks at the United Nations General Assembly in New York City. Che Guevara
  • 1960: French forces crack down in a violent clash with protesters in French Algeria during a visit by French president Charles de Gaulle. French Algeria
  • 1948: Arab–Israeli War: The United Nations passes General Assembly Resolution 194, creating a Conciliation Commission to mediate the conflict. 1948 Arab–Israeli War
  • 1941: World War II: Poland declares war on the Empire of Japan. Polish government-in-exile
  • 1937: Second Italo-Ethiopian War: Italy leaves the League of Nations. Second Italo-Ethiopian War
  • 1934: Bill Wilson, co-founder of Alcoholics Anonymous, takes his last drink and enters treatment for the final time. Bill W.
  • 1927: Guangzhou Uprising: Communist Red Guards launch an uprising in Guangzhou, China, taking over most of the city and announcing the formation of a Guangzhou Soviet. Guangzhou Uprising
  • 1920: Irish War of Independence: In retaliation for a recent IRA ambush, British forces burn and loot numerous buildings in Cork city. Many civilians report being beaten, shot at, robbed and verbally abused by British forces. Irish War of Independence
  • 1913: More than two years after it was stolen from the Louvre, Leonardo da Vinci's painting Mona Lisa is recovered in Florence, Italy. The thief, Vincenzo Peruggia, is immediately arrested. Louvre
  • 1905: A workers' uprising occurs in Kyiv, Ukraine (then part of the Russian Empire), and establishes the Shuliavka Republic. Kyiv
  • 1899: Second Boer War: In the Battle of Magersfontein the Boers commanded by general Piet Cronjé inflict a defeat on the forces of the British Empire commanded by Lord Methuen trying to relieve the Siege of Kimberley. Second Boer War
  • 1816: Indiana becomes the 19th U.S. state. Indiana
  • 1792: French Revolution: King Louis XVI of France is put on trial for treason by the National Convention. French Revolution
  • 1675: Antonio de Vea expedition enters San Rafael Lake in western Patagonia. Antonio de Vea expedition
  • 1602: A surprise attack by forces under the command of Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy, and his brother-in-law, Philip III of Spain, is repelled by the citizens of Geneva. (Commemorated annually by the Fête de l'Escalade.) Charles Emmanuel I
  • 1282: Battle of Orewin Bridge: Llywelyn ap Gruffudd, the last native Prince of Wales, is killed at Cilmeri near Builth Wells in mid-Wales. Battle of Orewin Bridge
  • 1041: Michael V, adoptive son of Empress Zoë of Byzantium, is proclaimed emperor of the Eastern Roman Empire. Michael V Kalaphates
  • 861: Assassination of the Abbasid caliph al-Mutawakkil by the Turkish guard, who raise al-Muntasir to the throne, start of the "Anarchy at Samarra". Abbasid Caliphate

About the date itself

It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Saturday, 2028: Monday, 2029: Tuesday, 2030: Wednesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 11 December 2027 in 2027 is 13 Rajab 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
