November 10: what happened on this day

What happened on 10 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2020: The British government announced that it had removed the last land mine from the Falkland Islands, laid by Argentine forces during the 1982 Falklands War. Land mines in the Falkland Islands
  • 2009: A skirmish occurred between South Korean and North Korean naval ships off Daecheong Island in the Yellow Sea. Daecheong incident
  • 2007: At the Ibero-American Summit in Santiago, Chile, King Juan Carlos I of Spain asked Venezuelan president Hugo Chávez "Why don't you shut up?" after Chávez repeatedly interrupted a speech by Spanish prime minister José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero. Ibero-American Summit
  • 2006: Sri Lankan Tamil politician and human rights lawyer Nadarajah Raviraj was assassinated in Colombo. Sri Lankan Tamils
  • 1975: SS Edmund Fitzgerald sank in Lake Superior, claiming all 29 of her crew's lives. SS Edmund Fitzgerald
  • 1975: Angolan Civil War: The MPLA decisively defeated the FNLA at the Battle of Quifangondo, while Portugal simultaneously withdrew all its colonial and military personnel from Luanda. Angolan Civil War
  • 1969: The first episode of the children's television series Sesame Street was broadcast in the United States. Sesame Street
  • 1945: Indonesian National Revolution: Following the killing of Brigadier A. W. S. Mallaby on 30 October, British forces retaliated by attacking Surabaya (present-day logo depicted). Indonesian National Revolution
  • 1937: Brazilian president Getúlio Vargas led a coup against his own constitutional government, establishing the dictatorial Estado Novo regime. Getúlio Vargas
  • 1898: White supremacists seized power and massacred black Americans during the Wilmington massacre (newspaper headline pictured), the only instance of a municipal government being overthrown in United States history. White supremacy
  • 1865: Henry Wirz, the Confederate superintendent of Andersonville Prison, was hanged after a controversial conviction, becoming the only American Civil War officer executed for war crimes. Henry Wirz
  • 1599: At the culmination of a Swedish civil war, supporters of the deposed King Sigismund III Vasa were publicly executed in the Åbo Bloodbath. Sigismund III Vasa

More events

  • 2020: Armenia and Azerbaijan sign a ceasefire agreement, ending the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War, and prompting protests in Armenia. Armenia
  • 2019: President of Bolivia Evo Morales and several of his government resign after 19 days of civil protests and a recommendation from the military. Evo Morales
  • 2008: Over five months after landing on Mars, NASA declares the Phoenix mission concluded after communications with the lander were lost. Mars
  • 2006: The National Museum of the Marine Corps in Quantico, Virginia is opened and dedicated by U.S. President George W. Bush, who announces that Marine Corporal Jason Dunham will posthumously receive the Medal of Honor. National Museum of the Marine Corps
  • 2001: The Taliban held city of Mazar-i-Sharif falls to the Northern Alliance under generals Abdul Rashid Dostum and Atta Muhammad Nur. Taliban
  • 1997: WorldCom and MCI Communications announce a $37 billion merger (the largest merger in US history at the time). WorldCom
  • 1989: Longtime Bulgarian leader Todor Zhivkov is removed from office and replaced by Petar Mladenov. People's Republic of Bulgaria
  • 1989: Germans begin to tear down the Berlin Wall. Fall of the Berlin Wall
  • 1983: Bill Gates introduces Windows 1.0. Bill Gates
  • 1975: The 729-foot-long freighter SS Edmund Fitzgerald sinks during a storm on Lake Superior, killing all 29 crew on board. SS Edmund Fitzgerald
  • 1975: Israeli-Palestinian conflict: the United Nations General Assembly passes Resolution 3379, determining that Zionism is a form of racism. Israeli–Palestinian conflict
  • 1971: In Cambodia, Khmer Rouge forces attack the city of Phnom Penh and its airport, killing 44, wounding at least 30 and damaging nine aircraft. Cambodia
  • 1970: Vietnam War: Vietnamization: For the first time in five years, an entire week ends with no reports of American combat fatalities in Southeast Asia. Vietnam War
  • 1970: Luna 17: uncrewed space mission launched by the Soviet Union. Luna 17
  • 1967: The Nauru Independence Act 1967 passed the Parliament of Australia, giving independence to the UN Trust Territory of Nauru with effect from 31 January 1968. Nauru Independence Act 1967
  • 1954: U.S. President Dwight D. Eisenhower dedicates the USMC War Memorial (Iwo Jima memorial) in Arlington Ridge Park in Arlington County, Virginia. Dwight D. Eisenhower
  • 1946: A magnitude 6.9 earthquake in the Peruvian Andes mountains kills at least 1,400 people. 1946 Ancash earthquake
  • 1944: The ammunition ship USS Mount Hood explodes at Seeadler Harbour, Manus, Admiralty Islands, killing at least 432 and wounding 371. USS Mount Hood (AE-11)
  • 1940: The 1940 Vrancea earthquake strikes Romania killing an estimated 1,000 and injuring approximately 4,000 more. 1940 Vrancea earthquake
  • 1939: Finnish author F. E. Sillanpää is awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature. Frans Eemil Sillanpää
  • 1910: The date of Thomas A. Davis' opening of the San Diego Army and Navy Academy, although the official founding date is November 23, 1910. Thomas A. Davis
  • 1871: Henry Morton Stanley locates missing explorer and missionary, David Livingstone in Ujiji, near Lake Tanganyika, famously greeting him with the words, "Dr. Livingstone, I presume?" Henry Morton Stanley
  • 1847: The passenger ship Stephen Whitney is wrecked in thick fog off the southern coast of Ireland, killing 92 of the 110 on board. The disaster results in the construction of the Fastnet Rock lighthouse. Stephen Whitney (ship)
  • 1793: A Goddess of Reason is proclaimed by the French Convention at the suggestion of Pierre Gaspard Chaumette. Cult of Reason
  • 1766: The last colonial governor of New Jersey, William Franklin, signs the charter of Queen's College (later renamed Rutgers University). New Jersey
  • 1702: English colonists under the command of James Moore besiege Spanish St. Augustine during Queen Anne's War. James Moore Sr.
  • 1659: Chattrapati Shivaji Maharaj, Maratha King kills Afzal Khan, Adilshahi in the battle popularly known as Battle of Pratapgarh. Shivaji
  • 1444: Battle of Varna: The crusading forces of King Władysław III of Poland (aka Ulaszlo I of Hungary and Władysław III of Varna) are defeated by the Turks under Sultan Murad II and Władysław is killed. Battle of Varna
  • 1202: Fourth Crusade: Despite letters from Pope Innocent III forbidding it and threatening excommunication, Catholic crusaders begin a siege of Zara (now Zadar, Croatia). Fourth Crusade
  • 937: Ten Kingdoms: Li Bian usurps the throne and deposes Emperor Yang Pu. The Wu State is replaced by Li (now called "Xu Zhigao"), who becomes the first ruler of Southern Tang. Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period

About the date itself

November 10 is the 314th day of an ordinary year, with 51 days left after it (52 in a leap year, where it is the 315th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 10 November 2027 in 2027 is 11 Jumada al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 45.
