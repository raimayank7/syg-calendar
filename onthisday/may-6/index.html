May 6: what happened on this day

What happened on 6 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2023: King Charles III and Queen Camilla were crowned at Westminster Abbey in London. Charles III
  • 2013: Amanda Berry escaped from the Cleveland, Ohio, home of her captor, Ariel Castro, having been held there with two other women for ten years. Cleveland
  • 2004: The final episode of the television sitcom Friends was aired. The Last One (Friends)
  • 1991: Time magazine published the article "The Thriving Cult of Greed and Power" by Richard Behar criticizing the Church of Scientology, leading to years of legal conflict. Time (magazine)
  • 1976: An earthquake struck Northern Italy, killing 990 people and injuring up to 3,000. 1976 Friuli earthquake
  • 1975: About 100,000 Armenians gathered during a lull in fighting to mark the 60th anniversary of the Armenian genocide. 60th anniversary of the Armenian genocide commemorations in Beirut
  • 1942: World War II: Japanese troops overcame fierce American and Philippine resistance to win the Battle of Corregidor. World War II
  • 1937: The German airship Hindenburg caught fire and was destroyed during an attempt to dock at Lakehurst Naval Air Station in New Jersey, killing 36 people. LZ 129 Hindenburg
  • 1915: Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition: SY Aurora, anchored in McMurdo Sound, broke loose during a gale, beginning a 312-day ordeal in the Ross Sea and Southern Ocean for her 18-man crew. Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition
  • 1882: Irish civil servants Thomas Henry Burke and Lord Frederick Cavendish were stabbed to death by members of the radical Irish National Invincibles in Phoenix Park, Dublin. Thomas Henry Burke (civil servant)
  • 1801: French Revolutionary Wars: The 32-gun Spanish frigate El Gamo was captured by the outmanned and outgunned HMS Speedy. French Revolutionary Wars
  • 1757: English poet Christopher Smart (pictured) was admitted to St Luke's Hospital for Lunatics in London, beginning his six-year confinement in mental asylums. Christopher Smart

More events

  • 2023: The coronation of Charles III and Camilla as King and Queen of the United Kingdom and the other Commonwealth realms is held in Westminster Abbey, London. Coronation of Charles III and Camilla
  • 2013: Three women, kidnapped and missing for more than a decade, are found alive in Cleveland, Ohio, in the United States. Ariel Castro kidnappings
  • 2004: The final episode of the television sitcom Friends is aired. The Last One (Friends)
  • 2001: During a trip to Syria, Pope John Paul II becomes the first pope to enter a mosque. Syria
  • 1998: Kerry Wood strikes out 20 Houston Astros to tie the major league record held by Roger Clemens. He threw a one-hitter and did not walk a batter in his fifth career start. Kerry Wood
  • 1997: The Bank of England is given independence from political control, the most significant change in the bank's 300-year history. Bank of England
  • 1994: Elizabeth II of the United Kingdom and French President François Mitterrand officiate at the opening of the Channel Tunnel. Elizabeth II
  • 1984: One hundred and three Korean Martyrs are canonized by Pope John Paul II in Seoul. Korean Martyrs
  • 1976: The 6.5 Mw Friuli earthquake affects Northern Italy with a maximum Mercalli intensity of X (Extreme), leaving 900–978 dead and 1,700–2,400 injured. 1976 Friuli earthquake
  • 1972: Deniz Gezmiş, Yusuf Aslan and Hüseyin İnan are executed in Ankara after being convicted of attempting to overthrow the Constitutional order. Deniz Gezmiş
  • 1960: More than 20 million viewers watch the first televised royal wedding when Princess Margaret marries Antony Armstrong-Jones at Westminster Abbey. Wedding of Princess Margaret and Antony Armstrong-Jones
  • 1949: EDSAC, the first practical electronic digital stored-program computer, runs its first operation. EDSAC
  • 1945: World War II: The Prague Offensive, the last major battle of the Eastern Front, begins. Prague offensive
  • 1941: At California's March Field, Bob Hope performs his first USO show. California
  • 1940: John Steinbeck is awarded the Pulitzer Prize for his novel The Grapes of Wrath. John Steinbeck
  • 1935: New Deal: Under the authority of the newly-enacted Federal Emergency Relief Administration, President Franklin D. Roosevelt issues Executive Order 7034 to create the Works Progress Administration. New Deal
  • 1916: Twenty-one Lebanese nationalists are executed in Martyrs' Square, Beirut by Djemal Pasha. Martyrs' Square, Beirut
  • 1915: Babe Ruth, then a pitcher for the Boston Red Sox, hits his first major league home run. Babe Ruth
  • 1910: George V becomes King of Great Britain, Ireland, and many overseas territories, on the death of his father, Edward VII. George V
  • 1901: The first issue of Gorkhapatra, the oldest still running state-owned Nepali newspaper, is published. Gorkhapatra
  • 1882: Thomas Henry Burke and Lord Frederick Cavendish are stabbed to death by Fenian assassins in Phoenix Park, Dublin. Thomas Henry Burke (civil servant)
  • 1877: Chief Crazy Horse of the Oglala Lakota surrenders to United States troops in Nebraska. Crazy Horse
  • 1861: American Civil War: Arkansas secedes from the Union. American Civil War
  • 1840: The Penny Black postage stamp becomes valid for use in the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. Penny Black
  • 1801: Captain Thomas Cochrane in the 14-gun HMS Speedy captures the 32-gun Spanish frigate El Gamo. Thomas Cochrane, 10th Earl of Dundonald
  • 1757: Battle of Prague: A Prussian army fights an Austrian army in Prague during the Seven Years' War. Battle of Štěrboholy
  • 1757: English poet Christopher Smart is admitted into St Luke's Hospital for Lunatics in London, beginning his six-year confinement to mental asylums. Christopher Smart
  • 1659: English Restoration: A faction of the British Army removes Richard Cromwell as Lord Protector of the Commonwealth and reinstalls the Rump Parliament. Stuart Restoration
  • 1542: Francis Xavier reaches Old Goa, the capital of Portuguese India at the time. Francis Xavier
  • 1536: The Siege of Cuzco commences, in which Incan forces attempt to retake the city of Cuzco from the Spanish. Siege of Cusco

About the date itself

May 6 is the 126th day of an ordinary year, with 239 days left after it (240 in a leap year, where it is the 127th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 6 May 2027 in 2027 is 29 Dhu al-Qa'dah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 18.
