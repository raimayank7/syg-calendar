March 25: what happened on this day

What happened on 25 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2008: Invasion of Anjouan began as Comoros and African Union forces launched an assault to remove Mohamed Bacar from power in Anjouan. 2008 invasion of Anjouan
  • 2006: A gunman in Seattle, Washington, U.S., entered a rave afterparty and opened fire, killing six and wounding two, before committing suicide. Seattle
  • 1995: American computer programmer Ward Cunningham established the first wiki site, the WikiWikiWeb. Ward Cunningham
  • 1949: The Soviet Union began mass deportations of more than 90,000 "undesirable" people from the Baltic states to Siberia. Operation Priboi
  • 1934: Enrico Fermi published his discovery of neutron-induced radioactivity, for which he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics. Enrico Fermi
  • 1917: World War I: A delayed action bomb left by German troops exploded in the town hall of Bapaume, France, killing 24 soldiers and civilians. World War I
  • 1911: The Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire in New York City killed 146 sweatshop workers, many of whom could not escape because the doors to the stairwells and exits had been locked. Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire
  • 1807: The Slave Trade Act, which abolished the Atlantic slave trade in the British Empire, received royal assent. Slave Trade Act 1807
  • 1776: American Revolutionary War: American Patriot forces in Georgia conducted a raid on Tybee Island, with the primary goal of capturing runaway slaves seeking refuge with the British. American Revolutionary War
  • 1708: Jacobite risings: A French fleet anchored near Fife Ness as part of a planned French invasion of Britain. Jacobitism
  • 1458: Wars of the Roses: A formal reconciliation ceremony between the Lancastrians and Yorkists led to a brief period of peace. Wars of the Roses
  • 717: Byzantine emperor Theodosius III abdicated in favour of Leo the Isaurian after he captured Theodosius's son. Byzantine Empire

More events

  • 2018: Syrian civil war: Following the completion of the Afrin offensive, the Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) initiate an insurgency against the Turkish occupation of the Afrin District. Syrian civil war
  • 2006: Capitol Hill massacre: A gunman kills six people before taking his own life at a party in Seattle's Capitol Hill neighborhood. Capitol Hill massacre
  • 1996: The European Union's Veterinarian Committee bans the export of British beef and its by-products as a result of mad cow disease (Bovine spongiform encephalopathy). European Union
  • 1988: The Candle demonstration in Bratislava is the first mass demonstration of the 1980s against the communist regime in Czechoslovakia. Candle demonstration in Bratislava
  • 1975: Faisal of Saudi Arabia is shot and killed by his nephew. Faisal of Saudi Arabia
  • 1965: Civil rights activists led by Martin Luther King Jr. successfully complete their four-day 50-mile (80 km) march from Selma to the capitol in Montgomery, Alabama. Civil rights movement
  • 1959: Chain Island is sold by the State of California to Russell Gallaway III, a Sacramento businessman who plans to use it as a "hunting and fishing retreat", for $5,258.20 ($58,072 in 2025). Chain Island
  • 1957: The European Economic Community is established with West Germany, France, Italy, Belgium, Netherlands and Luxembourg as the first members. European Economic Community
  • 1948: The first successful tornado forecast predicts that a tornado will strike Tinker Air Force Base, Oklahoma. Tornado warning
  • 1941: The Kingdom of Yugoslavia joins the Axis powers with the signing of the Tripartite Pact. Kingdom of Yugoslavia
  • 1931: The Scottsboro Boys are arrested in Alabama and charged with rape. Scottsboro Boys
  • 1924: On the anniversary of Greek Independence, Alexandros Papanastasiou proclaims the Second Hellenic Republic. Greek War of Independence
  • 1918: The Belarusian People's Republic is established. Belarusian People's Republic
  • 1914: The Greek multi-sport club Aris Thessaloniki is founded in Thessaloniki. Aris Thessaloniki
  • 1911: Andrey Yushchinsky is murdered in Kiev, leading to the Beilis affair. Kyiv
  • 1894: Coxey's Army, the first significant American protest march, departs Massillon, Ohio for Washington, D.C. Coxey's Army
  • 1878: Last issue of the Bulletin de la Fédération jurassienne, the first or one of the first anarchist newspapers. Bulletin de la Fédération jurassienne
  • 1845: New Zealand Legislative Council pass the first Militia Act constituting the New Zealand Army. New Zealand Army
  • 1811: Percy Bysshe Shelley is expelled from the University of Oxford for publishing the pamphlet The Necessity of Atheism. Percy Bysshe Shelley
  • 1802: The Treaty of Amiens is signed as a "Definitive Treaty of Peace" between France and the United Kingdom. Treaty of Amiens
  • 1770: Daskalogiannis leads the people of Sfakia in the first Greek uprising against the Ottoman rule Daskalogiannis
  • 1725: Bach's chorale cantata "Wie schön leuchtet der Morgenstern, BWV 1", is first performed on the Feast of the Annunciation, coinciding with Palm Sunday. Chorale cantata (Bach)
  • 1655: Saturn's largest moon, Titan, is discovered by Christiaan Huygens. Saturn
  • 1576: Jerome Savage takes out a sub-lease to start the Newington Butts Theatre outside London. Newington Butts Theatre
  • 1410: The Yongle Emperor of Ming China launches the first of his military campaigns against the Mongols, resulting in the fall of the Mongol khan Bunyashiri. Yongle Emperor
  • 1306: Robert the Bruce becomes King of Scots (Scotland). Robert the Bruce
  • 1221: Coronation of Robert of Courtenay as Emperor of the Latin Empire. Robert of Courtenay
  • 1000: Fatimid caliph al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah assassinates the eunuch chief minister Barjawan and assumes control of the government. AD 1000
  • 717: Theodosius III resigns the throne to the Byzantine Empire to enter a monastery, allowing Leo III to take the throne and begin the Isaurian dynasty. Theodosius III
  • 421: Italian city Venice is founded with the dedication of the first church, that of San Giacomo di Rialto on the islet of Rialto. Venice

About the date itself

March 25 is the 84th day of an ordinary year, with 281 days left after it (282 in a leap year, where it is the 85th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 25 March 2027 in 2027 is 17 Shawwal 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 12.
