May 7: what happened on this day

What happened on 7 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2023: A tourist boat capsized, killing at least 22 people in Tanur, India. 2023 Tanur boat disaster
  • 2010: A draft sequence of the Neanderthal genome was published, demonstrating that most living humans have Neanderthal ancestors. Neanderthal
  • 2009: Police in Napier, New Zealand, began a 40-hour siege of the home of a former New Zealand Army member who had shot at officers during the routine execution of a search warrant. Napier, New Zealand
  • 1991: A fire and explosion at a fireworks factory in Sungai Buloh, Malaysia, killed 26 people. Bright Sparklers fireworks disaster
  • 1954: A convoy of circus wagons carrying lions, horses, ponies and an ass lost control and overturned into some shop fronts in Dungannon, Northern Ireland. Dungannon
  • 1946: Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita founded the telecommunications corporation Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo, later renamed Sony. Masaru Ibuka
  • 1937: Employees at Fleischer Studios in New York City went on strike in the animation industry's first major labor strike. Fleischer Studios
  • 1930: An earthquake struck northwestern Iran and southeastern Turkey, killing up to 3,000 people. 1930 Salmas earthquake
  • 1798: War of the First Coalition: A British garrison repelled a French attack on the Îles Saint-Marcouf off the Normandy coast, inflicting heavy losses. War of the First Coalition
  • 1697: The 13th-century castle of Tre Kronor in Stockholm burned down; plans for the current royal palace were presented within the year. Tre Kronor (castle)
  • 1544: During the Rough Wooing, an English army carried out the Burning of Edinburgh. Rough Wooing
  • 1487: Granada War: Forces of Aragon and Castile began a siege of Málaga, a Muslim city in the south of the Iberian Peninsula. Granada War

More events

  • 2025: The Indian Army and the Indian Air Force conduct surgical strikes code-named Operation SINDOOR on terrorist hideouts in Pakistan in response to the Pahalgam Attack that killed 26 people. Indian Army
  • 2023: Tanur boat disaster: At least 22 people are killed when a boat carrying tourists capsizes in Tanur, Malappuram, Kerala, India. 2023 Tanur boat disaster
  • 2002: An EgyptAir Boeing 737-500 crashes on approach to Tunis–Carthage International Airport, killing 14 people. EgyptAir Flight 843
  • 2000: Vladimir Putin is inaugurated as president of Russia. Vladimir Putin
  • 1999: Kosovo War: Three Chinese citizens are killed and 20 wounded when a NATO aircraft inadvertently bombs the Chinese embassy in Belgrade, Serbia. Kosovo War
  • 1998: Mercedes-Benz buys Chrysler for US$40 billion and forms DaimlerChrysler in the largest industrial merger in history. Mercedes-Benz
  • 1992: Michigan ratifies a 203-year-old proposed amendment to the United States Constitution making the 27th Amendment law. This amendment bars the U.S. Congress from giving itself a mid-term pay raise. Michigan
  • 1992: Three employees at a McDonald's Restaurant in Sydney, Nova Scotia, Canada, are brutally murdered and a fourth permanently disabled after a botched robbery. It is the first "fast-food murder" in Canada. McDonald's
  • 1991: A fire and explosion occurs at a fireworks factory at Sungai Buloh, Malaysia, killing 26. Bright Sparklers fireworks disaster
  • 1964: Pacific Airlines Flight 773 is hijacked by Francisco Gonzales and crashes in Contra Costa County, California, killing 44. Pacific Air Lines Flight 773
  • 1954: Indochina War: The Battle of Dien Bien Phu ends in a French defeat and a Viet Minh victory (the battle began on March 13). First Indochina War
  • 1948: The Council of Europe is founded during the Hague Congress. Council of Europe
  • 1945: World War II: Last German U-boat attack of the war, two freighters are sunk off the Firth of Forth, Scotland. U-boat
  • 1940: World War II: The Norway Debate in the British House of Commons begins, and leads to the replacement of Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain with Winston Churchill three days later. World War II
  • 1931: The stand-off between criminal Francis Crowley and 300 members of the New York Police Department takes place in his fifth-floor apartment on West 91st Street, New York City. Francis Crowley
  • 1920: Polish–Soviet War: Kyiv offensive: Polish troops led by Józef Piłsudski and Edward Rydz-Śmigły and assisted by a symbolic Ukrainian force capture Kyiv only to be driven out by the Red Army counter-offensive a month later. Polish–Soviet War
  • 1920: Treaty of Moscow: Soviet Russia recognizes the independence of the Democratic Republic of Georgia only to invade the country six months later. Treaty of Moscow (1920)
  • 1915: The Republic of China accedes to 13 of the 21 Demands, extending the Empire of Japan's control over Manchuria and the Chinese economy. Beiyang government
  • 1864: American Civil War: The Army of the Potomac, under General Ulysses S. Grant, breaks off from the Battle of the Wilderness and moves southwards. American Civil War
  • 1840: The Great Natchez Tornado strikes Natchez, Mississippi killing 317 people. It is the second deadliest tornado in United States history. Great Natchez Tornado
  • 1824: World premiere of Ludwig van Beethoven's Ninth Symphony in Vienna, Austria. The performance is conducted by Michael Umlauf under the composer's supervision. Ludwig van Beethoven
  • 1794: French Revolution: Robespierre introduces the Cult of the Supreme Being in the National Convention as the new state religion of the French First Republic. French Revolution
  • 1765: HMS Victory is launched at Chatham Dockyard, Kent. She is not commissioned until 1778. HMS Victory
  • 1763: Pontiac's War begins with Pontiac's attempt to seize Fort Detroit from the British. Pontiac's War
  • 1697: Stockholm's medieval castle Tre Kronor is destroyed by fire. It is replaced in the 18th century by the current Royal Palace. Stockholm
  • 1664: Inaugural celebrations begin at Louis XIV's new Palace of Versailles. Louis XIV
  • 1544: The Burning of Edinburgh by an English army is the first action of the Rough Wooing. Burning of Edinburgh
  • 1342: In Avignon, France, Cardinal Pierre Roger is elected Pope and takes the name Clement VI. Avignon
  • 1190: The Crusader army of emperor Frederick Barbarossa defeats an army of the Rum Seljuks in the battle of Philomelion. Frederick Barbarossa
  • 558: In Constantinople, the dome of the Hagia Sophia collapses, twenty years after its construction. Justinian I immediately orders that the dome be rebuilt. Constantinople
