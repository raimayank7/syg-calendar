August 24: what happened on this day

What happened on 24 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2006: The International Astronomical Union passed a resolution redefining the term planet and classifying Pluto as a dwarf planet. International Astronomical Union
  • 1992: Hurricane Andrew, the third-most intense Category 5 cyclone to impact the United States in the 20th century, made landfall in southern Florida. Hurricane Andrew
  • 1963: Buddhist crisis: The U.S. State Department ordered Ambassador Henry Cabot Lodge Jr. (pictured) to encourage South Vietnamese Army officers to oust Ngo Dinh Diem if he did not willingly remove Ngo Dinh Nhu from his unofficial position of power. Buddhist crisis
  • 1942: World War II: At the Battle of the Eastern Solomons, bombers from the U.S. aircraft carrier Saratoga sank the Japanese aircraft carrier Ryūjō near Santa Isabel Island, contributing to an Allied victory. World War II
  • 1941: Adolf Hitler ordered the suspension of the T4 euthanasia program of the mentally ill and disabled, although killings continued in secret for the remainder of World War II. Adolf Hitler
  • 1914: The Battle of Cer ended with the first Allied victory of World War I. Battle of Cer
  • 1888: 89 New Zealand Native football team
  • 1857: The New York City branch of the Ohio Life Insurance and Trust Co. collapsed following widespread embezzlement, leading to a severe recession that caused about 5,000 businesses to fail. Ohio Life Insurance and Trust Company
  • 1781: American Revolutionary War: Near present-day Aurora, Indiana, American Indians led by Joseph Brant killed or captured all members of a Pennsylvania militia. American Revolutionary War
  • 1662: The 1662 Book of Common Prayer was legally enforced as the liturgy of the Church of England, precipitating the Great Ejection of Dissenter ministers from their benefices. Book of Common Prayer (1662)
  • 1643: A Dutch expedition (depicted) arrived at the mouth of the Valdivia River, in present-day Chile, to establish a new colony in the ruins of the abandoned Spanish settlement of Valdivia. Dutch expedition to Valdivia
  • 410: Rome was sacked for the first time in approximately 800 years by the Visigoths under Alaric I. Sack of Rome (410)

More events

  • 2023: Japan officially begins discharging treated radioactive water from the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant into the Pacific Ocean, sparking international concerns and condemnation. Discharge of radioactive water of the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant
  • 2017: The National Space Agency of Taiwan successfully launches the observation satellite Formosat-5 into space. List of government space agencies
  • 2014: A magnitude 6.0 earthquake strikes the San Francisco Bay Area; it is the largest in that area since 1989. 2014 South Napa earthquake
  • 2010: In San Fernando, Tamaulipas, Mexico, 72 illegal immigrants are killed by Los Zetas and eventually found dead by Mexican authorities. San Fernando, Tamaulipas
  • 2008: Sixty-five passengers are killed when Iran Aseman Airlines Flight 6895 crashes during an emergency landing at Manas International Airport in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan. Iran Aseman Airlines Flight 6895
  • 2006: The International Astronomical Union (IAU) redefines the term "planet" such that Pluto is now considered a dwarf planet. International Astronomical Union
  • 1998: First radio-frequency identification (RFID) human implantation tested in the United Kingdom. Radio-frequency identification
  • 1992: Hurricane Andrew makes landfall in Homestead, Florida as a Category 5 hurricane, causing up to $25 billion (1992 USD) in damages. Hurricane Andrew
  • 1989: Colombian drug barons declare "total war" on the Colombian government. Colombia
  • 1981: Mark David Chapman is sentenced to 20 years to life in prison for murdering John Lennon. Mark David Chapman
  • 1963: Buddhist crisis: As a result of the Xá Lợi Pagoda raids, the US State Department cables the United States Embassy, Saigon to encourage Army of the Republic of Vietnam generals to launch a coup against President Ngô Đình Diệm if he did not remove his brother Ngô Đình Nhu. Buddhist crisis
  • 1954: Vice president João Café Filho takes office as president of Brazil, following the suicide of Getúlio Vargas. Café Filho
  • 1949: The treaty creating the North Atlantic Treaty Organization goes into effect. NATO
  • 1942: World War II: The Battle of the Eastern Solomons. Japanese aircraft carrier Ryūjō is sunk, with the loss of seven officers and 113 crewmen. The US carrier USS Enterprise is heavily damaged. World War II
  • 1937: Spanish Civil War: the Basque Army surrenders to the Italian Corpo Truppe Volontarie following the Santoña Agreement. Spanish Civil War
  • 1936: The Australian Antarctic Territory is created. Australian Antarctic Territory
  • 1931: Resignation of the United Kingdom's Second Labour Government. Formation of the UK National Government. Second MacDonald ministry
  • 1914: World War I: German troops capture Namur. World War I
  • 1909: Workers start pouring concrete for the Panama Canal. Panama Canal
  • 1870: The Wolseley expedition reaches Manitoba to end the Red River Rebellion. Wolseley expedition
  • 1820: Constitutionalist insurrection at Oporto, Portugal. Constitutionalism
  • 1815: The modern Constitution of the Netherlands is signed. Constitution of the Netherlands
  • 1789: The first naval battle of the Svensksund begins in the Gulf of Finland. Battle of Svensksund (1789)
  • 1743: The War of the Hats: The Swedish army surrenders to the Russians in Helsinki, ending the war and starting Lesser Wrath. Russo-Swedish War (1741–1743)
  • 1675: A Polish-Lithuanian army under John III Sobieski defeats an Ottoman-Tartar army in the battle of Lwow. Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
  • 1643: A Dutch fleet establishes a new colony in the ruins of Valdivia in southern Chile. Dutch expedition to Valdivia
  • 1516: The Ottoman Empire under Selim I defeats the Mamluk Sultanate and captures present-day Syria at the Battle of Marj Dabiq. Ottoman Empire
  • 1349: Six thousand Jews are killed in Mainz after being blamed for the bubonic plague. Mainz
  • 1200: King John of England, signer of the first Magna Carta, marries Isabella of Angoulême in Angoulême Cathedral. John, King of England
  • 410: The Visigoths under King Alaric I begin to pillage Rome. Visigoths

About the date itself

August 24 is the 236th day of an ordinary year, with 129 days left after it (130 in a leap year, where it is the 237th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Tuesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Friday, 2030: Saturday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 24 August 2027 in 2027 is 22 Rabi' al-awwal 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 34.
