July 28: what happened on this day

What happened on 28 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2010: In the deadliest air accident in Pakistan's history, Airblue Flight 202 crashed into the Margalla Hills north of Islamabad, killing all 152 aboard. Airblue Flight 202
  • 2005: Britain's costliest tornado struck Birmingham, injuring 39 people and causing £40 million of damage across the city. 2005 Birmingham tornado
  • 2005: The Provisional Irish Republican Army announced the formal end of its armed campaign to overthrow British rule in Northern Ireland and create a united Ireland. Provisional Irish Republican Army
  • 1995: Two followers of the Indian mystic Rajneesh were convicted of a 1985 plot to assassinate Charles Turner, the U.S. attorney for the District of Oregon. Rajneesh movement
  • 1976: An earthquake registering 7.6 Mw, one of the deadliest in history, devastated Tangshan, China, and killed at least 240,000 people. 1976 Tangshan earthquake
  • 1940: At the Salzburg Conference, Adolf Hitler demanded the replacement of much of Slovakia's cabinet. Salzburg Conference
  • 1939: During the excavation of a 7th-century ship burial at Sutton Hoo in Suffolk, England, archaeologists discovered the Sutton Hoo helmet (pictured), widely associated with King Rædwald of East Anglia. Ship burial
  • 1917: In New York City, the NAACP and church and community leaders organized a silent march of at least 8,000 people to protest violence directed towards African Americans. NAACP
  • 1911: The Australasian Antarctic Expedition began with the departure of SY Aurora from London. Australasian Antarctic Expedition
  • 1899: A Category 1 hurricane (map pictured) made landfall in Azua Province in the Dominican Republic, destroying three large schooners at Santo Domingo; only one crew member on the three vessels survived. 1899 Carrabelle hurricane
  • 1821: Peruvian War of Independence: Argentine general José de San Martín declared the independence of Peru from the Spanish Empire. Peruvian War of Independence
  • 1540: King Henry VIII of England had his chief minister Thomas Cromwell executed for treason and heresy. Henry VIII

More events

  • 2022: Catastrophic floods devastate Eastern Kentucky, resulting in 45 fatalities and causing damage to thousands of homes and businesses. 2022 Appalachian floods
  • 2018: Australian Wendy Tuck becomes the first female skipper to win the Clipper Round the World Yacht Race. Wendy Tuck
  • 2011: While flying from Seoul, South Korea to Shanghai, China, Asiana Airlines Flight 991 develops an in-flight fire in the cargo hold. The Boeing 747-400F freighter attempts to divert to Jeju International Airport, but crashes into the sea South-West of Jeju island, killing both crew members on board. Seoul
  • 2010: Airblue Flight 202 crashes into the Margalla Hills north of Islamabad, Pakistan, killing all 152 people aboard. It is the deadliest aviation accident in Pakistan history and the first involving an Airbus A321. Airblue Flight 202
  • 2002: Nine coal miners trapped in the flooded Quecreek Mine in Somerset County, Pennsylvania, are rescued after 77 hours underground. Coal mining
  • 2001: Australian Ian Thorpe becomes the first swimmer to win six gold medals at a single World Championship meeting. Ian Thorpe
  • 1996: The remains of a prehistoric man are discovered near Kennewick, Washington. Such remains will be known as the Kennewick Man. Prehistory
  • 1976: The Tangshan earthquake measuring between 7.8 and 8.2 moment magnitude flattens Tangshan in the People's Republic of China, killing 242,769 and injuring 164,851. 1976 Tangshan earthquake
  • 1974: Spetsgruppa A, Russia's elite special force, was formed. Alpha Group
  • 1965: Vietnam War: U.S. president Lyndon B. Johnson announces his order to increase the number of United States troops in South Vietnam from 75,000 to 125,000. Vietnam War
  • 1960: The German Volkswagen Act comes into force. Volkswagen Act
  • 1957: Heavy rain and a mudslide in Isahaya, western Kyushu, Japan, kills 992. Isahaya, Nagasaki
  • 1943: World War II: Operation Gomorrah: The Royal Air Force bombs Hamburg, Germany causing a firestorm that kills 42,000 German civilians. Bombing of Hamburg in World War II
  • 1942: World War II: Soviet leader Joseph Stalin issues Order No. 227. In response to alarming German advances, all those who retreat or otherwise leave their positions without orders to do so are to be tried in a military court, with punishment ranging from duty in a shtrafbat battalion, imprisonment in a Gulag, or execution. World War II
  • 1938: Hawaii Clipper disappears between Guam and Manila as the first loss of an airliner in trans-Pacific China Clipper service. Pan Am Flight 229
  • 1932: During the Great Depression, U.S. president Herbert Hoover orders the United States Army to forcibly evict the "Bonus Army" of World War I veterans gathered in Washington, D.C. Great Depression
  • 1917: Anti-lynching movement: The Silent Parade takes place in New York City, in protest against murders, lynchings, and other violence directed towards African Americans. Anti-lynching movement
  • 1914: World War I: In the culmination of the July Crisis, Austria-Hungary declares war on the Kingdom of Serbia and begins the Great War. World War I
  • 1911: The Australasian Antarctic Expedition began as the SY Aurora departed London. Australasian Antarctic Expedition
  • 1883: A moderate earthquake measuring magnitude 4.3–5.2 strikes the Italian island of Ischia, killing over 2,300 people. 1883 Casamicciola earthquake
  • 1866: At the age of 18, Vinnie Ream becomes the first and youngest female artist to receive a commission from the United States government for a statue (of Abraham Lincoln). Vinnie Ream
  • 1864: American Civil War: In the Battle of Ezra Church, Confederate troops make a third unsuccessful attempt under General John Bell Hood to prevent Union forces led by General William T. Sherman from approaching Atlanta, Georgia. American Civil War
  • 1821: José de San Martín declares the independence of Peru from Spain. José de San Martín
  • 1809: Peninsular War: Sir Arthur Wellesley's British, Portuguese and Spanish army repulse a French force led by Joseph Bonaparte in the Battle of Talavera. Peninsular War
  • 1794: French Revolution: Maximilien Robespierre and Louis Antoine de Saint-Just are executed by guillotine in Paris, France. French Revolution
  • 1656: Second Northern War: Battle of Warsaw begins. Northern War of 1655–1660
  • 1635: Eighty Years' War: The Spanish capture the strategic Dutch fortress of Schenkenschans. Eighty Years' War
  • 1540: Henry VIII of England marries his fifth wife, Catherine Howard. Henry VIII
  • 1540: Thomas Cromwell is executed after having been condemned untried, using a bill of attainder which Cromwell had used against his opponents. Thomas Cromwell
  • 1364: Troops of the Republic of Pisa and the Republic of Florence clash in the Battle of Cascina. Republic of Pisa

About the date itself

It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 28 July 2027 in 2027 is 24 Safar 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
