December 31: what happened on this day

What happened on 31 December: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 10 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2006: War in Somalia: Transitional Federal Government forces attacked the last stronghold of the Islamic Courts Union in the town of Jilib. War in Somalia (2006–2009)
  • 2004: Taipei 101 (pictured) in Taipei, Taiwan, opened to the public as the world's tallest building. Taipei 101
  • 1999: In accordance with the Torrijos–Carter Treaties, Panama assumed full control of the Panama Canal Zone from the United States. Torrijos–Carter Treaties
  • 1998: The European Exchange Rate Mechanism froze the exchange rates of the legacy currencies in the eurozone, establishing the value of the euro. European Exchange Rate Mechanism
  • 1986: Three disgruntled employees set fire to the Dupont Plaza Hotel in San Juan, Puerto Rico, killing more than 90 people and injuring 140 others, making it the second-deadliest hotel fire in American history. Dupont Plaza Hotel arson
  • 1983: Two Australian biologists published an article titled "A Synopsis of the Class Reptilia in Australia", initiating the Wells and Wellington affair. Wells and Wellington affair
  • 1965: Central African military officers led by Jean-Bédel Bokassa began a coup d'état against the government of President David Dacko. Jean-Bédel Bokassa
  • 1961: RTÉ, Ireland's first television network, began broadcasting. RTÉ
  • 1909: The Manhattan Bridge, connecting Lower Manhattan to Downtown Brooklyn and considered to be the forerunner of modern suspension bridges, opened to traffic. Manhattan Bridge
  • 1907: New York City held its first annual ball drop event in Times Square as part of New Year's Eve celebrations. Times Square Ball
  • 1857: Queen Victoria announced the choice of Ottawa , then a small logging town, to be the capital of the British colony of Canada. Queen Victoria
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: At the Battle of Quebec, British forces repulsed an attempt by the Continental Army to capture Quebec City and enlist French Canadian support. American Revolutionary War

More events

  • 2020: The World Health Organization issues its first emergency use validation for a COVID-19 vaccine. COVID-19 vaccine
  • 2018: Thirty-nine people are killed after a ten-story building collapses in the industrial city of Magnitogorsk, Russia. 2018 Magnitogorsk building collapse
  • 2014: A New Year's Eve celebration stampede in Shanghai kills at least 36 people and injures 49 others. 2014 Shanghai stampede
  • 2010: Tornadoes touch down in midwestern and southern United States, including Washington County, Arkansas; Greater St. Louis, Sunset Hills, Missouri, Illinois, and Oklahoma, with a few tornadoes in the early hours. A total of 36 tornadoes touched down, resulting in the deaths of nine people and $113 million in damages. 2010 New Year's Eve tornado outbreak
  • 2004: The official opening of Taipei 101, the tallest skyscraper at that time in the world, standing at a height of 509 metres (1,670 ft). Taipei 101
  • 1999: The first President of Russia, Boris Yeltsin, resigns from office, leaving Prime Minister Vladimir Putin as the acting President and successor. President of Russia
  • 1999: Indian Airlines Flight 814 hijacking ends after seven days with the release of 190 survivors at Kandahar Airport, Afghanistan. Indian Airlines Flight 814
  • 1995: The final comic of Calvin and Hobbes is published. Calvin and Hobbes
  • 1994: The First Chechen War: The Russian Ground Forces begin a New Year's storming of Grozny. First Chechen War
  • 1991: All official Soviet Union institutions have ceased operations by this date, five days after the Soviet Union is officially dissolved. Soviet Union
  • 1983: The AT&T Bell System is broken up by the United States Government. AT&T Corporation
  • 1983: In Nigeria, a coup d'état led by Major General Muhammadu Buhari ends the Second Nigerian Republic. Nigeria
  • 1968: The first flight of the Tupolev Tu-144, the first civilian supersonic transport in the world. Tupolev Tu-144
  • 1965: Jean-Bédel Bokassa, leader of the Central African Republic army, and his military officers begin a coup d'état against the government of President David Dacko. Jean-Bédel Bokassa
  • 1961: RTÉ, Ireland's state broadcaster, launches its first national television service. RTÉ
  • 1951: Cold War: The Marshall Plan expires after distributing more than US$13.3 billion in foreign aid to rebuild Western Europe. Cold War
  • 1944: World War II: Operation Nordwind, the last major Wehrmacht offensive on the Western Front, begins. Operation Northwind (1944)
  • 1942: World War II: The Royal Navy defeats the Kriegsmarine at the Battle of the Barents Sea. This leads to the resignation of Grand Admiral Erich Raeder a month later. World War II
  • 1906: Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar signs the Persian Constitution of 1906. Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar
  • 1878: Karl Benz, working in Mannheim, Germany, files for a patent on his first reliable two-stroke gas engine. He was granted the patent in 1879. Carl Benz
  • 1862: American Civil War: Abraham Lincoln signs an enabling act that would admit West Virginia to the Union, thus dividing Virginia in two. Abraham Lincoln
  • 1853: A dinner party is held inside a life-size model of an iguanodon created by Benjamin Waterhouse Hawkins and Sir Richard Owen in south London, England. Iguanodon
  • 1831: Gramercy Park is deeded to New York City. Gramercy Park
  • 1790: Efimeris, the oldest Greek newspaper of which issues have survived till today, is published for the first time. Efimeris
  • 1759: Arthur Guinness signs a 9,000-year lease at £45 per annum and starts brewing Guinness. Arthur Guinness
  • 1687: The first Huguenots set sail from France to the Cape of Good Hope. Huguenots
  • 1660: James, Duke of York is named Duke of Normandy by Louis XIV of France. James II of England
  • 1501: The First Battle of Cannanore commences, seeing the first use of the naval line of battle.[citation needed] First Battle of Cannanore
  • 1225: The Lý dynasty of Vietnam ends after 216 years by the enthronement of the boy emperor Trần Thái Tông, husband of the last Lý monarch, Lý Chiêu Hoàng, starting the Trần dynasty.[citation needed] Lý dynasty
  • 870: Battle of Englefield: The Vikings clash with ealdorman Æthelwulf of Berkshire. The invaders are driven back to Reading (East Anglia); many Danes are killed. Battle of Englefield

About the date itself

December 31 is the 365th day of an ordinary year, with 0 days left after it (1 in a leap year, where it is the 366th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Friday, 2028: Sunday, 2029: Monday, 2030: Tuesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 31 December 2027 in 2027 is 3 Sha'ban 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 52.
