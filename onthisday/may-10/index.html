May 10: what happened on this day

What happened on 10 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2017: Syrian civil war: The Syrian Democratic Forces, assisted by the U.S. military, captured the Tabqa Dam and surrounding countryside, completing the Battle of Tabqa. Syrian civil war
  • 2013: One World Trade Center (pictured) in New York City, the tallest building in the Western Hemisphere, was topped out at a height of 1,776 feet (541 m). One World Trade Center
  • 2012: Suicide bombers detonated two car bombs outside a military intelligence complex in Damascus, killing 55 people. Suicide attack
  • 1997: An earthquake registering 7.3 Mw struck near Qaen, Iran, killing at least 1,567 and leaving around 50,000 others homeless. 1997 Qayen earthquake
  • 1994: American serial killer John Wayne Gacy was executed by lethal injection for the murders of twelve teenage boys and young men in a series of killings committed between 1972 and 1978. John Wayne Gacy
  • 1940: British prime minister Neville Chamberlain resigned and formally recommended Winston Churchill as his successor. Neville Chamberlain
  • 1940: World War II: German forces commenced their invasion of Belgium. World War II
  • 1933: As part of Nazi Germany's persecution of transgender people, thousands of books taken from the Institute for Sexual Science were burned in Bebelplatz square for being "un-German". Persecution of transgender people in Nazi Germany
  • 1833: Siamese–Vietnamese wars: Lê Văn Khôi escaped from prison to begin a revolt against Emperor Minh Mạng, primarily to avenge his adoptive father, Vietnamese general Lê Văn Duyệt. Siamese–Vietnamese wars
  • 1801: First Barbary War: The Barbary corsairs of Tripoli declared war on the United States by cutting down the flagstaff in front of the U.S. consulate. First Barbary War
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: Delegates from the Thirteen Colonies met in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, to convene the Second Continental Congress, which would serve as the de facto national government of the future United States. American Revolutionary War
  • 1627: Dutch explorer Pieter Nuyts was appointed governor of Formosa (modern-day Taiwan) by the Dutch East India Company. Pieter Nuyts

More events

  • 2024: Start of the May 2024 solar storms, the most powerful set of geomagnetic storms since the 2003 Halloween solar storms. May 2024 solar storms
  • 2022: Queen Elizabeth II misses the State Opening of Parliament for the first time in 59 years. It was the first time that a new session of Parliament was opened by the Prince of Wales and the Duke of Cambridge acting as Counsellors of State. Elizabeth II
  • 2013: One World Trade Center becomes the tallest building in the Western Hemisphere. One World Trade Center
  • 2005: A hand grenade thrown by Vladimir Arutyunian lands about 20 m from U.S. President George W. Bush while he is giving a speech to a crowd in Tbilisi, Georgia, but it malfunctions and does not detonate. Grenade
  • 1997: The 7.3 Mw Qayen earthquake strikes Iran's Khorasan Province killing 1,567 people. Moment magnitude scale
  • 1994: Nelson Mandela is inaugurated as South Africa's first black president. Nelson Mandela
  • 1975: Sony introduces the Betamax videocassette recorder. Sony
  • 1967: The Northrop M2-F2 crashes on landing, becoming the inspiration for the novel Cyborg and TV series The Six Million Dollar Man. Northrop M2-F2
  • 1962: Marvel Comics publishes the first issue of The Incredible Hulk. Marvel Comics
  • 1946: First successful launch of an American V-2 rocket at White Sands Proving Ground. V-2 rocket
  • 1941: World War II: The House of Commons in London is damaged by the Luftwaffe in an air raid. House of Commons of the United Kingdom
  • 1940: World War II: German fighters accidentally bomb the German city of Freiburg. World War II
  • 1933: Censorship: In Germany, the Nazis stage massive public book burnings. Censorship
  • 1922: The United States annexes the Kingman Reef. Kingman Reef
  • 1908: Mother's Day is observed for the first time in the United States, in Grafton, West Virginia. Mother's Day
  • 1899: Finnish farmworker Karl Emil Malmelin kills seven people with an axe at the Simola croft in the village of Klaukkala. Karl Emil Malmelin
  • 1881: Carol I is crowned the King of the Romanian Kingdom. Carol I of Romania
  • 1872: Victoria Woodhull becomes the first woman nominated for President of the United States. Victoria Woodhull
  • 1865: American Civil War: In Kentucky, Union soldiers ambush and mortally wound Confederate raider William Quantrill, who lingers until his death on June 6. Kentucky
  • 1849: Astor Place Riot: A riot breaks out at the Astor Opera House in Manhattan, New York City over a dispute between actors Edwin Forrest and William Charles Macready, killing at least 22 and injuring over 120. Astor Place Riot
  • 1833: A revolt broke out in southern Vietnam against Emperor Minh Mang, who had desecrated the deceased mandarin Le Van Duyet. Lê Văn Khôi revolt
  • 1801: First Barbary War: The Barbary pirates of Tripoli declare war on the United States of America. First Barbary War
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: A small Colonial militia led by Ethan Allen and Colonel Benedict Arnold captures Fort Ticonderoga. American Revolutionary War
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: The Second Continental Congress takes place in Philadelphia. Second Continental Congress
  • 1773: The Parliament of Great Britain passes the Tea Act, designed to save the British East India Company by reducing taxes on its tea and granting it the right to sell tea directly to North America. The legislation leads to the Boston Tea Party. Parliament of Great Britain
  • 1713: Great Northern War: The Russian Navy led by Admiral Fyodor Apraksin land both at Katajanokka and Hietalahti during the Battle of Helsinki. Great Northern War
  • 1591: Elizabeth I is entertained by the Hermit's Welcome at Theobalds. Elizabeth I
  • 1503: Christopher Columbus visits the Cayman Islands and names them Las Tortugas after the numerous turtles there. Christopher Columbus
  • 1294: Temür, Khagan of the Mongols, is enthroned as Emperor of the Yuan dynasty. Temür Khan
  • 946: Alberic II of Spoleto arranges the election of pope Agapetus II following the death of Pope Marinus II. Alberic II of Spoleto

About the date itself

May 10 is the 130th day of an ordinary year, with 235 days left after it (236 in a leap year, where it is the 131st day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 10 May 2027 in 2027 is 4 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 19.
