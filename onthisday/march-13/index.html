March 13: what happened on this day

What happened on 13 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 10 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2013: Cardinal Jorge Mario Bergoglio was elected as Pope Francis, making him the first Jesuit pope, the first from the Americas, and the first from the Southern Hemisphere. 2013 conclave
  • 2004: The Filipino singer Rachelle Ann Go won the reality talent show Search for a Star, performing a cover of Mariah Carey's "Through the Rain" in the grand final. Rachelle Ann Go
  • 1996: A mass shooting at a primary school occurred in Dunblane, Scotland, killing 16 children and a teacher and prompting tighter gun control in the United Kingdom. Dunblane massacre
  • 1988: The 53.85 km (33.46 mi) Seikan Tunnel opened between the cities of Hakodate and Aomori, Japan. Seikan Tunnel
  • 1985: One of England's worst incidents of football hooliganism occurred when supporters of Luton Town and Millwall rioted before a match at Kenilworth Road stadium. Football hooliganism
  • 1964: Kitty Genovese was murdered in New York City, prompting research into the bystander effect due to the false story that neighbors witnessed the killing and did nothing to help her. Murder of Kitty Genovese
  • 1920: The Kapp Putsch (participants pictured), an attempted coup aiming to undo the German Revolution of 1918–1919, briefly ousted the government of the Weimar Republic. Kapp Putsch
  • 1888: The eruption of Ritter Island triggered tsunamis that killed up to 3,000 people on nearby islands. 1888 Ritter Island eruption and tsunami
  • 1845: German composer Felix Mendelssohn's Violin Concerto was premiered in Leipzig with Ferdinand David as the soloist. Felix Mendelssohn
  • 1811: Napoleonic Wars: A British frigate squadron defeated a much larger squadron of French and Italian frigates and smaller vessels in the Battle of Lissa (depicted) in the Adriatic Sea. Napoleonic Wars
  • 1741: War of Jenkins' Ear: The British began an assault against Spanish forts in the Caribbean in the Battle of Cartagena de Indias. War of Jenkins' Ear
  • 1697: Nojpetén, capital of the Itza Maya kingdom, fell to Spanish conquistadors, the final step in the Spanish conquest of Guatemala. Nojpetén

More events

  • 2020: President Donald Trump declares the COVID-19 pandemic to be a national emergency in the United States. Donald Trump
  • 2020: Breonna Taylor is killed by police officers who were forcibly entering her home in Louisville, Kentucky; her death sparked extensive protests against racism and police brutality. Breonna Taylor
  • 2019: The Suzano school shooting kills 10 people. Suzano massacre
  • 2016: The Ankara bombing kills at least 37 people. March 2016 Ankara bombing
  • 2015: Serbian Army Mi-17 helicopter crashes near Belgrade airport while transporting a 5-day-old baby with respiratory problems to hospital, killing all 7 on board. 2015 Belgrade helicopter crash
  • 2013: The 2013 papal conclave elects Cardinal Jorge Mario Bergoglio taking the name Pope Francis as the 266th Pope of the Catholic Church. 2013 conclave
  • 2003: An article in Nature identifies the Ciampate del Diavolo as 350,000-year-old hominid footprints. Nature (journal)
  • 1997: The Missionaries of Charity choose Sister Nirmala to succeed Mother Teresa as their leader. Missionaries of Charity
  • 1993: The 1993 Storm of the Century affects the eastern United States, dropping feet of snow in many areas. 1993 Storm of the Century
  • 1989: Space Shuttle Discovery launches on STS-29 carrying the TDRS-4 satellite. Space Shuttle Discovery
  • 1988: The Seikan Tunnel, the longest tunnel in the world with an undersea segment, opens between Aomori and Hakodate, Japan. Seikan Tunnel
  • 1974: Sierra Pacific Airlines Flight 802 crashes into the White Mountains near Bishop, California, killing 36. Sierra Pacific Airlines Flight 802
  • 1969: Apollo 9 returns safely to Earth after testing the Lunar Module. Apollo 9
  • 1957: Cuban student revolutionaries storm the presidential palace in Havana in a failed attempt on the life of President Fulgencio Batista. Cuba
  • 1954: The Battle of Điện Biên Phủ begins with an artillery barrage by Viet Minh forces under Võ Nguyên Giáp; Viet Minh victory led to the end of the First Indochina War and French withdrawal from Vietnam. Battle of Dien Bien Phu
  • 1940: The Winter War between Finland and the Soviet Union officially ends after the signing of the Moscow Peace Treaty. Winter War
  • 1920: The Kapp Putsch briefly ousts the Weimar Republic government from Berlin. Kapp Putsch
  • 1900: British forces occupy Bloemfontein, Orange Free State, during the Second Boer War. Bloemfontein
  • 1884: The Siege of Khartoum begins. It lasts until January 26, 1885. Siege of Khartoum
  • 1862: The Act Prohibiting the Return of Slaves is passed by the United States Congress, effectively annulling the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850 and setting the stage for the Emancipation Proclamation. Act Prohibiting the Return of Slaves
  • 1845: Felix Mendelssohn's Violin Concerto receives its première performance in Leipzig with Ferdinand David as soloist. Felix Mendelssohn
  • 1825: Pope Leo XII publishes the apostolic constitution Quo Graviora in which he renewed the prohibition on Catholics joining freemasonry. Pope Leo XII
  • 1811: A French and Italian fleet is defeated by a British squadron off the island of Vis in the Adriatic during the Napoleonic Wars. Battle of Lissa (1811)
  • 1781: William Herschel discovers Uranus. William Herschel
  • 1741: The Battle of Cartagena de Indias (part of the War of Jenkins' Ear) begins. Battle of Cartagena de Indias
  • 1639: Harvard College is named after clergyman John Harvard. Harvard College
  • 1591: At the Battle of Tondibi in Mali, Moroccan forces of the Saadi dynasty, led by Judar Pasha, defeat the Songhai Empire, despite being outnumbered by at least five to one. Battle of Tondibi
  • 1323: Siege of Warangal: Sultan Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq sends an expeditionary army led by his son, Muhammad bin Tughluq, to the Kakatiya capital Warangal – after ruler Prataparudra has refused to make tribute payments. He besieges the city and finally, after a campaign of 8 months, Prataparudra surrenders on November 9. Siege of Warangal (1323)
  • 1261: The Byzantine Empire and the Republic of Genoa sign a permanent treaty against the Venetians at Nymphaeum. Byzantine Empire
  • 483: Election of Pope Felix III following the death of Pope Simplicius earlier that month. Pope Felix III

About the date itself

March 13 is the 72nd day of an ordinary year, with 293 days left after it (294 in a leap year, where it is the 73rd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Saturday, 2028: Monday, 2029: Tuesday, 2030: Wednesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 13 March 2027 in 2027 is 5 Shawwal 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 10.
