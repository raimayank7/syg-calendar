June 13: what happened on this day

What happened on 13 June: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2013: Some of the closest advisors and collaborators of Czech prime minister Petr Nečas were arrested for corruption. Petr Nečas
  • 2011: A 6.0 Mw earthquake (aftermath pictured) caused up to NZ$6 billion of additional damage to Christchurch, New Zealand, which was still recovering from an earthquake four months earlier. June 2011 Christchurch earthquake
  • 1983: Pioneer 10 passed the orbit of Neptune, becoming the first man-made object to leave the proximity of the major planets of the Solar System. Pioneer 10
  • 1981: English teenager Marcus Sarjeant fired six blanks at Queen Elizabeth II as she rode down The Mall to the Trooping the Colour ceremony. Marcus Sarjeant
  • 1970: "The Long and Winding Road" became the Beatles' twentieth and final number-one single on the Billboard Hot 100 chart. The Long and Winding Road
  • 1969: Preston Smith, Governor of Texas, signed a law converting a research arm of Texas Instruments into the University of Texas at Dallas. Preston Smith (Texas politician)
  • 1944: Second World War: At the Battle of Villers-Bocage, German tank commander Michael Wittmann destroyed around 30 Allied vehicles in less than 15 minutes. World War II
  • 1935: In one of the biggest upsets in championship boxing, the underdog James J. Braddock defeated Max Baer to become the heavyweight champion of the world. Boxing
  • 1907: The Workers' Federation of Chile declares a general strike in solidarity with an ongoing rail workers' strike. Workers' Federation of Chile
  • 1881: The Jeannette expedition to reach the North Pole from the Pacific Ocean via the Bering Strait came to an end when the USS Jeannette (pictured), after having been trapped in ice for almost two years, was crushed and sank. Jeannette expedition
  • 1805: Lewis and Clark Expedition: Meriwether Lewis became the first white American to sight the Great Falls of the Missouri River. Lewis and Clark Expedition
  • 1525: Martin Luther married Katharina von Bora, beginning the practice of clerical marriage in Protestantism. Martin Luther

More events

  • 2025: Israel initiates air strikes against Iran, initiating the Twelve Day War. Twelve-Day War
  • 2023: At least 100 people are killed when a wedding boat capsizes on the Niger River in Kwara State, Nigeria. Kwara boat disaster
  • 2021: A gas explosion in Zhangwan district of Shiyan city, in Hubei province of China, kills at least 12 people and wounds over 138 others. 2021 Shiyan pipeline explosion
  • 2012: A series of bombings across Iraq, including Baghdad, Hillah and Kirkuk, kills at least 93 people and wounds over 300 others. 13 June 2012 Iraq attacks
  • 2007: The Al Askari Mosque is bombed for a second time. Al-Askari Shrine
  • 2002: The United States withdraws from the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty. Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty
  • 2000: President Kim Dae-jung of South Korea meets Kim Jong-il, leader of North Korea, for the beginning of the first ever inter-Korea summit, in the northern capital of Pyongyang. Kim Dae-jung
  • 1999: BMW win 1999 24 Hours of Le Mans. BMW
  • 1997: The Uphaar Cinema Fire takes place at Green Park, Delhi, resulting in the deaths of 59 people and seriously injuring 103 others. Uphaar Cinema fire
  • 1996: Garuda Indonesia flight 865 crashes during takeoff from Fukuoka Airport, killing three people and injuring 170. Garuda Indonesia Flight 865
  • 1990: First day of the June 1990 Mineriad in Romania. At least 240 strikers and students are arrested or killed in the chaos ensuing from the first post-Ceaușescu elections. June 1990 Mineriad
  • 1983: Pioneer 10 becomes the first man-made object to leave the central Solar System when it passes beyond the orbit of Neptune. Pioneer 10
  • 1982: Battles of Tumbledown and Wireless Ridge, during the Falklands War. Battle of Mount Tumbledown
  • 1977: Convicted Martin Luther King Jr. assassin James Earl Ray is recaptured after escaping from prison three days before. Martin Luther King Jr.
  • 1971: Vietnam War: The New York Times begins publication of the Pentagon Papers. Vietnam War
  • 1966: The United States Supreme Court rules in Miranda v. Arizona that the police must inform suspects of their Fifth Amendment rights before questioning them (colloquially known as "Mirandizing"). Supreme Court of the United States
  • 1956: Spanish football club Real Madrid CF wins the inaugural European Cup, UEFA's premier club competition, by winning 4–3 against French club Stade de Reims in the final. Real Madrid CF
  • 1944: World War II: The Battle of Villers-Bocage: German tank ace Michael Wittmann ambushes elements of the British 7th Armoured Division, destroying up to fourteen tanks, fifteen personnel carriers and two anti-tank guns in a Tiger I tank. World War II
  • 1944: World War II: Germany launches the first V1 Flying Bomb attack on England. Only four of the eleven bombs strike their targets. V-1 flying bomb
  • 1917: World War I: The deadliest German air raid on London of the war is carried out by Gotha G.IV bombers and results in 162 deaths, including 46 children, and 432 injuries. World War I
  • 1898: Yukon Territory is formed, with Dawson chosen as its capital. Yukon
  • 1895: Émile Levassor wins the world's first real automobile race. Levassor completed the 732-mile course, from Paris to Bordeaux and back, in just under 49 hours, at a then-impressive speed of about fifteen miles per hour (24 km/h). Émile Levassor
  • 1886: A fire devastates much of Vancouver, British Columbia. Great Vancouver Fire
  • 1878: Start of the Congress of Berlin in which the major powers of Europe revise the Treaty of San Stefano, signed on March 3 the same year, that Russia had imposed on a defeated Ottoman Empire. Congress of Berlin
  • 1850: The American League of Colored Laborers, the first African American labor union in the United States, is established in New York City. American League of Colored Laborers
  • 1777: American Revolutionary War: Gilbert du Motier, Marquis de Lafayette lands near Charleston, South Carolina, in order to help the Continental Congress to train its army. American Revolutionary War
  • 1774: Rhode Island becomes the first of Britain's North American colonies to ban the importation of slaves. Rhode Island
  • 1625: King Charles I of England marries Catholic princess Henrietta Maria of France and Navarre, at Canterbury. Charles I of England
  • 1514: Henry Grace à Dieu, at over 1,000 tons the largest warship in the world at this time, built at the new Woolwich Dockyard in England, is dedicated. Henry Grace à Dieu
  • 1325: Ibn Battuta begins his travels, leaving his home in Tangiers to travel to Mecca (gone 24 years). Ibn Battuta

About the date itself

June 13 is the 164th day of an ordinary year, with 201 days left after it (202 in a leap year, where it is the 165th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Sunday, 2028: Tuesday, 2029: Wednesday, 2030: Thursday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 13 June 2027 in 2027 is 8 Muharram 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
