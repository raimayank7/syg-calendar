March 1: what happened on this day

What happened on 1 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 1992: A Bosnian-Serb wedding procession was attacked in Sarajevo, resulting in what is widely considered the first casualty of the Bosnian War. Serbs of Bosnia and Herzegovina
  • 1973: Pink Floyd's critically acclaimed album The Dark Side of the Moon was released. Pink Floyd
  • 1950: In a trial lasting less than 90 minutes, German–British physicist Klaus Fuchs was convicted of violating the Official Secrets Act by supplying information from the Manhattan Project to the Soviet Union. Klaus Fuchs
  • 1944: World War II: American and Australian troops won the Battle of Sio against Japanese forces as part of the New Guinea campaign. World War II
  • 1936: Hoover Dam, straddling the Arizona–Nevada border on the Colorado River, was completed. Hoover Dam
  • 1921: The Kronstadt rebellion began following mass protests in Petrograd demanding greater freedom in Russia with sailors and citizens taking up arms against the Bolsheviks. Kronstadt rebellion
  • 1896: Ethiopia defeated Italy at the Battle of Adwa, ending the First Italo-Ethiopian War. Battle of Adwa
  • 1896: French physicist Henri Becquerel discovered the principle of radioactive decay when he exposed photographic plates to uranium salts. Henri Becquerel
  • 1872: Yellowstone National Park (bison pictured), located mostly in the present-day U.S. state of Wyoming, was established by President Ulysses S. Grant. Yellowstone National Park
  • 1870: Paraguayan War: Marshal Francisco Solano López died at the Battle of Cerro Corá. Paraguayan War
  • 1869: The Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev (pictured) finished his design of the first periodic table. Dmitri Mendeleev
  • 1805: Samuel Chase, the only Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States to be impeached, was acquitted by the Senate. Samuel Chase

More events

  • 2014: Thirty-five people are killed and 143 injured in a mass stabbing at Kunming Railway Station in China. 2014 Kunming attack
  • 2007: Tornadoes break out across the southern United States, killing at least 20 people, including eight at Enterprise High School. Tornado
  • 2005: In Roper v. Simmons, the U.S. Supreme Court rules that the execution of juveniles found guilty of any crime is unconstitutional. Roper v. Simmons
  • 2002: U.S. invasion of Afghanistan: Operation Anaconda begins in eastern Afghanistan. War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)
  • 2002: Space Shuttle Columbia is launched on STS-109 to service the Hubble Space Telescope. Space Shuttle Columbia
  • 1991: Uprisings against Saddam Hussein begin in Iraq, leading to the deaths of more than 25,000 people, mostly civilians. 1991 Iraqi uprisings
  • 1981: Provisional Irish Republican Army member Bobby Sands begins his hunger strike in HM Prison Maze. Provisional Irish Republican Army
  • 1973: Black September storms the Saudi embassy in Khartoum, Sudan, resulting in the assassination of three Western hostages. Black September Organization
  • 1966: Venera 3 Soviet space probe crashes on Venus becoming the first spacecraft to land on another planet's surface. Venera 3
  • 1964: Paradise Airlines Flight 901A crashes near Lake Tahoe, Nevada, killing 85. Paradise Airlines Flight 901A
  • 1961: Uganda becomes self-governing and holds its first elections. Uganda
  • 1956: The International Air Transport Association finalizes a draft of the Radiotelephony spelling alphabet for the International Civil Aviation Organization. International Air Transport Association
  • 1954: Nuclear weapons testing: The Castle Bravo, a 15-megaton hydrogen bomb, is detonated on Bikini Atoll in the Pacific Ocean, resulting in the worst radioactive contamination ever caused by the United States. Nuclear weapons testing
  • 1950: Cold War: Klaus Fuchs is convicted of spying for the Soviet Union by disclosing top secret atomic bomb data. Cold War
  • 1946: The Bank of England is nationalised. Bank of England
  • 1941: World War II: Bulgaria signs the Tripartite Pact, allying itself with the Axis powers. World War II
  • 1932: Aviator Charles Lindbergh's 20-month-old son Charles Jr is kidnapped from his home in East Amwell, New Jersey. His body would not be found until May 12. Charles Lindbergh
  • 1921: Following mass protests in Petrograd demanding greater freedom in the RSFSR, the Kronstadt rebellion begins, with sailors and citizens taking up arms against the Bolsheviks. Saint Petersburg
  • 1914: China joins the Universal Postal Union. Republic of China (1912–1949)
  • 1901: The Australian Army is formed. Australian Army
  • 1896: Henri Becquerel discovers radioactive decay. Henri Becquerel
  • 1872: Yellowstone National Park is established as the world's first national park. Yellowstone National Park
  • 1867: Nebraska is admitted as the 37th U.S. state. Nebraska
  • 1836: A convention of delegates from 57 Texas communities convenes in Washington-on-the-Brazos, Texas, to deliberate independence from Mexico. Convention of 1836
  • 1811: Leaders of the Mamluk dynasty are killed by Egyptian ruler Muhammad Ali. Mamluk
  • 1796: The Dutch East India Company is nationalized by the Batavian Republic. Dutch East India Company
  • 1633: Samuel de Champlain reclaims his role as commander of New France on behalf of Cardinal Richelieu. Samuel de Champlain
  • 1562: Sixty-three Huguenots are massacred in Wassy, France, marking the start of the French Wars of Religion. Huguenots
  • 1290: University of Coimbra, in Portugal, is officially chartered by King Denis. University of Coimbra
  • 350: Vetranio proclaims himself Caesar after being encouraged to do so by Constantina, sister of Constantius II. Vetranio

About the date itself

March 1 is the 60th day of an ordinary year, with 305 days left after it (306 in a leap year, where it is the 61st day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 1 March 2027 in 2027 is 22 Ramadan 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 9.
