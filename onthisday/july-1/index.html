July 1: what happened on this day

What happened on 1 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2008: Rioting erupted in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, over allegations of fraud surrounding the recent legislative election. 2008 riot in Mongolia
  • 1999: Legislative powers in Scotland were first devolved from the Scottish Office in London to the Scottish Parliament in Edinburgh. Devolution in the United Kingdom
  • 1979: Sony introduced the Walkman, a portable audio player that changed listening habits by offering users the ability to play one's own choice of music. Sony
  • 1960: Ghana became a republic, with Kwame Nkrumah as its first president. Kwame Nkrumah
  • 1948: The first flight departed New York City's John F. Kennedy International Airport, then known as New York International Airport. John F. Kennedy International Airport
  • 1935: The first Grant Park Music Festival was held in Chicago's Grant Park. Grant Park Music Festival
  • 1922: Seven of the sixteen American railroad labor organizations staged a nationwide strike (striking workers pictured) that lasted two months. List of American railway unions
  • 1916: First World War: The first day of the Battle of Albert, the opening phase of the Battle of the Somme, became the bloodiest day in the British Army's history, with 57,470 casualties, including 19,240 deaths. World War I
  • 1862: American Civil War: Confederate general Robert E. Lee launched a series of disjointed and ultimately unsuccessful assaults on the nearly impregnable Union position on Malvern Hill in Henrico County, Virginia. American Civil War
  • 1841: Thomas Lempriere and James Clark Ross carved a sandstone marker on Tasmania's Isle of the Dead, one of the oldest benchmarks for measuring sea level rise. Thomas Lempriere
  • 1690: Williamite forces defeated the Jacobites at the Battle of the Boyne near Drogheda, marking a turning point in the Williamite War in Ireland. Williamite
  • 1523: Jan van Essen and Hendrik Vos were burnt at the stake in Brussels (pictured), becoming the first Lutherans executed by the Council of Brabant. Jan van Essen and Hendrik Vos

More events

  • 2024: At the centennial ceremony of the Dominion of Newfoundland National War Memorial, the Commonwealth War Graves Commission allows an unprecedented second Canadian Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. The Royal Newfoundland Regiment soldier is entombed in the memorial at this ceremony. National War Memorial (Newfoundland)
  • 2008: Riots erupt in Mongolia in response to allegations of fraud surrounding the 2008 legislative elections. 2008 riot in Mongolia
  • 2004: Saturn orbit insertion of Cassini–Huygens begins at 01:12 UTC and ends at 02:48 UTC. Saturn
  • 1999: The Scottish Parliament is officially opened by Elizabeth II on the day that legislative powers are officially transferred from the old Scottish Office in London to the new devolved Scottish Executive in Edinburgh. In Wales, the powers of the Welsh Secretary are transferred to the National Assembly. Scottish Parliament
  • 1991: Cold War: The Warsaw Pact is officially dissolved at a meeting in Prague. Cold War
  • 1987: The American radio station WFAN in New York City is launched as the world's first all-sports radio station. WFAN (AM)
  • 1980: "O Canada" officially becomes the national anthem of Canada. O Canada
  • 1976: Portugal grants autonomy to Madeira. Madeira
  • 1968: The Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons is signed in Washington, D.C., London and Moscow by sixty-two countries. Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons
  • 1966: The People's Liberation Army Rocket Force (first known as the 2nd Artillery Corps) is founded. People's Liberation Army Rocket Force
  • 1962: Independence of Rwanda and Burundi. Rwanda
  • 1959: Specific values for the international yard, avoirdupois pound and derived units (e.g. inch, mile and ounce) are adopted after agreement between the US, the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth countries. Yard
  • 1949: The merger of two princely states of India, Cochin and Travancore, into the state of Thiru-Kochi (later re-organized as Kerala) in the Indian Union ends more than 1,000 years of princely rule by the Cochin royal family. Kochi
  • 1946: Crossroads Able is the first postwar nuclear weapon test. Operation Crossroads
  • 1942: The Australian Federal Government becomes the sole collector of income tax in Australia as State Income Tax is abolished. Australian Government
  • 1931: Wiley Post and Harold Gatty become the first people to circumnavigate the globe in a single-engined monoplane aircraft (a Lockheed Vega). Wiley Post
  • 1922: The Great Railroad Strike of 1922 begins in the United States. Great Railroad Strike of 1922
  • 1917: Chinese General Zhang Xun seizes control of Beijing and restores the monarchy, installing Puyi, last emperor of the Qing dynasty, to the throne. The restoration is reversed just shy of two weeks later, when Republican troops regain control of the capital. Zhang Xun
  • 1908: SOS is adopted as the international distress signal. SOS
  • 1898: Spanish–American War: The Battle of San Juan Hill is fought in Santiago de Cuba, Cuba. Spanish–American War
  • 1885: The Congo Free State is established by King Leopold II of Belgium. Congo Free State
  • 1878: Canada joins the Universal Postal Union. Universal Postal Union
  • 1870: The United States Department of Justice formally comes into existence. United States Department of Justice
  • 1863: American Civil War: The Battle of Gettysburg begins. Battle of Gettysburg
  • 1858: Joint reading of Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace's papers on evolution to the Linnean Society of London. Charles Darwin
  • 1837: A system of civil registration of births, marriages and deaths is established in England and Wales. Civil registration
  • 1782: Raid on Lunenburg: American privateers attack the British settlement of Lunenburg, Nova Scotia. Raid on Lunenburg, Nova Scotia (1782)
  • 1690: Glorious Revolution: Battle of the Boyne in Ireland (as reckoned under the Julian calendar). Glorious Revolution
  • 1543: Having been defeated in the Battle of Solway Moss in the previous year, the Scots conclude the peace Treaty of Greenwich with the Kingdom of England. In it, they also agree to a marriage between the infant Queen Mary and Prince Edward, son of Henry VIII. Battle of Solway Moss
  • 1431: The Battle of La Higueruela takes place in Granada, leading to a modest advance of the Kingdom of Castile during the Reconquista. Battle of La Higueruela

About the date itself

July 1 is the 182nd day of an ordinary year, with 183 days left after it (184 in a leap year, where it is the 183rd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 1 July 2027 in 2027 is 26 Muharram 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 26.
