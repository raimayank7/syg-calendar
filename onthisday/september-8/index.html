September 8: what happened on this day

What happened on 8 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2022: Queen Elizabeth II (pictured) died at Balmoral Castle in Scotland; her eldest son Charles III acceded to the throne as King of the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms. Elizabeth II
  • 1994: USAir Flight 427 crashed on approach to Pittsburgh International Airport, resulting in 132 deaths and the longest accident investigation in the history of the National Transportation Safety Board. USAir Flight 427
  • 1974: Watergate scandal: U.S. president Gerald Ford gave his recently resigned predecessor Richard Nixon a controversial full and unconditional pardon for any crimes he committed while in office. Watergate scandal
  • 1966: Queen Elizabeth II opened the Severn Bridge, suggesting that it marked the dawn of a new economic era for South Wales. Elizabeth II
  • 1954: Eight nations signed a collective-defense treaty in Manila to create the Southeast Asia Treaty Organization (flag pictured), modelled on NATO. Collective security
  • 1935: Huey Long, a U.S. senator, was shot in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, dying two days later. Huey Long
  • 1907: Gertrude Stein meets her life partner and muse, Alice B. Toklas in Paris. Gertrude Stein
  • 1860: The paddle steamer Lady Elgin (pictured) was rammed by a schooner on Lake Michigan and sank, resulting in the loss of about 300 lives. Paddle steamer
  • 1796: French Revolutionary Wars: The French defeated Austrian forces in Bassano, Venetia, Italy. French Revolutionary Wars
  • 1775: Maltese priests discontented with the Order of Saint John led an uprising that was quickly suppressed by the Order. Knights Hospitaller
  • 1726: J. S. Bach led the first performance of Geist und Seele wird verwirret, a solo cantata for alto voice. Johann Sebastian Bach
  • 1565: St. Augustine, Florida, the oldest continuously occupied settlement of European origin in the contiguous United States, was founded by Spanish admiral Pedro Menéndez de Avilés. St. Augustine, Florida

More events

  • 2025: Balçova police station shooting: Four people, including the perpetrator are killed an attack on a police station in Turkey. Turkey–Islamic State conflict
  • 2023: The 2023 Rugby World Cup, the tenth men's Rugby World Cup is held in France. The opening ceremony, directed and written by Jean Dujardin, Olivier Ferracci and Nora Matthey, took place at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis, before the opening match between France and New Zealand, which saw the host nation winning 27 to 13. 2023 Rugby World Cup
  • 2016: NASA launches OSIRIS-REx, its first asteroid sample return mission. The probe visited 101955 Bennu and returned with samples in September 2023. OSIRIS-REx
  • 2000: NASA launches Space Shuttle Atlantis on STS-106 to resupply the International Space Station. Space Shuttle Atlantis
  • 1988: Yellowstone National Park is closed for the first time in U.S. history due to ongoing fires. Yellowstone fires of 1988
  • 1975: Gays in the military: US Air Force Tech Sergeant Leonard Matlovich, a decorated veteran of the Vietnam War, appears in his Air Force uniform on the cover of Time magazine with the headline "I Am A Homosexual". He is given a general discharge, later upgraded to honorable. LGBTQ people and military service
  • 1973: World Airways Flight 802 crashes into Mount Dutton in King Cove, Alaska, killing six people. World Airways Flight 802
  • 1966: The landmark American science fiction television series Star Trek premieres with its first-aired episode, "The Man Trap". Star Trek: The Original Series
  • 1954: The Southeast Asia Treaty Organization (SEATO) is established. Southeast Asia Treaty Organization
  • 1946: A referendum abolishes the monarchy in Bulgaria. 1946 Bulgarian republic referendum
  • 1943: World War II: The Armistice of Cassibile is proclaimed by radio. OB Süd immediately implements plans to disarm the Italian forces. Armistice of Cassibile
  • 1935: US Senator from Louisiana Huey Long is fatally shot in the Louisiana State Capitol building. Huey Long
  • 1926: Germany is admitted to the League of Nations. Weimar Republic
  • 1921: Margaret Gorman, a 16-year-old, wins the Atlantic City Pageant's Golden Mermaid trophy; pageant officials later dubbed her the first Miss America. Margaret Gorman
  • 1905: The 7.2 Mw Calabria earthquake shakes southern Italy with a maximum Mercalli intensity of XI (Extreme), killing between 557 and 2,500 people. 1905 Calabria earthquake
  • 1892: The Pledge of Allegiance is first recited. Pledge of Allegiance
  • 1888: The Great Herding (Spanish: El Gran Arreo) begins with thousands of sheep being herded from the Argentine outpost of Fortín Conesa to Santa Cruz near the Strait of Magellan. Patagonian sheep farming boom
  • 1883: The Northern Pacific Railway (reporting mark NP) was completed in a ceremony at Gold Creek, Montana. Former president Ulysses S. Grant drove in the final "golden spike" in an event attended by rail and political luminaries. Northern Pacific Railway
  • 1860: The steamship PS Lady Elgin sinks on Lake Michigan, with the loss of around 300 lives. Steamship
  • 1831: November uprising: The Battle of Warsaw effectively ends the Polish insurrection. November Uprising
  • 1810: The Tonquin sets sail from New York Harbor with 33 employees of John Jacob Astor's newly created Pacific Fur Company on board. Tonquin (1807 ship)
  • 1796: French Revolutionary Wars: Battle of Bassano: French forces defeat Austrian troops at Bassano del Grappa. Battle of Bassano
  • 1775: The unsuccessful Rising of the Priests in Malta. Rising of the Priests
  • 1756: French and Indian War: Kittanning Expedition. Kittanning Expedition
  • 1726: J. S. Bach leads the first performance of Geist und Seele wird verwirret, BWV 35, a solo cantata for alto voice. Johann Sebastian Bach
  • 1522: Magellan–Elcano circumnavigation: Victoria arrives at Seville, completing the first circumnavigation. Magellan expedition
  • 1504: Michelangelo's David is unveiled in Piazza della Signoria in Florence. David (Michelangelo)
  • 1331: Stefan Dušan declares himself king of Serbia. Stefan Dušan
  • 1253: Pope Innocent IV canonises Stanislaus of Szczepanów, who was killed by King Bolesław II. Pope Innocent IV
  • 617: Battle of Huoyi: Li Yuan defeats a Sui dynasty army, opening the path to his capture of the imperial capital Chang'an and the eventual establishment of the Tang dynasty. Battle of Huoyi
