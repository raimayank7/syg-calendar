September 6: what happened on this day

What happened on 6 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2022: Liz Truss (pictured) succeeded Boris Johnson as prime minister following the July 2022 United Kingdom government crisis. Liz Truss
  • 2018: The Supreme Court of India invalidated part of Section 377 of the Penal Code, thus legalising homosexuality in India. Supreme Court of India
  • 2000: The Los Angeles Dodgers hosted the first Pride Night in Major League Baseball history. Los Angeles Dodgers
  • 1997: An estimated 2.5 billion people worldwide watched the funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales, on television. Funeral of Diana, Princess of Wales
  • 1995: Cal Ripken Jr. (pictured) played his 2,131st consecutive Major League Baseball game, breaking the 56-year-old record set by Lou Gehrig. Cal Ripken Jr.
  • 1970: Black September: The Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine hijacked four airliners, landing two at Dawson's Field in Jordan and one in Cairo, while the last hijacking attempt was foiled near London. Black September
  • 1952: A prototype aircraft crashed at the Farnborough Airshow in Hampshire, England, killing the pilot and test observer on board, and 29 spectators. Farnborough Airshow crash
  • 1944: World War II: Soviet forces captured the city of Tartu on their way to re-establishing their rule in Estonia. World War II
  • 1916: The first self-service grocery store, Piggly Wiggly, was founded in Memphis, Tennessee, U.S. Grocery store
  • 1863: General John S. Marmaduke fatally wounded his Confederate Army colleague Lucius M. Walker in a formal duel in Arkansas. John S. Marmaduke
  • 1781: American Revolutionary War: General Benedict Arnold led British forces to victory at the Battle of Groton Heights. American Revolutionary War
  • 1566: Suleiman the Magnificent, the longest-reigning sultan of the Ottoman Empire, died at the age of 71. Suleiman the Magnificent

More events

  • 2022: Boris Johnson resigns as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, and is replaced by Liz Truss. Their meetings with Queen Elizabeth II at Balmoral Castle were the Queen's final official duties before her death two days later. Boris Johnson

  • 1995: Cal Ripken Jr. of the Baltimore Orioles plays in his 2,131st consecutive game, breaking a record that had stood for 56 years. Cal Ripken Jr.
  • 1991: The Soviet Union recognizes the independence of the Baltic states Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania. Soviet Union
  • 1988: End of the last stage of the Anfal campaign of Ba'athist Iraq against its Kurdish population, which resulted in major destructions and major civilian losses. Anfal campaign
  • 1985: Midwest Express Airlines Flight 105 crashes near Milwaukee Mitchell International Airport in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, killing all 31 people on board. Midwest Express Airlines Flight 105
  • 1976: Cold War: Soviet Air Defence Forces pilot Viktor Belenko lands a Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-25 jet fighter at Hakodate in Japan and requests political asylum in the United States; his request is granted. Cold War
  • 1971: Paninternational Flight 112 crashes on the Bundesautobahn 7 highway near Hamburg Airport, in Hamburg, Germany, killing 22. Paninternational Flight 112
  • 1968: Swaziland becomes independent. Eswatini

  • 1962: Archaeologist Peter Marsden discovers the first of the Blackfriars Ships dating back to the second century AD in the Blackfriars area of the banks of the River Thames in London. Blackfriars shipwrecks
  • 1952: A prototype aircraft crashes at the Farnborough Airshow in Hampshire, England, killing 29 spectators and the two on board. Farnborough Airshow crash
  • 1944: World War II: The city of Ypres, Belgium is liberated by Allied forces. Ypres
  • 1943: The Monterrey Institute of Technology is founded in Monterrey, Mexico as one of the largest and most influential private universities in Latin America. Monterrey Institute of Technology and Higher Education
  • 1940: King Carol II of Romania abdicates and is succeeded by his son Michael. General Ion Antonescu becomes the Conducător of Romania. Carol II of Romania
  • 1939: World War II: Union of South Africa declares war on Germany. Union of South Africa
  • 1930: Democratically elected Argentine president Hipólito Yrigoyen is deposed in a military coup. Hipólito Yrigoyen
  • 1914: World War I: The First Battle of the Marne, which would halt the Imperial German Army's advance into France, begins. World War I
  • 1885: Eastern Rumelia declares its union with Bulgaria, thus accomplishing Bulgarian unification. Eastern Rumelia
  • 1863: American Civil War: Confederate forces evacuate Battery Wagner and Morris Island in South Carolina. Confederate States of America
  • 1859: Imam Shamil surrenders to the Russians, bringing the Caucasian Imamate to an end. Imam Shamil
  • 1781: American Revolutionary War: The Battle of Groton Heights takes place, resulting in a British victory. American Revolutionary War
  • 1628: Puritans settle Salem, which became part of Massachusetts Bay Colony. Puritans
  • 1620: The Pilgrim Fathers sail from Plymouth, England, on the Mayflower to settle in North America. (Old Style date; September 16 per New Style date.) Pilgrims (Plymouth Colony)
  • 1492: Christopher Columbus sails from La Gomera in the Canary Islands, his final port of call before crossing the Atlantic Ocean for the first time. Christopher Columbus

About the date itself

September 6 is the 249th day of an ordinary year, with 116 days left after it (117 in a leap year, where it is the 250th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 6 September 2027 in 2027 is 5 Rabi' al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 36.
