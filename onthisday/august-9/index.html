August 9: what happened on this day

What happened on 9 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2024: Voepass Linhas Aéreas Flight 2283 crashed near Vinhedo, São Paulo, Brazil, killing all 62 people on board. Voepass Flight 2283
  • 2018: A civilian school bus in Dahyan, Yemen, was bombed by Saudi Arabia killing at least 40 children under 15. Dahyan
  • 2014: Michael Brown, an 18-year-old African-American man, was killed by a white police officer in Ferguson, Missouri, resulting in widespread protests and unrest. Killing of Michael Brown
  • 2001: Second Intifada: A suicide bomber attacked a pizza restaurant in Jerusalem, killing 15 people and wounding 130 others. Second Intifada
  • 1979: Female workers of YH Trading in South Korea held a sit-in protest in response to the company's unilateral shutdown. YH incident
  • 1971: The Troubles: British forces began arresting and interning suspected Irish republican militants in Northern Ireland. The Troubles
  • 1960: Led by Albert Kalonji, South Kasai declared its unilateral secession from the Republic of the Congo. Albert Kalonji
  • 1945: World War II: The USAAF bomber Bockscar dropped a Fat Man atomic bomb (replica pictured) on Nagasaki, Japan. World War II
  • 1942: World War II: In the first major naval engagement of the Guadalcanal campaign, Japan forced the U.S. Navy to withdraw from the Solomon Islands. World War II
  • 1934: The Blue Lotus, the fifth volume of The Adventures of Tintin by the Belgian cartoonist Hergé and noted for its emphasis on countering negative misconceptions of Chinese people, began serialisation. The Blue Lotus
  • 1897: The first meeting of the International Congress of Mathematicians was held in Zürich, Switzerland. International Congress of Mathematicians
  • 1862: American Civil War: Confederate troops counter-attacked and won the Battle of Cedar Mountain, after nearly being driven from the field in the early part of the battle. American Civil War

  • 1960: South Kasai secedes from the Congo. South Kasai
  • 1945: World War II: Nagasaki is devastated when an atomic bomb, Fat Man, is dropped by the United States B-29 Bockscar. Thirty-five thousand people are killed outright, including 23,200–28,200 Japanese war workers, 2,000 Korean forced workers, and 150 Japanese soldiers. Nagasaki
  • 1944: The United States Forest Service and the Wartime Advertising Council release posters featuring Smokey Bear for the first time. United States Forest Service
  • 1942: World War II: Battle of Savo Island: Allied naval forces protecting their amphibious forces during the initial stages of the Battle of Guadalcanal are surprised and defeated by an Imperial Japanese Navy cruiser force. World War II

  • 1925: A train robbery takes place in Kakori, near Lucknow, India, by the Indian independence revolutionaries, against the British government. Kakori conspiracy
  • 1907: The first Boy Scout encampment concludes at Brownsea Island in southern England. Scouting
  • 1897: The first International Congress of Mathematicians is held in Zürich, Switzerland. International Congress of Mathematicians
  • 1892: Thomas Edison receives a patent for a two-way telegraph. Thomas Edison

  • 1855: Åland War: The Battle of Suomenlinna begins. Åland War

  • 1830: Louis Philippe becomes the king of the French following abdication of Charles X. Louis Philippe I
  • 1810: Napoleon annexes Westphalia as part of the First French Empire. Napoleon
  • 1638: An army under Bernard of Saxe-Weimar defeats imperial troops at the battle of Wittenweiher. Bernard of Saxe-Weimar
  • 1500: Ottoman–Venetian War (1499–1503): The Ottomans capture Methoni, Messenia. Ottoman–Venetian War (1499–1503)
  • 1329: Quilon, the first Indian Christian Diocese, is erected by Pope John XXII; the French-born Jordanus is appointed the first Bishop. Diocese of Quilon
  • 378: Gothic War: Battle of Adrianople: A large Roman army led by Emperor Valens is defeated by the Visigoths. Valens is killed along with over half of his army. Gothic War (376–382)
