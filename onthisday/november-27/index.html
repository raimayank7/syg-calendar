November 27: what happened on this day

What happened on 27 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2020: Nuclear physicist Mohsen Fakhrizadeh, regarded as the chief of Iran's nuclear program, was assassinated, allegedly by Mossad. Mohsen Fakhrizadeh
  • 2009: A bomb exploded under, and derailed, a Russian high-speed train travelling between Moscow and Saint Petersburg, killing 28 passengers. 2009 Nevsky Express bombing
  • 2009: Lady Gaga performed the first concert of The Monster Ball Tour, which became the highest-grossing tour in history for a debut headlining artist. Lady Gaga
  • 1999: The Labour Party defeated the governing National Party in the New Zealand general election, with Labour's Helen Clark becoming the country's first female prime minister to have won office at an election. New Zealand Labour Party
  • 1991: Operations at the Reagan Test Site were disrupted due to the nearby formation of Tropical Storm Zelda. Ronald Reagan Space and Missile Test Range
  • 1963: President Lyndon B. Johnson delivered the "Let Us Continue" speech, in which he advocated for civil-rights legislation and national cohesion, to a joint session of the U.S. Congress five days after the assassination of John F. Kennedy. Lyndon B. Johnson
  • 1950: Korean War: The Chinese People's Volunteer Army launched multiple attacks against United Nations forces, beginning the Battle of Chosin Reservoir. Korean War
  • 1945: A consortium of twenty-two U.S. charities founded CARE with the mission of delivering food aid to Europe in the aftermath of World War II. CARE International
  • 1943: World War II: Australian forces began the Battle of Wareo by conducting clearing operations between Bonga and North Hill. World War II
  • 1895: Swedish chemist and industrialist Alfred Nobel (pictured) signed his last will and testament, setting aside the bulk of his estate to establish the Nobel Prize after his death. Alfred Nobel
  • 1835: James Pratt and John Smith became the last people to be executed in England for sodomy. James Pratt and John Smith
  • 1703: The great storm of 1703, one of the most severe storms to strike southern Great Britain, destroyed the first Eddystone Lighthouse off Plymouth. Great storm of 1703

More events

  • 2024: Syrian rebel groups led by Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham launch a ground offensive into Syria. Military Operations Command
  • 2020: Iran's top nuclear scientist, Mohsen Fakhrizadeh, is assassinated near Tehran. Mohsen Fakhrizadeh
  • 2015: An active shooter inside a Planned Parenthood facility in Colorado Springs, Colorado, shoots at least four police officers. One officer later dies. Two civilians are also killed, and six injured. The shooter later surrendered. Planned Parenthood
  • 2008: XL Airways Germany Flight 888T: An Airbus A320 performing a flight test crashes near the French commune of Canet-en-Roussillon, killing all seven people on board. XL Airways Germany Flight 888T
  • 2004: Pope John Paul II returns the relics of Saint John Chrysostom to the Eastern Orthodox Church. Pope John Paul II
  • 2001: A hydrogen atmosphere is discovered on the extrasolar planet Osiris by the Hubble Space Telescope, the first atmosphere detected on an extrasolar planet. Hydrogen
  • 1997: Twenty-five people are killed in the second Souhane massacre in Algeria. Souhane massacre
  • 1989: Avianca Flight 203: A Boeing 727 explodes in mid-air over Colombia, killing all 107 people on board and three people on the ground. The Medellín Cartel claimed responsibility for the attack. Avianca Flight 203
  • 1984: Under the Brussels Agreement signed between the governments of the United Kingdom and Spain, the former agrees to enter into discussions with Spain over Gibraltar, including sovereignty. Brussels Agreement (1984)
  • 1978: In San Francisco, city mayor George Moscone and openly gay city supervisor Harvey Milk are assassinated by former supervisor Dan White. San Francisco
  • 1975: The Provisional IRA assassinates Ross McWhirter, after a press conference in which McWhirter had announced a reward for the capture of those responsible for multiple bombings and shootings across England. Provisional Irish Republican Army
  • 1973: Twenty-fifth Amendment: The United States Senate votes 92–3 to confirm Gerald Ford as Vice President of the United States. (On December 6, the House will confirm him 387–35). Twenty-fifth Amendment to the United States Constitution
  • 1968: Penny Ann Early becomes the first woman to play in a major professional men's basketball league, for the Kentucky Colonels in an ABA game against the Los Angeles Stars. Penny Ann Early
  • 1954: Alger Hiss is released from prison after serving 44 months for perjury. Alger Hiss
  • 1944: World War II: RAF Fauld explosion: An explosion at a Royal Air Force ammunition dump in Staffordshire kills seventy people. RAF Fauld explosion
  • 1940: In Romania, the ruling Iron Guard fascist party assassinates over 60 of arrested King Carol II of Romania's aides and other political dissidents. Kingdom of Romania
  • 1924: In New York City, the first Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade is held. Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade
  • 1917: P. E. Svinhufvud becomes the chairman of his first senate, technically the first Prime Minister of Finland. Pehr Evind Svinhufvud
  • 1901: The U.S. Army War College is established. United States Army War College
  • 1895: At the Swedish–Norwegian Club in Paris, Alfred Nobel signs his last will and testament, setting aside his estate to establish the Nobel Prize after he dies. Alfred Nobel
  • 1868: American Indian Wars: Battle of Washita River: United States Army Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer leads an attack on Cheyenne living on reservation land. American Indian Wars
  • 1863: American Civil War: Confederate cavalry leader John Hunt Morgan and several of his men escape the Ohio Penitentiary and return safely to the South. American Civil War
  • 1856: The Coup of 1856 leads to Luxembourg's unilateral adoption of a new, reactionary constitution. Luxembourg coup of 1856
  • 1835: James Pratt and John Smith are hanged in London; they are the last two to be executed for sodomy in England. James Pratt and John Smith
  • 1815: Adoption of the Constitution of the Kingdom of Poland. Constitution of the Kingdom of Poland
  • 1755: An earthquake in northern Morocco devastates the cities of Fes and Meknes. 1755 Meknes earthquake
  • 1542: Palace plot of Renyin year: A group of Ming dynasty palace women fail to murder the Jiajing Emperor, and are executed by slow-slicing. Palace plot of Renyin year
  • 1095: Pope Urban II declares the First Crusade at the Council of Clermont. Pope Urban II
  • 511: King Clovis I dies at Lutetia and is buried in the Abbey of St Genevieve. Clovis I
  • 176: Emperor Marcus Aurelius grants his son Commodus the rank of "Imperator" and makes him Supreme Commander of the Roman legions. AD 176

About the date itself

November 27 is the 331st day of an ordinary year, with 34 days left after it (35 in a leap year, where it is the 332nd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Saturday, 2028: Monday, 2029: Tuesday, 2030: Wednesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 27 November 2027 in 2027 is 28 Jumada al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 47.
