August 29: what happened on this day

What happened on 29 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2016: Chen Quanguo became the Chinese Communist Party secretary of Xinjiang, and in that role later oversaw the creation of the Xinjiang internment camps. Chen Quanguo
  • 2007: Six nuclear warheads were mistakenly loaded onto a United States Air Force heavy bomber that flew from Minot Air Force Base in North Dakota to Barksdale Air Force Base in Louisiana. 2007 United States Air Force nuclear weapons incident
  • 1996: Vnukovo Airlines Flight 2801 crashed on approach to Svalbard Airport, Norway, killing all 141 on board. Vnukovo Airlines Flight 2801
  • 1984: Followers of Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh (pictured) began deliberately infecting people in The Dalles, Oregon, with Salmonella in the first and largest bioterrorist attack in United States history. Osho
  • 1960: Air France Flight 343 crashed while attempting to land at Yoff Airport, Dakar, killing all 63 occupants. Air France Flight 343
  • 1949: The Soviet Union conducted its first nuclear weapons test, detonating the 22-kiloton RDS-1. List of nuclear weapons tests of the Soviet Union
  • 1916: Congress passed the Philippine Autonomy Act, the first formal and official declaration of the U.S.'s commitment to grant independence to the Philippines. United States Congress
  • 1911: The last member of the Yahi, known as Ishi, emerged from the wilderness near Oroville, California, to join European American society. Yana people
  • 1903: Slava (pictured), the last of five Borodino-class battleships, was launched by the Imperial Russian Navy. Russian battleship Slava
  • 1842: Under the Treaty of Nanking, an "unequal treaty" that ended the First Opium War, the Chinese island from which Hong Kong would grow was ceded to Britain. Treaty of Nanking
  • 1786: Angered by high tax burdens and disfranchisement, farmers in western Massachusetts led by Daniel Shays began an armed uprising against the U.S. federal government. Disfranchisement
  • 1475: France signed the Treaty of Picquigny with England, freeing Louis XI to deal with the threat posed by Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy. Treaty of Picquigny

More events

  • 2022: Russo-Ukrainian war: Ukraine begins its southern counteroffensive in the Kherson Oblast, eventually culminating in the liberation of the city of Kherson. Russo-Ukrainian war
  • 2012: At least 26 Chinese miners are killed and 21 missing after a blast in the Xiaojiawan coal mine, located at Panzhihua, Sichuan Province. Xiaojiawan coal mine disaster
  • 2005: Hurricane Katrina devastates much of the U.S. Gulf Coast from Louisiana to the Florida Panhandle, killing up to 1,392 people and causing $125 billion in damage. Hurricane Katrina
  • 1998: Eighty people are killed when Cubana de Aviación Flight 389 crashes during a rejected takeoff from the Old Mariscal Sucre International Airport in Quito, Ecuador. Cubana de Aviación Flight 389
  • 1997: At least 98 villagers are killed by the Armed Islamic Group of Algeria GIA in the Rais massacre, Algeria. Armed Islamic Group of Algeria
  • 1991: Libero Grassi, an Italian businessman from Palermo, is killed by the Sicilian Mafia after taking a solitary stand against their extortion demands. Libero Grassi
  • 1982: Meitnerium, a synthetic chemical element with the atomic number 109, is first synthesized at the Gesellschaft für Schwerionenforschung in Darmstadt, Germany. Meitnerium
  • 1966: The Beatles perform their last concert before paying fans at Candlestick Park in San Francisco. The Beatles
  • 1965: The Gemini V spacecraft returns to Earth, landing in the Atlantic Ocean. Gemini 5
  • 1958: United States Air Force Academy opens in Colorado Springs, Colorado. United States Air Force Academy
  • 1949: Soviet atomic bomb project: The Soviet Union tests its first atomic bomb, known as First Lightning or Joe 1, at Semipalatinsk, Kazakhstan. Soviet atomic bomb project
  • 1944: World War II: Slovak National Uprising takes place as 60,000 Slovak troops turn against the Nazis. Slovak National Uprising
  • 1930: The last 36 remaining inhabitants of St Kilda are voluntarily evacuated to other parts of Scotland. St Kilda, Scotland
  • 1916: The United States passes the Philippine Autonomy Act. Jones Law (Philippines)
  • 1912: A typhoon strikes China, killing at least 50,000 people. 1912 China typhoon
  • 1911: The Canadian Naval Service becomes the Royal Canadian Navy. Royal Canadian Navy
  • 1907: The Quebec Bridge collapses during construction, killing 75 workers. Quebec Bridge
  • 1885: Gottlieb Daimler patents the world's first motorcycle with an internal combustion engine, the Reitwagen. Gottlieb Daimler
  • 1869: The Mount Washington Cog Railway opens, making it the world's first mountain-climbing rack railway. Mount Washington Cog Railway
  • 1831: Michael Faraday discovers electromagnetic induction. Michael Faraday
  • 1807: British troops under Sir Arthur Wellesley defeat a Danish militia outside Copenhagen in the Battle of Køge. Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington
  • 1778: American Revolutionary War: British and American forces battle indecisively at the Battle of Rhode Island. American Revolutionary War
  • 1756: Frederick the Great attacks Saxony, beginning the Seven Years' War in Europe. Frederick the Great
  • 1728: The city of Nuuk in Greenland is founded as the fort of Godt-Haab by the royal governor Claus Paarss. Nuuk
  • 1541: The Ottoman Turks capture Buda, the capital of the Hungarian Kingdom. Ottoman Turks
  • 1521: The Ottoman Turks capture Nándorfehérvár (Belgrade). Ottoman Turks
  • 1475: Hundred Years' War: The Treaty of Picquigny formally ends the war between the kingdoms of France and England. Hundred Years' War
  • 1350: Battle of Winchelsea (or Les Espagnols sur Mer): The English naval fleet under King Edward III defeats a Castilian fleet of 40 ships. Battle of Winchelsea
  • 1219: The Battle of Fariskur occurs during the Fifth Crusade. Battle of Fariskur (1219)
  • 1009: Mainz Cathedral suffers extensive damage from a fire, which destroys the building on the day of its inauguration. Mainz Cathedral
