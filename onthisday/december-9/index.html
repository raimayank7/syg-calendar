December 9: what happened on this day

What happened on 9 December: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 14 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2022: Members of the European Parliament, including Vice President Eva Kaili (pictured), and their associates are arrested in connection with the Qatar corruption scandal. Member of the European Parliament
  • 2017: Same-sex marriage in Australia became legal as the Marriage Amendment (Definition and Religious Freedoms) Act 2017 came into effect. Same-sex marriage in Australia
  • 2008: Rod Blagojevich, the governor of Illinois, was arrested on corruption charges, including for attempting to sell the U.S. Senate seat being vacated by president-elect Barack Obama. Rod Blagojevich
  • 1981: Mumia Abu-Jamal was arrested for the murder of Philadelphia police officer Daniel Faulkner; his subsequent conviction and death sentence generated controversy in the United States. Mumia Abu-Jamal
  • 1969: U.S. secretary of state William P. Rogers proposed a plan, later called the Rogers Plan, for a ceasefire in the War of Attrition; Egypt's and Jordan's acceptance of the plan over Palestine Liberation Organization objections led to civil war in Jordan in September 1970. William P. Rogers
  • 1968: Douglas Engelbart gave what became known as "The Mother of All Demos", publicly debuting the computer mouse (pictured), hypertext, and the bit-mapped graphical user interface using the computer system NLS. Douglas Engelbart
  • 1961: Tanganyika Territory gained independence from Britain before becoming part of Tanzania three years later. Tanganyika Territory
  • 1940: Second World War: British and Commonwealth forces began Operation Compass, the first major Allied military operation of the Western Desert campaign. World War II
  • 1911: A mine explosion near Briceville, Tennessee, killed 84 miners despite a well-organized rescue effort led by the United States Bureau of Mines. Cross Mountain Mine disaster
  • 1897: French actress, journalist and leading suffragette Marguerite Durand founded the feminist newspaper La Fronde. Marguerite Durand
  • 1888: The first edition of the Argosy magazine was published under the title The Golden Argosy. Argosy (magazine)
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: After their loss in the Battle of Great Bridge, British authorities were forced to evacuate from the Colony of Virginia. American Revolutionary War

  • 1961: Tanganyika becomes independent from Britain. Tanganyika (1961–1964)

  • 1946: The Constituent Assembly of India meets for the first time to write the Constitution of India. Constituent Assembly of India
  • 1941: World War II: China, Cuba, Guatemala, and the Philippine Commonwealth declare war on Germany and Japan. Republic of China (1912–1949)
  • 1937: Second Sino-Japanese War: Battle of Nanking: Japanese troops under the command of Lt. Gen. Yasuhiko Asaka launch an assault on the Chinese city of Nanking. Second Sino-Japanese War
  • 1935: Walter Liggett, an American newspaper editor and muckraker, is killed in a gangland murder. Walter Liggett

  • 1893: National Assembly bombing by Auguste Vaillant during the Ère des attentats (1892–1894). National Assembly bombing
  • 1868: The first traffic lights are installed, outside the Palace of Westminster in London. Resembling railway signals, they use semaphore arms and are illuminated at night by red and green gas lamps. Traffic light

  • 1835: Texas Revolution: The Texian Army captures San Antonio following the Siege of Béxar. Texas Revolution
  • 1824: Patriot forces led by General Antonio José de Sucre defeat a Royalist army in the Battle of Ayacucho, putting an end to the Peruvian War of Independence. Antonio José de Sucre
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: British troops and Loyalists, misinformed about Patriot militia strength, lose the Battle of Great Bridge, ending British rule in Virginia. American Revolutionary War
  • 1531: The Virgin of Guadalupe first appears to Juan Diego at Tepeyac, Mexico City. Our Lady of Guadalupe
  • 730: Battle of Marj Ardabil: The Khazars annihilate an Umayyad army and kill its commander, al-Jarrah ibn Abdallah al-Hakami. Battle of Marj Ardabil

About the date itself

December 9 is the 343rd day of an ordinary year, with 22 days left after it (23 in a leap year, where it is the 344th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 9 December 2027 in 2027 is 11 Rajab 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 49.
