September 9: what happened on this day

What happened on 9 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2015: Elizabeth II (pictured) became the longest-reigning monarch of the United Kingdom, surpassing Queen Victoria. Elizabeth II
  • 2001: Two al-Qaeda attackers assassinated Ahmad Shah Massoud, a pivotal Afghan resistance leader, two days before the September 11 attacks in the United States. Al-Qaeda
  • 1999: The first banknotes of the Portrait Series of the Singapore dollar were introduced by the Board of Commissioners of Currency. Singapore Portrait Series currency notes
  • 1969: Allegheny Airlines Flight 853 collided in mid-air with a Piper PA-28 Cherokee flown by a student pilot near Fairland, Indiana, killing all 83 occupants of and destroying both aircraft. Allegheny Airlines Flight 853
  • 1962: French president Charles de Gaulle delivered his "speech to the German youth" to 20,000 people in the courtyard of Ludwigsburg Palace (pictured). Charles de Gaulle
  • 1954: A magnitude-6.7 earthquake struck near Chlef, killing over 1,200 people and forcing the Algerian government to implement comprehensive reforms in building codes. 1954 Chlef earthquake
  • 1939: World War II: About 3,000 Polish Army troops began a nearly month-long defence of the Hel Peninsula during the German invasion of Poland. World War II
  • 1892: At the Lick Observatory in California, Edward Emerson Barnard discovered Amalthea, a moon of Jupiter and the last natural satellite to be discovered by visual observation. Lick Observatory
  • 1791: The commissioners overseeing the construction of the United States' new capital city named it Washington, in honor of the first president. Washington, D.C.
  • 1739: The Stono Rebellion, at the time the largest slave rebellion in the Thirteen Colonies of British America, erupted near Charleston, South Carolina. Stono Rebellion
  • 1493: Ottoman wars in Europe: A large Croatian army intercepted Ottoman forces returning to the Sanjak of Bosnia, but was defeated. Ottoman wars in Europe
  • 1141: Yelü Dashi, the Liao general who founded the Qara Khitai, defeated Seljuq and Kara-Khanid forces at the Battle of Qatwan, near Samarkand in present-day Uzbekistan. Yelü Dashi

More events

  • 2025: Six people are killed when Israel carries out an airstrike on Doha in a failed attempt to decapitate the Hamas leadership. Israel
  • 2016: The government of North Korea conducts its fifth and reportedly biggest nuclear test. World leaders condemn the act, with South Korea calling it "maniacal recklessness". North Korea
  • 2014: The album Songs of Innocence by U2 is digitally released at no charge to all customers of the iTunes Music Store, appearing automatically in the "purchased" section of over 500 million users worldwide. Songs of Innocence (U2 album)
  • 2012: A wave of attacks kills more than 100 people and injure 350 others across Iraq. 9 September 2012 Iraq attacks
  • 2006: Space Shuttle Program: Space Shuttle Atlantis is launched on STS-115 to resume assembling the International Space Station. It is the first ISS assembly mission after the Columbia disaster back in 2003. Space Shuttle Atlantis
  • 1993: Israeli–Palestinian peace process: The Palestine Liberation Organization officially recognizes Israel as a legitimate state. Israeli–Palestinian peace process
  • 1990: Batticaloa massacre: Massacre of 184 Tamil civilians by the Sri Lankan Army in Batticaloa District. 1990 Batticaloa massacre
  • 1976: Two Aeroflot flights collide in mid-air over Anapa, Soviet Union, killing 70. 1976 Anapa mid-air collision
  • 1971: The four-day Attica Prison riot begins, eventually resulting in 39 dead, most killed by state troopers retaking the prison. Attica Prison riot
  • 1969: In Canada, the Official Languages Act comes into force, making French equal to English throughout the Federal government. Official Languages Act (Canada)
  • 1965: The United States Department of Housing and Urban Development is established. United States Department of Housing and Urban Development
  • 1956: Elvis Presley appears on The Ed Sullivan Show for the first time. Elvis Presley
  • 1948: Kim Il Sung is appointed as premier of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea). This is celebrated annually as its national holiday. Kim Il Sung
  • 1944: World War II: The Fatherland Front takes power in Bulgaria through a military coup in the capital and armed rebellion in the country. A new pro-Soviet government is established. Fatherland Front (Bulgaria)
  • 1942: World War II: A Japanese floatplane drops incendiary bombs on Oregon. Empire of Japan
  • 1939: World War II: The Battle of Hel begins, the longest-defended pocket of Polish Army resistance during the German invasion of Poland. World War II
  • 1936: The crews of Portuguese Navy frigate NRP Afonso de Albuquerque and destroyer Dão mutiny against the Salazar dictatorship's support of General Franco's coup and declare their solidarity with the Spanish Republic. Portuguese Navy
  • 1924: Hanapepe massacre occurs on Kauai, Hawaii. Hanapepe massacre
  • 1922: The Greco-Turkish War effectively ends with Turkish victory over the Greeks in Smyrna. Greco-Turkish War (1919–1922)
  • 1892: Amalthea becomes the last moon to be discovered without the use of photography. Amalthea (moon)
  • 1855: Crimean War: The Siege of Sevastopol comes to an end when Russian forces abandon the city. Crimean War
  • 1850: California is admitted as the thirty-first U.S. state. California
  • 1839: John Herschel takes the first glass plate photograph. John Herschel
  • 1796: Grenelle camp affair, a failed uprising by supporters of Gracchus Babeuf against the French Directory. Grenelle camp affair
  • 1776: The Continental Congress officially names its union of states the United States. Continental Congress
  • 1561: The ultimately unsuccessful Colloquy of Poissy opens in an effort to reconcile French Catholics and Protestants. Colloquy of Poissy
  • 1513: James IV of Scotland is defeated and dies in the Battle of Flodden, ending Scotland's involvement in the War of the League of Cambrai. James IV
  • 1493: Battle of Krbava Field, a decisive defeat of Croats in Croatian struggle against the invasion by the Ottoman Empire. Battle of Krbava Field
  • 1488: Anne becomes sovereign Duchess of Brittany, becoming a central figure in the struggle for influence that leads to the union of Brittany and France. Anne of Brittany
  • 1141: Yelü Dashi, the Liao dynasty general who founded the Qara Khitai, defeats the Seljuq and Kara-Khanid forces at the Battle of Qatwan. Yelü Dashi

About the date itself

September 9 is the 252nd day of an ordinary year, with 113 days left after it (114 in a leap year, where it is the 253rd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 9 September 2027 in 2027 is 8 Rabi' al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 36.
