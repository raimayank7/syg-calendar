May 17: what happened on this day

What happened on 17 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2014: A military plane crash killed 17 people and left one survivor in northern Laos. 2014 Lao People's Liberation Army Air Force An-74 crash
  • 2004: Massachusetts became the first U.S. state to legalize same-sex marriage. Massachusetts
  • 1997: The First Congo War came to an end when Laurent-Désiré Kabila proclaimed himself president of Zaire, which was also renamed the Democratic Republic of the Congo. First Congo War
  • 1995: Six-year-old Gedhun Choekyi Nyima, the 11th Panchen Lama selected by the 14th Dalai Lama, was kidnapped by the Chinese government, who advocated a proxy. Gedhun Choekyi Nyima
  • 1974: The Troubles: The Ulster Volunteer Force detonated a series of car bombs in Dublin and Monaghan, Ireland, killing 34 people and injuring almost 300 others. The Troubles
  • 1954: The U.S. Supreme Court ruled in the landmark case Brown v. Board of Education, outlawing racial segregation in public schools because "separate educational facilities are inherently unequal" and therefore unconstitutional. Supreme Court of the United States
  • 1914: Under the Protocol of Corfu, the Principality of Albania officially recognized Northern Epirus as an autonomous self-governing region. Protocol of Corfu
  • 1900: The first copies of the children's novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz by L. Frank Baum were printed. The Wonderful Wizard of Oz
  • 1900: Second Boer War: The Siege of Mafeking in South Africa was lifted after 217 days, a decisive victory for the British against the Boers. Second Boer War
  • 1865: The International Telecommunication Union, which standardizes and regulates international radio and telecommunications, was founded as the International Telegraph Union in Paris. International Telecommunication Union
  • 1863: American Civil War: At the Battle of Big Black River Bridge in Mississippi, Union forces under John A. McClernand defeated a Confederate rearguard and captured around 1,700 men. American Civil War
  • 1521: Edward Stafford (pictured), whose father had been beheaded for rebelling against King Richard III of England, was himself executed for treason against King Henry VIII. Edward Stafford, 3rd Duke of Buckingham

More events

  • 2014: A military plane crash in northern Laos kills 17 people. 2014 Lao People's Liberation Army Air Force An-74 crash
  • 2007: Trains from North and South Korea cross the 38th Parallel in a test-run agreed by both governments. This is the first time that trains have crossed the Demilitarized Zone since 1953. North Korea
  • 2004: The first legal same-sex marriages in the U.S. are performed in the state of Massachusetts. Same-sex marriage
  • 1997: Troops of Laurent-Désiré Kabila march into Kinshasa. Zaire is officially renamed Democratic Republic of the Congo. Laurent-Désiré Kabila
  • 1994: Malawi holds its first multi-party elections. Malawi
  • 1990: The General Assembly of the World Health Organization (WHO) eliminates homosexuality from the list of psychiatric diseases. World Health Organization
  • 1984: Prince Charles calls a proposed addition to the National Gallery, London, a "monstrous carbuncle on the face of a much-loved and elegant friend", sparking controversies on the proper role of the Royal Family and the course of modern architecture. Charles III
  • 1983: Lebanon, Israel, and the United States sign an agreement on Israeli withdrawal from Lebanon. Lebanon
  • 1980: On the eve of presidential elections, Maoist guerrilla group Shining Path attacks a polling location in Chuschi (a town in Ayacucho), starting the Internal conflict in Peru. Maoism
  • 1977: Nolan Bushnell opens the first Chuck E. Cheese's Pizza Time Theatre (later renamed Chuck E. Cheese) in San Jose, California. Nolan Bushnell
  • 1974: Police in Los Angeles raid the Symbionese Liberation Army's headquarters, killing six members, including Camilla Hall. Los Angeles Police Department
  • 1969: Venera program: Soviet Venera 6 begins its descent into the atmosphere of Venus, sending back atmospheric data before being crushed by pressure. Venera program
  • 1959: In Cuba, the First Agrarian Reform Law – a cornerstone of the Cuban Revolution – is signed by Fidel Castro, aiming to eliminate large foreign-owned estates and redistribute land to over 100,000 peasants. Agrarian reforms in Cuba
  • 1953: Delta Air Lines Flight 318 crashes near Marshall, Texas, killing 19. Delta Air Lines Flight 318
  • 1940: World War II: Germany occupies Brussels, Belgium. World War II
  • 1937: Spanish Civil War: The Largo Caballero government resigns in the wake of the Barcelona May Days, leading Juan Negrín to form a government, without the anarcho-syndicalist CNT, in its stead. Spanish Civil War
  • 1915: The last British Liberal Party government (led by H. H. Asquith) falls. Liberal Party (UK)
  • 1902: Greek archaeologist Valerios Stais discovers the Antikythera mechanism, an ancient mechanical analog computer. Valerios Stais
  • 1875: Aristides wins the first Kentucky Derby with the jockey Oliver Lewis (2:37.75). Aristides (horse)
  • 1863: Rosalía de Castro publishes Cantares Gallegos, the first book in the Galician language. Rosalía de Castro
  • 1859: Members of the Melbourne Football Club codify the first rules of Australian rules football. Melbourne Football Club
  • 1814: The Constitution of Norway is signed and Crown Prince Christian Frederick of Denmark is elected King of Norway by the Norwegian Constituent Assembly. Constitution of Norway
  • 1805: Muhammad Ali becomes Wāli of Egypt. Muhammad Ali of Egypt
  • 1760: French forces besieging Quebec retreat after the Royal Navy arrives to relieve the British garrison. Siege of Quebec (1760)
  • 1673: Louis Jolliet and Jacques Marquette begin exploring the Mississippi River. Louis Jolliet
  • 1642: Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve founds the Ville Marie de Montréal. Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve
  • 1590: Anne of Denmark is crowned Queen of Scotland. Anne of Denmark
  • 1536: Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn's marriage is annulled. Henry VIII
  • 1521: Edward Stafford, 3rd Duke of Buckingham, is executed for treason. Edward Stafford, 3rd Duke of Buckingham
  • 884: Election of Pope Hadrian III following the death of Pope Marinus I earlier that month. Pope Adrian III

About the date itself
