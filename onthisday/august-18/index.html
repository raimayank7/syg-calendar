August 18: what happened on this day

What happened on 18 August: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 14 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2017: Two people were fatally stabbed and eight others wounded by a rejected asylum seeker in an Islamist terrorist attack in Turku, Finland. Asylum seeker
  • 2008: Pakistani president Pervez Musharraf resigned under pressure from a movement to impeach him. Pervez Musharraf
  • 1966: Vietnam War: Members of the 6th Battalion of the Royal Australian Regiment were surrounded by a much larger Viet Cong unit at the Battle of Long Tan (helicopter pictured), but held them off for several hours until reinforcements arrived. Vietnam War
  • 1948: Australia won the fifth Test of the 1948 Ashes series, becoming the first Test cricket team to go undefeated in England, earning them the nickname "The Invincibles". Fifth Test, 1948 Ashes series
  • 1945: World War II: Amid a Soviet invasion of Japanese-held Sakhalin, Japanese police massacred 18 Koreans in Kamishisuka. World War II
  • 1937: A lightning strike started the Blackwater Fire (pictured) in Shoshone National Forest, Wyoming, consuming 1,700 acres (7 km2) of old-growth forest and killing 15 firefighters. Blackwater Fire of 1937
  • 1919: Russian Civil War: British motor torpedo boats raided the Bolshevik Baltic Fleet's home base of Kronstadt, sinking a depot ship and damaging a battleship. Russian Civil War
  • 1891: A hurricane struck the Caribbean island of Martinique, killing about 700 people, injuring at least 1,000 others, and causing severe damage. 1891 Martinique hurricane
  • 1864: American Civil War: At the Battle of Globe Tavern, Union forces attempted to sever the Weldon Railroad during the siege of Petersburg. American Civil War
  • 1783: A meteor procession blazed across the night sky over Great Britain. Meteor procession
  • 1612: The trials of the Pendle and Samlesbury witches (statue pictured), among the most famous of England's witch trials, began at the assizes in Lancaster. Pendle witches
  • 1487: Reconquista: After a four-month siege, the Catholic Monarchs of Spain conquered the city of Málaga from the Emirate of Granada. Reconquista

More events

  • 2019: One hundred activists, officials, and other concerned citizens in Iceland hold a funeral for Okjökull glacier, which has completely melted after having once covered six square miles (15.5 km2). Iceland
  • 2017: The Turku terror attack occurs in Finland when a knifeman kills two and injures eight. 2017 Turku attack
  • 2008: The President of Pakistan, Pervez Musharraf, resigns under threat of impeachment. Pervez Musharraf
  • 2005: A massive power blackout hits the Indonesian island of Java; affecting almost 100 million people, it is one of the largest and most widespread power outages in history. 2005 Java–Bali blackout
  • 1993: American International Airways Flight 808 crashes at Leeward Point Field at Guantanamo Bay Naval Base in Guantánamo Bay, Cuba, injuring the three crew members. American International Airways Flight 808
  • 1989: Leading presidential hopeful Luis Carlos Galán is assassinated near Bogotá in Colombia. Luis Carlos Galán
  • 1977: Steve Biko is arrested at a police roadblock under Terrorism Act No. 83 of 1967 in King William's Town, South Africa. He later dies from injuries sustained during this arrest, bringing attention to South Africa's apartheid policies. Steve Biko
  • 1976: The Soviet Union's robotic probe Luna 24 successfully lands on the Moon. Soviet Union
  • 1971: Vietnam War: Australia and New Zealand decide to withdraw their troops from Vietnam. Vietnam
  • 1966: Vietnam War: The Battle of Long Tan ensues after a patrol from the 6th Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment clashes with a Viet Cong force in Phước Tuy Province. Battle of Long Tan
  • 1963: Civil rights movement: James Meredith becomes the first African American to graduate from the University of Mississippi. Civil rights movement
  • 1958: Brojen Das from Bangladesh swims across the English Channel in a competition as the first Bengali and the first Asian to do so, placing first among the 39 competitors. Brojen Das
  • 1949: Kemi strike: Two protesters die in the scuffle between the police and the strikers' protest procession in Kemi, Finland. 1949 Kemi strike
  • 1945: Sukarno takes office as the first president of Indonesia, following the country's declaration of independence the previous day. Sukarno
  • 1940: World War II: The Hardest Day air battle, part of the Battle of Britain, takes place. At that point, it is the largest aerial engagement in history with heavy losses sustained on both sides. World War II
  • 1937: A lightning strike starts the Blackwater Fire of 1937 in Shoshone National Forest, killing 15 firefighters within three days and prompting the United States Forest Service to develop their smokejumper program. Blackwater Fire of 1937
  • 1923: The first British Track and Field championships for women are held in London, Great Britain. 1923 WAAA Championships
  • 1917: A Great Fire in Thessaloniki, Greece, destroys 32% of the city leaving 70,000 individuals homeless. Great Thessaloniki Fire of 1917
  • 1903: German engineer Karl Jatho allegedly flies his self-made, motored gliding airplane four months before the first flight of the Wright brothers. Karl Jatho
  • 1877: American astronomer Asaph Hall discovers Phobos, one of Mars's moons. Astronomer
  • 1868: French astronomer Pierre Janssen discovers helium. Astronomer
  • 1838: The Wilkes Expedition, which would explore the Puget Sound and Antarctica, weighs anchor at Hampton Roads. United States Exploring Expedition
  • 1826: Major Gordon Laing becomes the first European to enter Timbuktu. Alexander Gordon Laing
  • 1783: A huge fireball meteor is seen across Great Britain as it passes over the east coast. 1783 Great Meteor
  • 1721: The city of Shamakhi in Safavid Shirvan is sacked. Shamakhi
  • 1612: The trials of the Pendle witches and Samlesbury witches, one of England's most famous witch trials, begin at the Lancaster Assizes. Pendle witches
  • 1590: John White, the governor of the Roanoke Colony, returns from a supply trip to England and finds his settlement deserted. John White (colonist and artist)
  • 1492: The first grammar of the Spanish language (Gramática de la lengua castellana) is presented to Queen Isabella I. Grammar
  • 1304: The Battle of Mons-en-Pévèle is fought to a draw between the French army and the Flemish militias. Battle of Mons-en-Pévèle
  • 707: Princess Abe accedes to the imperial Japanese throne as Empress Genmei. AD 707
