June 4: what happened on this day

What happened on 4 June: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 13 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2025: A crowd crush outside M. Chinnaswamy Stadium during celebrations of Royal Challengers Bengaluru's Indian Premier League title killed 11 people and injured 56 others. 2025 Bengaluru crowd crush
  • 2005: The Civic Forum of the Romanians of Covasna, Harghita and Mureș was founded to represent and promote the interests of ethnic Romanians in areas with large Hungarian populations. Civic Forum of the Romanians of Covasna, Harghita and Mureș
  • 1998: Terry Nichols was sentenced to life in prison for his role in the Oklahoma City bombing. Terry Nichols
  • 1989: The People's Liberation Army suppressed the Tiananmen Square protests in Beijing, leaving hundreds of people dead and wounded. People's Liberation Army
  • 1987: American intelligence analyst Jonathan Pollard pleaded guilty to charges of spying for Israel. Intelligence assessment
  • 1977: The Humboldt Park riot began in Chicago, resulting in three deaths, three cars in the Humboldt Park lagoon, and increased tension between the Chicago Police Department and the Puerto Rican community. Humboldt Park riot
  • 1974: Major League Baseball's Cleveland Indians hosted Ten-Cent Beer Night, but had to forfeit the game to the Texas Rangers due to rioting by drunken fans. Major League Baseball
  • 1967: A chartered British Midland Airways aircraft crashed near Stockport, England, killing 72 of the 84 people on board. British Midland International
  • 1942: World War II: The Battle of Midway, a major battle of the Pacific War, began with a massive Japanese offensive on American forces on Midway Atoll. World War II
  • 1913: Emily Davison, an activist for women's suffrage in the United Kingdom, was fatally injured after being trampled by a horse owned by King George V at the Epsom Derby. Emily Davison
  • 1792: Captain George Vancouver of the Royal Navy claimed the land around Puget Sound in the Pacific Northwest for Great Britain. George Vancouver
  • 1561: The spire of Old St Paul's Cathedral in London was destroyed by fire. Old St Paul's Cathedral

More events

  • 2025: Eleven people are killed and 56 people are injured during a crowd crush incident outside M. Chinnaswamy Stadium in Bengaluru, India for the celebration of Royal Challengers Bengaluru's Indian Premier League victory. Crowd collapses and crushes
  • 2023: Protests begin in Poland against the PiS government. 2023 Polish protests
  • 2020: Death of Giovanni López: Protests over the death of Giovanni López Ramírez, who had died on 4 May while in custody, begin in Jalisco following the release of a video of his arrest going viral on social media, and inspired by the George Floyd protests. Later, these spread across Mexico. Death of Giovanni López
  • 2005: The Civic Forum of the Romanians of Covasna, Harghita and Mureș is founded. Civic Forum of the Romanians of Covasna, Harghita and Mureș
  • 1989: In the 1989 Iranian supreme leader election, Ali Khamenei is elected as the new Supreme Leader of Iran after the death and funeral of Ruhollah Khomeini. 1989 Iranian supreme leader election
  • 1989: Solidarity's victory in the 1989 Polish legislative election occurs, the first election since the Communist Polish United Workers' Party abandoned its monopoly of power. It sparks off the Revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe. Solidarity (Polish trade union)
  • 1988: Three cars on a train carrying hexogen to Kazakhstan explode in Arzamas, Gorky Oblast, USSR, killing 91 and injuring about 1,500. RDX
  • 1983: Gordon Kahl, who killed two US Marshals in Medina, North Dakota on February 13, is killed in a shootout in Smithville, Arkansas, along with a local sheriff, after a four-month manhunt. Gordon Kahl
  • 1977: JVC introduces its VHS videotape at the Consumer Electronics Show in Chicago. It will eventually prevail against Sony's rival Betamax system in a format war to become the predominant home video medium. JVC
  • 1970: Tonga gains independence from the British Empire. Tonga
  • 1961: Cold War: In the Vienna summit, the Soviet premier Nikita Khrushchev sparks the Berlin Crisis by threatening to sign a separate peace treaty with East Germany and ending American, British and French access to East Berlin. Cold War
  • 1946: in Argentina assumes its first presidency Juan Domingo Perón. Argentina
  • 1944: World War II: The United States Fifth Army captures Rome, although much of the German Fourteenth Army is able to withdraw to the north. United States Army North
  • 1942: World War II: The Battle of Midway begins. Japanese Admiral Chūichi Nagumo orders a strike on Midway Island by much of the Imperial Japanese Navy. Battle of Midway
  • 1940: World War II: The Dunkirk evacuation ends: The British Armed Forces completes evacuation of 338,000 troops from Dunkirk in France. To rally the morale of the country, Winston Churchill delivers, only to the House of Commons, his famous "We shall fight on the beaches" speech. World War II
  • 1932: Marmaduke Grove and other Chilean military officers lead a coup d'état establishing the short-lived Socialist Republic of Chile. Marmaduke Grove
  • 1920: Hungary loses 71% of its territory and 63% of its population when the Treaty of Trianon is signed in Paris. Hungary
  • 1919: Leon Trotsky bans the planned Fourth Regional Congress of Peasants, Workers and Insurgents. Leon Trotsky
  • 1916: World War I: Russia opens the Brusilov Offensive with an artillery barrage of Austro-Hungarian lines in Galicia. World War I
  • 1912: Massachusetts becomes the first state of the United States to set a minimum wage. Massachusetts
  • 1878: Cyprus Convention: The Ottoman Empire cedes Cyprus to the United Kingdom but retains nominal title. Cyprus Convention
  • 1876: An express train called the Transcontinental Express arrives in San Francisco via the first transcontinental railroad, 83 hours and 39 minutes after leaving New York City. Express train
  • 1859: Italian Independence wars: In the Battle of Magenta, the French army, under Louis-Napoleon, defeat the Austrian army. Second Italian War of Independence
  • 1825: General Lafayette, a French officer in the American Revolutionary War, speaks at what would become Lafayette Square in Buffalo, New York, during his visit to the United States. Marquis de Lafayette
  • 1802: King Charles Emmanuel IV of Sardinia abdicates his throne in favor of his brother, Victor Emmanuel. Charles Emmanuel IV
  • 1792: Captain George Vancouver claims Puget Sound for the Kingdom of Great Britain. Captain (Royal Navy)
  • 1783: The Montgolfier brothers publicly demonstrate their montgolfière (hot air balloon). Montgolfier brothers
  • 1745: Battle of Hohenfriedberg: Frederick the Great's Prussian army decisively defeat an Austrian army under Prince Charles Alexander of Lorraine during the War of the Austrian Succession. Battle of Hohenfriedberg
  • 1561: The steeple of St Paul's, the medieval cathedral of London, is destroyed in a fire caused by lightning, and is never rebuilt. Steeple
  • 1411: King Charles VI of France grants a monopoly for the ripening of Roquefort cheese to the people of Roquefort-sur-Soulzon, as they had been doing for centuries. Charles VI of France

About the date itself

June 4 is the 155th day of an ordinary year, with 210 days left after it (211 in a leap year, where it is the 156th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Friday, 2028: Sunday, 2029: Monday, 2030: Tuesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 4 June 2027 in 2027 is 29 Dhu al-Hijjah 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table.
