June 12: what happened on this day

What happened on 12 June: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2025: Air India Flight 171 (wreckage pictured) crashed shortly after take-off in Ahmedabad, India, killing 260 people. Air India Flight 171
  • 2016: An Islamic terrorist killed 49 people in a mass shooting at the gay nightclub Pulse in Orlando, Florida. Pulse nightclub shooting
  • 1999: In the aftermath of the bombing of Yugoslavia and the Kosovo War, the NATO-led Kosovo Force entered the territory with a mandate of establishing a secure environment. NATO bombing of Yugoslavia
  • 1991: More than 150 Sri Lankan Tamil civilians were massacred by members of the military in the village of Kokkadichcholai. Sri Lankan Tamils
  • 1987: Cold War: During a speech at the Brandenburg Gate by the Berlin Wall, US president Ronald Reagan challenged Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev to "tear down this wall!" (speech featured). Cold War
  • 1978: American serial killer David Berkowitz, popularly known as the "Son of Sam", was sentenced to 25-years-to-life in prison for each of six killings. David Berkowitz
  • 1963: The premiere was held in New York of the historical drama film Cleopatra, the most expensive film made to that point. Cleopatra (1963 film)
  • 1954: Dominic Savio, who was 14 years old at his death in 1857, was canonized by Pope Pius XII, making him one of the youngest non-martyred saints in the Catholic Church. Dominic Savio
  • 1921: Soviet politician Vladimir Antonov-Ovseenko authorised the use of chemical weapons against a peasant rebellion in the Tambov Governorate. Vladimir Antonov-Ovseenko
  • 1864: Union general Ulysses S. Grant pulled his troops out of the Battle of Cold Harbor in Hanover County, Virginia, ending one of the bloodiest, most lopsided battles in the American Civil War. Union (American Civil War)
  • 1798: Following the successful French invasion of Malta, the Knights Hospitaller surrendered Malta to Napoleon, initiating two years of occupation. French invasion of Malta
  • 1775: Thomas Gage, the governor of the Province of Massachusetts Bay, offered a general pardon to colonists who remained loyal to Britain. Thomas Gage

More events

  • 2026: South African-American businessman Elon Musk becomes the first US$ trillionaire with the initial public offering of SpaceX. Elon Musk
  • 2025: Air India Flight 171, a Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner, crashes shortly after takeoff into the B. J. Medical College, Ahmedabad, India, killing 241 out of 242 onboard as well as 19 on the ground. This marked the first fatal crash and hull loss of the Boeing 787 Dreamliner. Air India Flight 171
  • 2019: Kassym-Jomart Tokayev is inaugurated as the second president of Kazakhstan. Kassym-Jomart Tokayev
  • 2016: Forty-nine civilians are killed and 58 others injured in an attack on a gay nightclub in Orlando, Florida, United States; the gunman, Omar Mateen, is killed in a gunfight with police. Pulse nightclub shooting
  • 2009: A disputed presidential election in Iran leads to wide-ranging local and international protests. 2009 Iranian presidential election
  • 1993: An election takes place in Nigeria and is won by Moshood Kashimawo Olawale Abiola. Its results are later annulled by the military government of Ibrahim Babangida. 1993 Nigerian presidential election
  • 1991: Kokkadichcholai massacre: The Sri Lankan Army massacres 152 minority Tamil civilians in the village of Kokkadichcholai near the Eastern Province town of Batticaloa. 1991 Kokkadichcholai massacre
  • 1988: Austral Líneas Aéreas Flight 046, a McDonnell Douglas MD-81, crashes short of the runway at Libertador General José de San Martín Airport, killing all 22 people on board. Austral Líneas Aéreas Flight 046
  • 1987: Cold War: At the Brandenburg Gate, U.S. President Ronald Reagan publicly challenges Mikhail Gorbachev to tear down the Berlin Wall. Cold War
  • 1981: The first of the Indiana Jones film franchise, Raiders of the Lost Ark, is released in theaters. Indiana Jones
  • 1975: State of Uttar Pradesh v. Raj Narain: Judge Jagmohanlal Sinha rules against Indira Gandhi in a case on her election to the Indian Parliament, and that she should be banned from holding any public office, triggering a political crisis. State of Uttar Pradesh v. Raj Narain
  • 1964: Anti-apartheid activist and ANC leader Nelson Mandela's original 5-year sentence is extended to life sentence for high treason together with Denis Goldberg, Ahmed Kathrada, Govan Mbeki, Raymond Mhlaba, Andrew Mlangeni, Elias Motsoaledi and Walter Sisulu in the Rivonia Trial for sabotage in South Africa. Apartheid
  • 1963: The film Cleopatra, starring Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton, is released in US theaters. It was the most expensive film made at the time. Cleopatra (1963 film)
  • 1950: An Air France Douglas DC-4 crashes near Bahrain International Airport, killing 46 people. 1950 Bahrain Air France Douglas DC-4 crashes
  • 1943: The Holocaust: Germany liquidates the Jewish Ghetto in Brzeżany, Poland (now Berezhany, Ukraine). Around 1,180 Jews are led to the city's old Jewish graveyard and shot. The Holocaust
  • 1940: World War II: Thirteen thousand British and French troops surrender to General Erwin Rommel at Saint-Valery-en-Caux. World War II
  • 1939: The Baseball Hall of Fame opens in Cooperstown, New York. National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum
  • 1921: Mikhail Tukhachevsky orders the use of chemical weapons against the Tambov Rebellion, bringing an end to the peasant uprising. Mikhail Tukhachevsky
  • 1900: The Reichstag approves new legislation continuing Germany's naval expansion program, providing for construction of 38 battleships over a 20-year period. Germany's fleet would be the largest in the world. Reichstag building
  • 1898: Philippine Declaration of Independence: General Emilio Aguinaldo declares the Philippines' independence from Spain. Philippine Declaration of Independence
  • 1830: Beginning of the Invasion of Algiers: Thirty-four thousand French soldiers land 27 kilometers west of Algiers, at Sidi Ferruch. Invasion of Algiers (1830)
  • 1817: The earliest form of bicycle, the dandy horse, is driven by Karl von Drais. Bicycle
  • 1798: Irish Rebellion of 1798: Battle of Ballynahinch. Irish Rebellion of 1798
  • 1775: American War of Independence: British general Thomas Gage declares martial law in Massachusetts. The British offer a pardon to all colonists who lay down their arms. There would be only two exceptions to the amnesty: Samuel Adams and John Hancock, if captured, were to be hanged. American Revolutionary War
  • 1758: French and Indian War: Siege of Louisbourg: James Wolfe's attack at Louisbourg, Nova Scotia, commences. French and Indian War
  • 1653: First Anglo-Dutch War: The Battle of the Gabbard begins, lasting until the following day. First Anglo-Dutch War
  • 1550: The city of Helsinki, Finland (belonging to Sweden at the time) is founded by King Gustav I of Sweden. Helsinki
  • 1418: Armagnac–Burgundian Civil War: Parisians slaughter sympathizers of Bernard VII, Count of Armagnac, along with all prisoners, foreign bankers, and students and faculty of the College of Navarre. Armagnac–Burgundian Civil War
  • 1240: At the instigation of Louis IX of France, an inter-faith debate, known as the Disputation of Paris, starts between a Christian monk and four rabbis. Louis IX of France
  • 1042: Constantine IX Monomachos is crowned as Byzantine Emperor, one day after his marriage to Empress Zoe Porphyrogenita. Constantine IX Monomachos

About the date itself

June 12 is the 163rd day of an ordinary year, with 202 days left after it (203 in a leap year, where it is the 164th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Saturday, 2028: Monday, 2029: Tuesday, 2030: Wednesday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 12 June 2027 in 2027 is 7 Muharram 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 23.
