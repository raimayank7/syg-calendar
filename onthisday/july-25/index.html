July 25: what happened on this day

What happened on 25 July: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2022: The Duffield Memorial (central medallion pictured) in Great Baddow, an "unusual example of Art Nouveau design in metal work", was designated a listed building by Historic England. Duffield Memorial
  • 2010: WikiLeaks published 75,000 classified documents about the War in Afghanistan in one of the largest leaks in U.S. military history. WikiLeaks
  • 2007: Pratibha Patil was sworn in as the first female president of India. Pratibha Patil
  • 2001: Phoolan Devi, a serving Indian member of Parliament, was shot outside her house in New Delhi, India. Phoolan Devi
  • 1976: The orbiting spacecraft Viking 1 took a photograph of an apparent face on Mars in a classic example of pareidolia. Viking 1
  • 1950: Korean War: After American troops withdrew, North Korean forces captured the village of Yongdong in South Korea. Korean War
  • 1943: The Grand Council of Fascism voted a motion of no confidence against Benito Mussolini, who was arrested the same day by King Victor Emmanuel III and replaced by Pietro Badoglio. Grand Council of Fascism
  • 1898: Spanish–American War: After more than two months of sea-based bombardment, the United States invaded Puerto Rico. Spanish–American War
  • 1788: Mozart completed his Symphony No. 40, one of his two extant symphonies in a minor key. Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart
  • 1591: Eighty Years' War: Dutch and English forces relieved the siege of Knodsenburg in the Spanish Netherlands, having defeated the Duke of Parma's army in the field. Eighty Years' War
  • 1547: Henry II was crowned King of France at Reims Cathedral. Henry II of France
  • 1261: Struggle for Constantinople: Alexios Strategopoulos led Nicaean forces to recapture Constantinople, leading to the reestablishment of the Byzantine Empire and the end of the Latin Empire. Struggle for Constantinople (1204–1261)

More events

  • 2019: National extreme heat records set this day in the UK, Belgium, The Netherlands and Germany during the July 2019 European heat wave. 2019 European heatwaves
  • 2010: WikiLeaks publishes classified documents about the War in Afghanistan, one of the largest leaks in U.S. military history. WikiLeaks
  • 2000: Concorde Air France Flight 4590 crashes outside of Paris shortly after taking off at Charles de Gaulle Airport, killing 113 people. Concorde
  • 1994: Israel and Jordan sign the Washington Declaration, that formally ends the state of war that had existed between the nations since 1948. Jordan
  • 1984: Salyut 7 cosmonaut Svetlana Savitskaya becomes the first woman to perform a space walk. Salyut 7
  • 1979: In accord with the Egypt–Israel peace treaty, Israel begins its withdrawal from the Sinai Peninsula. Egypt–Israel peace treaty
  • 1976: Viking program: Viking 1 takes the famous Face on Mars photo. Viking program
  • 1969: Vietnam War: U.S. President Richard Nixon declares the Nixon Doctrine, stating that the United States now expects its Asian allies to take care of their own military defense. This is the start of the "Vietnamization" of the war. Vietnam War
  • 1958: The African Regroupment Party holds its first congress in Cotonou. African Regroupment Party
  • 1956: Forty-five miles south of Nantucket Island, the Italian ocean liner SS Andrea Doria collides with the MS Stockholm in heavy fog and sinks the next day, killing 51. Nantucket
  • 1943: World War II: Benito Mussolini is forced out of office by the King (encouraged by the Grand Council of Fascism) and is replaced by Pietro Badoglio. World War II
  • 1940: General Henri Guisan orders the Swiss Army to resist German invasion and makes surrender illegal. Henri Guisan
  • 1917: Sir Robert Borden introduces the first income tax in Canada as a "temporary" measure (lowest bracket is 4% and highest is 25%). Robert Borden
  • 1915: RFC Captain Lanoe Hawker becomes the first British pursuit aviator to earn the Victoria Cross. Royal Flying Corps
  • 1908: Ajinomoto is founded. Kikunae Ikeda of the Tokyo Imperial University discovers that a key ingredient in kombu soup stock is monosodium glutamate (MSG), and patents a process for manufacturing it. Ajinomoto
  • 1894: The First Sino-Japanese War begins when the Japanese fire upon a Chinese warship. First Sino-Japanese War
  • 1866: The United States Congress passes legislation authorizing the rank of General of the Army. Lieutenant General Ulysses S. Grant becomes the first to be promoted to this rank. Military rank
  • 1837: The first commercial use of an electrical telegraph is successfully demonstrated in London by William Cooke and Charles Wheatstone. Electrical telegraph
  • 1824: Costa Rica annexes Guanacaste from Nicaragua. Costa Rica
  • 1797: Horatio Nelson loses more than 300 men and his right arm during the failed conquest attempt of Tenerife (Spain). Horatio Nelson, 1st Viscount Nelson
  • 1783: American Revolutionary War: The war's last action, the Siege of Cuddalore, is ended by a preliminary peace agreement. American Revolutionary War
  • 1722: Dummer's War begins along the Maine-Massachusetts border. Dummer's War
  • 1693: Ignacio de Maya founds the Real Santiago de las Sabinas, now known as Sabinas Hidalgo, Nuevo León, Mexico. Sabinas Hidalgo
  • 1603: James VI and I and Anne of Denmark are crowned in Westminster Abbey. James VI and I
  • 1567: Don Diego de Losada founds the city of Santiago de Leon de Caracas, modern-day Caracas, the capital city of Venezuela. Caracas
  • 1538: The city of Guayaquil is founded by the Spanish Conquistador Francisco de Orellana and given the name Muy Noble y Muy Leal Ciudad de Santiago de Guayaquil. Guayaquil
  • 1467: The Battle of Molinella: The first battle in Italy in which firearms are used extensively. Battle of Molinella
  • 1261: The city of Constantinople is recaptured by Nicaean forces under the command of Alexios Strategopoulos, re-establishing the Byzantine Empire. Constantinople
  • 1137: Eleanor of Aquitaine marries Prince Louis, later King Louis VII of France, at the Cathedral of Saint-André in Bordeaux. Eleanor of Aquitaine
  • 677: Climax of the Siege of Thessalonica by the Slavs in a three-day assault on the city walls. Siege of Thessalonica (676–678)

About the date itself

July 25 is the 206th day of an ordinary year, with 159 days left after it (160 in a leap year, where it is the 207th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Sunday, 2028: Tuesday, 2029: Wednesday, 2030: Thursday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 25 July 2027 in 2027 is 21 Safar 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 29.
