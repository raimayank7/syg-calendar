September 12: what happened on this day

What happened on 12 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2015: An explosion involving illegally stored mining detonators in Petlawad, India, killed 104 people and injured more than 150 others. Petlawad explosion
  • 2008: A Metrolink train collided head-on with a freight train in Los Angeles, California, resulting in 25 deaths and 135 injuries; the Metrolink driver had passed through a red signal, having likely been distracted by text messaging. Metrolink (California)
  • 2003: The first public release of Steam, a distribution service for computer games, was made available for download. Steam (service)
  • 1995: Hurricane Ismael formed off the southwest coast of Mexico; it went on to kill over a hundred people in the country. Hurricane Ismael
  • 1983: The clandestine Boricua Popular Army staged a bank robbery in West Hartford, Connecticut, making off with $7 million in the largest cash theft in U.S. history at the time. Boricua Popular Army
  • 1977: South African anti-apartheid activist Steve Biko died after being beaten in police custody in Port Elizabeth. Apartheid
  • 1952: Three boys in Flatwoods, West Virginia, U.S., reported seeing a ten-foot-tall (3 m) monster in the woods while investigating a UFO. Flatwoods, West Virginia
  • 1942: RMS Laconia was sunk by a U-boat off the coast of West Africa, which then attempted to rescue the passengers as it was acting under the old prize rules. RMS Laconia (1921)
  • 1942: World War II: The Imperial Japanese Army began the Battle of Edson's Ridge in an effort to retake Henderson Field on Guadalcanal in the Solomon Islands from the Allies. World War II
  • 1928: The Okeechobee hurricane first struck the island of Guadeloupe; eventually it reached the United States and caused over 4,000 deaths overall. 1928 Okeechobee hurricane
  • 1910: Gustav Mahler's Symphony No. 8, one of the largest-scale choral works in the classical concert repertoire and popularly known as the "Symphony of a Thousand", was first performed in Munich. Gustav Mahler
  • 1309: Reconquista: Castilian forces captured Gibraltar from the Emirate of Granada. Reconquista

More events

  • 2021: Siberian Light Aviation Flight 51 crashes short of the runway at Kazachinskoye Airport, killing four. Siberian Light Aviation Flight 51
  • 2013: NASA confirms that its Voyager 1 probe has become the first manmade object to enter interstellar space. NASA
  • 2008: The 2008 Chatsworth train collision in Los Angeles between a Metrolink commuter train and a Union Pacific freight train kills 25 people. 2008 Chatsworth train collision
  • 2007: Two earthquakes measuring 8.4 and 7.9 on the Richter Scale hits the Indonesian island of Sumatra, killing 25 people and injuring 161. 2007 Bengkulu earthquakes
  • 2003: The United Nations lifts sanctions against Libya after that country agreed to accept responsibility and recompense the families of victims in the 1988 bombing of Pan Am Flight 103. United Nations
  • 2001: Ansett Australia, Australia's first commercial interstate airline, collapses due to increased strain on the international airline industry, leaving 10,000 people unemployed. Ansett Australia
  • 1993: NASA launches Space Shuttle Discovery on STS-51. STS-51
  • 1992: Abimael Guzmán, leader of the Shining Path, is captured by Peruvian special forces; shortly thereafter the rest of Shining Path's leadership fell as well. Abimael Guzmán
  • 1990: The two German states and the Four Powers sign the Treaty on the Final Settlement with Respect to Germany in Moscow, paving the way for German reunification. Allied Control Council
  • 1984: Dwight Gooden sets the baseball record for strikeouts in a season by a rookie with 276, previously set by Herb Score with 246 in 1954. Gooden's 276 strikeouts that season, pitched in 218 innings, set the current record. Dwight Gooden
  • 1983: The USSR vetoes a United Nations Security Council Resolution deploring the Soviet destruction of Korean Air Lines Flight 007. Soviet Union
  • 1980: Florida Commuter Airlines Flight 65 crashes into the Atlantic Ocean off West End, Bahamas, killing 34 people. Florida Commuter Airlines Flight 65
  • 1974: Emperor Haile Selassie of Ethiopia, 'Messiah' of the Rastafari movement, is deposed following a military coup by the Derg, ending a reign of 58 years. Haile Selassie
  • 1966: Launch of Gemini 11, the penultimate mission of NASA's Gemini program, and the current human altitude record holder (except for the Apollo lunar missions and Artemis II). Gemini 11
  • 1961: The African and Malagasy Union is founded. African and Malagasy Union
  • 1959: The Soviet Union launches a large rocket, Lunik II, at the Moon. Soviet Union
  • 1958: Jack Kilby demonstrates the first working integrated circuit while working at Texas Instruments. Jack Kilby
  • 1944: World War II: The liberation of Yugoslavia from Axis occupation continues. Bajina Bašta in western Serbia is among the liberated cities. World War II in Yugoslavia
  • 1942: World War II: First day of the Battle of Edson's Ridge during the Guadalcanal Campaign. U.S. Marines protecting Henderson Field are attacked by Imperial Japanese Army troops. Battle of Edson's Ridge
  • 1940: The Hercules Powder plant disaster in the United States kills 51 people and injures over 200. Hercules Powder plant disaster
  • 1933: Leó Szilárd, waiting for a red light on Southampton Row in Bloomsbury, conceives the idea of the nuclear chain reaction. Leo Szilard
  • 1915: French soldiers rescue over 4,000 Armenian genocide survivors stranded on Musa Dagh. Armenian genocide
  • 1897: Tirah campaign: In the Battle of Saragarhi, ten thousand Pashtun tribesmen suffer several hundred casualties while attacking 21 Sikh soldiers in British service. Tirah campaign
  • 1885: Arbroath 36–0 Bon Accord, a world record scoreline in professional association football. Arbroath F.C. 36–0 Bon Accord F.C.
  • 1848: A new constitution marks the establishment of Switzerland as a federal state. Federalism in Switzerland
  • 1814: Battle of North Point: an American detachment halts the British land advance to Baltimore in the War of 1812. Battle of North Point
  • 1634: A gunpowder factory explodes in Valletta, Malta, killing 22 people and damaging several buildings. 1634 Valletta explosion
  • 1309: The first siege of Gibraltar takes place in the context of the Spanish Reconquista, pitting the forces of the Kingdom of Castile against the Emirate of Granada and resulting in a Castilian victory. First siege of Gibraltar
  • 1229: Battle of Portopí: The Aragonese army under the command of James I of Aragon disembarks at Santa Ponça, Mallorca, with the purpose of conquering the island. Battle of Portopí
  • 1185: Isaac Angelos is crowned Byzantine emperor following the deposition of Andronikos I Komnenos and after the crowd rejects the coronation of Isaac's uncle John Doukas. Isaac II Angelos

About the date itself

September 12 is the 255th day of an ordinary year, with 110 days left after it (111 in a leap year, where it is the 256th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Sunday, 2028: Tuesday, 2029: Wednesday, 2030: Thursday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 12 September 2027 in 2027 is 11 Rabi' al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 36.
