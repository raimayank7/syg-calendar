November 11: what happened on this day

What happened on 11 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2008: After 30 years in power, Maumoon Abdul Gayoom was succeeded by Mohamed Nasheed as the president of the Maldives. Maumoon Abdul Gayoom
  • 1999: The House of Lords Act was given royal assent, removing most hereditary peers from the British House of Lords. House of Lords Act 1999
  • 1975: During a constitutional crisis, Governor-General Sir John Kerr (pictured) dismissed Prime Minister Gough Whitlam's government and dissolved the Parliament of Australia for a double-dissolution election. 1975 Australian constitutional crisis
  • 1965: Rhodesia, led by Prime Minister Ian Smith (pictured), unilaterally declared independence from the United Kingdom. Rhodesia
  • 1940: Second World War: The Royal Navy launched the first all-aircraft ship-to-ship naval attack in history against the Italians in the Battle of Taranto. World War II
  • 1934: The Shrine of Remembrance (pictured), a memorial to all Australians who have served in war, opened in Melbourne. Shrine of Remembrance
  • 1926: The plan for the United States Numbered Highway System was approved by the American Association of State Highway Officials. United States Numbered Highway System
  • 1920: In London, the Cenotaph was unveiled and the Unknown Warrior was buried in Westminster Abbey in remembrance of the First World War. The Cenotaph
  • 1918: Józef Piłsudski (pictured), appointed the commander-in-chief of Polish forces by the Regency Council, was entrusted with the creation of a national government for the newly independent Poland. Józef Piłsudski
  • 1880: In spite of sympathizers holding rallies for his reprieve, Australian bushranger and outlaw Ned Kelly was hanged in Melbourne. Bushranger
  • 1813: War of 1812: British–Canadian forces repelled an American attack at the Battle of Crysler's Farm, forcing the United States to give up their attempt to capture Montreal. War of 1812
  • 1805: War of the Third Coalition: French, Austrian and Russian units suffered heavy losses at the Battle of Dürenstein. War of the Third Coalition

More events

  • 2026: The historic ocean liner SS United States is sunk off the coast of Destin, Florida, to become the world's largest artificial reef. SS United States
  • 2022: Russo-Ukrainian War: Ukrainian armed forces enter the city of Kherson following a successful two-month southern counteroffensive. Russo-Ukrainian war
  • 2012: A strong earthquake with the magnitude 6.8 hits northern Burma, killing at least 26 people. 2012 Shwebo earthquake
  • 2006: Queen Elizabeth II unveils the New Zealand War Memorial in London, United Kingdom, commemorating the loss of soldiers from the New Zealand Army and the British Army. Elizabeth II
  • 2004: The Palestine Liberation Organization confirms the death of Yasser Arafat from unidentified causes. Mahmoud Abbas is elected chairman of the PLO minutes later. Palestine Liberation Organization
  • 2001: Journalists Pierre Billaud, Johanne Sutton and Volker Handloik are killed in Afghanistan during an attack on the convoy they are traveling in. Pierre Billaud
  • 1999: The House of Lords Act is given Royal Assent, restricting membership of the British House of Lords by virtue of a hereditary peerage. House of Lords Act 1999
  • 1982: Space Shuttle Columbia launches from the Kennedy Space Center on STS-5, the first operational mission of the Space Shuttle program. Space Shuttle Columbia
  • 1977: A munitions explosion at a train station in Iri, South Korea kills at least 56 people. Iri station explosion
  • 1975: Independence of Angola. Angola
  • 1967: Vietnam War: In a propaganda ceremony in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, three American prisoners of war are released by the Viet Cong and turned over to "new left" antiwar activist Tom Hayden. Vietnam War
  • 1965: Southern Rhodesia's Prime Minister Ian Smith unilaterally declares the colony independent as the unrecognised state of Rhodesia. Southern Rhodesia
  • 1961: Thirteen Italian Air Force servicemen, deployed to the Congo as a part of the UN peacekeeping force, are massacred by a mob in Kindu. Kindu atrocity
  • 1949: The People's Liberation Army Air Force is founded. People's Liberation Army Air Force
  • 1940: World War II: In the Battle of Taranto, the Royal Navy launches the first all-aircraft ship-to-ship naval attack in history. World War II
  • 1934: The Shrine of Remembrance is opened in Melbourne, Australia. Shrine of Remembrance
  • 1926: The United States Numbered Highway System is established. United States Numbered Highway System
  • 1919: The Industrial Workers of the World attack an Armistice Day parade in Centralia, Washington, ultimately resulting in the deaths of five people. Industrial Workers of the World
  • 1918: World War I: Germany signs an armistice agreement with the Allies in a railroad car in the forest of Compiègne. World War I
  • 1911: Many cities in the Midwestern United States break their record highs and lows on the same day as a strong cold front rolls through. Midwestern United States
  • 1869: The Victorian Aboriginal Protection Act is enacted in Australia, giving the government control of indigenous people's wages, their terms of employment, where they could live, and of their children, effectively leading to the Stolen Generations. Victoria (state)
  • 1839: The Virginia Military Institute is founded in Lexington, Virginia. Virginia Military Institute
  • 1813: War of 1812: Battle of Crysler's Farm: British and Canadian forces defeat a larger American force, causing the Americans to abandon their Saint Lawrence campaign. War of 1812
  • 1778: Cherry Valley massacre: Loyalists and Seneca Indian forces attack a fort and village in eastern New York during the American Revolutionary War, killing more than forty civilians and soldiers. Cherry Valley massacre
  • 1724: Joseph Blake, alias Blueskin, a highwayman known for attacking "Thief-Taker General" (and thief) Jonathan Wild at the Old Bailey, is hanged in London. Joseph Blake (criminal)
  • 1675: Gottfried Leibniz demonstrates integral calculus for the first time to find the area under the graph of y = ƒ(x). Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
  • 1620: The Mayflower Compact is signed in what is now Provincetown Harbor near Cape Cod. Mayflower Compact
  • 1572: Tycho Brahe observes the supernova SN 1572. Tycho Brahe
  • 1215: The Fourth Council of the Lateran meets, defining the doctrine of transubstantiation, the process by which bread and wine are, by that doctrine, said to transform into the body and blood of Christ. Fourth Council of the Lateran
  • 1028: Constantine VIII dies, ending his uninterrupted reign as emperor or co-emperor of the Byzantine Empire of 66 years. Constantine VIII

About the date itself

November 11 is the 315th day of an ordinary year, with 50 days left after it (51 in a leap year, where it is the 316th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Saturday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 11 November 2027 in 2027 is 12 Jumada al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 45.
