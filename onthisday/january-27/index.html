January 27: what happened on this day

What happened on 27 January: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 12 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2019: MBC Newsdesk reported on a nightclub assault in Seoul, South Korea, leading to the Burning Sun scandal, which involved several Korean idols and police officials. MBC Newsdesk
  • 2011: Arab Spring: The Yemeni Revolution began as more than 16,000 protesters demonstrated in Sanaa to demand governmental changes. Arab Spring
  • 2010: Porfirio Lobo Sosa became the new president of Honduras, ending a constitutional crisis that had begun in 2009 when Manuel Zelaya was forcibly removed from office. Porfirio Lobo Sosa
  • 2003: The first selections for the United States National Recording Registry were announced by the Library of Congress. National Recording Registry
  • 1980: Six American diplomats who had evaded capture in the Iran hostage crisis escaped with the help of Canadian government officials to Zürich, Switzerland. Iran hostage crisis
  • 1967: A fire during a launch simulation for the Apollo 1 mission at Cape Kennedy killed astronauts Gus Grissom, Ed White and Roger Chaffee. Apollo 1
  • 1945: The Soviet Red Army liberated about 7,000 prisoners left behind by the Nazis in Auschwitz concentration camp. Red Army
  • 1869: Former members of the overthrown Tokugawa shogunate proclaimed Japan's second-largest island, Hokkaido, to be the independent Republic of Ezo. Tokugawa shogunate
  • 1825: On the advice of John C. Calhoun, President James Monroe asked Congress to organize Indian Territory (map pictured) west of the Mississippi River, laying the groundwork for Indian removal in the United States. John C. Calhoun
  • 1785: The University of Georgia, the oldest state-chartered public university in the United States, was founded. University of Georgia
  • 1726: J. S. Bach led the first performance of Alles nur nach Gottes Willen, BWV 72, concluding his third Christmas season in Leipzig on the Third Sunday after Epiphany. Johann Sebastian Bach
  • 945: The brothers Stephen and Constantine Lekapenos, having deposed their father as Byzantine emperor a few weeks earlier, were themselves overthrown by Constantine VII, their co-emperor. Stephen Lekapenos

More events

  • 2023: Protests and public outrage spark across the U.S. after the release of multiple videos by the Memphis Police Department showing officers punching, kicking, and pepper spraying Tyre Nichols as a result of running away from a traffic stop, which resulted him dying in the hospital three days later after the incident. Tyre Nichols protests
  • 2023: A shooting at a synagogue in Neve Yaakov, East Jerusalem, kills seven people and injures three others. 2023 Neve Yaakov shooting
  • 2017: A naming ceremony for the chemical element tennessine takes place in the United States. Tennessine

  • 1996: Germany first observes the International Holocaust Remembrance Day. International Holocaust Remembrance Day
  • 1980: Through cooperation between the U.S. and Canadian governments, six American diplomats secretly escape hostilities in Iran in the culmination of the Canadian Caper. Iran hostage crisis
  • 1973: The Paris Peace Accords officially ends the Vietnam War. Colonel William Nolde is killed in action becoming the conflict's last recorded American combat casualty. Paris Peace Accords
  • 1967: Cold War: The Soviet Union, the United States, and the United Kingdom sign the Outer Space Treaty in Washington, D.C., banning deployment of nuclear weapons in space, and limiting the usage of the Moon and other celestial bodies to peaceful purposes. Cold War
  • 1961: The Soviet submarine S-80 sinks when its snorkel malfunctions, flooding the boat. Soviet submarine S-80
  • 1951: Nuclear testing at the Nevada Test Site begins with Operation Ranger. Nuclear weapons testing
  • 1944: World War II: The 872-day Siege of Leningrad is lifted. Siege of Leningrad
  • 1939: First flight of the Lockheed P-38 Lightning. Lockheed P-38 Lightning
  • 1928: Bundaberg tragedy: a diphtheria vaccine is contaminated with Staph. aureus bacterium, resulting in the deaths of twelve children in the Australian town of Bundaberg. Bundaberg tragedy
  • 1924: Six days after his death, Vladimir Lenin's body is carried into a specially erected mausoleum. Death and state funeral of Vladimir Lenin
  • 1916: World War I: The British government passes the Military Service Act that introduces conscription in the United Kingdom. World War I
  • 1880: Thomas Edison receives a patent for his incandescent lamp. Thomas Edison
  • 1869: Boshin War: Tokugawa rebels establish the Ezo Republic in Hokkaidō. Republic of Ezo
  • 1825: The U.S. Congress approves Indian Territory (in what is present-day Oklahoma), clearing the way for forced relocation of the Eastern Indians on the "Trail of Tears". United States Congress
  • 1820: A Russian expedition led by Fabian Gottlieb von Bellingshausen and Mikhail Petrovich Lazarev discovers the Antarctic continent, approaching the Antarctic coast. Russian Empire
  • 1776: American Revolutionary War: Henry Knox's "noble train of artillery" arrives in Cambridge, Massachusetts. American Revolutionary War
  • 1726: J. S. Bach leads the first performance of Alles nur nach Gottes Willen, BWV 72, concluding his third Christmas season in Leipzig on the Third Sunday after Epiphany. Johann Sebastian Bach
  • 1695: Mustafa II becomes the Ottoman sultan and Caliph of Islam in Istanbul on the death of Ahmed II. Mustafa rules until his abdication in 1703. Mustafa II
  • 1343: Pope Clement VI issues the papal bull Unigenitus, laying out the scriptural justification for indulgences, identifying only the Pope and episcopate as capable of accessing the treasury of merit, and establishing a jubilee year every half century. Pope Clement VI
  • 1186: Henry VI, the son and heir of the Holy Roman Emperor Frederick I, marries Constance of Sicily. Henry VI, Holy Roman Emperor
  • 945: The co-emperors Stephen and Constantine are overthrown and forced to become monks by Constantine VII, who becomes sole emperor of the Byzantine Empire. Stephen Lekapenos
  • 417: Pope Innocent I declares Pelagius and his follower Caelestius excommunicated unless they return to orthodoxy. Pope Innocent I

About the date itself

January 27 is the 27th day of an ordinary year, with 338 days left after it (339 in a leap year, where it is the 27th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Thursday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 27 January 2027 in 2027 is 19 Sha'ban 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 4.
