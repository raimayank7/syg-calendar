October 25: what happened on this day

What happened on 25 October: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2022: At 49 days, Liz Truss concluded the shortest tenure as prime minister of the United Kingdom. Liz Truss
  • 2010: Mount Merapi in Central Java, Indonesia, began an increasingly violent series of eruptions that lasted over a month. Mount Merapi
  • 2001: Windows XP, one of the most popular and widely used versions of the Microsoft Windows operating system, was released for retail sale. Windows XP
  • 1944: Heinrich Himmler ordered a crackdown on the Edelweiss Pirates, a nonconformist youth group that assisted army deserters and others hiding from the Nazis. Heinrich Himmler
  • 1944: USS Tang, the U.S. Navy submarine credited with sinking more ships than any other American submarine, sank when it was struck by its own torpedo. USS Tang (SS-306)
  • 1929: Hundreds of students at Armstrong College in Newcastle, England, tarred and feathered Statue of Industry (pictured), a sculpture by Herbert Maryon. Colleges of Durham University
  • 1924: The Daily Mail published the Zinoviev letter, a hoax purported to be a directive from Moscow to increase communist agitation. Daily Mail
  • 1920: Irish playwright and politician Terence MacSwiney died after a hunger strike in Brixton Prison, bringing the Irish struggle for independence to international attention. Terence MacSwiney
  • 1864: American Civil War: During Price's Missouri Expedition, Union troops defeated Sterling Price's Confederate army in three successive battles: Marais des Cygnes, Mine Creek, and Marmiton River. American Civil War
  • 1760: George III became King of Great Britain and Ireland, succeeding his grandfather George II. George III
  • 1616: Dutch explorer Dirk Hartog left a plate on an island in Shark Bay, the oldest-known artefact of European exploration in Australia still in existence. Dirk Hartog
  • 1415: Hundred Years' War: The army of Henry V of England, consisting mostly of archers, unexpectedly defeated the numerically superior French cavalry at the Battle of Agincourt (pictured) on Saint Crispin's Day. Hundred Years' War

More events

  • 2025: Vipul Patel performed the first telesurgery between two United States hospitals, AdventHealth Celebration and AdventHealth Minneola. Vipul Patel
  • 2023: A mass shooting occurs in two locations in Lewiston, Maine. 18 people are killed and 13 more injured. 2023 Lewiston shootings
  • 2009: The October 2009 Baghdad bombings kill 155 and wound at least 721. October 2009 Baghdad bombings
  • 2001: Microsoft releases Windows XP, which becomes one of Microsoft's most successful operating systems. Microsoft
  • 1997: After a civil war, Denis Sassou Nguesso proclaims himself President of the Republic of the Congo. Republic of the Congo Civil War (1997–1999)
  • 1995: A commuter train slams into a school bus in Fox River Grove, Illinois, killing seven students. 1995 Fox River Grove bus–train collision
  • 1989: The first leg of the 1989 Supertaça Cândido de Oliveira is held at the Estádio da Luz in Lisbon, Portugal. 1989 Supertaça Cândido de Oliveira
  • 1983: The United States and its Caribbean allies invade Grenada, six days after Prime Minister Maurice Bishop and several of his supporters are executed in a coup d'état. United States invasion of Grenada
  • 1973: Egypt and Israel accept United Nations Security Council Resolution 339. United Nations Security Council Resolution 339
  • 1971: The People's Republic of China replaces the Republic of China at the United Nations. China
  • 1968: Soyuz 2 is launched. Soyuz 2
  • 1962: Cuban Missile Crisis: Adlai Stevenson shows the United Nations Security Council reconnaissance photographs of Soviet ballistic missiles in Cuba. Cuban Missile Crisis
  • 1945: Fifty years of Japanese administration of Taiwan formally ends when the Republic of China assumes control. Geography of Taiwan
  • 1944: World War II: Heinrich Himmler orders a crackdown on the Edelweiss Pirates, a loosely organized youth culture in Nazi Germany that had assisted army deserters and others to hide from the Third Reich. World War II
  • 1944: World War II: The final attempt of the Imperial Japanese Navy to win the war climaxes at the Battle of Leyte Gulf. Battle of Leyte Gulf
  • 1932: George Lansbury became the leader of the opposition British Labour Party. George Lansbury
  • 1927: The Italian luxury liner SS Principessa Mafalda sinks off the coast of Brazil, killing 314. SS Principessa Mafalda
  • 1920: After 74 days on hunger strike in Brixton Prison, England, the Sinn Féin Lord Mayor of Cork, Terence MacSwiney dies. Terence MacSwiney
  • 1918: British forces attack Turkish forces under Mustafa Kemal in the battle of Aleppo, which the British capture the following day. Mustafa Kemal Atatürk
  • 1911: The Xinhai Revolution spreads to Guangzhou, where the Qing general Fengshan is assassinated by the Chinese Assassination Corps. 1911 Revolution
  • 1875: Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky's Piano Concerto No. 1 in B♭ minor, Op. 23 premieres in Boston, Massachusetts, with Benjamin Johnson Lang as conductor and Hans von Bülow as soloist. Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky
  • 1864: American Civil War: A large contingent of Confederate troops participating in Price's Missouri Expedition are captured by Union forces during the Battle of Mine Creek, the largest battle fought in Kansas and one of the largest cavalry engagements of the war. Confederate States of America
  • 1861: The Toronto Stock Exchange is created. Toronto Stock Exchange
  • 1854: The Battle of Balaclava takes place during the Crimean War. It is soon memorialized in verse as The Charge of the Light Brigade. Battle of Balaclava
  • 1822: Greek War of Independence: The First Siege of Missolonghi begins. First siege of Missolonghi
  • 1809: Golden Jubilee of George III is celebrated in Britain as he begins the fiftieth year of his reign. Golden Jubilee of George III
  • 1760: King George III succeeds to the British throne on the death of his grandfather George II. George III
  • 1616: Dutch sea-captain Dirk Hartog makes the second recorded landfall by a European on Australian soil, at the later-named Dirk Hartog Island off the West Australian coast. Dirk Hartog
  • 1415: Hundred Years' War: Henry V of England, with his men-at-arms and archers, defeats the larger French army in the Battle of Agincourt. Battle of Agincourt
  • 1147: Reconquista: After a siege of four months, crusader knights conquer Lisbon. Siege of Lisbon

About the date itself

October 25 is the 298th day of an ordinary year, with 67 days left after it (68 in a leap year, where it is the 299th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 25 October 2027 in 2027 is 25 Jumada al-awwal 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 43.
