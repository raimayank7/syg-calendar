November 13: what happened on this day

What happened on 13 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2015: Coordinated terrorist attacks in Paris perpetrated by the Islamic State killed 130 people and injured 413 others. November 2015 Paris attacks
  • 1992: In Dietrich v The Queen, the High Court of Australia ruled that, although there is no absolute right to have publicly funded counsel, a judge should grant any request for an adjournment or stay in most circumstances in which an accused is unrepresented. Dietrich v The Queen
  • 1985: The volcano Nevado del Ruiz (pictured) erupted, causing a volcanic mudslide that buried the town of Armero, Colombia, killing approximately 23,000 people. Nevado del Ruiz
  • 1982: South Korean boxer Kim Duk-koo suffered fatal brain injuries during a match with American Ray Mancini in Las Vegas, leading to significant rule changes in the sport. Kim Duk-koo
  • 1966: Arab–Israeli conflict: In response to a Fatah landmine incident, the Israeli military conducted a large cross-border assault on the Jordanian-controlled West Bank village of Samu. Arab–Israeli conflict
  • 1963: A man wielding a dagger was subdued as he was about to attack Sanzō Nosaka, the chairman of the Japanese Communist Party. Sanzō Nosaka
  • 1945: FC Dynamo Moscow opened their exhibition tour of the United Kingdom with a 3–3 draw against Chelsea, breaking the attendance record for a British club football match. FC Dynamo Moscow
  • 1940: Walt Disney's Fantasia, the first commercial film shown with stereophonic sound, premiered at the Broadway Theatre in New York City. Walt Disney
  • 1914: Zaian War: Zaian Berber tribesmen routed French forces at the Battle of El Herri in Morocco. Zaian War
  • 1809: As part of the Persian Gulf campaign, a British flotilla arrived at and launched an amphibious assault on Ras Al Khaimah. Persian Gulf campaign of 1809
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: American forces captured Montreal without significant opposition as part of the Invasion of Quebec. American Revolutionary War
  • 1642: First English Civil War: Royalist forces engaged the much larger Parliamentarian army at the Battle of Turnham Green near Turnham Green, Middlesex. First English Civil War

More events

  • 2022: A mass stabbing occurs in Moscow, Idaho in which four University of Idaho students are killed in off-campus housing. 2022 Idaho student murders
  • 2015: Islamic State operatives carry out a series of coordinated terrorist attacks in Paris, including suicide bombings, mass shootings and a hostage crisis. The terrorists kill 130 people, making it the deadliest attack in France since the Second World War. Islamic State
  • 2002: Iraq disarmament crisis: Iraq agrees to the terms of the UN Security Council Resolution 1441. Iraq disarmament crisis
  • 2000: Philippine House Speaker Manny Villar passes the articles of impeachment against Philippine President Joseph Estrada. Philippines
  • 1996: As part of the Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search (GIMPS) project, Joel Armengaud discovers the project's first Mersenne prime number, 21398269−1, a number with 420,921 digits. Great Internet Mersenne Prime Search
  • 1995: Nigeria Airways Flight 357 crashes at Kaduna International Airport in Kaduna, Nigeria, killing 11 people and injuring 66. Nigeria Airways Flight 357
  • 1993: China Northern Airlines Flight 6901 crashes on approach to Ürümqi Diwopu International Airport in Ürümqi, China, killing 12 people. China Northern Airlines Flight 6901
  • 1991: The Republic of Karelia, an autonomous republic of Russia, is formed from the former Karelian ASSR. Republic of Karelia
  • 1990: In Aramoana, New Zealand, David Gray shoots dead 13 people in a massacre before being tracked down and killed by police the next day. Aramoana
  • 1985: The volcano Nevado del Ruiz erupts and melts a glacier, causing a lahar (volcanic mudslide) that buries Armero, Colombia, killing approximately 23,000 people. Nevado del Ruiz
  • 1982: Ray Mancini defeats Duk Koo Kim in a boxing match held in Las Vegas. Kim's subsequent death (on November 18) leads to significant changes in the sport. Ray Mancini
  • 1970: Bhola cyclone: A 240 km/h (150 mph) tropical cyclone hits the densely populated Ganges Delta region of East Pakistan (now Bangladesh), killing an estimated 500,000 people in one night. 1970 Bhola cyclone
  • 1969: Vietnam War: Anti-war protesters in Washington, D.C. stage a symbolic March Against Death. Vietnam War
  • 1966: In response to Fatah raids against Israelis near the West Bank border, Israel launches an attack on the village of As-Samu. Fatah
  • 1965: The SS Yarmouth Castle catches fire and sinks, killing 87. SS Yarmouth Castle
  • 1954: Great Britain defeats France to capture the first ever Rugby League World Cup in Paris in front of around 30,000 spectators. Great Britain national rugby league team
  • 1950: General Carlos Delgado Chalbaud, President of Venezuela, is assassinated in Caracas. Carlos Delgado Chalbaud
  • 1945: FC Dynamo Moscow open their exhibition tour of the United Kingdom with a 3–3 draw against Chelsea, breaking the record attendance for a British club football match. FC Dynamo Moscow
  • 1940: Walt Disney's animated musical film Fantasia is first released at New York's Broadway Theatre, on the first night of a roadshow. Walt Disney
  • 1927: The Holland Tunnel opens to traffic as the first Hudson River vehicle tunnel linking New Jersey to New York City. Holland Tunnel
  • 1918: World War I: Allied troops occupy Constantinople, the capital of the Ottoman Empire. Allies of World War I
  • 1916: World War I: Prime Minister of Australia Billy Hughes is expelled from the Labor Party over his support for conscription. World War I
  • 1901: The 1901 Caister lifeboat disaster occurs, killing 9 of the 12 crew members. 1901 Caister lifeboat disaster
  • 1893: 13 November stabbing is committed by Léon Léauthier during the Ère des attentats. This is an influential event in the birth of modern terrorism. 13 November 1893 stabbing
  • 1864: American Civil War: The three-day Battle of Bull's Gap ends in a Union rout as Confederates under Major General John C. Breckinridge pursue them to Strawberry Plains, Tennessee. American Civil War
  • 1841: James Braid first sees a demonstration of animal magnetism by Charles Lafontaine, which leads to his study of the subject he eventually calls hypnotism. James Braid (surgeon)
  • 1809: A British flotilla arrived at Ras Al Khaimah and launched an amphibious assault on the town, as a part of the Persian Gulf campaign of 1809. Ras Al Khaimah
  • 1775: American Revolutionary War: Patriot revolutionary forces under Gen. Richard Montgomery occupy Montreal. American Revolutionary War
  • 1642: First English Civil War: Battle of Turnham Green: The Royalist forces withdraw in the face of the Parliamentarian army and fail to take London. First English Civil War
  • 1093: Battle of Alnwick: in an English victory over the Scots, Malcolm III of Scotland and his son Edward are killed. Battle of Alnwick (1093)
