March 31: what happened on this day

What happened on 31 March: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2023: A tornado caused the collapse of a venue hosting a sold-out concert in Belvidere, Illinois, United States, resulting in one death and 48 injuries. Belvidere Apollo Theatre collapse
  • 2018: Nikol Pashinyan began his protest walk, starting in the city of Gyumri, opening the 2018 Armenian revolution. Gyumri
  • 1995: American singer-songwriter Selena, known as the "queen of Tejano music", was murdered by the president of her fan club, Yolanda Saldívar, in Corpus Christi, Texas, deeply affecting the Latino community. Selena
  • 1992: USS Missouri, the last active United States Navy battleship, was decommissioned in Long Beach, California. USS Missouri (BB-63)
  • 1964: The Brazilian Armed Forces overthrew President João Goulart, establishing a military dictatorship that lasted 21 years. Brazilian Armed Forces
  • 1942: Second World War: Because of a mutiny by Indian soldiers against their British officers, Japanese troops captured Christmas Island without any resistance. World War II
  • 1921: The Australian Air Force was formed, replacing the short-lived Australian Air Corps and separating it from the Army. Royal Australian Air Force
  • 1910: Six English towns amalgamated to form a single county borough called Stoke-on-Trent, the first union of its type. Federation of Stoke-on-Trent
  • 1899: Philippine–American War: Malolos, capital of the First Philippine Republic, was captured by American forces. Philippine–American War
  • 1800: After fighting through the night, the French Navy ship of the line Guillaume Tell surrendered to a British squadron in the Malta Channel. Action of 31 March 1800
  • 1521: Ferdinand Magellan and members of his crew participated in the first Catholic Mass in the Philippines. Ferdinand Magellan
  • 1174: A conspiracy against Saladin, aiming to restore the Fatimid Caliphate, was revealed in Cairo. Pro-Fatimid conspiracy against Saladin

More events

  • 1990: Approximately 200,000 protesters take to the streets of London to protest against the newly introduced Poll Tax. Poll tax riots

  • 1968: American President Lyndon B. Johnson speaks to the nation of "Steps to Limit the War in Vietnam" in a television address. At the conclusion of his speech, he announces: "I shall not seek, and I will not accept, the nomination of my party for another term as your President." President of the United States
  • 1966: The Labour Party under Harold Wilson wins the 1966 United Kingdom general election. Labour Party (UK)
  • 1959: The 14th Dalai Lama crosses the border into India and is granted political asylum. 14th Dalai Lama
  • 1957: Elections to the Territorial Assembly of the French colony Upper Volta are held. After the elections PDU and MDV form a government. 1957 Upper Voltan Territorial Assembly election
  • 1949: The Dominion of Newfoundland joins the Canadian Confederation and becomes the 10th Province of Canada. Dominion of Newfoundland
  • 1942: World War II: Japanese forces invade Christmas Island, then a British possession. World War II
  • 1933: The Civilian Conservation Corps is established with the mission of relieving rampant unemployment in the United States. Civilian Conservation Corps
  • 1931: An earthquake in Nicaragua destroys Managua; killing 2,000. 1931 Nicaragua earthquake
  • 1930: The Motion Picture Production Code is instituted, imposing strict guidelines on the treatment of sex, crime, religion and violence in film, in the U.S., for the next thirty-eight years. Hays Code
  • 1918: Massacre of ethnic Azerbaijanis is committed by allied armed groups of Armenian Revolutionary Federation and Bolsheviks. Nearly 12,000 Azerbaijani Muslims are killed. March Days
  • 1917: According to the terms of the Treaty of the Danish West Indies, the islands become American possessions. Treaty of the Danish West Indies
  • 1909: Serbia formally withdraws its opposition to Austro-Hungarian actions in the Bosnian Crisis. Bosnian Crisis
  • 1905: Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany declares his support for Moroccan independence in Tangier, beginning the First Moroccan Crisis. Wilhelm II
  • 1899: Philippine–American War: Malolos, capital of the First Philippine Republic, is captured by American forces. Philippine–American War
  • 1885: The United Kingdom establishes the Bechuanaland Protectorate. United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland
  • 1814: The Sixth Coalition occupies Paris after Napoleon's Grande Armée capitulates. War of the Sixth Coalition
  • 1761: The 1761 Lisbon earthquake strikes off the Iberian Peninsula with an estimated magnitude of 8.5, six years after another quake destroyed the city. 1761 Lisbon earthquake
  • 1706: The last session of history of the Catalan Courts, the parliament of the Principality of Catalonia, ends. Catalonia's constitutional modernisation passed by the Courts aims to improve the guarantee of individual, political and economic rights (such as the secrecy of correspondence). General Court of Catalonia
  • 1521: Ferdinand Magellan and fifty of his men came ashore to present-day Limasawa to participate in the first Catholic mass in the Philippines. Ferdinand Magellan
  • 1272: Pope Gregory X calls for a General Church Council to discuss reunion of Churches, Crusade to the Holy Land and Church reform. Pope Gregory X
  • 1146: Bernard of Clairvaux preaches his famous sermon in a field at Vézelay, urging the necessity of a Second Crusade. Louis VII is present, and joins the Crusade. Bernard of Clairvaux

About the date itself

March 31 is the 90th day of an ordinary year, with 275 days left after it (276 in a leap year, where it is the 91st day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Wednesday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Saturday, 2030: Sunday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 31 March 2027 in 2027 is 23 Shawwal 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 13.
