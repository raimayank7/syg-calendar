November 12: what happened on this day

What happened on 12 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2014: The European Space Agency's lander Philae touched down on 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko, becoming the first spacecraft to land on a comet. European Space Agency
  • 2011: An explosion in the Shahid Modarres missile base led to the deaths of 17 members of the Revolutionary Guards, including Hassan Tehrani Moghaddam, a key figure in Iran's missile program. Bid Kaneh explosion
  • 2001: American Airlines Flight 587 crashed into residential buildings shortly after takeoff from John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York, killing 265 people. American Airlines Flight 587
  • 1991: Indonesian forces opened fire on student demonstrators protesting the occupation of East Timor in the capital Dili, killing at least 250 people. Santa Cruz massacre
  • 1970: The deadliest tropical cyclone in history made landfall on the coast of East Pakistan (present-day Bangladesh), killing at least 250,000 people. 1970 Bhola cyclone
  • 1970: The Oregon Highway Division unsuccessfully attempted to destroy a rotting beached sperm whale near Florence, Oregon, with dynamite. Oregon Department of Transportation
  • 1944: Second World War: In Operation Catechism, the Royal Air Force sank the German battleship Tirpitz (video featured) near Tromsø, Norway, killing about 1,000 sailors on board. World War II
  • 1940: World War II: Free French forces captured Gabon from Vichy France. World War II
  • 1932: At the request of the Government of Western Australia, the Australian military officially resumed fighting the Emu War after their prior withdrawal. Government of Western Australia
  • 1928: The British ocean liner SS Vestris sank in the western Atlantic Ocean, killing 111 people. Ocean liner
  • 1912: Eight months after perishing during the ill-fated Terra Nova Expedition, the bodies of Robert Falcon Scott (pictured) and his companions were discovered on the Ross Ice Shelf in Antarctica. Terra Nova Expedition
  • 1892: William Heffelfinger was paid $500 by the Allegheny Athletic Association, becoming the first professional American football player. Pudge Heffelfinger

More events

  • 2022: A Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress and a Bell P-63 Kingcobra collide in mid-air over Dallas Executive Airport during an airshow, killing six. Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress
  • 2020: The PlayStation 5 is released. PlayStation 5
  • 2015: Two suicide bombers detonate explosives in Bourj el-Barajneh, Beirut, killing 43 people and injuring over 200 others. Suicide attack
  • 2014: An Armenian Mil Mi-24 attack helicopter is shot down by Azerbaijani forces, killing all three people on board. Mil Mi-24
  • 2011: A blast in Iran's Shahid Modarres missile base leads to the death of 17 of the Revolutionary Guards members, including Hassan Tehrani Moghaddam, a key figure in Iran's missile program. Bid Kaneh explosion
  • 2003: Shanghai Transrapid sets a new world speed record of 501 kilometres per hour (311 mph) for commercial railway systems, which remains the fastest for unmodified commercial rail vehicles. Shanghai maglev train
  • 2001: War in Afghanistan: Taliban forces abandon Kabul, ahead of advancing Afghan Northern Alliance troops. War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)
  • 1997: Ramzi Yousef is found guilty of masterminding the 1993 World Trade Center bombing. Ramzi Yousef
  • 1995: Erdut Agreement regarding the peaceful resolution to the Croatian War of Independence is reached. Erdut Agreement
  • 1991: Santa Cruz massacre: The Indonesian Army open fire on a crowd of student protesters in Dili, East Timor. Santa Cruz massacre
  • 1982: USSR: Yuri Andropov becomes the General Secretary of the Communist Party's Central Committee, succeeding Leonid I. Brezhnev. Soviet Union
  • 1980: The NASA space probe Voyager I makes its closest approach to Saturn and takes the first images of its rings. NASA
  • 1977: France conducts the Oreste nuclear test as 14th in the group of 29, 1975–78 French nuclear tests series. France
  • 1971: Vietnam War: As part of Vietnamization, U.S. President Richard Nixon sets February 1, 1972 as the deadline for the removal of another 45,000 American troops from Vietnam. Vietnamization
  • 1970: The Oregon Highway Division attempts to destroy a rotting beached sperm whale with explosives, leading to the now infamous "exploding whale" incident. Oregon Department of Transportation
  • 1969: Vietnam War: Independent investigative journalist Seymour Hersh breaks the story of the My Lai Massacre. Vietnam War
  • 1958: A team of rock climbers led by Warren Harding completes the first ascent of The Nose on El Capitan in Yosemite Valley. Warren Harding (climber)
  • 1956: In the midst of the Suez Crisis, Palestinian refugees are shot dead in Rafah by Israel Defense Force soldiers following the invasion of the Gaza Strip. Suez Crisis
  • 1948: Aftermath of World War II: In Tokyo, the International Military Tribunal for the Far East sentences seven Japanese military and government officials, including General Hideki Tojo, to death for their roles in World War II. Aftermath of World War II
  • 1942: World War II: Naval Battle of Guadalcanal between Japanese and American forces begins near Guadalcanal. The battle lasts for three days and ends with an American victory. Naval Battle of Guadalcanal
  • 1940: World War II: The Battle of Gabon ends as Free French Forces take Libreville, Gabon, and all of French Equatorial Africa from Vichy French forces. World War II
  • 1938: Nazi Germany issues the Decree on the Elimination of Jews from Economic Life prohibiting Jews from selling goods and services or working in a trade, totally segregating Jews from the German economy. Economy of Nazi Germany
  • 1933: Nazi Germany uses a referendum to ratify its withdrawal from the League of Nations. Nazi Germany
  • 1927: Leon Trotsky is expelled from the Soviet Communist Party, leaving Joseph Stalin in undisputed control of the Soviet Union. Leon Trotsky
  • 1920: Italy and the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes sign the Treaty of Rapallo. Kingdom of Yugoslavia
  • 1912: First Balkan War: King George I of Greece makes a triumphal entry into Thessaloniki after its liberation from 482 years of Ottoman rule. First Balkan War
  • 1905: Norway holds a referendum resulting in popular approval of the Storting's decision to authorise the government to make the offer of the throne of the newly independent country. 1905 Norwegian monarchy referendum
  • 1892: Pudge Heffelfinger becomes the first professional American football player on record, participating in his first paid game for the Allegheny Athletic Association. Pudge Heffelfinger
  • 1614: The treaty of Xanten ends the Jülich-Cleves Succession Crises, which nearly brought Europe to the brink of war, and divides the disputed lands between Brandenburg and Neuburg. War of the Jülich Succession
  • 1330: Battle of Posada ends: Wallachian Voievode Basarab I defeats the Hungarian army by ambush. Battle of Posada
