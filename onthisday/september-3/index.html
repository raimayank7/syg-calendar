September 3: what happened on this day

What happened on 3 September: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2017: North Korea conducted its sixth and most powerful nuclear test at Punggye-ri, causing a magnitude-6.3 earthquake. 2017 North Korean nuclear test
  • 2017: North Korea conducted its sixth and most powerful nuclear test at Punggye-ri, causing a magnitude-6.3 earthquake. 2017 North Korean nuclear test
  • 2001: The Troubles: Ulster loyalists resumed a picket outside a Catholic girls' primary school in the Protestant portion of Ardoyne, in Belfast, Northern Ireland. The Troubles
  • 1987: While he was abroad, Burundian president Jean-Baptiste Bagaza was deposed in a military coup d'état by Pierre Buyoya. Jean-Baptiste Bagaza
  • 1942: The Holocaust: In possibly the first Jewish ghetto uprising, residents of the Łachwa Ghetto in occupied Poland, informed of the upcoming "liquidation" of the ghetto, unsuccessfully fought against their Nazi captors. The Holocaust
  • 1941: The mass expulsion of Soviet Germans from the Volga German ASSR began. Deportation of Soviet Germans (1941–1942)
  • 1935: On the Bonneville Salt Flats in Utah, British racing motorist Malcolm Campbell (pictured) became the first person to drive an automobile over 300 mph (480 km/h). Bonneville Salt Flats
  • 1901: At the Royal Exhibition Building (pictured) in Melbourne, the flag of Australia flew for the first time. Royal Exhibition Building
  • 1878: The passenger steamship SS Princess Alice sank in the River Thames after colliding (pictured) with the collier Bywell Castle, killing more than 600 people. Sinking of SS Princess Alice
  • 1650: Under Oliver Cromwell, the English New Model Army ambushed a poorly prepared Scottish force at the Battle of Dunbar, the first battle of the Third English Civil War. Oliver Cromwell
  • 1411: The Treaty of Selymbria was concluded between the Republic of Venice and the Ottoman prince Musa Çelebi. Treaty of Selymbria
  • 863: Arab–Byzantine wars: The Byzantine Empire decisively defeated the Emirate of Melitene at the Battle of Lalakaon, beginning the era of Byzantine ascendancy. Arab–Byzantine wars

More events

  • 2017: North Korea conducts its sixth and most powerful nuclear test. North Korea
  • 2016: The U.S. and China, together responsible for 40% of the world's carbon emissions, both formally ratify the Paris global climate agreement. China
  • 2004: Beslan school siege ends, resulting in over 330 fatalities, including 186 children. Beslan school siege
  • 1997: Vietnam Airlines Flight 815 (Tupolev Tu-134) crashes on approach into Phnom Penh airport, killing 64. Vietnam Airlines
  • 1989: Varig Flight 254 crashes in the Amazon rainforest near São José do Xingu in Brazil, killing 12. Varig Flight 254
  • 1981: The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, an international bill of rights for women, is instituted by the United Nations. Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women
  • 1976: Viking program: The American Viking 2 spacecraft lands at Utopia Planitia on Mars. Viking program
  • 1967: Dagen H in Sweden: Traffic changes from driving on the left to driving on the right overnight. Dagen H
  • 1954: The People's Liberation Army begins shelling the Republic of China-controlled islands of Quemoy, starting the First Taiwan Strait Crisis. People's Liberation Army
  • 1945: A three-day celebration begins in China, following the Victory over Japan Day on September 2. Victory over Japan Day
  • 1943: World War II: British and Canadian troops land on the Italian mainland. On the same day, Walter Bedell Smith and Giuseppe Castellano sign the Armistice of Cassibile, although it is not announced for another five days. Operation Baytown
  • 1941: The Holocaust: Karl Fritzsch, deputy camp commandant of the Auschwitz concentration camp, experiments with the use of Zyklon B in the gassing of Soviet POWs. The Holocaust
  • 1939: World War II: The United Kingdom and France begin a naval blockade of Germany that lasts until the end of the war. This also marks the beginning of the Battle of the Atlantic. Blockade of Germany (1939–1945)
  • 1933: Yevgeniy Abalakov is the first man to reach the highest point in the Soviet Union, Communism Peak (now called Ismoil Somoni Peak and situated in Tajikistan) (7495 m). Yevgeniy Abalakov
  • 1916: World War I: Leefe Robinson destroys the German airship Schütte-Lanz SL 11 over Cuffley, north of London; this is the first German airship to be shot down on British soil. Leefe Robinson
  • 1914: French composer Albéric Magnard is killed defending his estate against invading German soldiers. Albéric Magnard
  • 1914: World War I: Start of the Battle of Grand Couronné, a German assault against French positions on high ground near the city of Nancy. World War I
  • 1895: John Brallier becomes the first openly paid professional American football player, when he was paid US$10 by David Berry, to play for the Latrobe Athletic Association in a 12–0 win over the Jeanette Athletic Association. John Brallier
  • 1878: Over 640 die when the crowded pleasure boat Princess Alice collides with the Bywell Castle in the River Thames. Sinking of SS Princess Alice
  • 1861: American Civil War: Confederate General Leonidas Polk invades neutral Kentucky, prompting the state legislature to ask for Union assistance. American Civil War
  • 1843: King Otto of Greece is forced to grant a constitution following an uprising in Athens. Otto of Greece
  • 1812: Twenty-four settlers are killed in the Pigeon Roost Massacre in Indiana. Pigeon Roost State Historic Site
  • 1783: American Revolutionary War: The war ends with the signing of the Treaty of Paris by the United States and the Kingdom of Great Britain. Treaty of Paris (1783)
  • 1777: American Revolutionary War: During the Battle of Cooch's Bridge, the Flag of the United States is flown in battle for the first time. American Revolutionary War
  • 1658: The death of Oliver Cromwell; Richard Cromwell becomes Lord Protector of England. Oliver Cromwell
  • 1650: Victory over the royalists in the Battle of Dunbar opens the way to Edinburgh for the New Model Army during the English invasion of Scotland. Battle of Dunbar (1650)
  • 1335: At the congress of Visegrád Charles I of Hungary mediates a reconciliation between two neighboring monarchs, John of Bohemia and Casimir III of Poland. Charles I of Hungary
  • 1189: Richard I of England (a.k.a. Richard "the Lionheart") is crowned at Westminster. Richard I of England
  • 673: King Wamba of the Visigoths puts down a revolt by Hilderic, governor of Nîmes (France) and rival for the throne. Wamba (king)
  • 301: San Marino, one of the smallest nations in the world and the world's oldest republic still in existence, is founded by Saint Marinus. San Marino

About the date itself
