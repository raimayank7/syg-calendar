February 11: what happened on this day

What happened on 11 February: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2018: Saratov Airlines Flight 703 crashed shortly after take-off in Russia due to icing conditions, killing all 71 people on board. Saratov Airlines Flight 703
  • 2015: Turkish student Özgecan Aslan was murdered during a rape attempt, sparking mass demonstrations across the country after her body was discovered two days later. Murder of Özgecan Aslan
  • 2008: Rebel East Timorese soldiers invaded the homes of President José Ramos-Horta and Prime Minister Xanana Gusmão, seriously wounding the former. 2008 East Timorese assassination attempts
  • 2001: The computer worm Anna Kournikova, which would affect millions of users worldwide, was released by a 20-year-old Dutch student. Computer worm
  • 1990: Anti-apartheid activist Nelson Mandela, having been a political prisoner for 27 years, was released from Victor Verster Prison near Paarl, South Africa. Nelson Mandela
  • 1976: The Frente de Liberación Homosexual made their final public appearance, shortly before the group's dissolution due to political repression after the Argentine coup d'état. Frente de Liberación Homosexual
  • 1938: The BBC aired an adaptation of a section of Czech writer Karel Čapek's play R.U.R. in the first broadcast of science fiction on television. BBC
  • 1919: Friedrich Ebert was elected the provisional president of Germany by the Weimar National Assembly. Friedrich Ebert
  • 1851: As part of celebrations marking the separation of Victoria from New South Wales, the inaugural first-class cricket match in Australia began at the Launceston Racecourse in Tasmania. Victoria (state)
  • 1840: La fille du régiment (audio featured), an opéra comique by Gaetano Donizetti, premiered in Paris to highly negative reviews but later became a success. La fille du régiment
  • 1826: London University, later University College London (pictured), was founded as the first secular university in England. University College London
  • 1823: Around 110 boys were killed in a human crush at the Convent of the Minori Osservanti (corridor pictured) in Valletta on the last day of the Maltese Carnival. Carnival tragedy of 1823

More events

  • 2024: 2024 Finnish presidential election: Alexander Stubb is elected as the 13th president of Finland. 2024 Finnish presidential election
  • 2020: COVID-19 pandemic: The World Health Organization officially names the coronavirus outbreak as COVID-19, with the virus being designated SARS-CoV-2. COVID-19 pandemic
  • 2017: North Korea test fires a ballistic missile across the Sea of Japan. North Korea
  • 2015: A university student is murdered as she resists an attempted rape in Turkey, sparking nationwide protests and public outcry against harassment and violence against women. Murder of Özgecan Aslan
  • 2014: A military transport plane crashes in a mountainous area of Oum El Bouaghi Province in eastern Algeria, killing 77 people. 2014 Algerian Air Force C-130 crash
  • 2013: Militants claiming to be from the Sultanate of Sulu invade Lahad Datu District, Sabah, Malaysia, beginning the Lahad Datu standoff. Sultanate of Sulu
  • 2008: Rebel East Timorese soldiers seriously wound President José Ramos-Horta. Rebel leader Alfredo Reinado is killed in the attack. Timor-Leste
  • 2000: Space Shuttle Endeavour is launched on STS-99 to conduct the Shuttle Radar Topography Mission. Space Shuttle Endeavour
  • 1997: Space Shuttle Discovery is launched on a mission to service the Hubble Space Telescope. Space Shuttle Discovery
  • 1990: Nelson Mandela is released from Victor Verster Prison outside Cape Town, South Africa after 27 years as a political prisoner. Nelson Mandela
  • 1979: The Iranian Revolution establishes an Islamic theocracy under the leadership of Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini. Iranian Revolution
  • 1971: Cold War: The Seabed Arms Control Treaty opens for signature, outlawing nuclear weapons on the ocean floor in international waters. Seabed Arms Control Treaty
  • 1963: The Beatles record their first album, Please Please Me. The Beatles
  • 1953: Cold War: U.S. President Dwight D. Eisenhower denies all appeals for clemency for Julius and Ethel Rosenberg. Cold War
  • 1946: The New Testament of the Revised Standard Version of the Bible, the first significant challenge to the Authorized King James Version, is published. New Testament
  • 1938: BBC Television produces the world's first ever science fiction television programme, an adaptation of a section of the Karel Čapek play R.U.R. that coined the term "robot". BBC One
  • 1937: The Flint sit-down strike ends when General Motors recognizes the United Auto Workers trade union. Flint sit-down strike
  • 1929: The Kingdom of Italy and the Vatican sign the Lateran Treaty. Kingdom of Italy
  • 1906: Pope Pius X publishes the encyclical Vehementer Nos. Pope Pius X
  • 1889: The Meiji Constitution of Japan is adopted. Meiji Constitution
  • 1861: American Civil War: The United States House of Representatives unanimously passes a resolution guaranteeing noninterference with slavery in any state. American Civil War
  • 1856: The Kingdom of Awadh is annexed by the British East India Company and Wajid Ali Shah, the king of Awadh, is deposed. Awadh
  • 1843: Giuseppe Verdi's opera I Lombardi alla prima crociata receives its first performance in Milan, Italy. Giuseppe Verdi
  • 1829: Facing backlach of Treaty of Turkmenchay, Alexander Griboyedov, Russian Empire's Ambassador to Tehran, and all the other embassy staff, were massacred by an angry mob. Treaty of Turkmenchay
  • 1823: Carnival tragedy of 1823: About 110 boys are killed during a human crush at the Convent of the Minori Osservanti in Valletta, Malta. Carnival tragedy of 1823
  • 1808: Jesse Fell burns anthracite on an open grate as an experiment in heating homes with coal. Jesse Fell
  • 1659: The assault on Copenhagen by Swedish forces is beaten back with heavy losses. Assault on Copenhagen (1659)
  • 1584: A naval expedition led by Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa founds Nombre de Jesús, the first of two short-lived Spanish settlements in the Strait of Magellan. Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa
  • 1144: Robert of Chester completes his translation from Arabic to Latin of the Liber de compositione alchemiae, marking the birth of Western alchemy. Robert of Chester
  • 55: The death under mysterious circumstances of Tiberius Claudius Caesar Britannicus, heir to the Roman Empire, on the eve of his coming of age clears the way for Nero to become Emperor. 50s

About the date itself

February 11 is the 42nd day of an ordinary year, with 323 days left after it (324 in a leap year, where it is the 42nd day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Thursday, 2028: Friday, 2029: Sunday, 2030: Monday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 11 February 2027 in 2027 is 4 Ramadan 1448 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 6.
