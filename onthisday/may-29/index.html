May 29: what happened on this day

What happened on 29 May: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 2020: An oil spill in Norilsk released 17,500 tons of diesel oil into nearby rivers. Norilsk oil spill
  • 2011: Residents of Portland, Oregon, held a rally called Hands Across Hawthorne in response to an attack against a gay couple holding hands while crossing the Hawthorne Bridge. Portland, Oregon
  • 1999: President Olusegun Obasanjo took office as Nigeria's first elected and civilian head of state after 16 years of military dictatorship. Olusegun Obasanjo
  • 1954: Diane Leather became the first woman to run a mile in less than five minutes. Diane Leather
  • 1942: Bing Crosby recorded his version of the song "White Christmas", which went on to become the best-selling single worldwide, with more than 50 million copies sold. Bing Crosby
  • 1935: A strike by copper miners in Northern Rhodesia ended after six workers were shot and killed by police. 1935 Copperbelt strike
  • 1913: During the premiere of the ballet Le Sacre du printemps by Igor Stravinsky at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées in Paris, the avant-garde nature of the music and choreography caused a near-riot in the audience (report pictured). The Rite of Spring
  • 1900: N'Djamena, now the capital of Chad, was founded as Fort-Lamy by French commander Émile Gentil. N'Djamena
  • 1852: Swedish operatic soprano Jenny Lind concluded a successful concert tour of the United States under the management of showman P. T. Barnum. Soprano
  • 1792: The Great Sejm of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth was disbanded following the Russian invasion of Poland. Great Sejm
  • 1453: With the fall of Constantinople, the Byzantine Empire fell to the Ottomans. Fall of Constantinople
  • 1233: Mongol–Jin War: The Mongols entered and began looting Kaifeng, the capital of the Jin dynasty of China, after a 13-month siege. Mongol conquest of the Jin dynasty

More events

  • 2022: Tara Air Flight 197 crashes in Nepal's Mustang District, killing 22. Tara Air Flight 197
  • 2020: An oil spill in Norilsk releases 17,500 tons of diesel oil into nearby rivers. Norilsk oil spill
  • 2012: A 5.8-magnitude earthquake hits northern Italy near Bologna, killing at least 24 people. 2012 Northern Italy earthquakes
  • 2005: France rejects the Constitution of the European Union in a national referendum. France
  • 2001: The U.S. Supreme Court rules that the disabled golfer Casey Martin can use a cart to ride in tournaments. Supreme Court of the United States
  • 1999: Space Shuttle Discovery completes the first docking with the International Space Station. Space Shuttle Discovery
  • 1989: Signing of an agreement between Egypt and the United States that allows for the manufacture of F-16 Falcon parts in Egypt. Egypt
  • 1985: Heysel Stadium disaster: Thirty-nine association football fans die and hundreds are injured when a dilapidated retaining wall collapses. Heysel Stadium disaster
  • 1982: Pope John Paul II becomes the first pontiff to visit Canterbury Cathedral. Pope John Paul II
  • 1974: SETA, a Finnish LGBT rights organisation, is founded in Helsinki. Seta (organization)
  • 1964: The Arab League meets in East Jerusalem to discuss the Palestinian question, leading to the formation of the Palestine Liberation Organization. Arab League
  • 1962: Chinese police open fire on protesters in Yining, Xinjiang, killing at least five people and wounding a dozen others. Yi–Ta incident
  • 1948: United Nations Truce Supervision Organization is founded. United Nations Truce Supervision Organization
  • 1945: First combat mission of the Consolidated B-32 Dominator heavy bomber. Combat
  • 1932: World War I veterans begin to assemble in Washington, D.C., in the Bonus Army to request cash bonuses promised to them to be paid in 1945. World War I
  • 1920: The Louth flood of 1920, a severe flash flood in the Lincolnshire market town of Louth, results in 23 fatalities in 20 minutes. It has been described as one of the most significant flood disasters in the United Kingdom during the 20th century. 1920 Louth flood
  • 1918: Armenia defeats the Ottoman Army in the Battle of Sardarabad. Armenia
  • 1913: Igor Stravinsky's ballet score The Rite of Spring receives its premiere performance in Paris, France, provoking a near-riot. Igor Stravinsky
  • 1886: The pharmacist John Pemberton places his first advertisement for Coca-Cola, which appeared in The Atlanta Journal. Pharmacist
  • 1864: Emperor Maximilian I of Mexico arrives in Mexico for the first time. Emperor of Mexico
  • 1852: Jenny Lind leaves New York after her two-year American tour. Jenny Lind
  • 1825: The Coronation of Charles X of France takes place in Reims Cathedral, the last-ever coronation of a French monarch. Coronation of Charles X of France
  • 1798: United Irishmen Rebellion: Between 300 and 500 United Irishmen are executed as rebels by the British Army in County Kildare, Ireland. Irish Rebellion of 1798
  • 1780: American Revolutionary War: At the Waxhaws Massacre, the British continue attacking after the Continentals lay down their arms, killing 113 and critically wounding all but 53 that remained. American Revolutionary War
  • 1658: Battle of Samugarh: Decisive battle in the struggle for the throne during the Mughal war of succession (1658–1659). Battle of Samugarh
  • 1453: Fall of Constantinople: Ottoman armies under Sultan Mehmed II capture Constantinople after a 53-day siege, ending the Roman Empire after over 2,000 years. Fall of Constantinople
  • 1328: Philip VI is crowned King of France. Philip VI of France
  • 1176: Battle of Legnano: The Lombard League defeats Emperor Frederick I. Battle of Legnano
  • 1138: Antipope Victor IV gives up his claim to the papacy due to the instigation of Bernard of Clairvaux. Antipope Victor IV (1138)
  • 757: Election of pope Paul I following the death of pope Stephen II in the previous month. AD 757

About the date itself
