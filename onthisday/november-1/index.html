November 1: what happened on this day

What happened on 1 November: 42 events, 24 births and 20 deaths from history and 15 observances, with the weekday and day of the year.

Selected events

  • 1998: Karl Bushby set off from Punta Arenas, Chile, to walk around the world towards England, completing the journey in 2026. Karl Bushby
  • 1981: The Associated State of Antigua gained independence from the United Kingdom as Antigua and Barbuda. Associated State of Antigua
  • 1972: Elvis on Tour, a concert film that documented Elvis Presley's tour throughout the United States, opened. Elvis on Tour
  • 1963: Lê Quang Tung, loyalist head of the South Vietnam Special Forces, was executed in a U.S.-backed coup against president Ngô Đình Diệm following a period of religious unrest. Lê Quang Tung
  • 1959: Dominique Mbonyumutwa, one of the few Hutu sub-chiefs in colonial Rwanda, was attacked by Tutsi activists, precipitating the Rwandan Revolution. Dominique Mbonyumutwa
  • 1959: After being struck in the face with a hockey puck, Jacques Plante played the rest of the game wearing a face mask (pictured), now mandatory equipment for goaltenders in ice hockey. Hockey puck
  • 1944: World War II: An American F-13 Superfortress made the first flight by an Allied aircraft over Tokyo since the Doolittle Raid in April 1942. World War II
  • 1941: American photographer Ansel Adams (pictured) shot Moonrise, Hernandez, New Mexico, one of his most famous photographs. Ansel Adams
  • 1921: Frances Kyle and Averil Deverell were called to the Bar of Ireland, becoming the first female barristers in Ireland or Great Britain. Frances Kyle
  • 1914: World War I: The first contingent of the First Australian Imperial Force departed Albany, Western Australia. World War I
  • 1824: The disposable ship Columbus (pictured) arrived in the The Downs off England, becoming, at that time, the largest vessel to have crossed the Atlantic Ocean. Disposable ship
  • 1214: Byzantine–Seljuk wars: Seljuq Turks captured the important port city of Sinope. Byzantine–Seljuk wars

More events

  • 2026: Andrea Kimi Antonelli becomes the youngest ever Formula One World Champion, as well as the first Italian champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953, securing the title at the Mexican Grand Prix. Kimi Antonelli
  • 2024: A concrete canopy collapses at the Novi Sad railway station, killing 16 people and injuring three. Novi Sad railway station canopy collapse
  • 2009: An Ilyushin Il-76 crashes near the Mir mine after takeoff from Mirny Airport in Yakutia, killing all 11 aboard. Ilyushin Il-76
  • 2000: Chhattisgarh officially becomes the 26th state of India, formed from sixteen districts of eastern Madhya Pradesh. Chhattisgarh
  • 1993: The Maastricht Treaty takes effect, formally establishing the European Union. Maastricht Treaty
  • 1984: After the assassination of Indira Gandhi, Prime Minister of India on 31 October 1984, by two of her Sikh bodyguards, anti-Sikh riots erupt. Assassination of Indira Gandhi
  • 1979: In Bolivia, Colonel Alberto Natusch executes a bloody coup d'état against the constitutional government of Wálter Guevara. Alberto Natusch
  • 1973: Watergate scandal: Leon Jaworski is appointed as the new Watergate Special Prosecutor. Watergate scandal
  • 1970: Club Cinq-Sept fire in Saint-Laurent-du-Pont, France kills 146 young people. Club Cinq-Sept fire
  • 1963: The 1963 South Vietnamese coup begins. 1963 South Vietnamese coup d'état
  • 1956: Hungarian Revolution: Imre Nagy announces Hungary's neutrality and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact. Soviet troops begin to re-enter Hungary, contrary to assurances by the Soviet government. János Kádár and Ferenc Münnich secretly defect to the Soviets. Hungarian Revolution of 1956
  • 1955: The bombing of United Air Lines Flight 629 occurs near Longmont, Colorado, killing all 39 passengers and five crew members aboard the Douglas DC-6B airliner. United Air Lines Flight 629
  • 1951: Operation Buster–Jangle: Six thousand five hundred United States Army soldiers are exposed to 'Desert Rock' atomic explosions for training purposes in Nevada. Participation is not voluntary. Operation Buster–Jangle
  • 1948: Athenagoras I, Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople, is enthroned. Athenagoras I of Constantinople
  • 1943: World War II: The 3rd Marine Division, United States Marines, landing on Bougainville in the Solomon Islands, secures a beachhead, leading that night to a naval clash at the Battle of Empress Augusta Bay. 3rd Marine Division
  • 1938: Seabiscuit defeats War Admiral in an upset victory during a match race deemed "the match of the century" in horse racing. Seabiscuit
  • 1928: The Law on the Adoption and Implementation of the Turkish Alphabet, replaces the Arabic alphabet with the Latin alphabet. Turkish alphabet
  • 1918: World War I: With a brave action carried out into the waters of the Austro-Hungarian port of Pula, two officers of the Italian Regia Marina sink with a manned torpedo the enemy battleship SMS Viribus Unitis. Pula
  • 1916: In Russia, Pavel Milyukov delivers in the State Duma the famous "stupidity or treason" speech, precipitating the downfall of the government of Boris Stürmer. Pavel Milyukov
  • 1911: World's first combat aerial bombing mission takes place in Libya during the Italo-Turkish War. Second Lieutenant Giulio Gavotti of Italy drops several small bombs. Italo-Turkish War
  • 1897: Italian Sport-Club Juventus is founded by a group of students of Liceo Classico Massimo d'Azeglio. Juventus FC
  • 1894: Buffalo Bill, 15 of his Native Americans, and Annie Oakley were filmed by Thomas Edison in his Black Maria Studio in West Orange, New Jersey. Buffalo Bill
  • 1861: American Civil War: U.S. President Abraham Lincoln appoints George B. McClellan as the commander of the Union Army, replacing General Winfield Scott. American Civil War
  • 1848: In Boston, Massachusetts, the first medical school for women, Boston Female Medical School (which later merged with the Boston University School of Medicine), opens. Boston
  • 1800: John Adams becomes the first President of the United States to live in the Executive Mansion (later renamed the White House). John Adams
  • 1755: In Portugal, Lisbon is totally devastated by a massive earthquake and tsunami, killing an estimated 40,000 to 60,000 people. Lisbon
  • 1570: The All Saints' Flood devastates the Dutch coast. All Saints' Flood (1570)
  • 1512: The ceiling of the Sistine Chapel, painted by Michelangelo, is exhibited to the public for the first time. Sistine Chapel
  • 1348: The anti-royalist Union of Valencia attacks the Jews of Murviedro on the pretext that they are serfs of the King of Valencia and thus "royalists". Union of Valencia
  • 1141: Empress Matilda's reign as "Lady of the English" ends as Stephen of Blois regains the title of "King of England". Empress Matilda

About the date itself

November 1 is the 305th day of an ordinary year, with 60 days left after it (61 in a leap year, where it is the 306th day). It falls on these weekdays: 2027: Monday, 2028: Wednesday, 2029: Thursday, 2030: Friday. In the Hijri (Islamic) calendar, 1 November 2027 in 2027 is 2 Jumada al-thani 1449 AH, by the Umm al-Qura table. ISO week number in 2027: 44.
