Why Nowruz is on 20 or 21 March

Nowruz is fixed to the astronomical equinox rather than to a date. The equinox falls on 19, 20 or 21 March depending on the year, and the Iranian rule moves the new year to the next day when the equinox is after noon in Tehran. In the table, Nowruz falls on 20 March in 9 of the 21 years and on 21 March in 12.

The days of Nowruz

The festival is traditionally kept for thirteen days. In the days before it families clean the house (khaneh-tekani), buy new clothes and set the Haft-sin table; on the last Wednesday evening of the old year people jump over small fires (Chaharshanbe Suri). At the moment of the equinox the family gathers, and the first days are spent visiting elders and relatives (didobazdid) and giving small gifts of money to children (eidi). On the thirteenth day, Sizdah Bedar, families picnic outdoors. These are general descriptions; customs differ by region, family and country.

Where Nowruz is a holiday

It is a public holiday in Iran and Afghanistan and several Central Asian and Caucasian countries, and the United Nations recognises 21 March as the International Day of Nowruz. Official holiday dates are set by each government and can differ by a day from the dates here.
