The Persian year

The Persian calendar is a solar calendar: its year is the time between two spring equinoxes, about 365.24 days, and it has no leap-month; the last month gets an extra day in leap years. The first six months have 31 days, the next five 30, and the last month 29 or 30. Years are counted from the Hijra of the Prophet Muhammad in 622 CE, but in solar years, which is why the Persian year number is the Gregorian year minus 621 from Nowruz to the end of December, and minus 622 from January to Nowruz.

Seasons

The months follow the seasons: Farvardin, Ordibehesht and Khordad are spring; Tir, Mordad and Shahrivar are summer; Mehr, Aban and Azar are autumn; Dey, Bahman and Esfand are winter. The long months of 31 days are the first six, spring and summer, when the sun moves most slowly along the ecliptic, as seen from the northern hemisphere; the 30-day months fall in autumn and winter, and Esfand is the shortest. Because the new year follows the equinox, the dates of the months are almost fixed in the Gregorian calendar: the first of Tir is 22 June, the first of Mehr is 23 September, the first of Dey is 22 December in most years.
