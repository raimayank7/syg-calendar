Reading the table

For a solar eclipse a city is counted if any part of the sun is covered while the sun is above the horizon; for a lunar eclipse a city is counted if the moon is above the horizon at the greatest eclipse. A city may still miss the start or the end of a lunar eclipse at moonrise or moonset, and cloud decides everything.

Sutak

In Hindu tradition, the period called sutak before an eclipse is observed in places where the eclipse is visible. Panchangs commonly give it as about 12 hours before a solar eclipse and about 9 hours before a lunar eclipse, ending when the eclipse ends. Customs and exact times vary between communities and panchangs, so follow your own temple or panchang. This site does not give sutak times.
