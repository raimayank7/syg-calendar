About this eclipse

The partial lunar eclipse of Saturday, 15 June 2030 reaches its greatest at 18:33 UTC (00:03 IST). In a partial eclipse the moon covers only part of the sun, so nobody on Earth sees a complete eclipse. The partial phase lasts 02h24m.

Its magnitude is 0.502. For a solar eclipse that is the fraction of the sun's width covered at the greatest point on Earth, and for a lunar eclipse the fraction of the moon's width inside the Earth's dark shadow. It belongs to Saros series 140.

From India

Indian cities where the moon is up at the greatest eclipse: Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Surat, Pune, Jaipur, Kanpur, Navi Mumbai. Local sunrise, sunset and cloud decide what you actually see; times in the table are of the local maximum.

Watching

A lunar eclipse is safe to look at with the naked eye, binoculars or a telescope. The moon turns darker and often coppery red in a total eclipse. Pick a dark place and give your eyes a few minutes to adjust.
