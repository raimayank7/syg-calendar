What is in this list

There are 35 lunar eclipses from 2026 to 2040: 13 total, 13 penumbral, 9 partial. A lunar eclipse can be seen from the whole night side of the Earth, so more people see each one than a solar eclipse.

See the guide to how eclipses work.

By type

  • Total: 13, the next on Sunday, 31 December 2028. In a total solar eclipse the moon covers the whole sun for a few minutes along a narrow path; outside the path people see a partial eclipse.
  • Penumbral: 13, the next on Saturday, 20 February 2027. In a penumbral lunar eclipse the moon passes only through the faint outer shadow of the Earth. The dimming is slight and hard to see with the eye.
  • Partial: 9, the next on Wednesday, 12 January 2028. In a partial eclipse the moon covers only part of the sun, so nobody on Earth sees a complete eclipse.

How often

Over these 15 years there are 35 lunar eclipses, an average of 2.3 a year, and the most in one year is 4 (2038). Eclipses come in seasons about six months apart, because the orbit of the moon is tilted and crosses the line to the sun only twice a year.
