About this eclipse

The annular solar eclipse of Saturday, 1 June 2030 reaches its greatest at 06:27 UTC (11:57 IST). In an annular solar eclipse the moon is too far from Earth to cover the sun completely, so a bright ring of sun ("ring of fire") stays visible along a narrow path; elsewhere it is a partial eclipse. The longest duration of the total or annular phase on the central line is 05m21s.

Its magnitude is 0.944. For a solar eclipse that is the fraction of the sun's width covered at the greatest point on Earth, and for a lunar eclipse the fraction of the moon's width inside the Earth's dark shadow. It belongs to Saros series 128.

From India

Indian cities with a partial eclipse: Srinagar (38%), Amritsar (31%), Ludhiana (29%), Meerut (22%), Delhi (21%), Najafgarh (21%), Ghaziabad (21%), Faridabad (21%), Jodhpur (17%), Jaipur (17%). Local sunrise, sunset and cloud decide what you actually see; times in the table are of the local maximum.

Safety

Never look at the sun without proper eclipse glasses (solar filters made to the ISO 12312-2 standard) or a solar filter on the front of a telescope or camera, even for a partial eclipse; sunglasses are not enough. Read the safety guide.
