About this eclipse

The total solar eclipse of Monday, 20 March 2034 reaches its greatest at 10:17 UTC (15:47 IST). In a total solar eclipse the moon covers the whole sun for a few minutes along a narrow path; outside the path people see a partial eclipse. The longest duration of the total or annular phase on the central line is 04m09s.

Its magnitude is 1.046. For a solar eclipse that is the fraction of the sun's width covered at the greatest point on Earth, and for a lunar eclipse the fraction of the moon's width inside the Earth's dark shadow. It belongs to Saros series 130.

Indian cities with a partial eclipse: Srinagar (101%), Amritsar (94%), Ludhiana (92%), Meerut (85%), Delhi (84%), Ghaziabad (84%), Najafgarh (84%), Faridabad (83%), Agra (79%), Jaipur (79%). Local sunrise, sunset and cloud decide what you actually see; times in the table are of the local maximum.

Safety

Never look at the sun without proper eclipse glasses (solar filters made to the ISO 12312-2 standard) or a solar filter on the front of a telescope or camera, even for a partial eclipse; sunglasses are not enough. Read the safety guide.
