How solar and lunar eclipses work

Why eclipses happen, the difference between total, annular, partial and penumbral, and why there are only a few each year.

A solar eclipse happens at a new moon, when the moon passes between the Earth and the sun and its shadow falls on part of the Earth. A lunar eclipse happens at a full moon, when the Earth is between the sun and the moon and the moon moves through the Earth's shadow. We do not have an eclipse every month because the moon's orbit is tilted by about 5 degrees to the Earth's: most months the moon passes above or below the line between the sun and the Earth.

Types

  • Total solar eclipse: the moon covers the sun completely along a path usually less than 250 km wide; the day goes dark for up to a few minutes.
  • Annular solar eclipse: the moon is a little too small in the sky to cover the sun and leaves a ring.
  • Hybrid: annular on part of the path and total on another.
  • Partial solar eclipse: the moon covers only part of the sun. Everyone outside the narrow central path sees a partial eclipse, so there are many more people who see a partial one.
  • Total lunar eclipse: the moon is entirely inside the Earth's dark shadow and often looks red or copper because sunlight bent through the Earth's atmosphere still reaches it.
  • Partial lunar eclipse: part of the moon is in the dark shadow.
  • Penumbral lunar eclipse: only the faint outer shadow; the moon is slightly dimmer, which is hard to notice.

Magnitude

The magnitude is the fraction of the sun's diameter (solar) or the moon's diameter (lunar, "umbral magnitude") that is covered by the shadow at the greatest eclipse. A value above 1 means a total eclipse. Penumbral lunar eclipses have a negative umbral magnitude because the moon does not enter the dark shadow.

Saros series

Eclipses repeat in cycles of about 18 years and 11 days called the Saros. An eclipse and the one that comes a Saros later are similar in type and place, shifted about 120 degrees west. The tables give each eclipse's Saros number so you can find its predecessor.
