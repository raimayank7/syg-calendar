Days of the week in Hindi, Punjabi and English

The seven days in three languages, with the heavenly body each one is named after.

The seven days

The ending vaar (वार, ਵਾਰ) means "day". So Somvaar is "Moon's day", exactly as Monday is.

Other names you will hear

  • Sunday: इतवार (Itvaar) in everyday Hindi, and आदित्यवार (Adityavaar) in formal use. Punjabi ਐਤਵਾਰ comes from the same word.
  • Thursday: बृहस्पतिवार (Brihaspativaar), after Brihaspati, the teacher of the gods and the name of the planet Jupiter. In Hindi गुरुवार and बृहस्पतिवार are both common, and वीरवार (Veervaar) is heard in the north-west as in Punjabi. In Urdu Thursday is जुमेरात (Jumeraat).
  • Friday: जुमा (Juma) in Urdu and in everyday speech in many places.
  • Saturday: शनिश्चर (Shanichar) in some regions; in Punjabi also ਛਨਿੱਛਰਵਾਰ.
  • Wednesday: बुध (Budh) alone is often enough: "बुध को आना", come on Wednesday.

Why the names match across languages

Compare the columns and a pattern appears. English Sunday and Monday are the Sun's and Moon's days. In French, Spanish and Italian, Tuesday to Friday are named after Mars, Mercury, Jupiter and Venus (mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi in French). The English names for those days use the Germanic gods who were matched with the same Roman ones: Tiw, Woden, Thor and Frigg. Saturday is Saturn's day in English as in Hindi.

So from India to Europe, each day belongs to the same heavenly body in the same order: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn. These are the seven "wanderers" that people could see moving against the fixed stars before telescopes. The seven-day week named after them spread across the ancient world in the early centuries of the common era and reached India and Rome alike through astronomy and astrology.

Why that order?

The order is not the order of the planets in the sky, and it puzzled people for a long time. The usual explanation comes from ancient astrology. The seven bodies were ranked by how slowly they seemed to move: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon. Each hour of the day was assigned to one of them in turn, and each day was named after the ruler of its first hour. With 24 hours in a day and 7 rulers, the first hour of the next day falls three places further along the list every time. Start with Saturn and step three places again and again, and you get Saturn, Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus: the week.

Using the names

  • Asking the day: आज कौन सा दिन है? (Aaj kaun sa din hai?) In Punjabi: ਅੱਜ ਕਿਹੜਾ ਦਿਨ ਹੈ? (Ajj kihra din hai?)
  • Answering: आज सोमवार है। (Aaj Somvaar hai.) ਅੱਜ ਸੋਮਵਾਰ ਹੈ। (Ajj Somvaar hai.)
  • "On Tuesday": मंगलवार को (Mangalvaar ko), ਮੰਗਲਵਾਰ ਨੂੰ (Mangalvaar nu).
  • Weekly off: साप्ताहिक अवकाश (saptahik avkash) or simply छुट्टी (chhutti), ਛੁੱਟੀ.

Many families keep a weekly fast or avoid certain foods on the day of a deity they honour, such as Monday, Tuesday, Thursday or Saturday. Customs differ widely from home to home.

What is Thursday called in Hindi?

गुरुवार (Guruvaar) or बृहस्पतिवार (Brihaspativaar). In Punjabi it is ਵੀਰਵਾਰ (Veervaar).

What is Sunday called in Hindi?

रविवार (Ravivaar). In everyday speech many people say इतवार (Itvaar).

Which is the first day of the week?

Calendars in India usually begin the week on Sunday. The international standard ISO 8601 begins it on Monday.
