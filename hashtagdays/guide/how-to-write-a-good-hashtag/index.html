How to Choose and Write a Good Hashtag

Short rules for hashtags: capital letters, length, checking meanings, how many to use and where to look for the official one.

The rules in short

  • Capitalise each word (#WorldWaterDay, not #worldwaterday). It reads better and screen readers can read it aloud properly.
  • Keep it short: under 25 characters when you can.
  • No spaces or punctuation: they end the tag.
  • Three to five per post is enough.

Check before you use

Search the tag on the platform first. A tag you think is plain may be used for something else, or may have a second meaning when words run together. Read the first twenty posts that show up.

Official tags

The organisation behind a day often promotes its own tag and may ask people to use it. Look on the UN or agency page for the day, or at the account of the lead organisation, and use it if one is given. The tags suggested on this site are made from the name of the day and are not official.

Local tags

Add one tag for your own town or group (#LudhianaCleanUp). A local tag makes it easy for your community to find each other, even when the main tag has millions of posts.
