Zero Discrimination Day: date, background and post ideas

Zero Discrimination Day is on 1 March every year. It is led by UNAIDS; below are the background, hashtags and 5 post ideas.

What it is

Zero Discrimination Day is an annual day celebrated on 1 March each year by the United Nations (UN) and other international organizations. The day aims to promote equality before the law and is practiced throughout all of the member countries of the UN. It was first celebrated on March 1, 2014, and was launched by UNAIDS' executive director Michel Sidibé on 27 February that year with a major event in Beijing.

In February 2017, UNAIDS called on people to "make some noise around zero discrimination, to speak up and prevent discrimination from standing in the way of achieving ambitions, goals and dreams".

The day is particularly noted by organizations like UNAIDS that combat discrimination against people living with HIV/AIDS. "HIV related stigma and discrimination is pervasive and exists in almost every part of the world including our Liberia," according to Dr. Ivan F. Camanor, chairman of the National AIDS Commission of Liberia. The UN Development Programme also paid tribute in 2017 to LGBTI people with HIV/AIDS who face discrimination.

Campaigners in India have used this day to speak out against laws making discrimination against the LGBTI community more likely, especially during the previous campaign to repeal the law (Indian Penal Code, s377) that used to criminalize homosexuality in that country, before that law was overturned by the Indian Supreme Court in September 2018.

In 2015, Armenian Americans in California held a "die-in" on Zero Discrimination Day to remember the victims of the Armenian genocide.

The theme for 2026 is "People First".

Who set it up

The United Nations list names no resolution as the basis and the agency UNAIDS as the body that leads it.

Hashtag suggestions

  • #ZeroDiscriminationDay
  • #Discrimination

Mix one tag for the day with one or two for your own topic or town. Keep to three to five tags; long strings of tags look like spam.

5 ways to post about it

  1. Share the plain-language text of one right named by Zero Discrimination Day, from the United Nations or your national constitution, with a link.
  2. Tell a short story of a person or group helped by this work, with their agreement, instead of a general slogan.
  3. List where to get help locally: a helpline, legal aid centre or commission. Check each number is current before you post it.
  4. Thank a person who works on Zero Discrimination Day: a lawyer, a social worker, a journalist. Name them only with consent.
  5. Ask a question that invites listening: what would make your neighbourhood fairer? Moderate the replies and remove abuse.

These ideas are for the topic group "Human rights, law and justice".

Take care

These days concern people who have been harmed. Use respectful, factual words; avoid graphic images; do not name victims without permission; and be ready to moderate comments. Do not give legal advice, but point to an official or legal-aid source.

Timing

In 2027 the day falls on a Monday. Schedule your post for the morning of the day itself, and make a short teaser three days before and a thank-you the day after. If the day falls on a weekend, prepare and schedule the posts in advance.
