International Migrants Day: date, background and post ideas

International Migrants Day is on 18 December every year. It is on the United Nations list; below are the background, hashtags and 5 post ideas.

What it is

On 18 December 1990, the General Assembly of the United Nations adopted a resolution on the International Convention on the Protection of the Rights of All Migrant Workers and Members of Their Families.

Each year on 18 December, the United Nations, through the UN-related agency International Organization for Migration (IOM), uses International Migrants Day to highlight the contributions made by the roughly 272 million migrants, including more than 41 million internally displaced persons, and the challenges they face.

This global event, supported by events organized by IOM's nearly 500 country offices and sub-offices as well as governmental, international and domestic civil society partners examines a wide range of migration themes, Social Cohesion, Dignity, Exploitation, Solidarity to advocate for migration guided by the principle that humane and orderly migration benefits migrants and society.

Who set it up

It was established by resolution A/RES/55/93 of the United Nations General Assembly, adopted in its 55th session (September 2000 to September 2001).

Hashtag suggestions

  • #InternationalMigrantsDay
  • #MigrantsDay
  • #Migrants

Mix one tag for the day with one or two for your own topic or town. Keep to three to five tags; long strings of tags look like spam.

5 ways to post about it

  1. Share the plain-language text of one right named by International Migrants Day, from the United Nations or your national constitution, with a link.
  2. Tell a short story of a person or group helped by this work, with their agreement, instead of a general slogan.
  3. List where to get help locally: a helpline, legal aid centre or commission. Check each number is current before you post it.
  4. Thank a person who works on International Migrants Day: a lawyer, a social worker, a journalist. Name them only with consent.
  5. Ask a question that invites listening: what would make your neighbourhood fairer? Moderate the replies and remove abuse.

These ideas are for the topic group "Human rights, law and justice".

Take care

These days concern people who have been harmed. Use respectful, factual words; avoid graphic images; do not name victims without permission; and be ready to moderate comments. Do not give legal advice, but point to an official or legal-aid source.

Timing

In 2027 the day falls on a Saturday, so a weekend: schedule it. Schedule your post for the morning of the day itself, and make a short teaser three days before and a thank-you the day after. If the day falls on a weekend, prepare and schedule the posts in advance.
