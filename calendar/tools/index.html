Date tools

Calculators for dates and printable calendars. They run in your browser and store nothing.

  • Days between dates Choose two dates
  • Add days to a date Choose a starting date, then add or subtract days, weeks, months, years or working days
  • Age calculator Choose the date of birth
  • Day of the week Choose any date to see which day of the week it falls on, with its day number, week number and Saka date
  • Week number Find the week number of any date, or the dates of any numbered week, by the international (ISO 8601) rule
  • Countdown Tap an occasion or choose your own date to see how many days are left, or how many have passed
  • Working days Count the working days between two dates, for a five-day or a six-day week, with or without public holidays
  • Leap year Type a year to see whether it is a leap year, and which part of the rule decides it
  • Saka date Convert a common (Gregorian) date to the Indian national calendar, or a Saka date back to the common calendar
  • Reuse a calendar Type a year to see which earlier and later years share its calendar, weekday for weekday
  • Print a month Choose a month and year, set the options and print
  • Print a year Type a year and print all twelve months on one sheet

Choosing the right tool

Most date questions are one of five kinds.

  • "How long between these two dates?" Use days between two dates. For office time, working days leaves out weekends and, for 2027, the gazetted holidays. For a person's age, the age calculator counts completed years, months and days.
  • "What date is so many days from now?" Use add or subtract days, which also steps in weeks, months, years and working days.
  • "How long until it comes?" The countdown counts to any date and can be shared on WhatsApp.
  • "What kind of day or year is it?" The day of the week page gives the weekday of any date, the week number page the ISO week, the leap year checker the length of a year, and the Saka date converter the date in the Indian national calendar.
  • "I need a calendar on paper." The printable month and printable year pages make a clean sheet for any year, and same calendar years tells you when an old calendar can be used again.

How the tools count

Every tool states its counting rule in plain words on its own page, because small differences in rules are the usual reason two people get different answers. The main ones:

  • The first day is not counted and the last day is, unless you tick the box to count both (days between dates) or the page says both are counted (working days).
  • Months are calendar months. Adding one month to the 31st stops at the last day of a shorter month.
  • Weeks start on Monday for week numbers, following ISO 8601. The printable calendars let you choose Sunday or Monday.
  • Today is the date on your device.

All arithmetic is done in whole days, free of clock time and time zones, so daylight saving changes cannot shift an answer. The code is tested against thousands of dates checked by an independent calendar library.

Privacy

The tools run in your browser. Dates you type are not sent to us and are not stored. There is no login.

Are these calculators accurate for past and future years?

Yes, for any year from 1 to 9999 under today's calendar rules. Before the calendar change of 1582 (1752 in Britain and its colonies) people used the older Julian calendar, and the pages say so where it matters.

Do the tools work without the internet?

Once a page has loaded, its calculator works offline, because all the arithmetic is done on your device.
