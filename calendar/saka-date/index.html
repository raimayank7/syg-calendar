Saka date today: Indian national calendar converter

Convert a common (Gregorian) date to the Indian national calendar, or a Saka date back to the common calendar.

What the Saka calendar is

The Indian national calendar is the official civil calendar of India, used alongside the common (Gregorian) calendar since 22 March 1957. Its years are counted in the Saka era, which runs 78 years behind the common era for most of the year: 2027 CE is partly Saka 1948 and partly Saka 1949.

You meet it in the Gazette of India, in news broadcasts of All India Radio, in calendars issued by the Government of India and in government orders, where the Saka date is printed beside the common date. The official holiday list for 2027 gives each holiday both ways; for example Republic Day, 26 January 2027, is 6 Magha 1948.

The twelve months

Unlike the traditional lunar calendars, the national calendar is a solar calendar with months of fixed length, so it converts exactly to the common calendar.

The year begins on the day after the spring equinox. The five months of the northern summer half of the year, when the Sun appears to move more slowly, are the long ones.

Converting by hand

To find the Saka year: subtract 78 from the common year for dates from 22 March onward (21 March in a leap year), and 79 for dates before it.

To find the month and day: find the month in the table whose start date is on or just before your date, and count from it. For 15 August: Sravana begins on 23 July, so 23 July is Sravana 1 and 15 August is Sravana 24. Independence Day 2027 is therefore 24 Sravana 1949, which is exactly what the government's list prints.

Leap years: a Saka year is a leap year when the common year in which it begins is one. Add 78 to the Saka year and apply the usual rule. In a leap year Chaitra starts a day early, on 21 March, and has 31 days, so every later month still begins on its usual date.

How this differs from a panchang

The national calendar should not be confused with the traditional Hindu calendars (panchang) by which festivals are fixed. Those are lunisolar: their months follow the Moon, with tithis, an extra month in some years, and regional differences between the amanta and purnimanta systems. They share month names with the national calendar, but the dates do not match. Diwali, Holi and other festivals follow the traditional reckoning, which is why their common-calendar dates change every year, while 1 Chaitra of the national calendar is always 22 or 21 March.

This page converts only the national calendar. It does not compute tithis or festival dates.

Which Saka year is 2027?

Saka 1948 until 21 March 2027, and Saka 1949 from 22 March 2027.

Is the Saka calendar the same as Vikram Samvat?

No. Vikram Samvat is a different era, about 57 years ahead of the common era, and is reckoned with lunar months. The Saka era of the national calendar is 78 years behind the common era.
