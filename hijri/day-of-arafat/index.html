Hijri Calendar › Holidays

Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

What it is

The Day of Arafat is the ninth of Dhu al-Hijjah and the most important day of the Hajj. Pilgrims gather on the plain of Arafat, near Mecca, from noon until sunset, in prayer and asking forgiveness. Without being present at Arafat the Hajj is not valid. It is remembered as the day on which the Prophet delivered his farewell sermon.

Muslims who are not on the pilgrimage are encouraged to fast on this day. The Prophet is reported to have said that fasting it erases the sins of the year before and the year after. For pilgrims, it is not recommended, so that they have strength for the prayers.

Because the date depends on the Hajj calendar of Saudi Arabia, communities elsewhere that follow their own moon sighting may observe it a day later. The date in the table follows the Umm al-Qura calendar, which Saudi Arabia uses for its civil dates; the Hajj ministry may announce a different day after sighting the moon.

How the date moves

In 2026 it falls on 26 May and in 2035 on 18 February, so across ten years it drifts through the Gregorian year, about ten or eleven days earlier every year, and now and then jumps back by about three weeks when the calendar catches up.

Over these ten years the weekday count is: Tuesday (3), Friday (2), Sunday (2), Saturday (1), Thursday (1), Monday (1). A lunar year has 354 or 355 days, 11 or 10 days fewer than a Gregorian one, so no observance keeps a Gregorian date. It returns to the same season about every 33 years.

Plan around uncertainty

Treat the date as the earliest likely one. If you must book travel, leave from work, or schedule an exam, wait for the announcement in your own country, usually the evening before; moon sighting can shift the date by a day, and for some countries the observance follows Saudi Arabia, while for others it follows local sighting.
