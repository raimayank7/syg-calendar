Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

What it is

Ashura falls on the tenth day of Muharram. For Sunni Muslims it recalls the day on which, in the tradition, Musa (Moses) and his people were saved from Pharaoh, and the Prophet Muhammad fasted on it and encouraged fasting also on the day before or after it. Many Sunni Muslims fast one or two days; it is voluntary, and the fast of Ramadan is the only fast that is obligatory.

For Shia Muslims it is a day of deep mourning. It marks the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali, a grandson of the Prophet, at Karbala in the year 61 AH (680 CE). Processions, recitations of the story of Karbala and gatherings for the poor mark the first ten days of Muharram, and in some countries, including India, Pakistan, Iraq and Iran, Ashura is a public holiday and processions take place.

The word Ashura comes from the Arabic for 'ten'. Because there are two traditions with very different moods, it is worth knowing which tradition the people around you follow before you send greetings. Many people choose to say nothing cheerful on the day.

How the date moves

In 2026 it falls on 25 June and in 2035 on 20 March, so across ten years it drifts through the Gregorian year, about ten or eleven days earlier every year, and now and then jumps back by about three weeks when the calendar catches up.

Over these ten years the weekday count is: Tuesday (3), Friday (2), Thursday (1), Saturday (1), Wednesday (1), Monday (1), Sunday (1). A lunar year has 354 or 355 days, 11 or 10 days fewer than a Gregorian one, so no observance keeps a Gregorian date. It returns to the same season about every 33 years.

Plan around uncertainty

Treat the date as the earliest likely one. If you must book travel, leave from work, or schedule an exam, wait for the announcement in your own country, usually the evening before; moon sighting can shift the date by a day, and for some countries the observance follows Saudi Arabia, while for others it follows local sighting.
