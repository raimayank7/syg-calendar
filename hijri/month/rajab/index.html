Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

About Rajab

Rajab means 'to respect' and is one of the four sacred months. It prepares the way for Sha'ban and Ramadan. Many Muslims remember the Isra and Mi'raj, the Prophet's night journey to Jerusalem and ascension, on the night of the 27th, and some fast voluntarily during the month.

This is one of the four sacred months (Muharram, Rajab, Dhu al-Qa'dah and Dhu al-Hijjah).

What happens in it

  • Isra and Mi'raj on the 27th

How long the month is

Of the 11 years in the table, Rajab has 29 days in 3 of them and 30 days in 8. A month begins when the new moon is seen, or in the Umm al-Qura table when it can be calculated to be visible, so its length is 29 or 30 days and cannot be predicted by a fixed rule. That is why a table is needed and why the same month can have a different length from year to year.

Where it falls in the Gregorian year

The month starts in December in 1447 AH and in September in 1457 AH. It drifts about ten or eleven days earlier each Gregorian year, so over 33 years it passes through every season.

Planning notes

In 1448 AH the month runs from Thursday, 10 December 2026 to Friday, 8 January 2027, 30 days. The year-by-year table above gives the first and last day of the month in each Hijri year; open a year page to see the whole year in one view. Months do not keep their place in the Gregorian calendar, so a date you remember from last year is not a guide to the date this year.

Counting from the first of the month, the first Friday falls on day 2 in 1448 AH and the first Sunday on day 4; useful if you plan weekly gatherings, classes or travel inside the month.
