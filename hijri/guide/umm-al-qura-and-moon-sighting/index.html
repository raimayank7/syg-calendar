Hijri Calendar

Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

Umm al-Qura and Moon Sighting: Why Dates Differ by a Day

Why Ramadan and Eid can start on different days in different countries: the Umm al-Qura calculation versus local moon sighting, and what to do about it.

Two ways to start a month

A Hijri month begins when the new crescent moon shows after the astronomical new moon. People have done this in two ways. In the first, committees of observers go out at sunset on the 29th and look for the crescent; if it is seen, the new month starts next day, and if not, the old month completes 30 days. In the second, the start of each month is calculated in advance by rules that say when the crescent should be visible.

Umm al-Qura

Saudi Arabia uses a calculated calendar called Umm al-Qura for official dates such as the dates of government salaries and school terms. The rule, in short, is that a month starts on the next day when the astronomical new moon occurs before sunset in Mecca and the moon sets after the sun. The tables are published in advance, so this site can list dates ten years ahead. For the holy months, including Ramadan and Dhu al-Hijjah, the authorities have in recent years also relied on sighting, and their announcement may differ from the table.

Why India, Pakistan and Bangladesh are often a day later

Many countries require a local sighting. The crescent is visible further west first, so Saudi Arabia, further west, may see it a day before South Asia. Some years the whole of the region starts on the same day; in others, Eid in Saudi Arabia is a day before Eid in India. Within one country, different committees sometimes announce different days.

What to do

  • Treat dates on this site as the earliest likely date.
  • Listen for the announcement of your country's moon-sighting committee or religious authority, usually on the evening of the 29th.
  • For travel and leave from work, ask for flexible days around Eid.

This site does not decide when a month starts, and nothing here is a religious ruling.

A note on the word "official"

An official calendar is a convenience for civil life: it tells everyone, in advance, which day an office is closed. It is not itself an act of worship. The start of Ramadan and the two Eids are matters on which religious authorities in each country announce their own decision, and Muslims follow it. Some communities follow the announcement of Saudi Arabia, some follow their own country, some a global sighting rule. The difference is a recognised one among scholars and is not a sign of error on either side.
