Hijri Calendar › Holidays

Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

What it is

Eid al-Adha, 'the festival of sacrifice', is the tenth of Dhu al-Hijjah, the day after Arafat, and the second major festival of Islam. It remembers the willingness of Ibrahim (Abraham) to sacrifice his son at God's command, and the ram that was provided in his place. It is celebrated by Muslims everywhere and also by the pilgrims at Mina, near Mecca, who are completing the Hajj.

The day begins with the Eid prayer. Afterwards, Muslims who can afford it sacrifice a sheep, goat, cow or camel, and the meat is divided into three parts: one for the family, one for relatives and friends, one for the poor. In India, the festival is called Bakrid, in Turkey Kurban Bayram, in Pakistan Bakra Eid. It lasts three or four days, and is a public holiday in most countries with a large Muslim population.

In many places, laws for animal slaughter apply to the sacrifice: sacrifices are to be done in licensed places, and rules differ by state or city. Check with your local authority.

How the date moves

In 2026 it falls on 27 May and in 2035 on 19 February, so across ten years it drifts through the Gregorian year, about ten or eleven days earlier every year, and now and then jumps back by about three weeks when the calendar catches up.

Over these ten years the weekday count is: Wednesday (3), Saturday (2), Monday (2), Sunday (1), Friday (1), Tuesday (1). A lunar year has 354 or 355 days, 11 or 10 days fewer than a Gregorian one, so no observance keeps a Gregorian date. It returns to the same season about every 33 years.

Plan around uncertainty

Treat the date as the earliest likely one. If you must book travel, leave from work, or schedule an exam, wait for the announcement in your own country, usually the evening before; moon sighting can shift the date by a day, and for some countries the observance follows Saudi Arabia, while for others it follows local sighting.
