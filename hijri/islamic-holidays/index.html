Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

Reading the list

The Islamic calendar is lunar: twelve months of 29 or 30 days, so a year is 354 or 355 days. The festivals therefore move about ten or eleven days earlier in the Gregorian calendar every year and return to the same season about every 33 years. Ramadan began in February in 2026 and in January by 2028, and it comes round to the same season only after about 33 years.

The dates are those of the Umm al-Qura calendar. Moon-sighting countries may differ by a day: see the guide. Muslims also keep many days that are not public holidays: the six voluntary fasts of Shawwal, the white days of the 13th, 14th and 15th of each month, and Mondays and Thursdays. They are not listed here because they repeat every month and do not need a table.

The dates also show the evening before: an Islamic day starts at sunset, so a festival that is on Thursday in the table begins on Wednesday evening.
