Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

The Hijri year

The Hijri year has twelve months of 29 or 30 days, starting with Muharram and ending with Dhu al-Hijjah. Four are sacred: Muharram, Rajab, Dhu al-Qa'dah and Dhu al-Hijjah. The year is counted from 622 CE, the year of the Hijra. A new month begins at the sighting of the new crescent, and each day begins at sunset.

Because the year is about 11 days shorter than a solar year, every month moves earlier through the seasons. Open a month for its dates.

Which month is which

Month 1 is Muharram, the new year. Month 9 is Ramadan, the month of fasting, and month 10, Shawwal, opens with Eid al-Fitr. Month 12, Dhu al-Hijjah, holds the Hajj and Eid al-Adha. Four months are sacred and the others are ordinary months, but each has its own page here with the dates for 1447 to 1457 AH, its length in each year and the observances that fall in it.

The names are written in different ways in English: Rabi’ al-awwal also appears as Rabi al-Awal, Dhu al-Hijjah as Zul Hijjah, Sha’ban as Shaban. The Arabic spellings in the table are the same everywhere.
