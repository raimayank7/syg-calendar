Hijri Calendar › Holidays

Dates can differ by a day or more. Islamic months begin with the sighting of the new moon, and countries and religious authorities decide differently, so Ramadan, Eid and other dates may be observed one day (sometimes two) later or earlier than shown. The dates on this site are calculated by the Umm al-Qura method, the official civil calendar of Saudi Arabia (King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology, ummulqura.org.sa). They are an estimate for planning, not an announcement: wait for your own authority's announcement.

What it is

Isra and Mi'raj is the Night Journey. According to tradition, in one night the Prophet Muhammad was carried from the sacred mosque in Mecca to the farthest mosque in Jerusalem (the Isra) and then ascended through the heavens (the Mi'raj), where the five daily prayers were prescribed. The journey is mentioned in the Qur'an, in chapter 17, and described in greater detail in the traditions.

Most communities remember the night of the 27th of Rajab, though the exact date has been debated since early times and not every community marks it. In many countries it is a night of extra prayer, reading about the journey and giving to the poor. It is a public holiday in some countries, among them Indonesia and Malaysia, and not in others.

Because an Islamic day begins at sunset, the night of the 27th comes on the evening before the Gregorian date given for the 27th. Keep that in mind when you plan: if the table says a Thursday, the night is Wednesday evening.

How the date moves

In 2026 it falls on 16 January and in 2035 on 29 September, so across ten years it drifts through the Gregorian year, about ten or eleven days earlier every year, and now and then jumps back by about three weeks when the calendar catches up.

Over these ten years the weekday count is: Tuesday (3), Saturday (3), Friday (2), Thursday (2), Monday (1). A lunar year has 354 or 355 days, 11 or 10 days fewer than a Gregorian one, so no observance keeps a Gregorian date. It returns to the same season about every 33 years.

Plan around uncertainty

Treat the date as the earliest likely one. If you must book travel, leave from work, or schedule an exam, wait for the announcement in your own country, usually the evening before; moon sighting can shift the date by a day, and for some countries the observance follows Saudi Arabia, while for others it follows local sighting.
