Milestone days

A milestone day is the date that is exactly that many days after the day you were born: birth date plus 10,000 days, for example. Most people reach their 10,000th day at the age of 27 years and 4 to 5 months, and their 20,000th day at 54 and a half. One billion seconds is 11,574.07 days, or about 31 years and 8 months; the tool uses the whole number of days. The dates are calendar arithmetic, checked against Python for 3,000 birth dates.

How age is counted

Your age in years goes up on your birthday. The calculator counts whole years first, then whole months, then the remaining days. If you were born on 29 February, your birthday comes on 28 February in years that have no leap day. The totals of days, weeks, hours and minutes count from the start of the day of birth to the start of the other date, so a baby born today is zero days old.

Not an age calculator only

This site adds what a plain age calculator leaves out: the dates of milestone days, the weekday of your birth and of your next birthday, heartbeats and the planets. For a plain age in years, months and days, any age calculator gives the same numbers, and ours is tested to do so.

For fun

The planet ages show how old you would be if a year were as long as one orbit of each planet round the sun: Mercury's year is 88 Earth days, and Neptune's is nearly 165 Earth years. The heartbeat count assumes 70 beats a minute; the real number depends on you and changes with fitness, age and activity. Nothing on this site is a medical or legal age proof.
