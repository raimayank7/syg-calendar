What the numbers mean

Your mass, the amount of matter in your body, is the same everywhere. Your weight is the pull of gravity on that mass, and gravity is stronger on larger planets. A scale that is made to show kilograms on Earth shows a different number elsewhere, in proportion to the surface gravity: 235.7 kg for every 100 kg on Earth on Jupiter and 37.8 kg on Mars. The numbers use the surface gravity of NASA's Planetary Fact Sheet. For Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune the sheet gives gravity at the level where the pressure is one bar, since those planets have no solid surface to stand on.

Weight on the moon is about one sixth of that on Earth (gravity 1.6 m/s2 in the same table).
