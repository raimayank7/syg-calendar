Neptune in numbers

Neptune goes round the sun once in 59,800 Earth days, so a person who is 30 on Earth is 0.18 Neptune-years old, and a baby born today would not have a first birthday there until 59,800 Earth days (163.72 Earth years) had passed. The length of day on Neptune, measured as one rotation relative to the sun, is 16.1 hours. The surface gravity in NASA's table is 11.0 m/s2 compared with 9.8 on Earth, so a 60 kg person would be shown as 67.3 kg on a bathroom scale there (at the level where the pressure equals that of Earth's air; there is no surface to stand on). The mean temperature is -200 degrees Celsius and the planet has 16 known moons.

How the age is calculated

Your age in Neptune years is the number of days since you were born divided by the length of a Neptune year in days (59,800.0), taken from NASA's Planetary Fact Sheet. Birthdays on Neptune would come at those intervals, but nobody can have a birthday there on a particular date; the number is only for fun.
