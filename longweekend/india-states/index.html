Why most states are not on the list yet

Each Indian state decides its own holidays, usually by a notification of its General Administration, Personnel or Home department, and the notification for the next year often appears between November and January. On 13 November 2026 only three of the 33 states and union territories had an official 2027 list we could read: Meghalaya (dated 15 October 2026), Kerala (dated 1 October 2026, published in the Gazette on 18 October) and Puducherry (dated 5 November 2026). Odisha and Gujarat were reported by news sites to have announced theirs, but the official text could not be read. Punjab's 2026 list was published in December 2025, so a 2027 list from Punjab is expected from about December 2026.

What to use meanwhile

  • The Central Government list for 2027 (Delhi offices: 17 gazetted holidays; offices elsewhere: 14 compulsory holidays plus three from twelve optional ones) is official and is on the calendar section. Many states follow its dates for the big festivals but add their own days.
  • Banks follow the Reserve Bank of India's list for each state; schools follow their own state education department.
  • Dates of moon-based festivals (Id-ul-Fitr, Id-ul-Zuha, Milad-un-Nabi, Muharram) are marked "subject to change" in the notifications and can move by a day.

How this page is kept

When a state publishes its 2027 list, it is read from the notification itself, typed into the data of this site, checked against the weekday printed in the notification, and given its own page of long weekends and bridge days like Meghalaya, Kerala and Puducherry. States without a list are not guessed from earlier years.
