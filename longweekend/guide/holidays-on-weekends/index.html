When a Holiday Falls on a Weekend

What happens when a public holiday lands on a Saturday or Sunday: lost days, observed days and moved holidays, and how this site treats them.

Three possible rules

Countries handle a holiday on a weekend in one of three ways. In some, the holiday is simply lost: nothing changes for those with a Saturday-Sunday weekend. In some, the holiday is observed on the nearest weekday: a Saturday holiday on Friday, a Sunday holiday on Monday. In some, a substitute day is given later, often the following Monday.

How this site treats them

The tables show the date as listed by the holiday data source. A holiday on a weekend is counted as a weekend holiday and gives no extra day off in the plans, even if your country moves it. The page for each country counts how many holidays fall on a weekend in each year, so that you can see the loss, and you should add the observed day yourself if your country observes it.

Why it matters

The number of weekday holidays differs between years. A year in which Christmas Day and New Year's Day are on a Saturday and a Sunday has fewer days off in countries that do not observe them. The same fixed-date holidays move forward by one weekday each year, and two weekdays after a leap day, so the pattern repeats only every 28 years or so in the middle of a century. Moving holidays such as Easter Monday always fall on a Monday, so they always make a long weekend.
