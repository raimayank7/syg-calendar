What the 2028 calendar looks like in Hong Kong

The list above has 17 national public holidays for 2028. 14 of them fall on a Monday to Friday, which are the ones that give a day off work; 3 fall on a Saturday or Sunday (New Year's Day, Holy Saturday and Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Establishment Day), which mean no extra day off unless the country moves the holiday, as many do by rule or decree.

By weekday, 5 fall on a Monday, 2 on a Friday and 7 in the middle of the week. Mondays and Fridays make a long weekend of three days without any leave; holidays on Tuesday or Thursday leave one working day between the holiday and the weekend, which is the bridge day. The mid-week ones are Lunar New Year (Wednesday), Second day of Lunar New Year (Thursday), Ching Ming Festival (Tuesday), Buddha's Birthday (Tuesday), Day following the Mid-Autumn Festival (Wednesday), Chung Yeung Festival (Thursday) and Boxing Day (Tuesday).

The longest break without leave

The longest run of days off made by holidays and weekends alone is 26 to 30 January: 5 days, around Lunar New Year, Second day of Lunar New Year, Third day of Lunar New Year.

The best use of leave days

The most efficient plan is to take 3 October (Tuesday) off: that gives 5 days off in a row from 30 September to 4 October, a return of 5.0 days off for each day of leave. Plans are sorted by that return, and each uses only whole working days between weekends and holidays; they may overlap, so check before you combine them.

2028 against 2027

In 2027 there are 7 long weekends from holidays alone and the best plan gives 4 days off for 1 day of leave; in 2028 there are 6 and 5 days off for 1 day. The changes come from the weekday on which each holiday falls: the same date moves forward by one weekday each year, two in a leap year. Open the 2027 page for Hong Kong.
