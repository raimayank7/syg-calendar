Lunar and solar

Most traditional Chinese festivals follow the lunar calendar and move around the Gregorian calendar; Qingming follows the sun, with a date of 4 or 5 April, which is why it is the only one that stays almost fixed. Each festival has its own year page: 2026 2027 2028 2029 2030.

What each festival is

  • Spring Festival: the lunar new year, a family reunion with a feast on the eve and red envelopes.
  • Lantern Festival: the 15th day, the first full moon of the year, which ends the new year season with lanterns and sweet rice balls.
  • Qingming: a day for visiting and cleaning the graves of ancestors in early spring.
  • Dragon Boat Festival: dragon boat races and rice dumplings (zongzi), remembering the poet Qu Yuan by tradition.
  • Qixi: the seventh evening of the seventh month, a day of love often compared to Valentine day.
  • Mid-Autumn Festival: the full moon of autumn, with mooncakes and family gatherings.
  • Double Ninth: climbing hills and honouring older people.

These are short descriptions of widely known customs; local customs differ between regions and communities.
