Why the date moves

The Chinese calendar is lunisolar: months follow the moon and an extra (leap) month is added about every three years to keep the new year near the start of spring. Lunar New Year therefore falls on a different Gregorian date each year, always between 21 January and 20 February. It is the second new moon after the winter solstice in most years. Earliest and latest in the table: Thursday, 22 January 2004 and Thursday, 19 February 2015.

January or February

In the 51 years from 2000 to 2050, Lunar New Year falls in January 19 times and in February 32 times. The animal years with 13 lunar months (so 383 to 385 days instead of about 354) are 2001, 2004, 2006, 2009, 2012, 2014, 2017, 2020, 2023, 2025, 2028, 2031, 2033, 2036, 2039, 2042, 2044, 2047; each has a leap month, which is how the calendar stays in step with the seasons.

Using the table

Open a year for the dates of the other festivals, or an animal for all its years. For people born in January or February, check the date of the new year in the table: those born before it belong to the animal of the year before.
