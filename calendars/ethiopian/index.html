The Ethiopian Calendar

The Ethiopian calendar has twelve months of 30 days and a short thirteenth month. Its new year, Enkutatash, falls on 11 or 12 September.

How it works

Each of the first twelve months has 30 days. The thirteenth month, Pagume, has 5 days, or 6 in a leap year. A leap year comes every four years with no century exception, which is why the Ethiopian calendar keeps a fixed relation to the Julian calendar. The year number is about seven or eight years behind the Gregorian year, depending on the date, because the Ethiopian church counts the year of the incarnation differently from the Western church.

Months

The months are Meskerem, Tikimt, Hidar, Tahsas, Tir, Yekatit, Megabit, Miazia, Genbot, Sene, Hamle, Nehase and Pagume. The Coptic calendar used in Egypt is the same calendar with different month names and a year number 276 lower.

New year

Enkutatash, the Ethiopian new year, is on 11 September, or on 12 September in the year before a Gregorian leap year. In 2027 it is on 12 September and the new year is 2020. The converter gives the Ethiopian date of any day from 1900 to 2200.
