The Indian National Calendar (Saka)

The Indian national calendar, also called the Saka calendar, has 365 days, begins with Chaitra on 22 March, or 21 March in a leap year, and was adopted in 1957.

How it works

The Saka calendar was adopted as the national calendar of India on 22 March 1957, on the recommendation of the Calendar Reform Committee. It counts years from the Saka era, which begins 78 years after the Gregorian era, so the Saka year that starts in March of Gregorian year Y is Y - 78. Its first month, Chaitra, has 30 days (31 in a leap year) and starts on 22 March, or 21 March in a Gregorian leap year. The next five months, Vaisakha to Bhadra, have 31 days and the last six, Asvina to Phalguna, have 30 days.

Months

Chaitra, Vaisakha, Jyaistha, Asadha, Sravana, Bhadra, Asvina, Kartika, Agrahayana, Pausa, Magha, Phalguna. The national calendar is used by the Gazette of India, All India Radio and government calendars together with the Gregorian date. It is a solar calendar with fixed month lengths, different from the traditional lunisolar panchang used for festivals, so Diwali and other festival dates cannot be found with it.
