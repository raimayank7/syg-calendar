Daylight Saving Time and Sunrise: What Changes and What Does Not

Daylight saving time changes the clock, not the sun. How it shifts sunrise and sunset on the clock, and which countries change.

The sun does not change

On the day clocks go forward, the sun rises and sets at the same moment as it would have done. It is the number on the clock that shifts by an hour, so sunrise and sunset appear an hour later by the clock. In spring that makes evenings lighter and mornings darker; in autumn the opposite.

What our tables do

Every table on this site uses the clock time in force on that day, taken from the IANA time-zone database for 2027. On the date of a change, you will see a jump of 60 minutes (30 in a few places). Cities such as Delhi, Singapore, Nairobi and Lima, which do not change their clocks, glide through the year without a jump.

Who changes

Most of Europe, North America and parts of Australia, New Zealand and South America change. India, China, Japan, most of Africa and most of Asia do not. Rules are set by governments and have changed often; the database used here is the one that computers use, but a government may change the rule at short notice.

Checking your own clocks

If your phone is set to automatic time zone, it follows the rules without help. Computers and wall clocks that are changed by hand often drift by the hour for a few days. If a meeting or a prayer time looks wrong in the week of the change, check which side of the change each person is on, because countries do not all change on the same date: in 2027 the United States and Europe, for instance, change clocks on different Sundays, which leaves a gap of a few weeks when the time difference between them is one hour more or less than usual.

Near the equator

Close to the equator day length hardly changes, so there is no reason to move the clocks, and almost no country there does. The further from the equator, the greater the difference between summer and winter daylight, and the more likely the country is to change its clocks.
