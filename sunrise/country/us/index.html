Reading the table

The two dates in the table are close to the longest and the shortest day of the year in the northern hemisphere; in the southern hemisphere it is the other way round. They are spread over 4 time zones: America/Chicago, America/Los_Angeles, America/New_York, America/Phoenix.

On 21 June, the longest day among these cities is in Chicago (15 h 14 min) and the shortest in San Antonio (14 h 02 min), a spread of 72 minutes. On 21 December the longest is in San Antonio (10 h 16 min) and the shortest in Chicago (9 h 08 min), a spread of 68 minutes. The spread comes from latitude: places further from the equator have a bigger change in daylight during the year.

The northernmost of these cities is Chicago at 41.9° and the southernmost San Antonio at 29.4°, a difference of 12.4 degrees of latitude (about 1379 km). Each degree of latitude changes the length of the longest day by a few minutes in temperate regions, which is why the two ends of a long country do not share the same daylight.

Each city name opens a page with the whole of 2027: a month-by-month summary, every day in a folding list, the longest and shortest days, the earliest and latest sunrise and sunset, and a comparison with the capital or the largest neighbouring city. Dates and times are local clock times, so they include any daylight saving change in force.

The list holds the cities of United States with the largest populations in the GeoNames data, plus its capital. For a smaller town, use the calculator on the home page with the place that is nearest: within about 50 kilometres east or west the clock times differ by less than four minutes, and north or south the day length differs by a minute or less.
