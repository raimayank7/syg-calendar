Reading the table

The two dates in the table are close to the longest and the shortest day of the year in the northern hemisphere; in the southern hemisphere it is the other way round. All of them use the time zone Asia/Kolkata.

On 21 June, the longest day among these cities is in Srinagar (14 h 26 min) and the shortest in Thiruvananthapuram (12 h 37 min), a spread of 109 minutes. On 21 December the longest is in Thiruvananthapuram (11 h 38 min) and the shortest in Srinagar (9 h 53 min), a spread of 105 minutes. The spread comes from latitude: places further from the equator have a bigger change in daylight during the year.

The northernmost of these cities is Srinagar at 34.1° and the southernmost Thiruvananthapuram at 8.5°, a difference of 25.6 degrees of latitude (about 2842 km). Each degree of latitude changes the length of the longest day by a few minutes in temperate regions, which is why the two ends of a long country do not share the same daylight.

Guwahati lies 21.7° east of Jamnagar while both keep the same clock, so the sun rises about 87 minutes earlier by the clock in Guwahati on the same date. This is the effect of longitude: four minutes for every degree.
