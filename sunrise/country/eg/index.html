Reading the table

The two dates in the table are close to the longest and the shortest day of the year in the northern hemisphere; in the southern hemisphere it is the other way round. All of them use the time zone Africa/Cairo.

On 21 June, the longest day among these cities is in Alexandria (14 h 11 min) and the shortest in Giza (14 h 05 min), a spread of 6 minutes. On 21 December the longest is in Giza (10 h 13 min) and the shortest in Alexandria (10 h 07 min), a spread of 6 minutes. The spread comes from latitude: places further from the equator have a bigger change in daylight during the year.

The northernmost of these cities is Alexandria at 31.2° and the southernmost Giza at 30.0°, a difference of 1.2 degrees of latitude (about 132 km). Each degree of latitude changes the length of the longest day by a few minutes in temperate regions, which is why the two ends of a long country do not share the same daylight.

Cairo lies 1.3° east of Alexandria while both keep the same clock, so the sun rises about 5 minutes earlier by the clock in Cairo on the same date. This is the effect of longitude: four minutes for every degree.

Dates and times are local clock times, so they include any daylight saving change in force.

The list holds the cities of Egypt with the largest populations in the GeoNames data, plus its capital.
