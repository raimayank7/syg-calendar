What the numbers show

Of the 602 cities on this site, 1 have a sun that does not set on 21 June (Longyearbyen) and 1 have a sun that does not rise on 21 December (Longyearbyen). In those places the sun stays above the horizon all day, a midnight sun, and in winter there is a polar night. The same latitude gives about the same day length in the north and in the south, but at opposite times of year. Near the equator, the daylight of the longest and the shortest day differs by a few minutes only: in Singapore, for example, it stays close to 12 hours all year.

Why the swing grows with latitude

The change in day length over the year depends on how far the sun climbs and falls in the sky, and that depends on how far from the equator you stand. At the equator the sun is always near overhead at noon and always rises and sets nearly vertically, so there is little difference. At 30° latitude the longest day is already about four hours longer than the shortest. At 60° it is about ten hours longer. Beyond 66.6° a whole day of sun or of darkness can occur. This is why the same four lists at the top of the page hold cities at very different latitudes.

These are pure calculations for 21 June and 21 December. The actual longest and shortest days are one day either side, and the differences are a matter of seconds.
