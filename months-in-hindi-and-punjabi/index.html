Months of the year in Hindi and Punjabi

India uses two sets of month names side by side: the twelve months of the common calendar, and the traditional months that follow the seasons and the Moon.

The traditional months

The traditional Hindu months, and the Punjabi "desi" months that come from the same roots, are the ones in which festivals, fasts, sowing and harvest are reckoned. Villagers across north India still speak of the heat of Jeth or the rains of Saawan.

The "falls roughly in" column is only a guide. In the traditional lunar reckoning each month begins with a new moon or a full moon, so its dates in the common calendar shift from year to year, and about every third year an extra month (अधिक मास, adhik maas) is added to keep the months in their seasons.

Why the same name can mean different dates

Three different calendars use these month names.

  1. The lunar panchang, by which most Hindu festivals are fixed. In north India a month usually runs from full moon to full moon (the purnimanta system); in Gujarat, Maharashtra and the south it runs from new moon to new moon (the amanta system). The same festival day can therefore carry two different month names in two regions.
  2. Solar regional calendars, in which a month begins when the Sun enters a new zodiac sign (the sankranti). The Punjabi months are reckoned this way in everyday use: Vaisakh begins at Vaisakhi in mid-April, and Magh begins at Maghi in mid-January, the day after Lohri. The Sikh Nanakshahi calendar fixes the same months to set dates.
  3. The Indian national calendar, the government's official calendar, in which Chaitra always begins on 22 March (21 March in a leap year) and the months have fixed lengths. See the Indian national calendar and the Saka date converter.

So before using a traditional date, ask which reckoning it belongs to. For festival dates, follow a panchang or the government's holiday list; this site prints only the officially announced dates on its holiday pages.

The six seasons

Months in songs and sayings

The traditional months live on in folk culture. The Barahmasa ("twelve months") is an old form of poem and song that follows a year month by month, usually through the feelings of someone waiting for a loved one. Gurbani includes Barah Maha compositions built on the twelve months from Chet to Phaggan. Farmers' sayings tie work to the months: sowing with the rains of Asadh and Saawan, harvest at Vaisakh.

What are the 12 months in Hindi?

जनवरी, फ़रवरी, मार्च, अप्रैल, मई, जून, जुलाई, अगस्त, सितंबर, अक्टूबर, नवंबर, दिसंबर. The traditional months are चैत्र, वैशाख, ज्येष्ठ, आषाढ़, श्रावण, भाद्रपद, आश्विन, कार्तिक, मार्गशीर्ष, पौष, माघ and फाल्गुन.

Which is the first month of the Hindu year?

Chaitra in most of India. Its first day in the lunar calendar is celebrated as Ugadi, Gudi Padwa and the start of the spring Navratri.

What is Saawan?

The everyday name of the month Shravan, the main month of the monsoon, falling in July and August.
