What it is

Eid al-Adha (Arabic: عيد الأضحى, romanized: ʿĪd al-ʾAḍḥā, lit.\u2009'Feast of the Sacrifice') is the second of the two main festivals in Islam (alongside Eid al-Fitr) and Druze. It falls on the 10th of Dhu'l-Hijja, the twelfth and final month of the Islamic calendar. Celebrations and observances are generally carried forward to the three following days, known as the Tashreeq days.

Eid al-Adha, depending on country and language is also called the Greater or Large Eid (Arabic: العيد الكبير, romanized: al-ʿĪd al-Kabīr). As with Eid al-Fitr, the Eid prayer is performed on the morning of Eid al-Adha, after which the udhiyah or the ritual sacrifice of a livestock animal, is performed. In Islamic tradition, it honours the willingness of Abraham to sacrifice his son as an act of obedience to God's command. Depending on the narrative, either Ishmael or Isaac are referred to with the honorific title "Sacrifice of God". Pilgrims performing the Hajj typically perform the tawaf and saee of Hajj on Eid al-Adha, along with the ritual stoning of the Devil on the Eid day and the following days.

How the date is decided

The Islamic calendar is purely lunar: twelve months of 29 or 30 days, a year of 354 or 355 days. Eid al-Adha moves about ten or eleven days earlier each Gregorian year. The dates here are from the Umm al-Qura calendar, the civil calendar of Saudi Arabia; many countries decide the start of the month by sighting the new moon and may observe Eid al-Adha a day later.

An Islamic day begins at sunset, so the evening before the date is the start. See the Hijri calendar section for the converter and the other Islamic holidays.

Questions people ask

When is Eid al-Adha in 2027?

Eid al-Adha falls on Sunday, 16 May 2027 in 2027 (the evening before, for the start).

Why does the date of Eid al-Adha change every year?

The Islamic year is lunar, about 11 days shorter than a solar year, so festivals move earlier each year.

Is the date the same in every country?

Mostly, but not always. Lunar festivals start at sunset or at moon sighting, which differ by place and time zone, and traditions in different regions can choose different days.
