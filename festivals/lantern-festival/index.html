What it is

The Lantern Festival (traditional Chinese: 元宵節; simplified Chinese: 元宵节; pinyin: Yuánxiāo jié), also called the Shangyuan Festival or the Yuanxiao Festival, is a Chinese traditional festival celebrated on the fifteenth day of the first month in the lunisolar Chinese calendar, during the full moon. Usually falling in February or early March on the Gregorian calendar, it marks the final day of the traditional Chinese New Year celebrations. As early as the Western Han dynasty (206 BC–AD 25), it had become a festival with great significance.

During the Lantern Festival, children go out at night carrying paper lanterns and solving riddles written on them (traditional Chinese: 猜燈謎; simplified Chinese: 猜灯谜; pinyin: cāidēngmí). In ancient times, lanterns were fairly simple, and only the emperor and noblemen had large, ornate ones. In modern times, lanterns have been embellished with intricate designs. For example, lanterns are now often made in the shape of animals. The lanterns can symbolize the people letting go of their past selves and getting new ones, which they will let go of the next year. The lanterns are almost always red to symbolize good fortune.

The festival acts as an Uposatha day on the Chinese calendar. It should not be confused with the Mid-Autumn Festival, which is sometimes also known as the "Lantern Festival" in locations such as Indonesia, Malaysia, and Singapore. Lantern Festivals have also become popular in Western countries, such as the Water Lantern Festival held in multiple locations in the United States.

How the date is decided

The traditional Chinese calendar is lunisolar. A month begins at the new moon and a leap month is added about every three years to keep the calendar in step with the seasons. Chinese New Year is the first day of the first month, always the new moon closest to the start of spring, which puts it between 21 January and 20 February. Lantern Festival is on day 15 of month 1.

Each lunar year has an animal in the twelve-year cycle: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig. The animal changes at Chinese New Year, not on 1 January. The dates here come from the Hong Kong Observatory tables as packaged in an open-source library.

Questions people ask

When is Lantern Festival in 2027?

Lantern Festival falls on Saturday, 20 February 2027 in 2027.

Why does the date of Lantern Festival change every year?

The Chinese calendar follows the moon and the sun, so it moves between 21 January and 20 February.

Is the date the same in every country?

Mostly, but not always. Lunar festivals start at sunset or at moon sighting, which differ by place and time zone, and traditions in different regions can choose different days.
