What it is

Mawlid (Arabic: مولد) is an annual festival commemorating the birthday of the Islamic prophet Muhammad on the date of 12 Rabi I, the third month of the Islamic calendar.

The Muslim general Gökböri, a deputy of Saladin (r.\u20091174–1193), is believed to have been the first to publicly celebrate Mawlid, which he did in an impressive ceremony at the Prophet's Mosque in Medina. The Ottomans under Sultan Murad III (r.\u20091574–1595) declared it an official holiday.

Celebrants hold mahfils on Mawlid in which religious poetry is recited in praise of Muhammad accompanied by a feast. Other customs affiliated with Mawlid are supererogatory fasting, prayer and dhikr.

Mawlid observance is a recognized national holiday in most Muslim-majority countries of the world. It is generally approved by the Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi'i schools of Sunni Islam, as well as by Shia Muslims. However, Salafism and Wahhabism regard celebration of the Mawlid a reprehensible bid'a (innovation) and prohibit its observance. Deobandi jurists take an intermediate position, viewing the matter as an issue of Ikhtilaf (scholarly disagreement).

How the date is decided

The Islamic calendar is purely lunar: twelve months of 29 or 30 days, a year of 354 or 355 days. Mawlid moves about ten or eleven days earlier each Gregorian year. The dates here are from the Umm al-Qura calendar, the civil calendar of Saudi Arabia; many countries decide the start of the month by sighting the new moon and may observe Mawlid a day later.

An Islamic day begins at sunset, so the evening before the date is the start. See the Hijri calendar section for the converter and the other Islamic holidays.

Questions people ask

When is Mawlid in 2027?

Mawlid falls on Saturday, 14 August 2027 in 2027 (the evening before, for the start).

Why does the date of Mawlid change every year?

The Islamic year is lunar, about 11 days shorter than a solar year, so festivals move earlier each year.

Is the date the same in every country?

Mostly, but not always. Lunar festivals start at sunset or at moon sighting, which differ by place and time zone, and traditions in different regions can choose different days.
