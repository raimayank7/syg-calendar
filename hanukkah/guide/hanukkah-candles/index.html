Hanukkah candles

Eight nights, one more candle each night, and a helper candle.

Hanukkah lasts eight nights. A menorah for Hanukkah, a hanukkiah, has nine places: eight for the nights and a ninth, higher or set apart, for the helper candle, the shamash, which is used to light the others. On the first night one candle is lit besides the shamash, on the second night two, and so on up to eight on the last night, which makes 36 candles in all, not counting the shamash (44 with it).

The order

The first night's candle is placed at the right end, as you face the menorah. On each following night a new candle is added to its left, and the candles are lit from the newest, at the left, towards the right. Candles are lit after nightfall, or shortly before sunset on Friday, because Hanukkah candles may not be lit after the Sabbath begins; on Saturday night they are lit after the Sabbath ends.

Why eight nights

Hanukkah remembers the rededication of the Temple in Jerusalem in the second century BC, and the tradition that a small amount of oil lasted eight days. Many families also eat foods fried in oil, play dreidel and give gifts or coins.

Check your tradition

Customs differ between communities, for example about the position of the menorah and the blessings. This page describes the commonly followed practice; ask your own rabbi for the rule you keep.
