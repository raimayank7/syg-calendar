  • the ISO week number with the Monday and Sunday that bound the week;
  • the date in the Indian national calendar (Saka);
  • whether the year is a leap year.

Working out the weekday in your head

There is a neat method, often called the doomsday method, that needs no paper once practised.

Step 1: learn the anchor dates. In any one year these dates all fall on the same weekday: 4 April (4/4), 6 June (6/6), 8 August (8/8), 10 October (10/10), 12 December (12/12), and also 9 May, 5 September, 11 July and 7 November. The last day of February joins them: 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year.

Step 2: know that weekday for the year. For 2027 the anchor weekday is Sunday: 4 April, 6 June, 8 August, 10 October and 12 December 2027 are all Sundays. The anchor moves forward one weekday each ordinary year and two after a leap day, so for 2028 it is Tuesday and for 2029 it is Wednesday.

Step 3: count from the nearest anchor. What day is 15 August 2027? The anchor 8 August is a Sunday; 15 August is exactly a week later, so it is a Sunday too. What day is 2 October 2027? The anchor 10 October is a Sunday, so 3 October is a Sunday and 2 October is a Saturday.

You can check each of these with the tool above.

Why the same date moves through the week

An ordinary year has 365 days, which is 52 weeks and 1 day. So each date falls one weekday later the next year. A leap year has 52 weeks and 2 days, so dates after February jump two weekdays. That is why your birthday moves along the week from year to year, and why a calendar can be reused only in certain years. The same calendar years page lists them.

Famous dates to try

  • 15 August 1947, the day India became independent: a Friday.
  • 26 January 1950, when the Constitution of India came into force: a Thursday.
  • 1 January 2000: a Saturday.

A caution about very old dates

The tool applies the calendar we use today, the Gregorian calendar, to every year. That calendar began in October 1582, and countries adopted it at different times: Britain and its colonies, including British India, in September 1752. Before the change, people dated events in the Julian calendar, which had drifted by ten or eleven days. A weekday computed here for a date before the change is the weekday of that date in the modern calendar, not necessarily the weekday people living then would have named.

Does every year start on a different weekday?

Each year starts one weekday later than the year before, or two weekdays later if the earlier year was a leap year.

How many times can the 13th fall on a Friday in one year?

At least once and at most three times in any year.
