What is ZHR?

The zenithal hourly rate is the number of meteors an ideal observer would see in an hour at best.

The International Meteor Organization defines the ZHR, the zenithal hourly rate, as the calculated maximum number of meteors an ideal observer would see in perfectly clear skies, with the faintest stars visible to magnitude +6.5 and the radiant of the shower overhead. It is given in meteors per hour. It is a way of comparing showers, not what you will see on the night: with the radiant lower in the sky, with a bright moon, haze or city lights, the real rate is often a third or a tenth of the ZHR.

Other columns in the table

Radiant is the point in the sky from which the meteors seem to come, given by right ascension and declination. V∞ is the speed in kilometres a second: about 11 is very slow, 40 medium and up to 72 very fast. r is the population index: a smaller number means more bright meteors. A plus sign after a ZHR means that the rate can be higher; "Var" means the shower varies from year to year.

Making the most of a shower

Choose the night of the peak or the night before or after, find a dark place away from lights, let your eyes adjust for 20 to 30 minutes and look at a wide part of the sky rather than at the radiant. Check the page of the shower for the moon on that night.
