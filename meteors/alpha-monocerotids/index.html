Alpha Monocerotids in 2027

The Alpha Monocerotids (IMO code 246 AMO) are active from Nov 15 to Nov 25 and are expected to peak on 22 November 2027. At its best this shower can give up to about Var meteors an hour to an ideal observer under a perfectly dark, clear sky with the radiant overhead; in practice you will see fewer. The meteors seem to come from a point at right ascension 117° and declination +1° (north or south of the celestial equator by that many degrees), travelling at 65 km/s, which is fast.

The moon on the night

On 22 November the moon is waning crescent, 34% lit and 23.9 days after the new moon. That means the moon brightens the sky for part of the night, so go out when it is low or has set. A bright moon hides the faint meteors and leaves the bright ones, so the rate you see can be much lower than the ZHR.

Where to look

Meteors can appear anywhere in the sky; trace one back to its start and you find the radiant, the point where the shower seems to begin. The table above gives the height of the radiant at 03:00 local solar time in four cities: a radiant high in the sky gives the best rates, and a radiant below the horizon gives no meteors from this shower. The heights are approximate, calculated for the radiant of the table, which drifts a little through the shower.
